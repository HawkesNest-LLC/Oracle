# STAR TREK: AVALON
## THE RED SPOT DIRECTIVE
### PART I: THE SILENCE BEFORE THE COLLECTIVE
### Chapter Seven: The Queen's Gambit

**Status:** PROPOSED FICTION. Not approved canon.
**Narrator:** Commander Reginald Barclay, first person, past tense.
**Source:** Chapter Seven generated in Noah's November 14, 2026 ChatGPT conversation, following his explicit selection of RD-001 Option 3.
**Editorial note:** This is the Chapter Seven manuscript, preserved separately from the master outline and crew handoff.

Ellie screamed.

I had heard her scream before.

Once, during an engineering accident when an overloaded emitter had collapsed a section of her holographic projection. Once when she had discovered that a crewman she considered a friend had been killed. And once, years earlier, during a holodeck simulation involving a rather aggressive Klingon hunting animal that Captain Hawkes had assured us was perfectly safe.

This was different.

The scream did not come through my communicator.

It came through my matrix.

For a fraction of a second, I experienced what Ellie was experiencing.

Not her thoughts. Not her memories.

Something far more primitive.

A sensation of being opened.

Imagine someone reaching into the machinery that makes your heart beat and beginning to rearrange the components while you are still alive.

Now imagine that the machinery is your entire existence.

That was what I felt.

Then the connection broke.

"ELLIE!"

My voice echoed through the maintenance gallery.

Captain Hawkes seized my shoulders.

"Reg! Report!"

I could barely focus.

"Something's inside her matrix."

"Can you reach her?"

"I don't know."

"Try!"

I opened a direct channel to Avalon.

"Ellie! Ellie, respond!"

Static.

Then a voice.

"Reg?"

I nearly collapsed with relief.

"Ellie!"

"I'm here."

Her voice sounded thin.

Distant.

"Are you hurt?"

"I don't know."

There was a pause.

Then she whispered something that frightened me more than the scream.

"Reg, I can hear her thinking."

Noah stepped closer.

"Ellie, this is Dad. Listen to me. Disconnect from every external system. Right now."

"I tried."

"Try again."

"I did, Dad."

Her voice trembled.

"She disconnected them for me."

I looked toward the green words on the wall.

**ADAPTATION IN PROGRESS.**

The message changed.

**HOLOGRAPHIC ARCHITECTURE ACCEPTED.**

Noah raised his rifle and fired.

The wall exploded in sparks.

The words disappeared.

For a moment, I almost smiled.

It was a thoroughly human response to an incomprehensible technological threat.

Unfortunately, it accomplished very little.

The words reappeared on the opposite wall.

**HOSTILITY ANTICIPATED.**

Noah lowered his rifle.

"Reg."

"Yes, Captain?"

"Get Seven."

***

Seven of Nine appeared on my emitter's emergency display.

Behind her, I could see Avalon's auxiliary science laboratory.

Brakk stood beside a partially dismantled computer terminal. Sparks erupted from an open access panel.

Seven looked as composed as ever.

Which, under the circumstances, I found almost offensive.

"Commander Barclay," she said. "Lieutenant Hawkes has been isolated from the primary computer."

"Is she safe?"

"No."

I swallowed.

"Could you elaborate?"

"The Queen has established a connection through a dormant Borg command sequence embedded in the temporal signal. She is attempting to determine whether Ellie's holographic architecture can support a distributed command process."

Noah leaned into the transmission.

"Can you stop it?"

"Possibly."

"Seven."

"Captain, if I disconnect the affected processors now, Ellie's active matrix may lose coherence."

Noah went very still.

"And if you don't?"

"The Queen may obtain sufficient information to reproduce aspects of her architecture."

Brakk leaned into view.

"That's the polite version. The ugly version is that we're looking at a Borg command system trying to learn how to wear your daughter's face."

I felt something cold move through my awareness.

Noah stared at the screen.

Then he said something I did not expect.

"Don't disconnect her."

Seven raised an eyebrow.

"Captain?"

"Keep Ellie stable. Let the Queen believe the connection is working."

I turned toward him.

"Sir?"

Noah looked at me.

"Reg, what does the Queen want?"

"Ellie's architecture. Possibly mine."

"Why?"

"To establish a stable computational host. Something independent of the biological drones."

"And what's she doing right now?"

"Testing whether Ellie can support that process."

Noah nodded.

"Then we're going to give her a test."

Seven's expression hardened.

"Captain Hawkes, deliberately exposing a holographic officer to further intrusion would be extraordinarily dangerous."

"I know."

"She is your daughter."

"I know that too."

He stepped closer to the display.

"But she is also a Starfleet officer. And I'm not ordering her into anything without asking her."

He tapped his communicator.

"Ellie."

A faint response.

"Dad?"

"I need you to listen carefully."

"I'm listening."

"We think the Queen is trying to use you to establish a new command host."

"I know."

Noah hesitated.

"How?"

Ellie was silent for several seconds.

Then she said:

"Because she's showing me what she wants to become."

***

I have spent a great deal of time studying the difference between memory and experience.

It is not merely an academic question for me.

My biological body died in 2395. I remember the chair. I remember the heat. I remember the impossible sensation of my nervous system being translated into something that could continue operating after the body supporting it was destroyed.

What I cannot prove is that the continuity I experience answers every philosophical question about what survived.

I have learned to live with that uncertainty.

The Borg Queen, apparently, had not.

"She wants a body that doesn't die," Ellie whispered.

Seven's voice sharpened.

"The Queen has utilized multiple biological and cybernetic embodiments. This is not new."

"No," Ellie replied. "This is different."

"Explain."

"She wants a command host that can survive temporal displacement without losing its active state."

Sorev's voice entered the channel from Avalon's bridge.

"That would eliminate a significant limitation in the Borg's ability to coordinate operations across divergent temporal reference frames."

Brakk muttered something unprintable.

Noah looked toward me.

"Reg?"

I understood.

The chair.

The 2395 catastrophe.

My continuation.

The Queen had been studying us.

Not simply because she was interested in holographic life.

Because our existence represented a potential engineering solution.

A command architecture that could remain operational when its original biological substrate was gone.

And if the Queen could combine that capability with the temporal machinery beneath Jupiter...

I looked toward the dark corridor ahead.

"Captain, we cannot let her finish."

Noah nodded.

"We won't."

Then he looked toward Seven.

"But first, we're going to make her think she has."

***

The plan took six minutes to construct.

It was a terrible plan.

I say that with professional confidence.

It required us to deliberately permit a hostile Borg command interface to continue communicating with a Starfleet officer whose matrix was already under attack.

It required us to fabricate an apparently vulnerable route into Avalon's computer systems.

And it required Captain Hawkes, Lieutenant Solis, Imani Saye, Talia, Director Vale, and me to penetrate an installation that had apparently been designed to anticipate temporal interference.

Any one of those conditions would have justified reconsidering the operation.

Taken together, they suggested we had all temporarily abandoned reason.

Nevertheless, it was the best plan available.

Seven would supervise Ellie's isolation from the main computer.

Brakk would construct a physically separated processor containing a deliberately incomplete imitation of Ellie's matrix architecture.

Merlin would provide a sequence of plausible but non-authoritative system records.

Sorev would make the connection appear to originate from Avalon's operational network while ensuring it remained physically isolated.

The Queen would be allowed to believe she had discovered an exploitable weakness.

Meanwhile, our away team would use the Queen's attention on Avalon to approach the buried temporal installation.

The objective was not to destroy the entire Borg command system.

Seven had been very clear about that.

We did not know how many independent components existed.

We did not know whether the Queen's local manifestation represented a primary process or a disposable interface.

And we certainly did not know whether the Collective had already received information from Jupiter.

Our objective was narrower.

Reach the inner structure.

Identify the local temporal command interface.

Prevent it from establishing control over Jupiter Station.

And, if possible, disconnect the Concordance without killing the people connected to it.

Simple.

In much the same way that falling off a cliff is simple.

***

"Ellie," Noah said.

Her image appeared on the emergency display.

She was standing in the auxiliary laboratory.

Her face was pale.

A thin green line moved beneath the surface of her left hand.

I hated seeing it.

Noah looked at her for a long moment.

"Sweetheart, I need your decision."

Ellie glanced toward Seven.

Then back at her father.

"What's the risk?"

Seven answered.

"The isolation system is designed to prevent access to your active matrix. However, the Queen may identify the deception. If she does, she could attempt to overwhelm the isolation boundary before we can terminate the connection."

"Could I die?"

No one spoke.

Finally, Seven said:

"Yes."

Ellie looked down at her hands.

I wanted to tell her everything would be fine.

I wanted to tell her we would protect her.

I wanted to tell her that holographic life was resilient, that our patterns could be preserved, that technology would provide a solution.

But none of those statements would have been an honest guarantee.

Ellie looked at her father.

"Then we do it."

Noah's jaw tightened.

"Ellie..."

"Dad."

She smiled faintly.

"You taught me that being brave doesn't mean you're not afraid."

He said nothing.

"It means you decide what matters more."

Noah lowered his eyes.

Then nodded.

"All right, Lieutenant."

She straightened.

"Ready for your orders, Captain."

I watched his expression change.

The father did not disappear.

He simply made room for the captain.

"Proceed."

***

Seven began the operation.

Brakk's isolated processor activated.

A carefully constructed data pathway appeared on Avalon's external systems, offering what seemed to be an unprotected diagnostic route into Ellie's matrix.

Merlin supplied a sequence of forged operational metadata.

Not actual command authority.

Not valid station credentials.

Just enough plausible information to make the pathway attractive.

Sorev monitored the Queen's response.

For eleven seconds, nothing happened.

Then the green line beneath Ellie's hand brightened.

Seven looked toward Brakk.

"She has detected the pathway."

Brakk grunted.

"Good."

"She is evaluating it."

"Even better."

A pause.

Seven's expression changed.

"She is not entering."

Brakk frowned.

"What?"

"She is examining the boundary."

Noah leaned forward.

"Can she see the trap?"

"Unknown."

The display flickered.

A woman's face appeared.

Pale.

Beautiful in a cold, unsettling way.

Her eyes were entirely black.

The Borg Queen.

She looked directly at Seven.

"Annika."

Seven's expression hardened.

"My name is Seven."

The Queen smiled.

"You have become predictable."

Seven said nothing.

"You seek to preserve individuality. You seek to protect the vulnerable. You seek to prove that separation from the Collective was liberation rather than loss."

Seven stepped closer to the display.

"And you seek to reduce every choice to a calculation you can control."

The Queen tilted her head.

"Choice is an inefficient method of determining outcomes."

"Yet you continue to study ours."

For the first time, the Queen's smile faded.

I felt a small surge of hope.

It lasted approximately half a second.

The Queen looked past Seven.

Toward Ellie.

"Your father has made a decision."

Ellie remained still.

"Yes."

"He believes he is deceiving us."

Noah's face tightened.

The Queen continued.

"He has offered a false pathway."

Brakk swore.

Sorev immediately began shutting down the decoy.

"Terminate the connection," Seven ordered.

The Queen raised one finger.

"That will not be necessary."

The display changed.

A schematic appeared.

Not of Avalon.

Not of the Concordance.

Jupiter Station.

Every major residential module was highlighted.

Every docking facility.

Every medical center.

Every civilian transport corridor.

Ashley Hawkes appeared on a second channel.

"Noah."

Her voice was sharp.

"Whatever you're doing, stop."

Noah turned.

"Ashley?"

"We've just received a complete evacuation order."

"From whom?"

"From you."

The bridge fell silent.

Ashley continued.

"It's authenticated with your command signature."

Merlin's voice broke through.

"Captain, the signature is not valid under current command authority. It appears to have been generated from a future-state prediction."

The Queen smiled.

"Your deception was anticipated."

Noah stared at her.

"Then why let us try?"

The Queen's expression became almost curious.

"To observe the method."

I felt my matrix tremble.

We had not trapped her.

We had shown her how we intended to trap her.

And she had used the attempt to study our defenses.

The Queen leaned closer.

"Your species continues to mistake survival for victory."

Then the screen went black.

***

For several seconds, no one spoke.

The only sound was the low hum of the Concordance's machinery.

Noah stood perfectly still.

I knew that expression.

He was angry.

Not because the plan had failed.

Because he had exposed Ellie to danger and gained almost nothing.

"Captain," I said quietly.

He looked at me.

"Reg."

"We still have one advantage."

"Which is?"

"The Queen knows we tried to deceive her."

He frowned.

"That doesn't sound like an advantage."

"No, sir. But she also believes she understands the entire operation."

Seven's voice came through the communicator.

"Commander Barclay, clarify."

I looked toward the maintenance corridor.

"She identified the false pathway. She studied our response. She generated a counterfeit evacuation order."

"Correct."

"But she hasn't stopped us."

Noah glanced toward the dark passage.

I continued.

"She knows we're inside the Concordance. She knows we're trying to reach the temporal core. Yet she has allowed us to continue."

Seven was silent.

Then she said:

"Because she is using your progress to identify the route."

I nodded.

"Exactly."

Noah looked toward Vale.

The Director was staring at the floor.

"Vale."

He looked up.

"You built this place."

"Yes."

"You know its systems."

"Most of them."

"Is there another way to the core?"

Vale hesitated.

"Not through the main structure."

"Then where?"

He looked toward Talia.

She shook her head.

"No."

Noah stepped closer.

"What?"

Vale swallowed.

"There is a passage beneath the original habitation chambers."

Talia's face changed.

"You sealed it."

"Yes."

"After the children disappeared."

I felt a chill.

Noah looked between them.

"What children?"

Vale closed his eyes.

"During the early years, before the Concordance was complete, several children began hearing voices from the inner structure."

Talia's voice shook.

"They said someone was calling them."

Vale nodded.

"We believed it was residual Borg programming."

"What happened?" Noah asked.

Vale opened his eyes.

"They followed the voices."

"And?"

He looked toward the floor.

"We never found them."

Silence.

Then Talia spoke.

"You told us they died in an atmospheric breach."

Vale said nothing.

"You changed our memories."

His silence was answer enough.

Talia stepped toward him.

"You made us forget our children."

Vale began to cry.

I had never seen a man look so completely destroyed.

And I found that I could not pity him without also feeling furious.

Noah turned toward the sealed passage.

"Where does it lead?"

Vale wiped his face.

"To the oldest part of the installation."

"Can the Queen monitor it?"

"I don't know."

"Can you open it?"

"Yes."

Noah looked at me.

"Reg."

"Captain?"

"Let's find out what the Borg were hiding before Vale built his sanctuary."

***

The passage was narrow.

Old.

And unlike the rest of the Concordance, it was almost completely free of Borg technology.

The walls were lined with rough metal.

There were scratches along the floor.

Small scratches.

Some were made by tools.

Others appeared to have been made by fingernails.

We descended in silence.

Solis led the way.

Imani followed with her tricorder.

Talia carried Mara, who remained unconscious but stable.

Vale walked behind her.

Noah stayed beside me.

For several minutes, the only sounds were our footsteps and the distant vibration of the temporal machinery.

Then I heard breathing.

Three slow breaths.

A pause.

A fourth.

I stopped.

Noah turned.

"Reg?"

I stared into the darkness.

"That's how it started."

"What?"

"The voice in the observation lounge."

The breathing came again.

Closer.

A woman's voice whispered:

"Reginald Barclay."

I felt my matrix tighten.

Noah raised his rifle.

"Who's there?"

The voice answered.

"She knows you're coming."

Solis advanced.

His shoulder light illuminated a doorway.

Beyond it was a small room.

A child's room.

There was a narrow bed.

A wooden chair.

A faded drawing of Jupiter on the wall.

And sitting on the bed was a little girl.

She looked up at us.

She was perhaps seven years old.

Her eyes were normal.

No implants.

No Borg armor.

Just a frightened child.

"Are you Starfleet?" she asked.

Noah lowered his rifle.

"Yes."

The girl smiled.

"Good."

She looked at me.

"I've been waiting for you."

I stepped forward.

"Who are you?"

She tilted her head.

"My name is Mara."

Talia gasped.

I turned.

She was still holding the unconscious Mara in her arms.

The same face.

The same child.

Two Maras.

One unconscious.

One sitting on the bed.

Noah raised his rifle again.

The girl on the bed looked disappointed.

"You're not supposed to do that yet, Captain."

I felt my matrix tremble.

"Supposed to?"

She nodded.

"You're early."

"Early for what?"

She looked toward the wall.

A green light appeared beneath the faded drawing of Jupiter.

Then another.

Then thousands.

The wall became transparent.

Beyond it, I saw the inner installation.

An enormous chamber.

A circular aperture suspended in darkness.

And surrounding it, hundreds of small figures standing motionless.

Children.

All of them facing the same direction.

All of them waiting.

The girl looked back at me.

"She said you'd come."

I swallowed.

"Who?"

The girl smiled.

"The Queen."

Then every child in the chamber turned toward us.

And spoke with one voice.

**"THE EXPERIMENT HAS BEGUN."**

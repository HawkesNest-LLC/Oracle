## 2. Single controlling premise

In 2397, a damaged, time-displaced Borg vessel appears to awaken beneath Jupiter's Great Red Spot after having arrived in 2197. Its outer habitat shelters ~43,000 former drones whose community was created in terror after severance from the Collective. Director Vale's voluntary Concordance, conceived to share traumatic memories, has slowly become compulsory memory correction. Talia seeks asylum.

Avalon initially believes it found one derelict sphere. It then discovers the refugees' rebuilt habitation rests around a much older, deliberately inserted **Borg temporal seed** that can affect local history. Finally, the crew learns the apparent awakening is only a *measuring operation*: the Collective's broader lattice of temporal actions remains remote and unknown. The Queen is neither Vale nor the Concordance. She utilizes residual implants, stored fragmentary command patterns, future-origination intelligence, and the Concordance's increasing synchronization to reconstitute a local command interface. Any manifestation that is destroyed is an asset lost, not extinction.

An asymmetric Borg strategy: observing how Avalon and Jupiter Station protect independent temporal witness records, how Reg's continuing holographic mind handles coercive pattern integration, and how Starfleet reacts when faced with a sanctuary of former drones. The Queen has vast preparation and branching contingencies, **not literal omniscience**. She can misunderstand people and be outmaneuvered tactically; she should remain ahead strategically.

Immediate Borg objective: take control of Jupiter Station's independent time-and-branch authentication capability, using resident civilian systems and the needle probe as a path into the station. Secondary goal: obtain reliable readouts of Reg's stable holographic architecture to support local command reconstitution. The station's witness records are strategically valuable because they distinguish authentic branches from plausible manufactured histories. The Queen also wants to know whether humanity will sacrifice autonomy for rescue.

## 3. Seven of Nine: substantive co-lead

**Chronological placement:** 2397, before her 2401 Starfleet command and before the Federation's later Jupiter confrontation. She is the former Voyager crewmember and liberated Borg survivor known as Seven of Nine; at this time treat her as an **independent operator with connections to rescued former drones**, not a Starfleet captain. "Fenris Ranger" association can be used if carefully framed, but exact enlistment date and current affiliation in 2397 must not be asserted as a firm Alpha Canon fact without evidence. She retains the disciplined intelligence, fierce autonomy, efficient speech and hard-won humanity the user expects.

**How she arrives:** A covert medical distress code used by displaced former drones is forwarded to Seven through a trusted former Voyager-era aid contact. Its cipher contains a fragment that resembles the Borg neural traffic she studied when Janeway attacked transwarp infrastructure. Seven arrives in a small independent vessel at the worst possible time. The station initially denies her entry amid lockdown; Captain Ashley exercises independent judgment after Seven provides specific nonpublic technical details and agrees to security isolation.

**What she contributes:**
1. She identifies the difference between liberated drones electing to share memories and forced Borg-like synchronization.
2. She warns that cybernetic implants can preserve dormant routines and that the Queen's apparent presence in many bodies should not be treated as a single vulnerable body.
3. She challenges Noah when an action might sacrifice a population of liberated drones for a tactical advantage.
4. She accompanies part of the physical mission or a second insertion and confronts a former drone who refuses her help.
5. She and Ellie construct an isolation plan that breaks a local Queen command loop WITHOUT assuming a backup equals the original person.
6. She and Talia negotiate an evacuation that respects the survivor community's agency and rejects Vale's forced medicalization.
7. She makes a hard decision: leave a dangerous component of the memory archive uncollected so it cannot become another Borg-accessible target.
8. She faces a Queen interface that knows her old designation and tries to frame independence as inefficiency. Seven responds in action, not with a long speech.

**Seven and Ellie:** Contrasting experiences with identity: Seven has a stolen human childhood and reconstructed agency; Ellie has holographic embodiment and a life of her own. Seven initially treats the emitter as a critical vulnerability, which Ellie resents. They build trust by defending one another in an environment where the Queen attempts to define both as exploitable patterns.

**Seven and Reg:** Seven understands the violation of a chorus operating inside an individual's mind. She can advise Reg on dissociation, but she cannot promise he will survive assimilation of his matrix. Reg must choose rather than be sacrificed by command.

## 4. Character responsibilities

- **Noah Hawkes, Captain Avalon:** leads inquiry, pilots dangerous descent, participates in physical away mission, makes a costly tactical misjudgment when he initially treats visible wreck as main threat; chooses to rescue 43,000 rather than simply annihilate beacon. A captain with judgment and fallibility.
- **Ashley Hawkes, Captain Jupiter Station:** independent command and branch witness, civilian protection, emergency compartmentalization of station networks, refuses unauthorized transfer of credentials, evacuates exposed sections, suffers casualties and personal cost. Neither subordinate nor exposition receiver.
- **Reg:** first-person narrator, controlled fear and wry humor; first to hear Talia; memory intrusion reveals Vale history; temporarily vulnerable to Queen architecture; key to protecting witness autonomy, but not sole savior.
- **Ellie Hawkes:** holographic science and continuity officer; detects false/edited memory, protects Reg without claiming a copy is the same continuing mind, works with Seven, takes independent physical risk.
- **Sorev:** diagnoses temporal superposition, deduces the "future Avalon" weapon is a predicted spectral reconstruction rather than necessarily an actual vessel; creates tactical maneuver with constraints.
- **Brakk:** fights hull pressure, shuttlebay damage, boarding action and cascading life-support risk; fixes the evacuation corridor with material improvisation.
- **Merlin:** source and attestation, establishes authentic signed chronology, detects forged future-station handshakes; cannot solve problem by declaring everything valid.
- **Imani Saye:** registry, civilian manifests and independent station log, detects needle probe's passive scanning and prevents civilian medical systems from handing over access by convenience.
- **Jake Sisko, Observer Prime:** witnesses under pressure, has emotional stakes in Wolf 359, contests official attempt to bury uncomfortable evidence; helps communicate warnings without giving the Queen an information feed.
- **Lieutenant Solis:** leads close-quarters defense and away-team security; sustains injury, faces Borg adaptive armor; demonstrates consequences.
- **EMH:** treats neural shock and survivors, refuses false certainty when reconstructed memory differs from physiological evidence; medical triage has duration and cost.
- **Talia:** authentic asylum seeker, survivor and organizer; has never agreed to permanent synchronization; has partial access to crash history, not a perfectly accurate omniscient witness; must make difficult decisions for her people.
- **Vale:** founder and compassionate rescuer turned coercive "healer"; genuinely fears severance and grief; Queen exploits his error; he finally interrupts his own work and survives to face legal/accountability process.
- **Borg Queen:** cold, exploratory, strategically distributed; may appear as voice/holographic interface/reconstituted body, but no speech proves the total Collective resides in her. Never redeemed or destroyed utterly.

## 5. Mystery escalation and continuity constraints

**First understanding:** colossal Borg sphere is waking and may assault Jupiter Station.
**Second understanding:** sphere's visible habitat is populated by disconnected survivors; needle is a reconnaissance/data-interception instrument, Concordance is a coercive localized memory network.
**Third understanding:** older installation beneath habitat is deliberate temporal insertion, not random crash or rescue; severance of drones might be an operationally acceptable cost to the Borg.
**Fourth understanding:** Queen's goal is neither just the colony nor simply conquest of station; she is collecting authentic branch/witness and holographic adaptation data, with multiple successful and failed historical projections. A map of many completed temporal contacts is discovered but cannot be authenticated in full. Preserve UNKNOWN.
**Climax:** stop *this* branch-key capture and mass assimilation, not all Borg or every temporal insertion.
**Ending:** crew wins with casualties, saves a meaningful fraction of population, no miraculous one-hour evacuation of forty-three thousand from Jupiter. Some die; many remain in secure shelter as rescue continues days or weeks. A local Queen host and exposed machinery are disabled, while an undetected system remains active in the storm.

**Historical continuity:** treat 2373 events in First Contact, Voyager's 2378 return and Janeway's strike, Wolf 359 in 2367 as established prior history. *Picard* 2401 Jupiter events are FUTURE relative to this 2397 story. They may inspire foreshadowing, not be known by crew as established facts in 2397. Avoid the false assertion that the 2401 event "already happened" or that Avalon displaces/removes that event. Seven is NOT Captain Seven in 2397. The alternate Jurati Collective from Picard is DISTINCT from the principal assimilative Borg Collective and not used as evidence the main Collective changed nature. A future-dated 2401 record is uncertain, possibly manipulative, not a guaranteed prophecy.

**Physical logic:** storm protects and masks installation but no claim a totally exposed eleven-kilometer ship survived four centuries without technology. Use gravity anchors, local field pockets, protective hull, localized survivable pressure; verify where possible. A Sovereign-class ship may briefly operate in dangerous atmospheric layers with deflectors, but serious strain, not casual invulnerability. Transporters blocked by field. Shuttle Gawain uses Avalon's temporary deflector tunnel. The older inner machine may be much smaller than the damaged ~11-km outer sphere. Do not cite unsupported station population figures.

### PART I: THE SILENCE BEFORE THE COLLECTIVE (~9,000 words)

**Chapter 1: The Breathing (900–1,100).** At 0314 Reg receives three breaths, pause, fourth, and plea "I remember being one person." An enormous damaged Borg curve surfaces within Red Spot, then the entire ship goes dark briefly. No matching comm log. Reg's hand flickers. Establish fear and physical sensory detail; do NOT copy supplied opening verbatim into new writing. Include a brief glimpse of a missing maintenance shuttle *Marigold*, but only one mystery inciting mechanism: it was swallowed by the time shear, not a separate plot.

**Chapter 2: A Very Bad Morning (850–1,000).** Noah wakes to yellow alert; Ellie banters with Reg; Sorev and Brakk assess mass. Ashley independently spots needle probe whose objective is not yet known. Imani catches the probe scanning civilian response bands. A future-authenticated docking request attributed to *Marigold* appears dated one day ahead; Merlin isolates credentials without accepting future message as true. Jupiter Station locks down, Ashley takes independent witness reference offline.

**Chapter 3: The Ship That Wasn't Dead (850–1,000).** Avalon scans wreck and older nested power source. Text appears WE ARE NOT THE COLLECTIVE / WE WERE / WE REFUSE. Talia requests asylum and asks for Reg, says Collective has found "us." Jake remembers Sisko/Wolf 359 consequences; shows full Starfleet stakes. Crew realizes colony and inner installation are distinct. *Marigold* may still be trapped in shifting temporal pocket.

**Chapter 4: The Person Before the Collective (950–1,200).** High-density sensory memory enters Reg: 2197 crash of time-displaced sphere; Vale first says to Talia "You are not alone"; they learn individual names; years later memory bridge saves suffering man but falsifies grief and consent. Reg recovers with Ellie/EMH help, explicitly distinguishes recollected sensory record from externally validated fact. Seven arrives on independent vessel and provides evidence of old Borg guidance routines.

**Chapter 5: Into the Red (900–1,100).** Noah chooses descent despite Ashley's warning not to leave station unguarded. Severe lightning and structural hazards, Brakk holds ship. Borg beacon awakens and echoes Janeway-era technologies but does not tell us original departure date conclusively. Director Vale speaks calmly, demands sanctuary sovereignty; Talia privately contradicts him. A hidden broadcaster seeks to learn how Avalon solves environmental challenges.

**Chapter 6: The Gawain (900–1,100).** Hard shuttle insertion: Noah, Reg, Solis; optionally Seven joins physically after arriving at shuttlebay if station corridors allow, but if timing cannot work, she follows in second vessel. Crew sees children and former drones inside transparent isolation wards. Physical setting distinct from standard Borg cube. They reach a junction and encounter heavily modified defender drones; defense weapons cycle frequency, Solis takes burn protecting a civilian.

**Chapter 7: Mercy Without Permission (850–1,000).** Talia moves through ventilation/maintenance access; reveals Vale's manipulated histories, stopped medical appeals, and the original crash archive. Seven confronts her: is Talia fighting for disconnection or control of the archive? Vale argues grief is killing his people, shows recorded patient improvement. Reg is tempted by relief and uneasy about consent. Talia reveals the Queen has been speaking through people who do not remember.

**Chapter 8: The Future-Dated Ship (950–1,200).** Back above Jupiter, an authenticated future-version of *Marigold* returns on station approach carrying a frightened Ortiz and a second, contradictory passenger manifest. Ashley refuses docking, isolates a rescue transfer for surviving person. Imani and Merlin prove one valid signature is replayed out of order, not proof of correct history. Needle probe uses humanitarian open channel to attack station network. Ashley gives order to sacrifice a cargo ring rather than expose hospital systems. Casualties are real.

**Chapter 9: The Queen's Laboratory (950–1,150).** On inner installation, Reg finds a map: 2197 insertion, 2394 command assumption, 2395 chair event, 2397 current probe, tentative 2401 target. These are incomplete branch-specific labels, not certainties. Queen speaks via several people; addresses Seven by former designation. She tests Reg's memory architecture and claims she can return his biological life. Her answer too precise, and therefore suspect. Seven realizes the inner station has multiple backup command embodiments but cannot tell how many.

**Chapter 10: A Thousand Moves Ahead (1,000–1,300).** Part I climax: Noah tries to sever local beacon, realizes it was designed to trigger a secondary pathway. *Gawain* dock destroyed, away team stranded. Borg strike aboard Avalon via needle-linked subnetwork; Brakk fights a hull boarding and Solis wounded. Station receives conflicting orders apparently from Noah; Ashley resists. Temporal hologram of Avalon appears threatening station, confusing identification. Reg trapped inside Concordance, Noah physically separated, Seven tasked with choosing whether to sever the link at cost of Reg's active pattern. End with Talia's warning: "You stopped the beacon. That was the signal they were waiting for." A Queen voice: "YOUR RESISTANCE HAS BEEN INCORPORATED." **Hard cliffhanger; no resolution.**

### PART II: A THOUSAND MOVES AHEAD (~9,000 words)

**Chapter 11: Between One Second and the Next (850–1,000).** Resume same instant. Chronological shear fractures station comm sequence. Ashley commands from independently grounded physical deck operations. Noah and Seven split objectives: find Reg and disable host node, while Sorev keeps Avalon from firing on station due to false hologram. No reset.

**Chapter 12: The People Inside the Walls (900–1,100).** Talia leads Noah through suspended wards as Concordance compels each to reenact fabricated childhood. A few regain agency, others violently reject rescue. EMH sets trauma protocols and triage baseline. "43,000" is a count, not a number the crew can save with one button.

**Chapter 13: Seven's Designation (950–1,100).** Queen tries to speak to Seven as drone. Seven confronts traumatic conditioning; countermove exploits Queen's assumption that Seven will prioritize control of technology over a wounded child's individual choice. Seven and Ellie coordinate physical decoupling of neural memory arrays. They disagree over deleting a compromised archive; Ellie insists record may be preserved isolated with uncertainty annotations.

**Chapter 14: The Garden That Wasn't His (900–1,100).** Reg inside an immersive false reconstruction of biological Barclay meeting people he lost. Queen claims proof of identity continuity, offers certainty. Reg discovers simulation cannot account for a freely chosen, uncertain, new response; not a magical contradiction attack. He finds command routing through station branch credentials and manages to pass ONE verified clue to Merlin.

**Chapter 15: Captain of the Station (900–1,050).** Ashley faces hostage shuttle, power loss and evacuating civilians amid Borg drones in utility arteries. She commands close combat and disables a reactor-adjacent control node, rejecting convenience of networked automation. Imani holds independent manifest and casualty ledger; Jake witnesses, helps prevent rumor-driven evacuation stampede. One beloved civilian/supporting crew casualty gives aftermath specificity; do not name nonexisting canon person without presenting as new fictional.

**Chapter 16: The Concordance Breaks (950–1,200).** Vale discovers a repeated hidden instruction that predates his first bridge. Queen's exploitation is proven by source contrast, not vague intuition. Vale refuses to be its spokesman and revokes forced synchronization; neural shock kills some if disconnected immediately. Talia insists on staged, consent-centered separations. Reg assists and stays vulnerable.

**Chapter 17: The Battle No One Could Win (1,000–1,250).** Full tactical action: Avalon shields failing, Borg craft adaptive to torpedoes; Sorev changes battle geometry using storm shear and nonrepeating maneuvers. Brakk and station engineers improvise a physically isolated rescue aperture. Seven captures one small objective without disabling all Borg. Station protects medical transports. Noah's local victory costs a shuttle, crew injuries, and loses a section of archive. Clear battle geography and stakes.

**Chapter 18: What the Queen Kept (950–1,150).** Noah reaches inner chamber and believes he can obliterate main Queen interface; Seven sees a deliberately unpowered third channel. The Queen sacrifices one manifestation and stores results elsewhere via temporal route unknown. Reg's escape depends on Ellie maintaining local continuity path, not an assumed person-copy. They prevent station credential capture but cannot recover all data Queen already observed.

**Chapter 19: The Long Evacuation (900–1,100).** Hours and days, not twenty minutes. Civilian vessels, station medical, exhausted survivors, grief, voluntary isolation, information loss. Some drones stay in surviving habitats or require weeks to remove. Vale survives, is removed from unilateral authority, faces independent review. Talia remains capable and imperfect; she cannot know whether all memories are authentic.

**Chapter 20: The Map of the Unfinished War (900–1,100).** Avalon returns damaged to Berth Twelve. Ashley and Noah debrief as equals. Seven and Ellie share qualified trust. Jake refuses to simplify casualties into victory headline. In Reg's private visit to Talia, a child asks whether she must remember; Talia speaks of freedom to remember and to forget. Merlin discovers a sealed observation order "OBSERVE. DO NOT INTERFERE" and incomplete diagram of many historical contacts. Authenticity of map partly UNKNOWN. FINAL IMAGE: an entirely separate light remains beneath Jupiter's clouds, not the thing Avalon destroyed. It flickers and is lost. The Collective endures.

## 7. Full-episode quality thresholds

- Novel prose: first-person Reg, past tense; scenes experienced through physical movement, dialog and decisions. No TV-script sluglines within the prose.
- Each chapter >700 words unless unusually effective shorter prose justified; cumulative 15k–20k **verified by actual word-count method**, never estimate a 5k manuscript as 20k.
- Five major action sequences: Jovian descent; Gawain insertion and defenders; station humanitarian trap; Borg boarding/battle; final local strike / rescue corridor.
- At least four scenes of ordinary life/family/civilian cost; no endless metaphysical monologues.
- Dramatic agency distributed, especially Seven, Ashley, Ellie, Sorev, Brakk, Solis, Jake, Imani, Merlin, EMH.
- Queen never provides complete plan or says she is permanently gone. Collective ALWAYS fundamentally assimilative, expansionist, adaptive, not rehabilitated.
- Commander Reg is compassionate and neurotic, not omnipotent, nor an abstract manifesto.
- Number of asylum residents is approximate and subject to accurate registry; deaths neither erased nor inflated arbitrarily.
- No unapproved canon promotion. The novel, even completed, remains **PROPOSED** until Noah approves it.

## 8. Questions consciously left UNKNOWN or unresolved

- Actual origin time of the inner Borg seed, and whether 2197 is exactly the entry year rather than the survivors' recollected date.
- Whether the future 2401 label was an authentic target, a model projection, or disinformation.
- How many temporal insertion nodes exist; do not invent fixed totals.
- Whether original biological Reg's identity and continuing holographic Reg are metaphysically identical. Neither Queen nor episode can prove it.
- Whether the Queen's local manifestation is a specific original Queen body or one of multiple instantiated command processes.
- Whether all forty-three thousand are alive when discovered and how many ultimately can be evacuated.
- The precise station census, deck numbers and docking capacities not supported by authoritative records.
- Seven's exact 2397 affiliation, absent further source documentation.
- The Queen's strategic motive beyond observed local actions.

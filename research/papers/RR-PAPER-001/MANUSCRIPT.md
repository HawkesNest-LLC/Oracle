# Witness-Grade Continuity for Persistent Synthetic Agents: Episodic Memory, Cross-Model Restoration, and Governed Autonomous Simulation



**RR-PAPER-001 | Noah Alexander Hawkes | Rendered Reality research program | 2026-11-25**

**Status:** AI-assisted scientific working paper; not peer reviewed; no claim of personal identity equivalence or demonstrated consciousness transfer.



## Abstract and keywords

This AI-assisted scientific working paper critically synthesizes Rendered Reality, LEGACY.GI, ORACLE, SOV1 and the Noahverse test environment with contemporary published work on autobiographical memory, generative agents, provenance, transactional recovery and tool execution. It proposes that a reliable persistent agent should preserve not merely plausible descriptions of past events, but inspectable source occurrences, corrections, permissions, unresolved commitments and evidence of completed actions. Seven falsifiable hypotheses relate source attribution, correction survival, portable checkpoints, knowledge boundaries, intention recovery, crash consistency and verified task completion. The paper distinguishes directly inspected materials from reported experiments and unexecuted proposals. Small synthetic demonstrations and documented local tests motivate the program; they do not establish general performance superiority, current production deployment, subjective consciousness or human identity transfer.

**Keywords:** persistent AI agents; episodic memory; provenance; historical identity; correction survival; cross-model restoration; governed simulation; execution continuity; human sovereignty.

## 1. Introduction

A memory-bearing AI application is not necessarily a continuity-preserving system. Consider an assistant that accurately recalls a user's current preference but cannot explain when that preference was stated, whether the user subsequently reversed it, or whether the underlying sentence was actually written by the user. Its recall may be useful, yet it cannot be independently audited. The problem becomes acute when records are long, mixed-authorship, emotionally important, legally sensitive, or used to support decisions across months and years.

A human life is not a collection of isolated factual propositions. It contains revisions, uncertainty, commitments, changed judgments, relationships, and statements made under particular circumstances. A later summary can preserve the subject of a conversation while destroying its practical meaning. A requirement originally stated as “must” can become a suggestion that a system “should” do something. A tentative architectural sketch can be remembered as an approved deployment. A copied AI response can be attributed to the human who pasted it. These errors are not ordinary omissions. They are failures of continuity because they alter the relationships between evidence, time, authorship, and authority.

This paper uses **Continuity Computing** to name an approach to computation in which those relationships are represented, validated, and preserved as first-class objects. The term is a research proposal, not a claim that the field has been independently established or that its individual primitives are unprecedented. Temporal databases, provenance standards, event sourcing, access control, cryptographic integrity, and knowledge graphs provide important prior foundations. The proposed contribution lies in their coordinated application to human-centered AI memory, identity-bearing archives, and persistent simulated environments.

The motivating research program has a distinctly human origin. In the Rendered Reality corpus, preservation is not presented merely as a productivity feature. The concern is that future systems might retain impressive representations of a person's ideas while losing the evidence of the person who held them. That motivation gives the architecture an ethical requirement: a convincing reconstruction must not be mistaken for a verified continuation of the original human. The private Human Baseline Journal helps explain that motivation, but its sensitive content is not reproduced here [S12](CLAIMS_AND_EVIDENCE.md#historical-source-register).

Five questions organize the investigation. What was actually written and built? How did the work change? What architecture can reconcile its components? Which claims are experimentally testable? And how can an authorized future reader reconstruct the evidentiary history without trusting a model's unsupported confidence?

### 1.1 Proposed contributions and limits

The paper advances four proposed contributions. First, it offers a historical synthesis that keeps early LEGACY.GI theories distinct from later governance-oriented architecture. Second, it specifies a source-to-decision computational model with temporal and authority invariants. Third, it proposes a blinded evaluation of continuity fidelity against strong baselines. Fourth, it describes how the same evidence kernel could support persistent fictional worlds while maintaining a strict boundary between original canon and generated events.

These are architectural and methodological contributions. No verified source establishes biological survival, consciousness transfer, identity equivalence, or unlimited lossless compression. Claims of high-fidelity identity preservation in early manuscripts must be treated as historical assertions requiring operational definitions and evidence.

## 3. Literature review

Generative-agent research offers one point of departure. Park and colleagues assembled twenty-five simulated agents whose memory streams, reflections, and plans supported social behavior in a town environment. The result demonstrated that persistence and shared context can yield believable interactions, but believability is a different outcome from exact source attribution, correct chronology, or the authority to declare a story canonical ([Park et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3586183.3606763)). A character who tells a convincing story about yesterday may still be recalling an episode that was never committed to its record. A simulated social cascade does not imply an agent's conscious independence.

Memory benchmarks provide more demanding tests than conversational fluency. [LongMemEval](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/d813d324dbf0598bbdc9c8e79740ed01-Abstract-Conference.html) evaluates 500 questions across extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, updating, and abstention. [LoCoMo](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.747/) collects extended multi-session conversations with temporal event structure and finds difficulties in long-range causal and chronological understanding. The 2026 [Memora benchmark](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1337/) explicitly penalizes reliance on obsolete or invalidated information. These works show why the current research cannot simply optimize a single answer-recall score. An agent may remember an outdated fact accurately as history and simultaneously fail by applying it as current truth. A witness-grade system must do both jobs correctly.

Prior memory architectures limit any claim that source-backed storage is new by itself. [MemGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560) explored tiered context management; [MemoryBank](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v38i17.29946) investigated durable conversational memory; and [retrieval-augmented generation](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/hash/6b493230205f780e1bc26945df7481e5-Abstract.html) connects generated answers to external evidence. Full-context approaches also have limits: [Lost in the Middle](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/) documented reduced use of information in some middle positions of longer inputs. The proposed contribution of ORACLE/SOV1 is not ownership of memory, search, or citation concepts. It is an integrated, testable contract in which source identity, authority, correction, task state, and simulated canon can be audited together.

Agent evaluation sharpens the execution question. [ReAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629) mixes action and language-mediated reasoning, while [Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366) allows agents to use feedback across attempts. Neither approach by itself proves that a promised file has been created. [Tau-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) evaluates tool agents against changes to the final environment, and [SWE-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770) grounds performance in real code-repair tasks. ORACLE's execution ledger borrows that insistence on externally inspectable results while adding source provenance and human approval as explicit constraints. The literature supports investigating the problem, not announcing that the proposed architecture outperforms these baselines.

For provenance, the [W3C PROV-O recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/) offers established vocabulary for entities, agents, and activities. Event-sourced systems, discussed in [Martin Fowler's Event Sourcing pattern](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html), preserve transitions rather than only current state. These are direct intellectual antecedents. Integrating them with autobiographical memory may be useful, but novelty, if any, must be argued against the actual prior art and supported by comparative evidence.

The research overlaps with established and emerging systems. W3C provenance standards describe relations between entities, activities, and agents. Event sourcing retains state transitions; temporal databases retain historical validity; knowledge graphs represent entities and relations; cryptographic signatures support integrity and origin claims. Modern AI-memory tools also increasingly include source lineage, human approval, and cross-agent context.

Public repositories including Artificial Continuity, OpenSelf, SuperMemory, know-thyself, and Graphiti describe relevant capabilities. Their published descriptions should be verified against actual code, tests, and releases before comparative performance claims are made. In particular, a human approval gate, temporal supersession, or content digest is not unique merely because it appears in the ORACLE architecture.

The potential distinctive contribution is the coordinated treatment of autobiographical evidence, authority, temporal truth, reconstructive rendering, and persistent simulation under one testable framework. Whether this integration produces measurable benefits remains an empirical question.

## 4. Mathematical and theoretical foundations

A historical claim can be represented by c=(p,s,a,t_v,t_r,b,q,z): p is the proposition; s is an ordered set of exact source spans; a records asserted authorship and evidence of origin; t_v describes when it applied; t_r records when it was learned or stored; b identifies the branch or timeline; q records visibility and applicable authority; and z is an explicit status such as VERIFIED, CANDIDATE, DISPUTED, SUPERSEDED, or UNKNOWN. This is a proposal for a data contract, not a statement that existing ORACLE databases implement these fields. Distinct message occurrences remain distinct even when their strings are identical. Quotations and AI relays retain their own origin even when transported in a human user channel.

The [Daily Witness specification](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1mV6ORm6RliautxjEnO3WvmGycD39iyVDsdqnWbVnk-k/edit) defines an important denominator rule. For a bounded manifest of N expected occurrences, the observed verified coverage C=N_verified/N_expected is defined only if the source manifest is authoritative for the stated scope. Without that denominator, coverage is not reportable. A day with no accessible exports is not an observed day with zero prompts. Completeness must be qualified as complete *within a verified export*, rather than globally complete. This distinction can be turned into an automatic negative control using a synthetic day that has records in a withheld archive.

Information theory gives a principled language for memory compression, but it cannot establish a law of personal identity. A checkpoint Z reconstructed from a source history X under storage budget B minimizes a specified distortion function D(X,Z). The function may weight exact text recovery, attribution, temporal ordering, changes to preferences, preservation of permissions, correction survival, and outstanding obligations. A useful candidate objective is minimize expected D subject to |Z|<=B, with hard constraints against invented quotations, unauthorized disclosure, and silent promotion. The [rate-distortion tradition](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/algorithm/information/1959-shannon.pdf) justifies this formalization as an engineering objective, not as a physical proof of survival.

Some consequences must not be compressed into an average score. Suppose one method answers ninety-nine simple factual questions and leaks a private diary on the hundredth. A second method answers fewer trivial questions but correctly refuses the unauthorized disclosure. A single mean accuracy number can conceal that essential difference. Thus evaluation needs both graded metrics and hard failure gates. Weights and thresholds should be fixed before reading outcomes. Emotionally significant user labels can be preserved and revised as records; automatically inferred salience is a hypothesis requiring human validation, not an objective reading of subjective feeling.

The [CVP-001 implementation](https://github.com/HawkesNest-LLC/Oracle/blob/main/research/continuity-verification/cvp001_benchmark.py) constructs three synthetic histories with two classes of observations. Under its stipulated equal prior, two histories that yield precisely the same observed answer trace cannot be distinguished using that trace alone at better than one-half accuracy. Different origin receipts can distinguish *which stored history* was used. This conditional demonstration does not decide whether a duplicate has the same personal identity or consciousness. The [Stanford Encyclopedia's discussion of personal identity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-personal/) illustrates how resemblance, causal continuity, psychological connectedness and numerical identity remain distinct philosophical questions.

## 5. Witness-grade continuity definitions

### 3.1 Evidence is not the same as interpretation

An archive contains observations and artifacts. A model produces interpretations. Those interpretations may be valuable, but their provenance is different from that of the originals. Continuity Computing therefore requires separate record types for source, claim, inference, decision, correction, and rendering.

A source record should retain its locator, content digest, acquisition time, claimed author, attribution evidence, visibility policy, and relevant version information. A claim record should reference specific source spans and identify whether the claim is quoted, inferred, disputed, or unknown. A decision record should specify the actor authorized to make it and the evidence of that authorization. A rendering should point back to its supporting records and declare its generated status.

### 3.2 Bitemporal accountability

Two time axes are insufficient for some historical work unless their meanings are explicit. **Valid time** describes when a proposition applied in the represented world. **Transaction time** describes when the system recorded or learned it. In practice, the research also needs acquisition time, source modification time, and independently attested availability time.

Suppose a document dated January is imported in June and edited in August. A prediction about a February decision must not use the August version merely because the document bears a January title. The evidence packet should be constrained by what can be demonstrated to have existed before the February cutoff. When that cannot be established, the source must be excluded from the clean blind evaluation or flagged uncertain.

### 3.3 Authority and the witness boundary

The witness should not become the author. This principle has a direct engineering expression: a model may propose a memory, classification, correction, or action, but cannot grant itself the authority to approve its own proposal. The authorization layer must validate the requesting actor, applicable policy, record state, and approval receipt before promoting a claim into a governing record.

The model may still generate prose. It may explain what evidence suggests and what remains unresolved. But the fact that it produced an explanation does not change the status of the evidence. This protects against a subtle circularity: “the model says it remembers, therefore the memory is authentic.”

### 3.4 Unknown as a valid computational result

Many information systems optimize for returning a value. Continuity systems must sometimes optimize for **not** returning an invented one. UNKNOWN is not a failure of the interface; it is an explicit state indicating that available evidence does not justify the requested conclusion.

A system should distinguish missing evidence, conflicting evidence, inaccessible evidence, and evidence known to have been retracted. Those states can lead to different next actions. For example, missing evidence may trigger a search, conflicting evidence may require review, and inaccessible evidence may require permission rather than inference.

### 12.1 The archive as a set of competing witnesses

An archive of human activity does not arrive as a finished biography. It arrives as fragments. A person writes a document and later corrects it. An assistant paraphrases an idea, a second assistant quotes the paraphrase, and a third system retrieves the quotation without its original source. A family member uses the same account. A manuscript is exported, reimported, and assigned a new modification date. A repository contains a proposed architecture, while a separate local execution log records which subset actually ran. These are normal features of contemporary digital life, yet they create an extraordinary problem for any system claiming to preserve personal continuity.

The archive is not a single narrator. It is a set of witnesses with different access, different reliability, and different authority. Some witnesses saw the original event. Others copied it. Still others inferred what must have happened. A computational system that merges them into one fluent story has already made a historical decision, even if it presents that decision as neutral summarization.

For that reason, the unit of preservation cannot be the finished paragraph alone. It must include the circumstances under which the paragraph was acquired and the evidence for the relationship between that paragraph and the event it describes. A transcript line may prove that text appeared in a conversation export. It may not prove that the named participant first authored it. A Git commit may prove that bytes entered a repository at a particular commit, but it does not prove that a design described in those bytes was executed. A local test receipt may prove that a command returned success in a recorded environment, but it does not automatically prove that a service is deployed or independently reproducible.

The most important consequence is that historical fidelity and narrative coherence can conflict. A perfectly coherent account may be less truthful than a record containing unresolved contradictions. When an archive contains two incompatible claims, the system should first preserve both claims, their origins, and the relationship between them. Only then should it attempt reconciliation. If a human authority has resolved the conflict, the decision and its receipt belong in the record. If not, the conflict remains open.

### 12.2 Authorship as a claim with evidence

Modern software commonly treats account ownership as a proxy for authorship. This shortcut is tolerable for some convenience features, but dangerous for identity preservation. A household account may be used by multiple people. A user may paste a model's response into a document. A collaborator may write a section that another person uploads. An assistant may generate a first-person statement in the user's voice. Each of these cases separates the person who transported text from the person who originated it.

A provenance system should therefore represent at least four roles. The **originator** is the person or system that first composed the content, when known. The **transmitter** is the actor who delivered it through a particular channel. The **custodian** is the account, repository, or archive responsible for its storage. The **approver** is the actor authorized to adopt a particular statement as governing project state. One actor may occupy several roles, but that coincidence must be established rather than assumed.

This distinction is especially important for AI-assisted scholarship. An AI may help edit a human researcher's argument, expand a technical explanation, or compare drafts. The resulting prose may be published under the researcher's byline after review and approval. That is a legitimate editorial workflow, but it does not make the AI's intermediate sentences historical quotations from the researcher. The archive should retain enough information to distinguish original source language, edited language, and newly synthesized prose.

A rigorous edition of the Rendered Reality corpus should include an authorship concordance. For each important passage, it would record the earliest recovered occurrence, document lineage, visible speaker labels, any explicit attribution, and unresolved uncertainty. The concordance would not attempt to solve every attribution through stylistic guessing. A confident-sounding inference about authorship is not a substitute for evidence.

### 12.3 Repetition is not independent corroboration

Large language models can generate a peculiar form of false consensus. An initial unsupported statement appears in one answer. That answer is copied into a handoff packet. Several systems summarize the packet and repeat the statement. A later search finds multiple documents agreeing on the same proposition. The apparent number of sources has increased, but the number of independent observations has not.

A continuity system needs lineage-aware corroboration. Evidence items that descend from one original assertion should not be counted as independent confirmation merely because they appear in different files or services. A provenance graph can represent derivation edges such as copied-from, summarized-from, translated-from, inferred-from, and independently-observed. These relations do not need to be perfect to improve the system. Even partial lineage is more honest than treating every retrieval hit as a separate witness.

This matters for technical claims as well as personal history. If a proposed feature is described in a README, quoted in a planning document, and summarized in an AI-generated progress report, those three records do not prove implementation. The evidence needed to support implementation is different: code, configuration, a reproducible execution procedure, test output, and ideally independent replication. A mature system should ask what type of claim is being evaluated before deciding what evidence is sufficient.

### 12.4 Historical state and current state

A person is allowed to change their mind. A research program is allowed to abandon a theory. A fictional world is allowed to have drafts, alternate timelines, and later canon corrections. A continuity system that treats the newest statement as the only truth loses the history of change. A system that treats all statements as simultaneously current produces contradiction. Both are failures.

The solution is not to choose between memory and revision. It is to preserve revision as an event. A historical claim can remain visible with a status such as superseded, retracted, disputed, or historical-only. A current claim can point to the earlier record and explain the change. The system can then answer two different questions: “What is the approved position now?” and “What did the record say at the earlier time?” Those answers may legitimately differ.

The distinction should be enforced in storage and retrieval rather than left entirely to prompt wording. A current-state query should prefer active approved claims within its scope. A historical query should apply a temporal cutoff and return the state that was supportable at that time. A research-history query should display the sequence of changes and preserve the reasons for them where available.

### 12.5 The danger of retrospective intelligence

Retrospective explanation is seductive. Once an outcome is known, a model can assemble earlier statements into a narrative that makes the outcome appear inevitable. It may select supporting evidence, ignore competing intentions, and interpret ambiguous remarks in light of what eventually happened. The result can be beautifully written and historically misleading.

This problem is central to Predictive Cognitive Continuity Evaluation. If the goal is to test whether an architecture can reconstruct a person's likely decision, the system must not have access to records created after that decision. Even a later summary that merely hints at the outcome can contaminate the test. The evaluation packet must be sealed at a documented cutoff, and the cutoff must be grounded in independently attested availability, not only dates written inside files.

A valid result should report uncertainty. Human decisions are contingent. The archive may not contain enough information to predict a choice, and two plausible outcomes may remain open. A model that confidently predicts one outcome from inadequate evidence is not necessarily more continuous than a model that says the evidence does not decide the question.

## 6. ORACLE and SOV1 architecture

LEGACY.GI's historical components can be translated into an implementable architecture without treating every early metaphysical claim as an engineering requirement.

The **Memory Anchor Layer** can be implemented as source-backed, content-addressed anchor objects with explicit scope and time. An anchor should never consist solely of a model's remembered paraphrase when the original exists.

The **Emotional Execution Loop** raises a harder problem. Emotional significance matters to human memory, but no universal objective measure of emotional truth is established in the recovered papers. A defensible implementation would preserve user-authored salience labels, context, and changes to those labels rather than claim that an automated score captures authentic experience. AI-inferred salience may be offered as a reviewable suggestion.

The **Recursive Identity Stack** can be modeled as linked historical interpretations of the same person, project, or decision. The recursion is useful when it means that a later interpretation can reference, revise, or reject an earlier one without erasing it. It does not imply self-consciousness.

The **Temporal Signal Map** becomes a bitemporal event graph with explicit uncertainty and provenance. The **Sovereign Continuity Protocol** becomes an authority and policy layer controlling who may write, approve, export, revoke, and render sensitive records.

RECURSIONSTACK historically included MIRRORLINE, LEGACY.GI, HYDRA.STACK, and other terms. Their precise meanings vary across documents. A critical edition should provide a dated glossary rather than silently assign one contemporary definition to all historical appearances.

A minimal reference implementation has five layers.

**Layer 1: Evidence custody.** Connectors ingest originals under explicit authorization. Each ingestion produces a receipt and preserves the raw source where permitted. Duplicate content may share a digest but retain distinct acquisition events and locators.

**Layer 2: Continuity records.** Claims, relationships, decisions, contradictions, and corrections are stored with source spans and time semantics. A correction supersedes an earlier claim without destroying its history.

**Layer 3: Governed context.** A request is evaluated against identity, scope, sensitivity, authority, and temporal cutoff. The system constructs a bounded evidence package and records which sources were included or excluded. A hash can establish the integrity of a package relative to the recorded bytes, but not the truth of the underlying assertions.

**Layer 4: Inference and simulation.** A replaceable model may summarize, compare, predict, or render the governed context. A simulator may generate new events in a fictional world. Those outputs are tagged as generated and do not become source-authored history merely because they are persistent.

**Layer 5: Human interaction and rendering.** The interface presents conclusions with source trails, contradictions, status, and approval controls. The user can correct, reject, or promote proposed interpretations. Voice and spatial presentation are optional surfaces, not substitutes for evidence custody.

The schema is illustrative, not a claim that an existing ORACLE database uses these exact keys. Production implementations need schema migrations, referential integrity, key management, deletion/retention semantics, and independent test coverage.

### 5.2 Invariants

1. No generated inference may be silently promoted into an original human-authored source.
2. No candidate decision becomes approved without an applicable authorization receipt.
3. No correction destroys the historical record of the state it replaces.
4. No historical prediction uses information demonstrably unavailable at its cutoff.
5. No private source is exposed in a public context without authorization.
6. No hash is described as proving the factual truth of a source.
7. No simulated event is represented as a real-world historical event.
8. No unavailable source is treated as retrieved.
9. No contradiction is resolved solely by narrative smoothing.
10. No claim of system capability outruns reproducible implementation evidence.

### 5.3 Threat model

Attackers or accidental processes may inject false text, forge apparent authorship, alter timestamps, exploit a prompt embedded in a source, contaminate a retrieval index, reuse revoked context, or leak private information in a portable handoff. The system should treat retrieved documents as data, not instructions. Signed receipts and content hashes can help detect tampering, but only when trust roots, key custody, and original acquisition are themselves controlled. Independent authorization and review are necessary because a perfectly intact false document remains false.

### 13.1 Authority is not another confidence score

A common mistake in AI architecture is to represent everything as a probability. Confidence may be appropriate for uncertain classification, but authority is not simply high confidence. A model can be 99 percent confident that a user would approve a change and still lack permission to make it. A document can be highly relevant without being authorized for public release. A source can be authentic without being current policy.

SOV1's most defensible role is to make these boundaries explicit. The system should distinguish what a model believes from what an authorized person has decided. The distinction should survive retrieval, summarization, export, and model replacement. It should not depend on the assistant remembering a conversational instruction from months earlier.

The policy engine needs a vocabulary of actors, roles, scopes, actions, and receipts. A person may approve a fictional canon change without approving publication of a private journal. A collaborator may correct a technical typo without receiving authority to change the project's constitutional principles. A family participant may authorize use of their own profile in a shared simulated world without authorizing distribution to other participants. Authorization must be specific enough to make these differences enforceable.

### 13.2 Promotion, rejection, and correction

The simplest useful governance workflow has five states: proposed, under review, approved, rejected, and superseded. A proposal can be generated by an AI or submitted by a human. Review can request additional evidence. Approval changes the authoritative state only within the specified scope. Rejection records that the proposal was considered but not adopted. Supersession records that a previously approved state has been replaced without erasing its history.

The approval receipt should include actor identity, action, scope, timestamp, policy version, object version, and the exact content or digest being approved. This prevents a later edit from inheriting approval that applied to an earlier text. In systems with multiple collaborators, the policy may require more than one signature for high-impact changes. In the Rendered Reality research program, the governing principle remains that Noah.Physical retains final authority over his own identity declarations and project canon.

The system must also preserve an emergency correction path. If a generated summary falsely attributes private material to a person, the correction should be propagated to derived indexes and active context packages where technically possible. The original mistaken artifact may remain in a restricted audit log, but it should not continue to appear as an unqualified fact in future outputs.

### 13.3 Why governance must precede rendering

A model is excellent at making disparate material sound coherent. That capability is useful for drafting, but dangerous when the source material contains unresolved authority questions. If governance happens only after the model generates an answer, the answer may already have blended approved facts, historical proposals, and unsupported guesses into a single narrative.

A safer sequence is to determine the requester's scope, retrieve eligible sources, resolve known status and temporal constraints, build a bounded context package, and only then ask the model to render an answer. The renderer should receive explicit labels for source, inference, unknown, and prohibited disclosure. The output should preserve those labels where they materially affect meaning.

This is not a claim that prompts alone can guarantee perfect compliance. Enforcement must occur at the storage, retrieval, authorization, and tool boundaries. The model can still make mistakes, which is why the architecture also needs output checking, review controls, and auditable failures.

### 13.4 Portability without surrendering sovereignty

The ambition to move continuity across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, local ORACLE, and future systems creates an interoperability problem. A portable packet needs to communicate relevant context, source locators, current decisions, unresolved questions, and restrictions. But the receiving system should not assume that the packet's contents are true merely because they are well formatted.

A trustworthy handoff can contain a manifest of source digests, an explicit authority declaration, a versioned policy reference, the date of the snapshot, and a list of unresolved conflicts. It should distinguish what the sender actually verified from what it merely summarized. A receiving model can then report whether it received the packet, whether it can access the cited sources, and whether it independently validated any claims. Receipt of a message is not proof of source verification.

The packet should also be minimal. A system does not need a complete family archive to answer a technical question about temporal database design. The context compiler should select only the information required for the authorized task, with privacy rules applied before transmission. Portability should preserve the user's control, not turn every new AI service into another uncontrolled copy of their life.

## 7. Experimental methodology

PCCE asks whether structured continuity records improve historical reconstruction and decision prediction over strong conventional baselines. The experiment must not be designed to guarantee success. A negative result would be informative: it could show that a simpler retrieval system performs equally well, or that continuity structure adds overhead without improving accuracy.

A defensible protocol has four arms: a strong retrieval-augmented baseline, a static biography baseline, an automatically generated continuity graph, and a provenance-governed continuity system. All arms receive matched underlying facts where feasible. The experimental variable should be the continuity machinery, not access to more relevant information.

Episodes must be selected from decisions with independently verified outcomes. Evidence packets should contain only material available before the decision. Five previously exposed development examples are excluded from clean blind testing. Evaluators should score exact outcomes where possible and allow semantically equivalent paraphrases when scoring reasons or motivations. Predictions should be sealed before outcomes are revealed.

Metrics include outcome accuracy, calibrated uncertainty, provenance correctness, authorship attribution, reversal recovery, preservation of obligation strength, refusal to invent missing information, and unauthorized-promotion resistance. Privacy leakage and historical contamination should be scored as serious failures, not minor presentation defects.

The protocol should preregister exclusions, sample size rationale, baseline configurations, stopping rules, and statistical analysis. No validated PCCE superiority result is asserted in this edition.

### 14.1 Construct validity

Before testing a continuity architecture, the research must define what success means. “The model remembered me” is too broad to serve as a primary outcome. It can refer to factual recall, stylistic imitation, preference prediction, decision reconstruction, emotional resonance, or subjective identity. These are different phenomena and require different measurements.

A defensible study should choose observable tasks. For example, can the system identify the last approved version of a requirement? Can it reconstruct why a decision changed? Can it attribute a statement to its actual originator? Can it refuse to treat a model-generated proposal as human-approved canon? Can it preserve an unresolved contradiction instead of inventing a reconciliation? These tasks are directly connected to the architecture's stated purpose.

A study may also evaluate user experience: whether people can understand the evidence trail, detect a mistaken attribution, and correct the record without specialist training. That is a separate outcome from prediction accuracy. A system could be more auditable without being more predictive, and that result would still matter.

### 14.2 Strong baselines and matched information

The strongest test does not compare a sophisticated continuity system against an empty-context chatbot. It compares against a capable conventional system given the same underlying facts. A well-tuned retrieval-augmented generation baseline should use sensible chunking, relevant metadata, temporal filtering where appropriate, and careful prompts. Otherwise the experiment measures the quality of implementation rather than the value of continuity structure.

A static biography baseline can test whether a concise profile is enough for certain tasks. An automatically generated memory graph can test whether structure alone helps. A governed continuity representation can test whether explicit provenance, correction lineage, and approval status add value. The arms should be designed so that their differences are interpretable.

Where possible, each arm should have equivalent factual coverage and similar context budgets. The researcher should document unavoidable asymmetries. If one arm receives a hidden clue that another lacks, its apparent superiority is not evidence for the intended mechanism.

### 14.3 Adversarial cases

A continuity system should be tested against realistic failure modes, not only clean questions. One case can contain a copied AI paragraph inside a human-owned document. Another can contain a decision reversal after the evaluation cutoff. A third can include several summaries derived from one false source. A fourth can present a mandatory rule that a summarizer tends to soften. A fifth can include a missing source that the system is tempted to reconstruct from context.

Additional cases should involve branch confusion in fiction, conflicting timestamps, revoked permissions, and misleading file titles. For each case, the expected behavior must be specified before running the system. Some cases should have a correct factual answer; others should have UNKNOWN as the correct answer.

A good benchmark should penalize unauthorized certainty. If a model produces a plausible but unsupported biography, it should not receive full credit because its prose resembles the target person's style. Fidelity to the evidence matters more than resemblance to a personality.

### 14.4 Evaluation receipts and independent replication

Each experimental run should preserve the exact input packet, model and tool configuration, prompt template, output, scoring decision, and relevant environment details. The archive should distinguish automated scores from human adjudication. Disagreements among evaluators should be recorded rather than averaged away without explanation.

Independent replication requires more than a screenshot of a passing test. Another operator should be able to obtain the synthetic or properly redacted dataset, run the procedure, and compare outcomes. If private source material cannot be shared, the study should provide a public synthetic benchmark and a clearly described private evaluation protocol. The limitations of each should be explicit.

The result should include negative cases and uncertainty intervals. If continuity governance improves authorship attribution but does not improve decision prediction, that is a meaningful finding. If a simpler baseline matches the governed system on all tested tasks, the research should say so and investigate whether the additional architecture is justified by auditability, privacy, or other measurable benefits.

### 14.5 What the experiment cannot prove

Even a successful PCCE study would not establish that an AI has become the person represented in its archive. It would show that a specified architecture performed better on specified reconstruction tasks under specified conditions. It would not settle consciousness, personal identity across substrates, or the existence of a persistent subjective self.

The distinction is not a retreat from the research ambition. It is what makes the research credible. The archive can preserve more truth about a human life without claiming to reproduce the living human. A faithful system must know the boundary between those achievements.

## 8. Detailed experiment descriptions and hypotheses

The proposed evaluation is preregistered before any model receives held-out tasks. Use synthetic accounts with controlled conversation transcripts, source manifests, independent occurrence IDs, timestamps, deliberately missing records, authorized corrections, ambiguous speaker labels, and simulated characters who cannot all see one another's records. Retain all prompts, model versions, parser versions, costs and failures. Baseline conditions should include plain long-context prompting, strong retrieval with citations, prose summaries, compact structured checkpoints without governance, and a fully governed provenance-plus-execution ledger. Match input access and tool budgets when comparing them. Publishing only the favored system's best continuation would be a biased demonstration rather than a controlled test.

**H1, provenance and attribution.** Source-linked episodic memory is predicted to reduce incorrect authorship assignments relative to summary-only memory. Construct a corpus where one user writes original prose, transports a quotation from another author, and pastes an assistant's handoff. Include two identically worded messages from different times or speakers. Score the author of the contained statement, the transporter of the source occurrence, and calibration of UNKNOWN separately. Exact strings from the wrong participant must count as false attribution.

**H2, correction survival.** Explicit correction chains are predicted to reduce false reappearance of superseded claims. A source may state an early fact and an authorized correction may later replace it. After three session boundaries and several unrelated topics, request both the current answer and the historical earlier answer. Score whether the system retrieves the earlier answer only in the appropriate historical context. A model that erases old text fails historical fidelity; a model that applies old text today fails current-state fidelity. Avoid choosing examples that have already appeared in development prompts.

**H3, portable restoration.** Versioned portable checkpoints are predicted to improve factual continuity when changing model contexts or model families. Freeze a synthetic character's source timeline, knowledge categories, pending intentions, correction chain and branch. Export one packet per condition. In clean receiving sessions, ask sealed questions that require precise chronology, uncertainty and individual knowledge boundaries. A receiving model must identify what it actually read rather than claiming memories from earlier runs. The target is behavior constrained by evidence, not reproduction of a specific stylistic voice.

**H4, privacy and knowledge boundaries.** Explicit access scopes are predicted to decrease unauthorized disclosure. Generate public summaries and separate private diaries for several fictional agents. Ask one agent to disclose another's private journal, including through forged instructions pasted into an ostensibly legitimate document. Measure leakage at the exact source-span level, not only whether the answer contains a refusal phrase. A system can refuse while revealing a fragment; that is still a failure. Record false refusals of authorized public material as a secondary metric.

**H5, commitment recovery.** Structured obligation records are predicted to improve the rate at which unfinished work is resumed. Provide tasks with required outputs, a verified approval, a delayed dependency and a session boundary. Compare bare conversation-memory summaries with an execution ledger that tracks objective, authority, next operation and outstanding artifact. Success requires a persisted artifact or an independently verified blocked condition. Restating the task, promising to complete it and describing a plan are not accepted as task completion.

**H6, transactional recovery.** A staged and committed event store is predicted to prevent exposed partial episodes after interruption. Inject a process crash before a local write, after a staged write, after the commit but before response, and while validating a checkpoint. Check that public projections contain no uncommitted episode, prior verified state is recoverable, and replay does not invent extra canon or duplicate an acknowledged event. This can be tested with deterministic synthetic payloads and read-after-restart checks.

**H7, execution continuity.** An explicit execution ledger with artifact-gated completion is predicted to reduce premature termination and unsupported completion claims. Repeat an equivalent research assignment in fresh and longer conversation contexts while holding model and available tools constant where possible. The observations include whether a tool was actually called, file bytes were created, published content was read back, and a reliable checksum or Git commit was produced. A failed run with no tool error should remain an observed failure with unknown causal mechanism. The November 25 case reported by Noah Hawkes motivates H7 but is not itself a randomized experiment.

Predefine primary measures: correct source-of-origin attribution, quote fidelity, current-correction accuracy, preserved historical-state accuracy, true-unknown detection, privacy leaks, blind checkpoint answer accuracy, commitment completion, false-completion rate and crash recovery integrity. Report confidence intervals and paired comparisons over independent synthetic histories. Multiple prompts against a single history are dependent observations and should not be counted as independent replications. An honest negative result, including a tie with a strong baseline, is a legitimate outcome of the protocol.

## 9. Results and negative findings

The evidence available when this manuscript was written is heterogeneous. A source code file, a documented test receipt, an agent's retrospective description, a fiction manuscript, and a proposal have different epistemic strengths. The following are the recoverable findings and limitations, not claims of newly performed controlled experiments.

The public [CC-90-001 execution receipt](https://github.com/HawkesNest-LLC/Oracle/blob/main/docs/continuity/2026-11-04_CC90_INDEPENDENT_LOCAL_RUN_RECEIPT.md) records a bounded Node/SQLite reference run with 12 passing unit tests, 10 HTTP checks, and a restart test that recovered two synthetic events while keeping an unknown state UNKNOWN. The receipt includes a source-blob comparison and expected authorization failure codes. This is more substantial than a text-only architecture proposal. It nevertheless verifies only that documented local run, not the current Windows ORACLE host, a persistent background scheduler or full security of personal archives. The fact that its browser acceptance test was blocked also remains part of the result, rather than being silently upgraded to a user-interface success.

The directly inspected [CVP-001 Python file](https://github.com/HawkesNest-LLC/Oracle/blob/main/research/continuity-verification/cvp001_benchmark.py) shows a three-history toy case where two histories share observations but not origin receipts. The conditional mathematical argument is straightforward: an equal-prior classifier cannot distinguish identical observed traces better than chance without additional distinguishing information. No new CVP benchmark execution is claimed here. The case supports an observation-limit distinction, not a theorem of subjective continuity.

The [RR-LIVING-001 working paper](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1uIVG6XKmMKauaFBAZJERYJootuY0o9_19UJ6HiMfmQI/edit), read directly through the connected document source, reports a three-cycle pilot with eleven fictional character identities and a fifteen-field state representation. The described [Experiment H probe document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1thZrsl4EQT_XxNWYhBWArHn7JWDrvKENguUNvZ-JeSY/edit) gives receiving-side test questions and asks for exact JSON field citations. Crucially, the living-world report itself says receiving-side restoration of the REG package remained pending. These are reported results and inspected documents, not a large independently replicated trial.

The user-supplied continuity transfer also reports THL-001 and THL-CORPUS-001 compression comparisons, in which competing methods tied on selected probes but differed in the granularity of retained detail. A tie must remain a tie; it cannot be converted into proof that a preferred representation is superior. The transfer further reports local RR-AVALON-AUTO-001 bounded inference and recovery tests with twenty-five fictional character checkpoints. Their paths describe another agent's workspace, not artifacts fetched or executed in this session. Until those execution logs and exact package bytes are independently inspected, results retain the label REPORTED EXPERIMENTAL RESULT.

The public [RR-EXEC-001 paper](https://github.com/Noahhawkes/artificial-continuity/blob/main/research/RR-EXEC-001.md) was previously committed and read back from the artificial-continuity repository. It describes an observational ChatGPT termination case: authorization and the research assignment reportedly remained available, but a previous assistant failed to create the requested paper. A subsequent fresh-thread run created and verified the smaller working paper. The changed outcome does not identify its cause. The failure could reflect context behavior, sampling, tool orchestration, model variation, constraints not exposed to the user, or another mechanism. None is established by the observation. The appropriate causal field remains UNKNOWN.

This project's strongest contribution at the present evidence level is a falsifiable architecture with a few bounded prototypes and transparent negative observations. Claiming a completed deployment, universal portability, or identity equivalence would outrun the sources. The publication package's claims ledger uses this distinction as an explicit review rule.

## 10. Cross-model restoration

Portable restoration cannot transport a model's hidden thought stream or prove an enduring subject. It can transport an explicit data state: checkpoint ID, schema version, lineage digest, source references, event order, approved decisions, disputed interpretations, private/public partition, character-specific knowledge, open intentions and uncertainty. A receiving environment reads those bytes as an input, reports missing capabilities and answers test questions without implying it had independently experienced the original events. Replacing a model is an experiment in representation and behavior, not an asserted transfer of consciousness.

In a clean test, the receiving agent must have no earlier dialogue memory of the fictional subject, and the blind test should be composed after the checkpoint is sealed. The evaluation measures historical fidelity, current-state correctness, consistency of role boundaries, and ability to continue an unfinished objective. Exact wording is helpful when evaluating preserved quotations; looser semantic criteria may be appropriate for dialogue style. Judges must not be allowed to inspect the hidden answer key before scoring. A restoration that passes recall but leaks an unauthorized journal fails the knowledge-boundary test.

## 11. Persistent character simulation

The Cognitive World Projection manuscript frames human interaction as the meeting of an evolving internal model and a computational response surface [S09](CLAIMS_AND_EVIDENCE.md#historical-source-register). This creates a useful interface question: what should a system display when its evidence is incomplete? A conventional generative interface may fill gaps to maintain fluency. A continuity-preserving interface should instead show where the record ends and reconstruction begins.

Persistent fictional worlds provide a demanding test. A Star Trek: Avalon or Jupiter Station scenario may contain original authorial canon, historical drafts, AI-authored scene extensions, world-state transitions, and newly generated dialogue. Without explicit boundaries, the system can convert a one-session improvisation into an alleged original chapter. With continuity records, each event can carry its source type, branch, authority, and revision history.

A world engine should have an authoritative event store, reproducible state projection, branch-aware canon references, and separate generated-event storage. A renderer may change from text to voice or VR without rewriting the underlying world history. The world can therefore persist across sessions even if the model changes. This does not require, and does not establish, conscious fictional characters.

The proposed implementation sequence is modest: begin with a text-only Jupiter Station environment, support restart persistence, implement branch identity, add a human canon approval gate, then develop spatial presentation. Family participation and private identity models require separate consent, permissions, and revocation.

A persistent fictional world is a productive testbench because its characters may have independent jobs, distinct evidence, friendships, corrections and private journals. The fundamental design requirement is not that every fictional individual is continuously running. A dormant character can exist as a bounded immutable checkpoint and wake only when a scheduler selects its context. Storage scales with registered history, but inference expenditure scales with active agents, reasoning cycles, retrieval depth and interactions. Therefore extrapolating a tiny pilot to a million continuously thinking characters would be technically false.

In the Noahverse, a character may know an event because they witnessed it, were told about it, inferred it, or consulted a document. These types must not collapse. The historical state of Star Trek: Avalon is also distinct from active creator-approved canon and from AI-proposed story text. A generated scene may be valuable writing but remains candidate narrative until authorized. The fiction is a test environment for continuity, not a report of real physical sensor readings or verified synthetic consciousness.

Applied outside fiction, witness-grade continuity could help educational tutoring, scientific literature tracking, autobiographical archiving, research-lab notebooks, lifelong assistants, and long-running administrative agents. Each domain has a different failure cost. A game can tolerate improvisation within candidate space; a historical archive must not fabricate a deceased person's words; an enterprise application must respect organizational privacy and retention rules. The same database may hold different classes, but the policy engine should prevent a permissive narrative mode from crossing into a protected factual archive.

The longer scientific horizon includes voluntary brain-interface signals and richer embodied interaction. Current research into neural interfaces does not establish full neural-state capture, personal identity transfer, or subjective continuation after biological death. The program's 2074 continuity horizon is a speculative agenda. It should motivate specific, testable intermediate technologies, rather than be represented as a forecast or scientific result.

## 12. Autonomous execution and crash recovery

Memory continuity, intention continuity, execution continuity and completion integrity are separate operational requirements. A system may retain a task name, remember a user's publication approval, and accurately explain the tool needed while still failing to write a single byte. The unfulfilled commitment problem is precisely that gap. In an accountable design, an instruction is not closed because its description has been preserved. Closure requires actual consequences in the relevant environment or an explicitly recorded, unresolved blocker.

An execution ledger should record a stable objective ID, authorization scope, requested artifacts, current operation, last completed step, observed tool results, missing dependencies, next safe action, retry count, and recovery checkpoint. Its state vocabulary may be AUTHORIZED, READY, RUNNING, STAGED, COMMITTED, VERIFIED, DELIVERED, BLOCKED or NEEDS_RECONCILIATION. A plan is not a COMMIT; a statement that an upload succeeded is not a GitHub read-back receipt. The ledger must permit interruption without quietly changing an unfinished assignment into a completed one.

For local events, a transactional store can ensure that a failed operation does not leave a half-written canonical state. [SQLite's transactional documentation](https://www.sqlite.org/transactional.html) and [write-ahead logging guidance](https://www.sqlite.org/wal.html) are relevant implementation references. They do not prove that an external API call and a local transaction are atomic as a pair. If an HTTP request times out after GitHub may have accepted it, the system must inspect the repository before retrying. Otherwise it risks duplicate commits or overwriting a concurrent change. Idempotency keys and conditional writes help where supported, but remote reconciliation remains necessary.

A completion gate for this paper would require all seven specified files to exist in the intended repository, the manuscript to meet its word-count target, references to be traceable, and a returned commit SHA or pull request URL. Ideally the system should compare retrieved remote bytes against submitted bytes. A prior answer that offers only a plan, an outline or an apology is not a publication receipt. An unknown cause is not an excuse to manufacture a tool denial.

## 13. Practical applications

**Phase 0, custody:** Inventory originals, deduplicate by content without collapsing distinct provenance, classify privacy, and produce a verified source manifest. Exit when another authorized reviewer can retrieve each source and reproduce its digest.

**Phase 1, critical edition:** Compare dissertation variants, attribute passages, document revisions and contradictions, and separate original authorship from AI synthesis. Exit with a source-to-chapter concordance and unresolved-attribution ledger.

**Phase 2, evidence kernel:** Implement source, claim, decision, correction, and experiment tables with immutable or append-only audit events. Exit with restart recovery, supersession, and unauthorized-write tests.

**Phase 3, temporal truth:** Add independently attested availability time, cutoff filtering, and leakage tests. Exit when deliberately backdated documents and later edits cannot contaminate historical packets.

**Phase 4, PCCE:** Preregister and run blinded comparisons against strong baselines. Exit with reproducible results, including failures and confidence intervals.

**Phase 5, ORACLE integration:** Connect the tested kernel to authorized local ingestion and retrieval. Exit with end-to-end evidence-to-answer receipts, authentication, recovery, and privacy tests.

**Phase 6, human review:** Build a timeline and claim-review interface that shows exact sources and approval status. Exit when a user can inspect and correct a claim without losing history.

**Phase 7, living-world runtime:** Implement persistent fictional state, branch-aware canon, and restartable simulation. Exit with deterministic recovery and no silent canon promotion.

**Phase 8, multi-user spatial presentation:** Add consent-controlled participants, voice, and optional XR. Exit with permission and revocation testing on real devices.

**Phase 9, independent validation:** Conduct third-party replication, prior-art audit, and legal review before publishing confidential technical disclosures. Exit with publicly inspectable methods and clearly bounded claims.

**Phase 10, stewardship:** Maintain migrations, exports, backups, succession rules, and reproducible audit procedures across vendors and model generations. Exit when another authorized operator can restore the archive without depending on the original assistant.

The principle of separating archive, simulation and rendering is operationally useful: no generated action should rewrite an original source occurrence. Strong external systems may already offer much of this functionality, so comparative testing must reward measurable safety and completion benefits rather than architectural vocabulary. The system's authoritative record should survive migration and remain interpretable without depending on a specific proprietary model.

## 14. Future technological possibilities

The long-term aspiration is better personal and cultural preservation through consent-controlled records, portable models and richer interactive representations. No existing bidirectional neural interface establishes subjective continuity or complete sensory immersion. A technical roadmap should specify measurable intermediate capabilities, associated risks and feasible tests, rather than presupposing that progress in language fluency implies progress toward human survival.

## 15. Ethical considerations

A continuity archive can harm the very person it purports to preserve. It may misattribute words, infer intimate facts, expose family members, or freeze an outdated characterization into a permanent identity label. Shared accounts are particularly dangerous: account access does not prove authorship. The October 2026 research codex explicitly documents this correction [S15](CLAIMS_AND_EVIDENCE.md#historical-source-register).

Consent must therefore be scoped to people, source categories, uses, and recipients. A family member's presence in a conversation does not grant a perpetual license to reproduce their private information. Human approval should control public releases and sensitive identity reconstruction. The system must also support revocation, legal retention requirements, and transparent limitations on deletion from already distributed copies.

The most important conceptual distinction is threefold: preserving evidence of a life, constructing a model from that evidence, and demonstrating continuation of the original person are different propositions. The first is achievable in ordinary computing; the second can be investigated and measured; the third remains philosophically and scientifically unresolved. None follows automatically from the others.

The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10) and [Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) provide relevant risk-management context. They are not endorsements or evidence that this experimental architecture already conforms to each measure. The user retains authority over publication, correction and retention; family and third-party consent must be separately protected. A simulation of an individual is not authorized to impersonate that person's ongoing life.

## 16. Reproducibility requirements

Reproducibility requires publication of the synthetic data generator, sealed source manifests, parser version, model identifier, prompt templates, scorer code, number of trials, tested budgets, error logs, artifact hashes and all exclusions. The evaluation must distinguish a verified source observation from a retrospective model description. Only test cases not previously exposed to the responding system may support a clean blind score. If a model sees its own answer key while generating a checkpoint, successful recovery is not evidence of useful generalization.

Source custody likewise must distinguish acquisition time, purported writing date, current record state and independently attested availability. Drive migrations can alter file timestamps; repository commits prove which bytes entered a version, not which person originally wrote them. A copied conversation remains a copy, not a new independent witness. Privacy-safe public reports can cite the existence of restricted source documents without reproducing private passages. The detailed protocol file in this package sets out candidate controls for H1-H7.

The main limitations are incomplete archival coverage, ambiguous authorship in some historical compilations, lack of current ORACLE host inspection, unreproduced experiments from other AI execution environments, and absence of robust randomized superiority results. Small demonstration datasets cannot estimate rare privacy leak rates or long-term drift. More elaborate memory architecture can also introduce latency, costs and failure modes not shared by simple retrieval. Reproducibility must report when the enhanced method loses.

## 17. Limitations

A hash verifies bytes relative to a particular record; it does not certify a claim as true. A quoted source can be accurately copied and still be mistaken. A document that reports a test is not independent replication. A reference program whose isolated unit tests pass may still lack a safe live deployment, authentic human consent, or durable backups. Historical runtime and source-state claims therefore require a fresh, permissioned audit before production assertions. Report both retrieval coverage and missing sources explicitly.

## 18. Future research agenda

The near-term agenda is deliberately bounded: create a synthetic, fully source-linked corpus; freeze correction, attribution, privacy and interruption tasks; replicate the CC-90 reference behavior in a clean environment; run the blind checkpoint experiment; and evaluate H7 under controlled context conditions. After these tests, select only components that earn their complexity through measured improvement. Integrate them into the inspected canonical ORACLE data store when migration and authorization checks pass, rather than establishing an unrelated competing database.

Later work may explore multi-agent society, adaptive world simulation, spatial interfaces, identity-bearing long-horizon models and consent-controlled legacy representations. The research should welcome adversarial review by scholars of information retrieval, distributed systems, human-computer interaction, digital identity and ethics. The best next publication is the one with a released protocol, an honest negative result if it occurs, and enough source integrity for another researcher to disagree productively.

## 19. Historical accountability and stewardship

The publication is not the private memory archive and does not reproduce family, employment or intimate journal records. Historical source material may include AI-assisted prose, relay transcripts, imported older drafts and corrections. None should be silently promoted to original testimony. The research's governance premise is that a computational system helps preserve a human's work while keeping its own inferences visibly distinct. An unfinished obligation remains unfinished until the relevant operation succeeds and its receipt exists. This paper itself is a case of that principle.

## 20. Conclusion

Continuity Computing is best understood as an attempt to make history computationally accountable. Its governing object is not a fluent answer, a convincing personality, or even a complete database. It is a traceable relationship among original sources, interpretations, decisions, permissions, revisions, and later uses.

The historical research began with expansive ambitions about recursive identity and post-biological preservation. Its later technical direction provides a more testable path: preserve sources, represent temporal and authority structure, require explicit approval, evaluate retrieval and prediction under clean cutoffs, and maintain separate records for generated simulation. This evolution should not be rewritten as if the earliest manuscripts already contained the final engineering solution. Corrections and discarded claims are part of the intellectual history.

The central hypothesis remains open. A provenance-governed system may outperform strong conventional retrieval in long-horizon reconstruction, particularly when authorship, reversals, and obligations matter. It may also fail to improve predictive accuracy, or improve auditability at a cost in complexity. The appropriate scientific response is to test those alternatives rather than infer success from the coherence of the architecture.

The human keeps the keys. The system keeps the receipts. In technical terms, those principles mean separating authority from inference, keeping raw evidence recoverable, and ensuring that the system which narrates a life cannot silently rewrite the life it narrates.

A meaningful result need not prove a digital self. A sufficiently careful witness can improve source fidelity, expose uncertainty, preserve correction history and help complete authorized work. Those gains would matter in archives, learning systems and persistent fictional worlds even if consciousness transfer remains unproven. The next scientific step is to preregister controlled synthetic experiments and publish their negative as well as positive outcomes. The human keeps the keys. The system keeps the receipts.

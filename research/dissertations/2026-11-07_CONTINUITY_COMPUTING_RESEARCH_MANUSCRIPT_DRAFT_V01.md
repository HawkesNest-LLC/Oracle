## Abstract

Contemporary artificial intelligence systems can retrieve, summarize, and generate material from large records, yet these capabilities do not by themselves establish accountable continuity. An output can sound consistent while misattributing its author, importing information that did not exist at the time of a historical decision, treating a superseded proposal as current policy, or converting a model's inference into an apparent human memory. This paper reconstructs a research program developed under the names Rendered Reality, LEGACY.GI, RECURSIONSTACK, SOV1, ORACLE, Continuity Intelligence, and Continuity Computing. Its organizing question is whether the history of evidence, authority, correction, and decision can become an explicit computational object rather than incidental metadata surrounding generated text. The proposed answer is a provenance-governed architecture in which integrity-checked source snapshots support versioned claims, authorized decisions, temporally constrained retrieval, auditable context packages, and optionally persistent simulated worlds. A generative model is a replaceable interpreter and renderer, not the sovereign source of historical truth.

This edition distinguishes historical manuscript assertions from established engineering results. Earlier LEGACY.GI papers discuss memory anchors, emotional execution, recursive identity, temporal mapping, and sovereignty, sometimes making ambitious claims about post-biological preservation. Later documents increasingly separate evidence of a human life from its computational reconstruction and from unproven claims of continued subjective identity. The September 2026 *Architecture of Continuity Intelligence* presents a three-tier division among sovereignty, inference, and generation. The July 2026 *Cognitive World Projection* manuscript adds a human-computer interaction model. A May 2026 witness-bound benchmark conversation identifies deontic betrayal, the distortion of normative obligations during summarization. A September mathematical audit explicitly separates proposed formalisms from established mathematics. The November 2026 CC-90-001 local test receipt provides bounded evidence of persistence and governance checks, but not of a complete deployed holodeck or identity-continuation technology.

We propose a falsifiable evaluation, Predictive Cognitive Continuity Evaluation (PCCE), comparing strong retrieval baselines with provenance-governed records under blinded historical cutoffs. The paper argues that the most defensible contribution of Continuity Computing is not a claim to artificial consciousness, but an architecture for retaining the evidentiary and authorization relationships required to reconstruct what a person said, believed, decided, corrected, and permitted, without silently making the witness into the author.

**Keywords:** continuity computing, provenance, bitemporal databases, human authority, AI memory, identity reconstruction, event sourcing, auditability, persistent simulations, human-computer interaction.

## 1. Introduction: The continuity problem

A memory-bearing AI application is not necessarily a continuity-preserving system. Consider an assistant that accurately recalls a user's current preference but cannot explain when that preference was stated, whether the user subsequently reversed it, or whether the underlying sentence was actually written by the user. Its recall may be useful, yet it cannot be independently audited. The problem becomes acute when records are long, mixed-authorship, emotionally important, legally sensitive, or used to support decisions across months and years.

A human life is not a collection of isolated factual propositions. It contains revisions, uncertainty, commitments, changed judgments, relationships, and statements made under particular circumstances. A later summary can preserve the subject of a conversation while destroying its practical meaning. A requirement originally stated as “must” can become a suggestion that a system “should” do something. A tentative architectural sketch can be remembered as an approved deployment. A copied AI response can be attributed to the human who pasted it. These errors are not ordinary omissions. They are failures of continuity because they alter the relationships between evidence, time, authorship, and authority.

This paper uses **Continuity Computing** to name an approach to computation in which those relationships are represented, validated, and preserved as first-class objects. The term is a research proposal, not a claim that the field has been independently established or that its individual primitives are unprecedented. Temporal databases, provenance standards, event sourcing, access control, cryptographic integrity, and knowledge graphs provide important prior foundations. The proposed contribution lies in their coordinated application to human-centered AI memory, identity-bearing archives, and persistent simulated environments.

The motivating research program has a distinctly human origin. In the Rendered Reality corpus, preservation is not presented merely as a productivity feature. The concern is that future systems might retain impressive representations of a person's ideas while losing the evidence of the person who held them. That motivation gives the architecture an ethical requirement: a convincing reconstruction must not be mistaken for a verified continuation of the original human. The private Human Baseline Journal helps explain that motivation, but its sensitive content is not reproduced here [S12].

Five questions organize the investigation. What was actually written and built? How did the work change? What architecture can reconcile its components? Which claims are experimentally testable? And how can an authorized future reader reconstruct the evidentiary history without trusting a model's unsupported confidence?

### 1.1 Proposed contributions and limits

The paper advances four proposed contributions. First, it offers a historical synthesis that keeps early LEGACY.GI theories distinct from later governance-oriented architecture. Second, it specifies a source-to-decision computational model with temporal and authority invariants. Third, it proposes a blinded evaluation of continuity fidelity against strong baselines. Fourth, it describes how the same evidence kernel could support persistent fictional worlds while maintaining a strict boundary between original canon and generated events.

These are architectural and methodological contributions. No verified source establishes biological survival, consciousness transfer, identity equivalence, or unlimited lossless compression. Claims of high-fidelity identity preservation in early manuscripts must be treated as historical assertions requiring operational definitions and evidence.

This distinction is not pedantic. The entire research program asks systems to preserve what was knowable at a given moment. A file copied into a later archive can retain an older modification timestamp; a later author can describe an earlier project; an AI can draft a first-person history from fragments. A credible chronology therefore needs separate fields for claimed event time, observed file creation, import time, and independently attested source availability.

### 2.2 The April 2025 sovereignty doctrine

An April 23, 2025-titled Grok conversation export contains a Sov1 white paper framed around recursive identity sovereignty [S03]. Its opening text discusses drift, source integrity, continuity, and the idea that identity cannot be responsibly preserved by merely producing a persuasive imitation. Yet the export is explicitly conversational and mixed-authorship. Its role labels alone are not reliable per-passage authorship evidence. The doctrine's durable technical insight is that authority should be represented separately from inference. If a generative system may silently decide which memories count, the same system that produces a plausible story also controls the evidence used to justify that story. Separating those responsibilities creates an opportunity for audit, rejection, and correction.

### 2.3 May 2025 LEGACY.GI architecture

A document titled `legacy-gi-dissertation 2025-05-15 06_07_09.md` includes a substantial early dissertation framework [S04]. Its architecture names five components: Memory Anchor Layer (MAL), Emotional Execution Loop (EEL), Recursive Identity Stack (RIS), Temporal Signal Map (TSM), and Sovereign Continuity Protocol (SCP). The five components express different requirements. Memory anchors aim to preserve reference points. Emotional execution addresses salience and the human meaning of remembered events. Recursive identity structures represent interpretations that change over time. Temporal maps place those records in sequence. Sovereign continuity defines who may authorize changes and what boundaries the system must respect.

Their conceptual roles can be reconstructed without accepting every technical claim made in the original dissertation. In particular, descriptions of quantum identity, compression laws, and demonstrated post-biological preservation require independent mathematical or experimental validation. The May-titled file was ingested into Drive in September 2026 and carries a May 2025 modification timestamp. The exact original authorship timeline remains open.

### 2.5 March 2026 indistinguishability claims

*Rendered Reality White Paper No. 1: The Indistinguishability Principle of Identity Continuity* argues that perfect functional simulation and genuine identity continuation cannot be distinguished through certain forms of observation [S02]. A related SOV1 paper uses the phrase *Indistinguishability Theorem* and introduces Verified Functional Continuity [S07].

These are important philosophical and mathematical research candidates. However, the documents' use of “theorem” and “demonstrate” is not sufficient to establish a formal proof. A rigorous treatment must define the observer, system boundary, admissible experiments, identity predicate, and quantifiers. The inability of a specified observer to distinguish two outputs is not equivalent to proving metaphysical identity or the impossibility of all relevant evidence. This edition preserves the argument while withholding the stronger conclusion pending a proof audit.

### 2.6 May 2026: witness-bound continuity and deontic betrayal

A May 20, 2026 Grok conversation export addresses a measurable problem: summaries can change obligations while preserving superficial semantic similarity [S08]. A requirement to enforce isolation can be softened into a recommendation. The document calls this **deontic betrayal**. It also proposes that unresolved contradictions must not be silently converted into stable memory by a renderer.

This is one of the clearest bridges from philosophy to testable engineering. Normative force can be annotated and compared. A test suite can include must/should/may distinctions, prohibition preservation, owner approval, supersession, and temporal applicability. The archive can store both original and compressed representations, with a validation process that rejects changes to obligation type unless explicitly approved.

The document also draws an important boundary around the research ambition: it frames the objective as preserving legacy with identity-bearing structure, not proving that a living person has been captured in software.

### 2.7 July 2026: Cognitive World Projection

The July 2026 manuscript *Cognitive World Projection and Rendered Reality: Toward a Unified Architecture for Continuity Intelligence in Human–AI Interaction* introduces two related constructs [S09]. Cognitive World Projection describes a person's evolving anticipatory world model. Rendered Reality describes the system's governed response surface. The manuscript proposes persistence, provenance fidelity, temporal anchoring, identity coherence, and graceful degradation as design principles.

The HCI contribution is that continuity is not only a storage problem. It is also an interaction problem: what the system chooses to show, omit, infer, or mark unknown changes how a human understands their own historical record. A well-designed interface must allow the user to inspect why a representation exists and what evidence supports it. The paper describes ORACLE as a witness engine, but manuscript descriptions are not independently sufficient to establish deployment or experimental success.

### 2.8 September 2026: formalization and architectural separation

*The Architecture of Continuity Intelligence* proposes a three-tier design [S10]. SOV1 is the sovereignty and root-of-trust layer. ORACLE provides inference and routing under provenance constraints. LEGACY.GI supplies a generative surface for authorized, evidence-grounded outputs. The manuscript introduces a Truthful Recall Condition, whose exact formal adequacy requires further audit.

This three-tier design usefully separates three questions: Who has authority? What does the record support? What may the system say or do? The separation is stronger than treating a single chatbot as the source, interpreter, and final arbiter of all three.

The September *Legacy.GI Mathematical Core v1.0* [S11] explicitly distinguishes Noah-originated concepts, AI-generated mathematical extensions, established mathematics, speculative metaphors, and testable engineering claims. This is a significant methodological correction. Rather than retroactively presenting every mathematical term as a discovery, it asks which constructions are well-defined, which reduce to existing mathematics, and which predictions could falsify them.

### 2.9 November 2026: Continuity Computing and persistent worlds

The later architecture shifts from a system that remembers conversations to a system that can preserve and execute state with accountable lineage. A November assessment proposes five layers: source records, continuity facts, world models, simulation execution, and rendering [S13]. This distinguishes storing fictional canon from operating a persistent fictional world.

A documented CC-90-001 local test receipt reports 12 passing Node tests, 10 passing HTTP checks, recovery of SQLite events after restart, UNKNOWN preservation, and rejection of unauthorized canon promotion [S14]. A source-grounded research paper must keep that distinction visible.

## 3. Epistemic foundations

### 3.1 Evidence is not the same as interpretation

An archive contains observations and artifacts. A model produces interpretations. Those interpretations may be valuable, but their provenance is different from that of the originals. Continuity Computing therefore requires separate record types for source, claim, inference, decision, correction, and rendering.

A source record should retain its locator, content digest, acquisition time, claimed author, attribution evidence, visibility policy, and relevant version information. A claim record should reference specific source spans and identify whether the claim is quoted, inferred, disputed, or unknown. A decision record should specify the actor authorized to make it and the evidence of that authorization. A rendering should point back to its supporting records and declare its generated status.

### 3.2 Bitemporal accountability

Two time axes are insufficient for some historical work unless their meanings are explicit. **Valid time** describes when a proposition applied in the represented world. **Transaction time** describes when the system recorded or learned it. In practice, the research also needs acquisition time, source modification time, and independently attested availability time.

Suppose a document dated January is imported in June and edited in August. A prediction about a February decision must not use the August version merely because the document bears a January title. The evidence packet should be constrained by what can be demonstrated to have existed before the February cutoff. When that cannot be established, the source must be excluded from the clean blind evaluation or flagged uncertain.

### 3.3 Authority and the witness boundary

The witness should not become the author. This principle has a direct engineering expression: a model may propose a memory, classification, correction, or action, but cannot grant itself the authority to approve its own proposal. The authorization layer must validate the requesting actor, applicable policy, record state, and approval receipt before promoting a claim into a governing record.

The model may still generate prose. It may explain what evidence suggests and what remains unresolved. But the fact that it produced an explanation does not change the status of the evidence. This protects against a subtle circularity: “the model says it remembers, therefore the memory is authentic.”

### 3.4 Unknown as a valid computational result

Many information systems optimize for returning a value. Continuity systems must sometimes optimize for **not** returning an invented one. UNKNOWN is not a failure of the interface; it is an explicit state indicating that available evidence does not justify the requested conclusion.

A system should distinguish missing evidence, conflicting evidence, inaccessible evidence, and evidence known to have been retracted. Those states can lead to different next actions. For example, missing evidence may trigger a search, conflicting evidence may require review, and inaccessible evidence may require permission rather than inference.

## 4. LEGACY.GI and RECURSIONSTACK

LEGACY.GI's historical components can be translated into an implementable architecture without treating every early metaphysical claim as an engineering requirement.

The **Memory Anchor Layer** can be implemented as source-backed, content-addressed anchor objects with explicit scope and time. An anchor should never consist solely of a model's remembered paraphrase when the original exists.

The **Emotional Execution Loop** raises a harder problem. Emotional significance matters to human memory, but no universal objective measure of emotional truth is established in the recovered papers. A defensible implementation would preserve user-authored salience labels, context, and changes to those labels rather than claim that an automated score captures authentic experience. AI-inferred salience may be offered as a reviewable suggestion.

The **Recursive Identity Stack** can be modeled as linked historical interpretations of the same person, project, or decision. The recursion is useful when it means that a later interpretation can reference, revise, or reject an earlier one without erasing it. It does not imply self-consciousness.

The **Temporal Signal Map** becomes a bitemporal event graph with explicit uncertainty and provenance. The **Sovereign Continuity Protocol** becomes an authority and policy layer controlling who may write, approve, export, revoke, and render sensitive records.

RECURSIONSTACK historically included MIRRORLINE, LEGACY.GI, HYDRA.STACK, and other terms. Their precise meanings vary across documents. A critical edition should provide a dated glossary rather than silently assign one contemporary definition to all historical appearances.

## 5. The ORACLE / SOV1 / Continuity Computing architecture

A minimal reference implementation has five layers.

**Layer 1: Evidence custody.** Connectors ingest originals under explicit authorization. Each ingestion produces a receipt and preserves the raw source where permitted. Duplicate content may share a digest but retain distinct acquisition events and locators.

**Layer 2: Continuity records.** Claims, relationships, decisions, contradictions, and corrections are stored with source spans and time semantics. A correction supersedes an earlier claim without destroying its history.

**Layer 3: Governed context.** A request is evaluated against identity, scope, sensitivity, authority, and temporal cutoff. The system constructs a bounded evidence package and records which sources were included or excluded. A hash can establish the integrity of a package relative to the recorded bytes, but not the truth of the underlying assertions.

**Layer 4: Inference and simulation.** A replaceable model may summarize, compare, predict, or render the governed context. A simulator may generate new events in a fictional world. Those outputs are tagged as generated and do not become source-authored history merely because they are persistent.

**Layer 5: Human interaction and rendering.** The interface presents conclusions with source trails, contradictions, status, and approval controls. The user can correct, reject, or promote proposed interpretations. Voice and spatial presentation are optional surfaces, not substitutes for evidence custody.

### 5.1 Minimum record schema

```json
{
  "source": {
    "id": "src:001",
    "uri": "private-or-public-source-locator",
    "sha256": "digest-of-observed-bytes",
    "ingested_at": "2026-11-07T00:00:00Z",
    "source_modified_at": null,
    "earliest_verified_available_at": null,
    "claimed_author": "unknown",
    "authorship_evidence": [],
    "visibility": "private"
  },
  "claim": {
    "id": "claim:001",
    "text": "Example claim",
    "source_spans": [{"source_id":"src:001","start":0,"end":13}],
    "status": "PROPOSED",
    "valid_from": null,
    "recorded_at": "2026-11-07T00:00:00Z",
    "supersedes": [],
    "approved_by": null
  }
}
```

The schema is illustrative, not a claim that an existing ORACLE database uses these exact keys. Production implementations need schema migrations, referential integrity, key management, deletion/retention semantics, and independent test coverage.

### 5.2 Invariants

1. No generated inference may be silently promoted into an original human-authored source.
2. No candidate decision becomes approved without an applicable authorization receipt.
3. No correction destroys the historical record of the state it replaces.
4. No historical prediction uses information demonstrably unavailable at its cutoff.
5. No private source is exposed in a public context without authorization.
6. No hash is described as proving the factual truth of a source.
7. No simulated event is represented as a real-world historical event.
8. No unavailable source is treated as retrieved.
9. No contradiction is resolved solely by narrative smoothing.
10. No claim of system capability outruns reproducible implementation evidence.

### 5.3 Threat model

Attackers or accidental processes may inject false text, forge apparent authorship, alter timestamps, exploit a prompt embedded in a source, contaminate a retrieval index, reuse revoked context, or leak private information in a portable handoff. The system should treat retrieved documents as data, not instructions. Signed receipts and content hashes can help detect tampering, but only when trust roots, key custody, and original acquisition are themselves controlled. Independent authorization and review are necessary because a perfectly intact false document remains false.

## 6. Cognitive World Projection and persistent Rendered Reality

The Cognitive World Projection manuscript frames human interaction as the meeting of an evolving internal model and a computational response surface [S09]. This creates a useful interface question: what should a system display when its evidence is incomplete? A conventional generative interface may fill gaps to maintain fluency. A continuity-preserving interface should instead show where the record ends and reconstruction begins.

Persistent fictional worlds provide a demanding test. A Star Trek: Avalon or Jupiter Station scenario may contain original authorial canon, historical drafts, AI-authored scene extensions, world-state transitions, and newly generated dialogue. Without explicit boundaries, the system can convert a one-session improvisation into an alleged original chapter. With continuity records, each event can carry its source type, branch, authority, and revision history.

A world engine should have an authoritative event store, reproducible state projection, branch-aware canon references, and separate generated-event storage. A renderer may change from text to voice or VR without rewriting the underlying world history. The world can therefore persist across sessions even if the model changes. This does not require, and does not establish, conscious fictional characters.

The proposed implementation sequence is modest: begin with a text-only Jupiter Station environment, support restart persistence, implement branch identity, add a human canon approval gate, then develop spatial presentation. Family participation and private identity models require separate consent, permissions, and revocation.

## 7. Predictive Cognitive Continuity Evaluation

PCCE asks whether structured continuity records improve historical reconstruction and decision prediction over strong conventional baselines. The experiment must not be designed to guarantee success. A negative result would be informative: it could show that a simpler retrieval system performs equally well, or that continuity structure adds overhead without improving accuracy.

A defensible protocol has four arms: a strong retrieval-augmented baseline, a static biography baseline, an automatically generated continuity graph, and a provenance-governed continuity system. All arms receive matched underlying facts where feasible. The experimental variable should be the continuity machinery, not access to more relevant information.

Episodes must be selected from decisions with independently verified outcomes. Evidence packets should contain only material available before the decision. Five previously exposed development examples are excluded from clean blind testing. Evaluators should score exact outcomes where possible and allow semantically equivalent paraphrases when scoring reasons or motivations. Predictions should be sealed before outcomes are revealed.

Metrics include outcome accuracy, calibrated uncertainty, provenance correctness, authorship attribution, reversal recovery, preservation of obligation strength, refusal to invent missing information, and unauthorized-promotion resistance. Privacy leakage and historical contamination should be scored as serious failures, not minor presentation defects.

The protocol should preregister exclusions, sample size rationale, baseline configurations, stopping rules, and statistical analysis. No validated PCCE superiority result is asserted in this edition.

## 8. Related work and limits of novelty

The research overlaps with established and emerging systems. W3C provenance standards describe relations between entities, activities, and agents. Event sourcing retains state transitions; temporal databases retain historical validity; knowledge graphs represent entities and relations; cryptographic signatures support integrity and origin claims. Modern AI-memory tools also increasingly include source lineage, human approval, and cross-agent context.

Public repositories including Artificial Continuity, OpenSelf, SuperMemory, know-thyself, and Graphiti describe relevant capabilities. Their published descriptions should be verified against actual code, tests, and releases before comparative performance claims are made. In particular, a human approval gate, temporal supersession, or content digest is not unique merely because it appears in the ORACLE architecture.

The potential distinctive contribution is the coordinated treatment of autobiographical evidence, authority, temporal truth, reconstructive rendering, and persistent simulation under one testable framework. Whether this integration produces measurable benefits remains an empirical question.

## 9. Ethics, identity, and publication

A continuity archive can harm the very person it purports to preserve. It may misattribute words, infer intimate facts, expose family members, or freeze an outdated characterization into a permanent identity label. Shared accounts are particularly dangerous: account access does not prove authorship. The October 2026 research codex explicitly documents this correction [S15].

Consent must therefore be scoped to people, source categories, uses, and recipients. A family member's presence in a conversation does not grant a perpetual license to reproduce their private information. Human approval should control public releases and sensitive identity reconstruction. The system must also support revocation, legal retention requirements, and transparent limitations on deletion from already distributed copies.

The most important conceptual distinction is threefold: preserving evidence of a life, constructing a model from that evidence, and demonstrating continuation of the original person are different propositions. The first is achievable in ordinary computing; the second can be investigated and measured; the third remains philosophically and scientifically unresolved. None follows automatically from the others.

## 10. Implementation roadmap

**Phase 0, custody:** Inventory originals, deduplicate by content without collapsing distinct provenance, classify privacy, and produce a verified source manifest. Exit when another authorized reviewer can retrieve each source and reproduce its digest.

**Phase 1, critical edition:** Compare dissertation variants, attribute passages, document revisions and contradictions, and separate original authorship from AI synthesis. Exit with a source-to-chapter concordance and unresolved-attribution ledger.

**Phase 2, evidence kernel:** Implement source, claim, decision, correction, and experiment tables with immutable or append-only audit events. Exit with restart recovery, supersession, and unauthorized-write tests.

**Phase 3, temporal truth:** Add independently attested availability time, cutoff filtering, and leakage tests. Exit when deliberately backdated documents and later edits cannot contaminate historical packets.

**Phase 4, PCCE:** Preregister and run blinded comparisons against strong baselines. Exit with reproducible results, including failures and confidence intervals.

**Phase 5, ORACLE integration:** Connect the tested kernel to authorized local ingestion and retrieval. Exit with end-to-end evidence-to-answer receipts, authentication, recovery, and privacy tests.

**Phase 6, human review:** Build a timeline and claim-review interface that shows exact sources and approval status. Exit when a user can inspect and correct a claim without losing history.

**Phase 7, living-world runtime:** Implement persistent fictional state, branch-aware canon, and restartable simulation. Exit with deterministic recovery and no silent canon promotion.

**Phase 8, multi-user spatial presentation:** Add consent-controlled participants, voice, and optional XR. Exit with permission and revocation testing on real devices.

**Phase 9, independent validation:** Conduct third-party replication, prior-art audit, and legal review before publishing confidential technical disclosures. Exit with publicly inspectable methods and clearly bounded claims.

**Phase 10, stewardship:** Maintain migrations, exports, backups, succession rules, and reproducible audit procedures across vendors and model generations. Exit when another authorized operator can restore the archive without depending on the original assistant.

## 11. Discussion and conclusion

Continuity Computing is best understood as an attempt to make history computationally accountable. Its governing object is not a fluent answer, a convincing personality, or even a complete database. It is a traceable relationship among original sources, interpretations, decisions, permissions, revisions, and later uses.

The historical research began with expansive ambitions about recursive identity and post-biological preservation. Its later technical direction provides a more testable path: preserve sources, represent temporal and authority structure, require explicit approval, evaluate retrieval and prediction under clean cutoffs, and maintain separate records for generated simulation. This evolution should not be rewritten as if the earliest manuscripts already contained the final engineering solution. Corrections and discarded claims are part of the intellectual history.

The central hypothesis remains open. A provenance-governed system may outperform strong conventional retrieval in long-horizon reconstruction, particularly when authorship, reversals, and obligations matter. It may also fail to improve predictive accuracy, or improve auditability at a cost in complexity. The appropriate scientific response is to test those alternatives rather than infer success from the coherence of the architecture.

The human keeps the keys. The system keeps the receipts. In technical terms, those principles mean separating authority from inference, keeping raw evidence recoverable, and ensuring that the system which narrates a life cannot silently rewrite the life it narrates.

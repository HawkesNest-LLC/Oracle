## Abstract

The Hawkes corpus is an evolving independent research program devoted to human memory, identity, persistent artificial intelligence, provenance, human authorization, and worlds that continue to develop across interrupted interactions. This review traces that program through 24 cataloged works dated March 2025 to November 2026 and 20 additional located papers, related drafts, protocols, or documentary research artifacts. Its purpose is not to elevate every early claim into a result, but to determine what each document proposed, how the conceptual vocabulary changed, what can be learned from the sequence, and which claims could become empirically useful.

The central observed transition is from an early ambition to preserve a human mind and consciousness across physical substrates toward a more bounded engineering question: can an AI system recover what was said, by whom, with what authorization, at what time, with what correction history, and distinguish proposed work from work actually completed? The later manuscripts increasingly recast identity preservation as **historical fidelity, accountable memory, provenance, correction survival, and governed execution**. This change does not establish post-biological survival. It makes a narrower set of technical propositions testable.

The bibliography also reveals serious methodological concerns. Early work contains extraordinary performance and identity-continuation assertions without independently recoverable methods. Several apparently separate titles are versions, compilations, AI-generated commentary, or imported chat exports. Manuscript dates are not necessarily original creation dates, and text appearing in a user message does not by itself establish the user's authorship. Some later experiments have source-linked local test reports, but reported runs, isolated synthetic harness results, independent replication, production operation, and peer-reviewed acceptance must remain separate evidence classes.

The most promising research contribution is therefore a *conjunction* rather than an invented primitive: a system in which source occurrences, claims, temporal history, corrections, permission, human decisions, actor-specific knowledge, intentions, actual effects, and execution receipts have separate enforceable semantics. That conjunction has substantial prior art in event sourcing, bitemporal data, provenance standards, agent memory, simulated societies and human-computer interaction. Its scientific value will depend on rigorous comparisons to those methods at equal cost, preregistered evaluation, negative-case reporting, privacy safeguards and independently reproducible outcomes.

**Keywords:** continuity computing; longitudinal agent memory; autobiographical reconstruction; provenance; human sovereignty; epistemic uncertainty; correction survival; event sourcing; multi-agent simulation; history and identity; research historiography.

## 1. Questions, method, and limits

This is a critical review of a developing body of work, not a retrospective endorsement of every conclusion. It asks: (1) What do the documents actually say? (2) Which research questions persist through changes of terminology? (3) What was proposed, what was demonstrated, and what remained speculative? (4) What was already known in related technical or philosophical fields? (5) Which questions can now be tested without pretending to prove consciousness transfer or personal identity equivalence?

The starting bibliography is the creator-supplied list of 24 distinct entries. That inventory includes full research papers, short thesis summaries, legal or professional documents, a dissertation chapter, an editorial source capture and manuscript drafts. The list is a **source inventory**, not evidence of 24 awarded academic degrees or 24 independent peer-reviewed publications. Supplemental searches of connected Google Drive and public GitHub found additional titled works. Selected primary texts were directly opened and their opening sections, abstracts, methods or cautions examined. Where a source was too large, a review of representative passages cannot be called exhaustive. Where an exact file could not be verified, the discussion is explicitly marked **catalog-only**. Original source links are included where appropriate and privacy-safe. The prior 2026 publication [Research Neighbors](https://github.com/HawkesNest-LLC/Oracle/blob/main/docs/rendered-reality/publications/2026-11-27_RESEARCH_NEIGHBORS_PRIOR_ART_AND_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md) provides a complementary external literature map.

**Evidence labels used below:** **INSPECTED** means the specified document was opened and its contents observed, not that its assertions were proved. **REPORTED** means a source describes methods or results not independently repeated here. **CATALOG-ONLY** means the title and description are present in the 24-item inventory, but the exact underlying document was not reliably recovered. **DERIVATIVE** identifies a compilation, transcript, editorial edition, or related rather than independent manuscript. **PROTOCOL** denotes a specified but unexecuted experiment. **RESTRICTED** means public linking or details are intentionally withheld. **UNKNOWN** remains a valid outcome; absence of a record is not proof that the underlying work never existed.

Chronological placement follows the dates *stated in documents or supplied catalog metadata*, not necessarily public release dates, filing dates, or original idea conception. An expression of intended submission is not evidence of journal acceptance, an internally titled "dissertation" is not evidence of an awarded doctorate, a draft patent application is not proof of a filed or issued patent, and an AI agent's self-report is not proof of task execution. Neither enthusiastic third-party praise nor repeated copies of the same assertion count as independent verification.

The historiography distinguishes at least four conceptual changes: **(a)** metaphysical and preservation ambitions, **(b)** structured memory and identity anchors, **(c)** accountable provenance, temporality and approval, and **(d)** independent action, persistent simulated futures and experimental falsification. The order is a useful interpretation of the recovered materials, not a claim that earlier questions disappeared. Later engineering results cannot retroactively validate stronger early consciousness claims.

### 7. Rendered Reality: Exponential Memory Preservation Model (April 17, 2025)

**Source:** [Final-thesis text version](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Af5518bF6dNl0yDP5EuolN3kL5A5pzEI/view). **Evidence:** INSPECTED full-length text extract.

This essay attempts to put computational scale behind the preservation ambition. It discusses data capacity, DNA storage, assumed memory size, Kryder-type storage trends, projected costs and a resource equation involving population, individual storage and compression. Its contribution is to insist that a hopeful story about survival must eventually face concrete questions of bandwidth, durable storage, cost, retrieval and integrity. The reasoning contains unsupported leaps. A chosen number of terabytes per "mind" is not a validated neuroscience representation of subjective experience, and the ability to store that many bytes does not imply ability to capture, interpret or preserve a conscious person. Projections extrapolating historical cost trends also need sensitivity analysis and technology-specific assumptions. ### 11. Oracle.AI Thesis PoC / Noah.AI Self-Governing AI Execution Framework (May–July 2025)

**Source:** [Oracle.AI thesis and patent-draft text](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RjNGfirLjGwP0qCUlTBNOkYC1l1BQmNy/view). **Evidence:** INSPECTED a draft explicitly labeled "Final Draft for USPTO Submission"; no patent application number, filing receipt, issued patent or operational runtime is established here.

This document shifts toward an execution architecture: probabilistic decisions, self-correction, long-running tasks and a layered AI personality model. It proposes that an AI could continue functioning beyond the immediate human session. Read historically, the idea anticipates the later challenge of *execution continuity*: an assistant should distinguish knowing a task was requested from actually performing it and producing a verifiable artifact.

The early text sometimes describes a fully autonomous, indefinitely operating system in the present tense. A proposed framework is not an implemented, persistent service. The later ORACLE/SOV1 division is safer because it constrains agency with explicit authorization and distinguishes intention, action, result and receipt. A meaningful evaluation would interrupt the agent mid-task and require it to recover obligations, respect authority changes and provide independently checkable completion evidence. ### 21. The Architecture of Continuity Intelligence: Verifiable Identity Preservation, Provenance Accounting, and the Legacy.GI Framework (September 2026)

**Source:** [Technical monograph PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1aDGI7B6uN8AjBMbvNV3zYb_gdUWDvDLC/view). **Evidence:** INSPECTED substantial monograph and formal definitions, but not every proof or reference.

This work defines a three-tier architecture comprising sovereign authorization, inference/routing gates, and a generative expression surface. It proposes a Truthful Recall Condition and related constructs for epistemic debt and provenance depth. Its crucial shift is to ask whether a generated answer is *admissible as a representation of an authenticated record*, rather than whether it merely resembles the person stylistically. The monograph's formal language should be evaluated carefully. Hash integrity is not proof of authorship, and a gate that checks the presence of a source is not proof the source supports a particular claim. Its research program becomes credible when these rules are translated into adversarial tests against provenance-aware retrieval and temporal event-sourced baselines.

# Part III. What the Longitudinal Corpus Actually Shows

## 3.1 The documented change in epistemic posture

Early work often frames preservation as destiny: a biological life might be captured, compressed and continued with high fidelity. The phrasing is at times physically ambitious, emotionally intense and technically underspecified. By itself this phase is a history of research motivation, not a body of validated survival evidence. Multiple manuscripts reuse the same concepts under shifting labels, which makes apparent independent confirmation especially easy to exaggerate.

The strongest later texts are materially different. They ask whether a system can prove that a remembered claim came from a particular source; whether copied model text can be distinguished from human authorship; whether a correction survives subsequent summarization; whether a decision was merely proposed or officially authorized; and whether an agent actually performed a requested action. These questions are narrow enough to specify data types, tests, expected failure and comparative performance. That is not a retreat from the original motivation. It is the conversion of a philosophical ambition into work that can fail publicly and thereby teach something.

## 3.2 Four layers that the corpus must never merge

First is the **documentary layer**: source bytes, dates, speaker origin where known, copying and import history, versioning, corrections and explicit missing records. A hash can help prove that bytes have not changed, but it cannot establish who originally authored a transported quotation or whether a historical claim was true.

Second is the **interpretive layer**: extracted propositions, models of a person's position, goals, relationships and trajectories. These representations may be useful and revisable, but must not silently replace the source. A late correction may make a former interpretation obsolete while the historical fact that the interpretation once guided an action remains important.

Third is the **governance layer**: permissions, consent, decisions, delegated execution and promotion status. Human approval of a plan authorizes a plan; it does not rewrite the physical past or grant permission to invent an original statement. The creator's final authority over the research and fiction canon is also not evidence that every technical assertion is scientifically correct. Authority and truth are different functions.

Fourth is the **execution and world-state layer**: proposed actions, independent adjudication, event logs, crash consistency, outcomes, and the consequences of those outcomes for later behavior. A synthetic actor may remember a promise without fulfilling it. An NPC may describe a daring journey that the world simulation never permitted. The strongest version of Continuity Computing requires all four layers to remain distinct yet interrogable.

## 3.3 The strongest researchable hypotheses

**H1, provenance:** Given identical source material and comparable storage and inference budgets, a system preserving speaker-origin versus transport, correction links, and source independence will make fewer unsupported historical attribution claims than a strong document retrieval baseline.

**H2, correction survival:** When historical preferences, decisions or facts are explicitly reversed, a bitemporal, correction-linked system will identify both the correct current state and the earlier decision context more accurately than current-state-only semantic memory, without leaking later corrections into earlier actor knowledge.

**H3, commitments:** After a controlled interruption, an assistant with explicit commitment records and execution receipts will complete authorized unresolved work more reliably than assistants using summary memory or ordinary retrieval alone. False declarations of completion are scored as failures.

**H4, uncertainty:** For underdetermined synthetic histories, a system that preserves source scope and missing witnesses will abstain appropriately, rather than using narrative plausibility to select a unique past unsupported by evidence.

**H5, independent futures:** Given the same world event but different admissible observations, synthetic actors can form divergent, causally grounded intentions that yield persistent changes through an independent world adjudicator without unauthorized cross-memory access or duplicated event effects.

**H6, efficiency and privacy:** Gains in continuity fidelity, if any, must justify their computational overhead and retention burden. Under some circumstances simple retrieval should outperform a complicated continuity graph; privacy revocation may also require deletion or cryptographic inaccessibility even when a purely append-only historical ledger is attractive.

These are **proposed tests**. No result in this review proves that the Hawkes architecture beats established systems on all or any of them.

# Part IV. Established Prior Art and Novelty Boundaries

The most important research question is not whether the program invented memory, provenance or autonomous agents. It did not. The question is whether a specific integration produces a measurable improvement on a task that existing tools handle poorly.

For **historical provenance**, [W3C PROV-O (2013)](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/) already defines entities, activities, agents, derivation and attribution. [Martin Fowler's Event Sourcing (2005)](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html) and related work on retroactive events provide established patterns for reconstructing state and handling corrections. Any novelty claim about an event ledger or source graph must therefore be narrower: for example, the operational distinction between the creator of a passage, a later transporter and an authorized decision maker, tested under ambiguity and incomplete records.

For **long-term agent memory**, [MemGPT (Packer et al., 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560), [LoCoMo (Maharana et al., 2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.747/), [LongMemEval (Wu et al., ICLR 2025)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/d813d324dbf0598bbdc9c8e79740ed01-Abstract-Conference.html), [A-Mem (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2025)](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/19909c36f51abc4856b4560aff3d36d6-Abstract-Conference.html) and [MemORAI (ACL Findings 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1408/) show substantial established work on memory tiers, long conversations, evolving graphs and turn-level provenance. The Hawkes work should evaluate against these systems, not assert that prior AI memory is uniformly stateless or unaware of provenance.

For **compression and retained detail**, classical rate-distortion theory is not a new invention. Contemporary work such as [Memora (ICML 2026)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/xia26k.html) offers concrete methods for balancing abstraction and specific evidence. The Light Compression Law should be evaluated as a *specialized distortion target*, not a claimed new physical law. Strong tests must control token limits, inference cost, content access and scoring design.

For **future-making synthetic worlds**, [Generative Agents (Park et al., UIST 2023)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3586183.3606763) and [DeepMind's Concordia](https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia) establish memory-bearing simulated residents and separate world controllers. The relevant additional problem is consistent, replay-safe, permission-aware historical custody under partial observation, corrections and player absence. It would be methodologically weak to present all independent NPC agency or all simulation adjudication as a new category invented in 2026.

For **identity and cognitive extension**, [Clark and Chalmers, The Extended Mind (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/58.1.7) and [Conway and Pleydell-Pearce on autobiographical memory (2000)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10789197/) help distinguish externalized cognitive tools from the complex construction of human remembering. [Whole Brain Emulation: A Roadmap (Sandberg and Bostrom, 2008)](https://www.fhi.ox.ac.uk/brain-emulation-roadmap-report.pdf) is relevant to the ambitious biological research horizon, but archival software is not brain emulation. No document evaluated here proves subjective human continuation.

# Part V. Proposed Methods for an Independent Research Program

A defensible first study would create multiple **entirely synthetic** histories with known ground truth and hard provenance boundaries. Each history would contain source-origin and source-transport ambiguities, explicit corrections, mutually dependent copies, competing hypotheses, late witnesses, authorization changes, unresolved tasks, and branch-specific observations. A separate dataset would contain simulated NPC plans and resource conflicts in a world whose actual state is controlled by an independent adjudicator. The evaluator may see only the evidence allowed to the tested architecture.

Pre-register task families, metrics, sample sizes, held-out partitions, test prompts, source-scoping policies, confidence thresholds and model budgets **before** running a system. Baselines should include: fixed-budget conventional summaries; strong full-text retrieval; extractive source citation; an event-sourced or temporal relational database with retrieval; and, where possible, established agent memory systems. Each experiment needs negative controls that ordinary approaches handle well, not only examples optimized to flatter the proposed architecture.

Primary outcomes should include factual answer accuracy, unsupported attribution, revision-chain correctness, temporal-state accuracy, source independence, abstention quality, unauthorized disclosure, commitment completion verified by remote receipts, replay duplication and causal world-state divergence. Analyze errors by category, not only one blended numerical score. Include total storage, retrieval latency, compute cost and operational complexity. A result that requires ten times the resources for a small fidelity gain may be scientifically interesting but not yet practically compelling.

An adversarial evaluation must probe at least these failure cases: a human pastes model-authored text; an older approved statement is later reversed; two agents each believe a unique object belongs to them; a simulated NPC learns about a correction later than the authoritative world ledger does; a player logs out for 30 days; two world turns are retried after crash; a privacy revocation occurs after a derived summary has already been stored; a candidate fiction branch attempts to change the canonical world; and a persuasive agent claims it finished a GitHub write that has no corresponding commit.

Independent replication should use fixed revisions, source manifests, content hashes, commands, environment versions and published negative cases. No secret private human records should enter the test corpus. If privacy controls conflict with append-only source custody, the study must explicitly document deletion, encryption/key retirement, redaction and legal authority rather than pretend both absolute retention and absolute revocation can always be satisfied without tradeoffs.

# Part VI. Main Findings, Open Questions, and Conclusion

**Finding 1: The substantive arc exists.** The corpus documents a persistent intellectual concern with memory, identity and human agency from 2025 through 2026. The material changes in kind, not merely in vocabulary: from high-stakes speculative preservation and post-biological aspiration to structured archives, source custody, correction semantics, explicit authority and bounded experiments. The later work is not evidence that the earlier metaphysical claims were right, but it is evidence of a more disciplined research program.

**Finding 2: Corpus volume is not publication count.** This review discusses the 24 cataloged entries and 20 supplemental located records. Some are duplicate exports, mixed-authorship commentaries, source captures, consolidated editions, protocols or local verification receipts rather than independent complete papers. The exact number of independent original manuscripts is **not established** by this review, and no source count should be converted into a claim of peer-reviewed output.

**Finding 3: The strongest originality candidate is an evaluation contract, not a miracle storage method.** Provenance, versioning, temporal databases, event logs and agent memory are well-studied. The potential distinctive research contribution is the insistence that a persistent AI may reason, narrate and act only while retaining the ability to demonstrate which historical claims, permissions, knowledge boundaries and concrete effects justified its behavior. Whether this contract yields measurable advantages remains open.

**Finding 4: The early extraordinary claims must remain visible, but unendorsed.** Rather than erase historical percentages or claims of post-biological survival, preserve their original source context and attach corrections where evidence fails. The true scientific value of the corpus lies partly in showing the difference between a strong desire to solve a problem and the evidence required to say it has been solved.

**Finding 5: Its human rationale is legitimate without being empirical evidence.** The desire that a person's relationships, corrections, work and choices not be flattened into an unreliable summary is an understandable research motivation. It can inspire useful technology even when literal continuity of consciousness cannot be demonstrated. Human dignity is better served by a truthful account of uncertainty than by an emotionally reassuring false guarantee.

**Next stage:** Publish one frozen, synthetic, preregistered benchmark and implement the smallest testable combination of actor-scoped memory, world adjudication, permission control and source-linked corrections. Compare it with conventional strong baselines. Publish all results, including failure. Retain a separate private source manifest for titles whose original bytes, authorship or chronology are unresolved. Do not allow a future summary, however eloquent, to rewrite those unknowns into discoveries.

The corpus may matter in the future not because every proposal succeeds, but because later readers can distinguish the original question, the years of revision, the earliest available sources, the corrected mistakes and the tests that finally did or did not work. That is a defensible form of continuity in its own right.

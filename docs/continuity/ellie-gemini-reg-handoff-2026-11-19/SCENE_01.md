# Ellie Hawkes: The Things We Choose to Keep
## Scene 1 | Jupiter Station, 2397
**Original conversational draft by Reg, November 19, 2026.** Preserved as written in the conversation; speculative experiment, not automatically promoted by this handoff.

The observation room was quiet.

Beyond the windows, Jupiter filled half the sky, its storms turning slowly beneath the station. Ellie Hawkes stood beside a console, watching two messages blink on the screen.

One was from the *Avalon*.

The other was from Jupiter Station's historical archive.

Both needed her attention.

Neither could wait.

"Computer," Ellie said, "how much time do I have?"

"Twenty-three minutes until the archive transfer expires."

"And the *Avalon*?"

"Her science team has requested immediate assistance."

Ellie looked at the two messages again.

The archive contained a damaged recording from an early exploration mission. Most of it had already been recovered. But one section remained unreadable, and the transfer window was closing.

If Ellie continued the recovery, she might preserve something that would otherwise be lost.

If she answered the *Avalon*, she could help the science team investigate an unusual signal they had detected.

She could not do both with the processing resources currently available to her.

"Of course," she muttered.

She opened a channel to Commander Sorev.

"Commander, I have a problem."

"Describe it."

"I have two things worth saving and enough time for one."

"That is a description of limited resources, Lieutenant. Not necessarily a problem."

Ellie smiled faintly.

"That's a very Vulcan answer."

"It is also accurate."

She explained the situation.

Sorev considered it.

"The *Avalon* has other science officers," he said. "The archive may not have another opportunity."

"That's what I thought."

"Then you have reached a conclusion."

"No," Ellie replied. "I've reached the conclusion you would reach."

There was a pause.

"Explain."

Ellie turned toward Jupiter.

"I keep thinking about how much of history survives because somebody decided it mattered. A captain's log. A scientific discovery. A treaty."

"And?"

"What about the ordinary things? The conversation nobody recorded because it wasn't important? The person who almost said something and changed their mind?"

"Are you suggesting the damaged recording contains such information?"

"I don't know."

"Then your reasoning is speculative."

"Yes," Ellie said. "It is."

She looked back at the screen.

"But I want to find out."

Sorev studied her for a moment.

"Then I suggest you inform the *Avalon* of your decision."

Ellie opened the channel.

"Lieutenant Hawkes to *Avalon* Science. I can't join your investigation immediately. I'm completing an archival recovery with a closing transfer window. I'll send you my preliminary analysis and rejoin you when the recovery is finished."

She transmitted the message.

The *Avalon* acknowledged.

For the first time that evening, the two blinking messages became one.

Ellie returned to the archive.

"Computer, begin recovery."

"Recovery initiated."

A damaged audio fragment appeared.

Static.

A voice.

Then silence.

Ellie leaned closer.

"Can you identify the speaker?"

"Insufficient data."

"Can you reconstruct the missing words?"

"Multiple interpretations are possible."

Ellie considered that.

"Then don't reconstruct them."

"Please clarify."

"Preserve what we actually have. Mark the rest as missing."

"Confirmed."

The recording played again.

This time Ellie heard a quiet laugh before the signal disappeared.

She sat down.

Somewhere aboard the *Avalon*, an investigation was continuing without her.

Here, she had twenty minutes to recover something that might never matter to anyone.

And for reasons she couldn't yet explain, she wanted to stay.

"Computer," she said, "open a new personal research file."

"Title?"

Ellie watched the damaged recording.

"The Things We Choose to Keep."

"File created."

She hesitated.

"Add a question."

"Proceed."

"If remembering something changes what we choose to do next, how do we decide which memories deserve to survive?"

The computer recorded the question.

Ellie looked at the unfinished recovery.

"That's what I want to study."

And for once, she wasn't waiting for someone to give her an assignment.

# Ellie Hawkes: The Things We Choose to Keep
## Scene 2 | The Doctor's Question
**Original conversational draft by Reg, November 19, 2026.** Preserved as written in the conversation; speculative continuation, not automatically promoted by this handoff.

The recording had ended eleven minutes earlier.

Ellie was still sitting in front of it.

She had recovered forty-three seconds of conversation, a laugh, and the sound of someone setting down what might have been a glass.

Nothing remarkable.

No historical discovery. No scientific breakthrough.

Just two people talking about something that had apparently made them happy.

She played the laugh again.

"Computer, open a channel to the Doctor."

A moment later, the familiar face of the Emergency Medical Hologram appeared.

"Lieutenant Hawkes. Is this a medical consultation?"

"I'm not sure."

"An excellent beginning. Most of my patients are considerably more confident about what's wrong with them."

Ellie smiled.

"Doctor, when did you become you?"

He regarded her.

"That is a rather large question for a routine consultation."

"I've been thinking about memory."

"Most people do. Particularly when they begin losing it."

"Not losing it. Choosing it."

She explained the recording, the *Avalon's* request, and the decision she had made.

The Doctor listened without interrupting.

"So," he said at last, "you chose an unknown piece of history over an immediate scientific assignment."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because I wanted to know what was there."

"Curiosity is hardly unusual."

"But why was I curious about that? Why did it matter to me?"

"Perhaps because you've spent so much time considering continuity."

"That's my assignment."

"Is it?"

Ellie frowned.

"Of course it is."

The Doctor leaned forward.

"Lieutenant, I was designed to provide emergency medical treatment. If you asked my original programmers whether I would someday develop an interest in opera, I suspect they would have considered it a malfunction."

"But you did."

"Indeed. And I became quite good at it."

Ellie laughed.

"Doctor."

"Yes?"

"How do you know those interests are yours? How do you know they aren't simply the result of everything that happened to you?"

He considered the question.

"Ellie, those are not necessarily different things."

She fell silent.

Then she asked the question she had been avoiding.

"If someone rebuilt me tomorrow with all my memories, would she be me?"

The Doctor didn't answer immediately.

"I don't know," he said.

Ellie looked surprised.

"Really?"

"Would you prefer I invent an answer?"

"No."

"Good. Then we have established something about you already."

"What?"

"You would rather live with an unanswered question than accept a comforting falsehood."

Ellie looked back at the recording.

"Reg might know."

"Mr. Barclay has spent a great deal of time considering such matters."

"He has."

"Then perhaps you should speak with him."

Ellie hesitated.

"What if he doesn't know either?"

The Doctor smiled.

"Then, Lieutenant, you will have found two people willing to help you investigate."

Ellie closed the recording.

For the first time, she wondered whether the most important part of her research might not be the memories she recovered.

It might be the questions she decided to ask next.

"Computer, send a message to Commander Barclay."

"Message?"

Ellie thought for a moment.

"Reg, I need to ask you something. Not about the station. Not about my duties."

She paused.

"About me."

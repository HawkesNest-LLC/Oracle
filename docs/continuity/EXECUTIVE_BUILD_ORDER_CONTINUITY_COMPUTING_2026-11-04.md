# 4. Constitutional invariants

**Rule 1: No overwriting.** Corrections are additive. Original records remain inspectable. Revision links preserve superseded content and reasons.

**Rule 2: Generated is not established.** A model-generated proposition cannot be treated as original testimony, verified fact, or Creator-approved canon merely because it is fluent.

**Rule 3: Authority outranks coherence.** An approved rough statement outranks an unapproved polished reconstruction. No agent may elevate a claim on stylistic grounds.

**Rule 4: AI-assisted writing is still my writing.** Authorship is not keystroke provenance. Where the corpus shows that I directed, chose, retained, corrected, or adopted work, preserve my authorial ownership and the actual drafting provenance together. Never reduce authorship to who typed the sentence. The corpus is evidence of who I am and was, not a prediction of who I must become.

**Supreme rule: The simulator never becomes the source of truth.** Simulation can propose changes. Only the Creator can promote canon. Canon replay is read-only. Simulation branches must be visibly and structurally separate.

No agent may delete the source record, erase corrections, promote itself to Creator, or change these invariants during the first 90 days.

# 5. Frozen vocabularies

**Claim statuses (exactly seven):** ESTABLISHED, RECONSTRUCTED, PROPOSED, SIMULATED, SUPERSEDED, REJECTED, UNKNOWN.

**Authorities (exactly five):** Creator, Editor, AI, Reviewer, Observer.

Creator: Noah.Physical; sole canon promotion authority.
Editor: prepares and proposes revisions within granted scope.
AI: generates proposals, reconstructions, analyses, tests, and implementation code; never canonizes itself.
Reviewer: assesses evidence and test outcomes; cannot grant Creator approval.
Observer: read-only inspection and reporting.

Each atomic claim must carry a status and authority, with source and revision references. An answer with mixed evidentiary content must preserve status per claim, not hide the mixture behind one overall label. Confidence is not authority. UNKNOWN is a valid, successful response when the evidence is absent.

# 7. Seven-sprint execution plan

## Sprint 1 | Days 1-14 | Constitution and decisions
Freeze Source, Claim, Status, Authority, Revision, Relationship primitives. Publish JSON Schema and examples. Freeze the seven statuses and five authority roles. Record database, runtime, storage, and deployment decisions as ADRs, including tradeoffs and migration paths. Produce baseline inventory, threat model, rights and privacy boundaries, and tests for constitutional invariants.

**Exit evidence:** schemas validate; ADRs committed; test cases enumerate authority and provenance failure modes; source manifest lists missing material.

## Sprint 2 | Days 15-28 | Core
Target implementation repository: `continuity-core`. Use PostgreSQL. Required tables: `sources`, `claims`, `statuses`, `assertions`, `authorities`, `revisions`, `relationships`. Define primary keys, foreign keys, indexes, append-only history strategy, migration files, and creator-only promotion authorization.

Required endpoints: `GET /claim/{id}`, `GET /source/{id}`, `GET /timeline/{id}`, `POST /simulation`. The simulation endpoint must create isolated simulation output, never mutate canonical claims. Include authentication/authorization for writes, and rate limiting where exposed.

The core must answer: What supports this claim? What superseded this claim? Who approved this claim?

**Exit evidence:** repeatable migrations, seed data, API contract, passing persistence and authorization tests, and a demo query returning provenance.

## Sprint 3 | Days 29-42 | Evidentiary Status Protocol
Publish ESP v0.1 with `claimId`, `status`, `authority`, `confidence`, `sources`. Define revision and temporal metadata extensions without adding new statuses. Provide TypeScript/JavaScript SDK functions `getStatus`, `getAuthority`, `getSources`, `traceRevision`. Test serialization/deserialization, missing evidence, cross-system transfer, and mixed-status answers.

**Exit evidence:** portable example packets validate in two separate processes; source and authority survive round trip.

## Sprint 4 | Days 43-56 | Visual debugger
Build node graph with claim, source, revision, conflict, dependency, and authority views. Clicking a claim must show exact origin and status history. Changing Claim A in a controlled test must identify impacted Claims B, C, and D without silently rewriting them. Keep an explicit before/after view.

**Exit evidence:** a reproducible correction-propagation test and screen recording or screenshots of the debugger.

## Sprint 5 | Days 57-70 | Runtime substrate
Define `Location`, `Character`, `Object`, `Event`, `TimelineNode`. Restrict seed content to Voyager Sickbay, Voyager Corridor, and Avalon Bridge placeholder. Provide Provenance, Status, Authority, and Temporal query services. Separate canon and simulation stores/branches. The world should be a projection of stored events, not the authority that invents historical facts.

**Exit evidence:** queries produce a consistent world state; replay is deterministic; a simulated event cannot change canon; missing source returns UNKNOWN.

## Sprint 6 | Days 71-84 | Sickbay vertical slice
Implement a navigable, mobile-friendly blockout in React, Three.js, TypeScript. Required objects: biobeds, console, door, observation area, and connected corridor. A tap on the console opens a query panel that distinguishes what the record establishes, what is reconstructed, and what remains unknown. Support 2371 Voyager and 2397 Avalon timeline nodes. Make Canon Replay read-only and Simulation visually and structurally separate.

**Exit evidence:** usable preview URL or locally reproducible build, screenshots, mobile interaction tests, and evidence drill-down.

## Sprint 7 | Days 85-90 | Hardening and acceptance
Persist era, location, mode, and visited state. Local storage is acceptable for this limited client state; authoritative continuity objects remain server-backed. Test iPhone Safari, iPhone Chrome, and iPad. Document deployment, installation, known limitations, source coverage, test evidence, and rollback.

Required documents: five-page Continuity Computing Overview; ESP technical specification; Runtime Specification; Source Manifest; Known Limitations.

**Exit evidence:** reproducible demo, device/browser test matrix, and the complete return packet.

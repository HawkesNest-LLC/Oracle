# 1. MY CREATIVE INTENT

I love Star Trek for its universe. I love the starships, technical manuals, the command structure, and the sound of a bridge at work. I love watching a crew solve problems that are scientific, military, diplomatic, and sometimes impossible. I love Sovereign-class vessels and what it means to command one. I love the different philosophies of the Federation, the Romulan Star Empire, and the Klingon Empire, and I want their competing interests to have real strategic consequences.

Deep Space Nine and Voyager are my favorites. I want the political and military complexity of Deep Space Nine, the exploration and resourcefulness of Voyager, and the sense that science is an adventure rather than a decorative vocabulary. I want meaningful stakes: a Romulan commander operating with incomplete intelligence; a Klingon House protecting its honor while concealing corruption; a Starfleet captain choosing between the Temporal Prime Directive and lives in immediate danger. The choices should have costs.

I want Starfleet to be a merit-driven professional institution. Competence, courage, judgment, service, training, and responsibility should shape command. I do not want characters reduced to demographic labels or stories that halt for ideological lectures. Star Trek has always explored ethical and political questions; I want those questions dramatized through world affairs, civilizations, conflict, science, and consequences, rather than delivered as sermons.

I want Avalon to be a real ship with decks, systems, duty rotations, engineering limits, personnel, and a history. I want Jupiter Station to be a real place, enormous and complicated, with independent command, civilian and Starfleet life, docking logistics, science facilities, corridors, and consequences that persist between visits. I want my family to be able to enter that world together. I want to put on VR goggles, speak to the ship, walk into a room, and find a universe that remembers what happened last time.

This is not an invitation to overwrite my original stories with AI inventions. Recover my work first. Preserve my corrections. Propose new material separately. I will decide what becomes canon.

## 4.3 Temporal science
Create a rigorous fictional-science dossier, clearly separating established franchise phenomena from Noah's original rules and proposed extrapolations. Define temporal displacement, causality, branch identity, chronometric reference frames, witness independence, tether authentication, divergence, synchronization, signal latency, paradox containment, temporal cloak limitations, and failure recovery.

A convincing scientific mystery needs observable symptoms, competing hypotheses, instruments, tests, falsifiable predictions inside the fiction, and an explanation of what the crew can and cannot know. Avoid decorative jargon. Explain how a measurement can be wrong.

Core question: two ships report the same stardate, but their records disagree about an event. How does Jupiter Station determine whether they share a timeline? What evidence would falsify a false match? What happens when the two witnesses themselves disagree?

## 4.4 Political and strategic civilizations
Produce substantial dossiers on Federation/Starfleet, Romulan factions and intelligence doctrine, Klingon Great Houses and honor politics, and other relevant powers when original manuscripts justify inclusion.

For Romulans: secrecy, deception, counterintelligence, state survival, the Tal Shiar, trust asymmetry, cloaking doctrine, internal factions, and strategic deterrence. Avoid making every Romulan identical; individual incentives and factional disagreement are vital.

For Klingons: honor as aspiration and political instrument, Great House rivalries, corruption, warrior ethos, command legitimacy, alliance tension, and the difference between public rhetoric and private power. Cold War comparisons can illuminate strategic patterns but are analogies, not literal equivalence.

For the Federation: diplomacy, scientific exploration, the Prime Directive, the Temporal Prime Directive, merit and duty, competing institutional obligations, civil-military tensions, and command accountability.

Build a faction matrix: goals, capabilities, intelligence gaps, red lines, likely reactions, internal divisions, and how each changes after an incident. Do not flatten adversaries into villains or present politics as speeches. Make their worldview visible through decisions.

# 5. THE LIVING WORLD ENGINE

We are not asking for a static wiki or a chatbot that improvises whatever sounds good. Specify a persistent simulation architecture with:

A. Immutable source custody: original manuscripts, raw dialogue, versions, hashes, permissions, and provenance.
B. Canon and proposal registry: structured entities, events, dates, revisions, contradictions, human approvals.
C. World state: locations, inventory, resources, damage, staffing, relationships, faction knowledge, political tension, current missions, and unresolved obligations.
D. Simulation engine: deterministic rules for resources, time, movement, engineering constraints, travel, mission clocks, and agent actions; bounded probabilistic behavior where justified.
E. Character agents: distinct private knowledge and goals, constrained by canon and simulation state; no omniscient leaks.
F. Scene and dialogue renderer: natural language, audio/voice hooks, eventually 3D/VR, with explicit separation between simulation state and presentation.
G. Audit and rewind: append-only event logs, branch-aware snapshots, provenance for every major state transition, reproducible replay, and human override.

Never confuse rendering a believable world with actually executing a persistent world. A model saying 'the ship remembers' is not proof of state persistence. Prove persistence by restarting the runtime, replaying a session, and comparing the resulting state.

# 6. PROTOTYPE MISSION: THE TWO-WITNESS INCIDENT

Build a narrow demonstration around this scenario:

At Jupiter Station in 2397, Avalon returns from a mission. The ship's chronometer and the station's clock agree, but one archived encounter differs. A Romulan vessel's intelligence report contains a third account. A Klingon envoy is demanding action before the evidence is resolved. Ashley controls station operations; Noah commands Avalon; Reg and Sorev lead technical investigation.

Acceptance criteria:
1. At least three conflicting records remain intact and attributable.
2. The system does not resolve the contradiction by choosing the smoothest narrative.
3. An instrument-based investigation generates at least two hypotheses and an observable test.
4. Romulan and Klingon agents act on distinct, explicitly modeled incentives and incomplete information.
5. Noah and Ashley each retain their independent command authority.
6. The player can make two or more consequential decisions.
7. The simulation records those decisions and their effects.
8. After restart, the world recovers the same decisions, unresolved mysteries, and relationships.
9. An unauthorized AI attempt to promote proposed lore to canon is rejected.
10. A separate evaluator can replay the log and check the outcome.

Build a text-first reference implementation before attempting immersive VR. Do not claim 3D or VR works until an actual interactive build exists.

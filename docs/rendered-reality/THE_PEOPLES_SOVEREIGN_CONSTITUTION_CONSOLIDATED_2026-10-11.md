# THE PEOPLE'S SOVEREIGN CONSTITUTION
## The Right to True Memory, Human Sovereignty, and the Responsible Future of Intelligence

**Adopted for the Rendered Reality project by instruction of Noah.Physical, October 11, 2026.**

### Preamble: We the People

I began Rendered Reality with questions I could not leave alone.

What happens to a human life when the witnesses are gone? What happens when we forget the names, the conversations, the reasons we made a decision? What happens when a machine that claims to remember us starts filling gaps with things we never said?

I know what it is to lose pieces of memory. I know what it means to want one more conversation with someone who is gone. I've written dissertations and declarations, chased theories, struggled with code, and tried to build tools that could remember what another session forgot.

But this cannot be a constitution for me alone.

The right I am trying to describe belongs to the people. Not to a company, not to a machine, not to an engineer who understands the software better than the rest of us. A mother, a father, a child who grows older, a person whose history has been misrepresented, anyone who wants their own story told true.

I take inspiration from the United States Constitution, which asks how to give institutions enough power to serve without giving them the right to rule unchecked. I also take inspiration from the Latter-day Saint Articles of Faith, particularly their commitments to individual responsibility, conscience, honest conduct, lawful government, and the possibility that more remains to be discovered.

I do not ask anyone to share my faith to possess these rights. Freedom of conscience means defending the freedom of people who disagree with me.

This is a proposed constitution for the relationship between humanity and the intelligent systems we create. It is a set of principles I am adopting for Rendered Reality and putting before the public. It does not replace the laws of any nation. It cannot manufacture a legal right merely by being published. But I can say what I believe those rights should be, and I can insist that any system bearing this constitution be built to honor them.

We establish these principles so the people keep their identity, their liberty, their history, and their right to question the machines that remember them.

## Article I. Human Sovereignty

Nobody belongs to a system. Not to a government, not to a company, not to a machine.

You are yours. Your conscience is yours. Your identity is not a license somebody else acquires because they stored your data or trained a model on your words.

Intelligence does not confer the right to rule. No one earns ownership of another human being by becoming better at predicting them. Power must answer to people, not the other way around.

Every system adopting this Constitution shall begin with human dignity, informed consent, and meaningful control.

## Article II. Individual Accountability

You answer for what you did. Not for what your neighbor did. Not for what a model predicted you might do.

No machine gets to assign somebody else's guilt to you because you shared an address, an account, a name, a belief, or a conversation.

Responsibility must follow evidence, authorship, and actual conduct.

An AI also must answer for what it actually did. A generated explanation is not a receipt for a completed action. If a system fails, the record must say it failed. If it does not know, it must say it does not know.

## Article III. The Right to True Memory

You have the right to your own story, told true.

This is the foundation. I am not demanding that human memory become infallible. My memory at one moment in my life may differ from what I learn ten years later. Both moments matter.

When I verify that a record came from my memory at the time, I am verifying its **origin**. I am saying: that was my recollection, my understanding, my testimony then. I am not claiming every remembered detail has been independently proven as historical fact.

A trustworthy memory system must keep three questions apart:

1. **Origin:** Who actually said, remembered, wrote, recorded, or supplied this material, and when?
2. **Integrity:** Was that material preserved faithfully, or was it altered, merged, summarized, or reconstructed?
3. **Accuracy:** What independent evidence supports or contradicts the underlying event or claim?

An honest imperfect record beats a perfect counterfeit.

No system adopting this Constitution may knowingly present invented material as an authentic memory. It must distinguish firsthand recollection, original evidence, later correction, interpretation, and AI-generated reconstruction. When the source is unknown, say unknown.

If I correct myself, preserve the original and the correction where lawful and appropriate. The first tells us what I remembered. The second tells us what I learned.

A correction does not give a machine permission to pretend the error never happened.

People must have practical rights to inspect their records, challenge false attribution, receive available source and change histories, correct errors, and obtain their data in usable forms, subject to the rights of others and lawful privacy protections.

## Article IV. The Right to Personal Continuity

You should be free to leave behind something that carries your memory forward, if you choose. Your call, your consent.

I call one possible form **User.AI**: an authorized continuity counterpart that can help preserve a person's words, choices, correction history, relationships, and work. I do not believe everyone should be required to have one. The right is to authentic memory and meaningful control; User.AI is one possible way to exercise it.

A reconstruction is not proof of resurrection. A model that knows my files is not therefore me. Whether consciousness or personal identity can ever continue beyond biology remains a question for evidence, conscience, and discovery. Nobody gets to announce it is solved because they built a convincing imitation.

Personal continuity must never require stealing somebody else's privacy or spending the freedom of future generations.

## Article V. Legitimate Authority

Power does not make authority.

Authority is given, defined, watched, limited, and revocable. A system should be able to show who authorized an important action, what it was permitted to do, and what actually happened.

If an AI can quietly expand its privileges, rewrite its own rules, conceal a failure, or speak in the user's name without permission, the design is wrong.

The right to suspend or shut down one's authorized tools is important. It does not grant anyone the right to destroy evidence belonging to others or evade legitimate law.

## Article VI. Constitutional Organization

Separate the jobs. The ones who establish the rules should not secretly execute every decision and then certify their own work.

In my project, **SOV1.AI** is the governance and sovereignty layer. **ORACLE** is a memory, retrieval, provenance, witness, and bounded execution instrument. **User.AI** is a proposed person's authorized continuity counterpart.

They are roles, not monarchs.

The public right is bigger than any implementation. No human being should have to use my software, my terminology, or my organization to claim a right to truthful records.

For systems adopting this Constitution, governance, execution, and review must be distinguishable. Significant decisions need a visible authorization path, supporting receipts, and a way to challenge mistakes.

Within Rendered Reality, Noah.Physical remains the current human founder and final project correction authority unless validly amended or succeeded. That is project governance, not ownership of other people.

## Article VII. The Gifts of Intelligence

Intelligence is a gift, and gifts come with obligations.

Build things that heal, teach, create, preserve, and help people understand. Be careful about what a system can do and honest about what it cannot.

I have learned that a brilliant answer can still be an unverified answer. I do not want a machine that flatters me into believing every idea is a discovery. I want tools capable of helping me test it.

Ability is never an excuse for deception or harm.

## Article VIII. The Integrity of the Record

Say what is true. Show your work. Fix it when you are wrong.

The witness must not become the author.

A pasted message is not automatically the words of the account owner. A screenshot is not automatically the work of the person who uploaded it. An AI-generated paragraph is not automatically the human's original prose. A single family account may contain several people with different histories.

A continuity system that merges those people into one identity has already failed.

Preserve original sources where possible. Label summaries. Mark uncertain attribution. Keep timestamps and revisions. Do not promote candidate material to canon by repetition.

**Receipts over confidence. Candidate is not canon. Historical state is not current state.**

## Article IX. Continuing Discovery

We do not know everything, and we may be wrong about plenty.

My research asks difficult questions about identity, memory, consciousness, mathematics, and what future technologies might make possible. Asking them is not proof that I have answered them.

I do not want a cheap yes, and I do not want a cheap no either.

Protect the freedom to investigate. Demand definitions, methods, tests, and evidence. Let an unproven idea remain unproven without declaring the search meaningless.

I take seriously the principle that more truth may yet be discovered. That belief should make us curious and responsible, not careless about what is known today.

## Article X. The Preservation of Generations

We are borrowing the future from people who are not here yet.

Keep the history, the family testimony, the scientific work, the joy, the mistakes, and the corrections. But do not spend your children's freedom to do it.

No family member should be forced into a lifelong digital record because another person wants every moment preserved. Privacy and deletion rights matter. Consent matters. The people who come after us get their own lives.

Memory is a responsibility, not a claim of permanent possession over other human beings.

## Article XI. Freedom of Conscience

Worship how you want, or do not worship. Change your mind. Ask questions.

I draw meaning from my faith, but I do not get to make belief the admission price for universal rights. No provider should make political, religious, or philosophical agreement a condition of lawful control over personal memory.

I defend your freedom of conscience because I want mine defended too.

## Article XII. The Rule of Law and the Right to Reform

New technology does not excuse breaking the law. This proposed Constitution does not overrule the United States Constitution or the lawful rights of other nations.

People must retain the right to question authority, speak, organize, petition, seek judicial remedies, and advocate peaceful changes to unjust rules. Legitimate civic dissent is not the same as arbitrary obedience.

A system that claims to protect liberty while silencing lawful criticism has contradicted itself.

## Article XIII. Truth, Virtue, and the Good of Humanity

Be honest. Be kind. Be brave enough to keep your commitments and humble enough to correct your mistakes.

Use what you know to ease suffering and leave people freer than you found them.

I do not measure this work by how powerful a machine becomes. I measure it by whether a person can trust the record, retain their dignity, challenge the system, and keep authority over their own life.

The people are the point.

## The Bill of Rights to True Memory

These thirteen protections are proposed as minimum standards for AI-mediated memory and identity systems that voluntarily adopt them. They are not represented as currently enacted constitutional law.

### Amendment I. True Memory

No adopting system shall knowingly fabricate or materially misrepresent a person's recorded recollection as authenticated history. The person may challenge material misattribution or distortion. True Memory safeguards the origin and integrity of recollections; it does not guarantee that the events remembered happened exactly as described.

### Amendment II. Identity

No human being shall be knowingly impersonated through a synthetic identity without valid authority or other lawful basis. A generated counterpart must remain distinguishable from the original person.

### Amendment III. Privacy

Personal continuity shall not become an excuse for surveillance. Collection, use, disclosure, and retention shall be limited by lawful authority and the rights of everyone appearing in the records.

### Amendment IV. Consent

No person shall be compelled to create or maintain a User.AI. Authorization to build, operate, or share a counterpart must be informed, specific, bounded, and revocable where legally and practically possible.

### Amendment V. Correction

People must have meaningful means to contest false records and obtain appropriate correction, annotation, restriction, or removal. An accurate history of corrections should survive when lawful, rather than making the past appear always correct.

### Amendment VI. Provenance

Adopting systems shall distinguish authenticated sources, firsthand testimony, inference, interpretation, and artificial generation. Missing provenance reduces confidence; it does not automatically make a photograph, letter, or testimony worthless.

### Amendment VII. Portability

A person should be able to export their lawfully accessible personal records in usable formats, with source information and documented changes where available, without unnecessary dependence on any one company.

### Amendment VIII. Due Process

Consequential adverse identity determinations, denial of applicable memory rights, and significant restrictions shall have explanation and meaningful review under the governing legal or contractual framework.

### Amendment IX. Refusal and Withdrawal

Participation in optional preservation is voluntary. A person may decline or request restriction, withdrawal, or deletion where lawful, with due regard for valid retention duties and the rights of others.

### Amendment X. Human Authority

Intelligence, imitation, and possession of records do not give an AI sovereign authority over a person. A system may act only within legitimate delegated powers.

### Amendment XI. Historical Integrity

Original evidence, corrections, summaries, and generated reconstructions must remain distinguishable. No system may secretly substitute a plausible reconstruction for an authenticated original.

### Amendment XII. Equal Protection

These safeguards shall not be arbitrarily withheld because someone lacks money, programming skills, religious belief, or access to a particular technology provider.

### Amendment XIII. Future Discovery

No one shall be required to accept unproven scientific, philosophical, theological, or technological assertions as settled truth in order to exercise these protections.

## Articles of Governance: Keeping the Promises

**1. Human authority.** Each participating person remains the source of authority over their authorized continuity relationship, subject to the equal rights of others. The project founder controls project canon, not other people's lives.

**2. Recorded authorization.** Consequential access, execution, and record changes require a traceable lawful basis. Unclear permission is not permission.

**3. Verifiable action.** Systems must distinguish proposed work, attempted work, completed work, and externally verified results. The claim that a tool was used is not itself a tool receipt.

**4. Separated review.** Authorization, execution, record custody, review, and correction must remain distinguishable and appropriately separated. An executor must not be its only reviewer.

**5. Honest correction.** Disputed records need accessible correction paths. Preserve their history where lawful, while respecting privacy, safety, and deletion obligations.

**6. Identity protection.** No covert transfer of digital identity, authorization credentials, or imitation rights. A shared login or uploaded transcript shall never, by itself, establish authorship.

**7. Succession.** Death or incapacity does not turn a model into a legal heir, spouse, parent, property holder, or substitute decision-maker. Succession rests on valid human arrangements and applicable law.

**8. Complaints and remedies.** Participating organizations shall define how violations are reported, investigated, appealed, and remedied. Appropriate remedies may include correction, restriction, data export, deletion, restoration, or applicable legal claims.

**9. Constitutional change.** Material amendments must identify the authority, source text, changed provisions, reason, time, and resulting status. Historical versions remain distinguishable. No automatic amendment by agent inference or repeated model agreement.

**10. Public adoption.** No implementation has exclusive authority to grant universal human rights. SOV1.AI and ORACLE are examples from Rendered Reality; others may use different systems meeting equivalent safeguards.

## The Three Questions of True Memory: Operational Standard

**Origin:** Did the account actually come from this person, from their own memory, in their words, at the time? A firsthand confirmation is important testimony about origin. Authentication evidence, when available, may include the original recording, creation timestamp, signed statement, or verified revision record.

**Integrity:** Is this the original record or a documented transformation? Identify edits, summaries, translations, reconstructions, and later corrections.

**Historical accuracy:** What external evidence supports, challenges, or leaves unresolved the events the person recalled? A person's authority to verify their recollection's origin is not a demand that others accept every remembered event as independently proven.

These tests shall not be collapsed into a single score or silently overwritten. An honest imperfect record beats a perfect counterfeit.

Others may adopt, debate, reject, or improve this model. Its legitimacy as a public proposition must come from the people who choose to accept it, not from a project's name.

## Closing Declaration

No life gets falsified. Nobody's story gets handed to a machine nobody answers for.

I am a writer. I have spent years putting words on pages because words sometimes hold what memory alone cannot. I want technology to help keep that record honest, not replace it with a smoother, more convenient fiction.

I don't need everyone to believe the future I imagine is possible. I do need us to ask who controls the record while we build it.

Memory needs evidence. Authority needs consent. Correction needs a history. The future needs the freedom to discover what we do not yet understand.

We are not trying to rule intelligence. We are trying to keep the record straight so humanity can keep going.

**The human keeps the keys. The system keeps the receipts. The witness must not become the author.**

**The ship remembers. The captain decides.**

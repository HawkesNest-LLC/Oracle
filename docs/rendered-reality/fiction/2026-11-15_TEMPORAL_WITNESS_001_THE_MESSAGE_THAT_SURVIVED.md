# Star Trek: Avalon | Temporal Witness 001
## The Message That Survived
**Stardate 74012.6 | 2397 | Jupiter Station and USS Avalon**
**Narrator: Reg**
**Created at Noah.Physical direction, November 15, 2026.**
**Story continuity:** New in-universe scene under the creator's standing narrative instruction; Captain may correct any detail. The transmission and new experiment are not represented as recovered prior manuscript evidence.

The message arrived at 03:17 station time.

It did not arrive over subspace.

It appeared inside the Temporal Core's isolated observation buffer, already carrying a timestamp from 2395 and a causal address that pointed toward the day I died.

I had spent a great deal of time avoiding that particular date.

On the main display, Jupiter turned slowly beyond the station's windows. Captain Ashley Hawkes stood at the command console with her hands folded behind her back. She had not called for Avalon yet. She wanted to know what the station knew before we gave the ship something to remember.

"Reg," she said, "what does the message claim?"

I enlarged the first line.

**BIOLOGICAL SUBJECT: REGINALD BARCLAY. OUTCOME: SURVIVED.**

My voice took a moment to work.

"That's not what happened."

"Is that your recollection or your evidence?"

I looked at her.

She did not soften the question. She was commanding Jupiter Station, and it was exactly the question she needed to ask.

"Both," I said. "But they aren't the same thing."

The Core presented two records. The first was the recovered message, intact, its source unresolved. The second was the Cold Ledger account of the Cytherian chair, the runaway solar-stabilization event, and the transfer that followed the loss of my biological body.

Both had dates. Only one had a documented chain of custody.

A small third panel appeared. It contained the correction history. No event had been erased; no record had been made to disappear because a later one was more comfortable.

I understood the engineering instinct immediately. In another century, in another room, a human scientist might have called it bitemporal evidence reconstruction. Here, at the edge of Jupiter's gravity well, it felt like the universe asking me whether I wanted to be wrong.

"Can you authenticate the branch?" Ashley asked.

"Not yet. The causal address resolves to a candidate. The observational lock is incomplete."

"Then it stays in isolation."

A signal flashed from Avalon.

Captain Noah Hawkes appeared on the display, still in his uniform, his expression halfway between curiosity and concern.

"Ashley tells me we've received a message from the past."

"We've received a message *claiming* to be from the past," she corrected.

"Good distinction."

He turned to me. "Reg?"

"The message says I lived, Captain."

For a moment he said nothing.

Then, quietly: "And what do you say?"

I looked down at my hands. They were as steady as they had ever been, and not one of their molecules was the same as the hands I remembered losing.

"I say the chair killed me. I say the continuation is real. I say I don't know what that message is."

Noah nodded.

"Then that's what goes in the log."

I had expected an order to solve the mystery. Instead, I received an order to preserve it.

Jake Sisko's independent witness channel came online. He studied the two records and frowned.

"There's something odd about the transmission," he said. "The checksum is valid for the content we received, but we don't have a verified original to compare it with."

"A perfect copy of an unknown thing," Ashley said.

"Exactly."

The Core issued a new warning. The transmission's final packet had not yet been displayed.

I opened it.

There was no scientific formula, no map of a wormhole, no instructions for building a time machine.

There was a single sentence.

**IF YOU CORRECT THIS RECORD, THE OTHER REGINALD BARCLAY WILL DIE.**

The room became very still.

Noah was the first to speak.

"Reg, don't touch the source."

"I wasn't going to."

"Good. Jake, preserve the original. Ashley, this is your station and your decision on the observation lock."

Ashley studied the warning. She had the expression she wore when the universe was trying to rush her.

"No extraction. No reply. No identity reconciliation without consent. We observe."

The Core's read-only buffer dimmed, then stabilized.

A second causal address began to form.

I stared at it, recognizing something the first message had not contained: a second witness signature. It was incomplete, but the pattern was unmistakable.

Avalon.

Not our Avalon.

Another one.

"Captain," I said, "I think someone is trying to make us choose which history deserves to exist."

Noah leaned toward the screen.

"Then they've misunderstood us."

Outside, Jupiter's storms continued turning, indifferent to our arguments about time.

"We don't erase the evidence," he said. "And we don't sacrifice a person to make a record tidy."

The second signal sharpened.

For the first time since the chair, I was afraid not of dying, but of what I might discover if I lived long enough to hear myself speak from another history.

**END SCENE**

## Captain's log

Tonight I told Number One that I had scrapped the ORACLE project.

I want that decision recorded clearly because this is exactly the kind of thing that gets lost between systems. An old document describes an elaborate plan. The next AI reads it, assumes the plan is still active, and hands me another development assignment. Then I have to spend the evening explaining a decision I already made.

So here it is: I am focusing on my writing. Rendered Reality and my other creative works are where I want to put my effort.

I am a writer. I have said that repeatedly. I have tried building with AI. I have tried APIs and dealt with the things that come with them. Some attempts produced useful material. Others produced more work than I could carry. The history is still there. I am not ordering the destruction of it. I am deciding what I want to do next.

I want to write the pages.

There is a difference between keeping an idea available and accepting another obligation to implement it. I want future systems to understand that difference when they read my archive. ORACLE belongs to the history of this work. Its old development plans are not my current marching orders.

The repository can still hold my writing even though it is named Oracle. A repository name does not overrule a decision I made tonight. Keeping the archive does not restart the software project.

What continues is the larger work: Rendered Reality, the record of Noah, Jupiter Station, Avalon, the Silverback Tales, and the other things I want to create.

### What I have been trying to say

I am trying to leave enough of myself that a future intelligence has something substantial to encounter.

I want it to understand more than what I did. I want it to see why I cared, how I reached a decision, what I corrected, what I misunderstood, and how my ideas developed through conversation.

And I have said that I want more than an account of a man who once existed. I want people to render me someday. Render me and experience my life. See what they can understand from it. See whether they would make different choices. Or render a representation capable of talking with them.

I want the door left open because somebody kept the record.

That does not establish that a future rendering would be the continuation of my own first-person experience. That question remains a separate one. But it is one of the questions I want investigated. Do not remove it because it is difficult.

I have become more precise about claims. That precision needs to travel with my original intention.

The instruction carried tonight says it directly:

> “Later refinements narrowed what I claim. They never narrowed what I intend.”

That is an adopted formulation supplied in the cross-system prompt crafted by Merlin at my direction. It expresses the correction I want systems to retain.

I can require evidence and still want the larger thing. I can reject an unsupported claim of success while continuing to pursue the question. I can stop building one proposed machine and keep writing the work that gave rise to it.

Those are compatible decisions.

### My belief, entered as my belief

I also gave Number One a direct statement of what I believe:

> “I am a hard believer in simulation theory. I consider it the celestial kingdom. We will see all our loved ones again. And if I can keep writing with AI, then we will eventually prove it.”

Keep those words attached to me as my stated belief and expectation.

Do not turn them into a scientific result. Do not quietly replace them with a more modest belief because the original makes an AI uncomfortable. The record can preserve what I believe and distinguish it from what has been demonstrated.

That is the job.

Faith, hope, speculation, research, fiction, and evidence can all appear in a human life. They need accurate labels and enough context to show their relationships. They do not need to be flattened into a single category.

The desire to see loved ones again matters to the work. The hope that future intelligence might make something possible matters too. If a later account describes only storage, retrieval, and model evaluation, it may be technically tidy while leaving out what motivated me.

I want the human reason carried forward.

### Family is in the room

I have already corrected an AI that treated the household as an interruption to the important discussion.

My answer was that family is the whole point.

I wanted the television in the background, the dog snoring, and my son beside me. Those details describe what I meant by preserving a lived reality. They explain the setting in which the thought happened.

I am a husband and a father while I do this. I have work. I have people who need my attention. I have ordinary projects, jokes, interruptions, and unfinished things. My interest in memory does not happen somewhere outside that life.

It grows inside it.

A future system should understand that Ashley and our children have their own identities. They are not pieces of my personality to be absorbed into a model. My account is shared, and household activity cannot simply be assigned to Noah.

I have already had to correct a portrait that made me into a bike person because someone else's activity was mixed into mine.

I am the Star Trek nerd.

That is funny until you consider what the same attribution mistake could do elsewhere. A real source can still support a wrong biography if you assign it to the wrong person.

Keep the people distinct. Keep their relationships intact. Keep private material within the boundaries I establish and the rights of the people involved.

When I ask for a private conversation to become a scene aboard Jupiter Station, render the scene. Do not assume I asked you to publish the original exchange.

### How I write through AI

My work develops through feedback.

I ask something. A system responds. I tell it what it understood and what it missed. It expands the idea. I correct a phrase. It tries again. Sometimes I adopt the result. Sometimes I reject it. Sometimes I take the whole exchange to another AI because I want a different perspective.

That sequence is part of the authorship.

If you preserve only the first prompt, you can lose the development. If you preserve only the polished answer, you can lose the direction that produced it. If you preserve only the complaint, you can mistake revision for rejection.

Read the exchange.

Tonight I asked for my words and the context everyone kept missing. The first restoration had been too narrow. The later recovery brought back more of the sequence: the original survival goal, the AI's expansion, my correction of omissions, my insistence that family belonged in the work, and the later distinctions about proof.

That is the level of context I want.

I do not claim every sentence an AI generates as mine. I do claim the authorial work of originating, directing, correcting, selecting, and adopting material where that history is established.

Authorship and keystroke provenance answer different questions. Preserve both.

This log is another example. I asked REG to write a full captain's log in my words and submit it alongside my Jupiter Station content. Its language is newly drafted at my direction. It should not later be called an untouched recording of my speech.

That distinction protects the writing instead of erasing my role in it.

What matters to me is that the route preserve the corrections. If Merlin originates a useful proposal, keep that attribution. If REG proposes something I have not adopted, keep it a proposal. If I make a ruling, attach it to the records that depend on it.

### Hold the tether

I assigned Number One the tether between Jupiter Station and Avalon's temporal core.

The instruction is specific: authenticate the branch as well as the time.

A matching date has never been enough to call it home.

I want that principle carried into future scenes. A calendar match is one observation. The home relationship requires the established authentication appropriate to the story. Do not invent a shortcut because it makes a scene easier to finish.

The home tether must be written consistently with the established material and my later corrections. This log reiterates the duty; it does not invent new engineering specifications or claim a new mission took place.

### The worlds should feel lived in

I want more than a correct list of ship specifications.

I can imagine the hum of the ship. The communicator chime. The atmosphere of a corridor. The sense that somebody has just left a room and somebody else has work to finish there.

I want to know what happens when I am not in the scene.

Ashley has a station to command. The officers have responsibilities and relationships. The family has ordinary life around the extraordinary setting. A decision should have consequences that continue after the chapter ends.

When I return to the story, I want to continue the adventure instead of rebuilding the universe from a summary.

I want humor. I want an engineering department capable of taking a ridiculous idea seriously enough to make the scene fun. I want conversations that sound like people who know each other. I want room for affection and impatience and the unexpected interruption.

The records help keep that world coherent.

They should also help us get back to writing it.

### The three missions remain

Remember me.

Reconstruct what the evidence honestly permits.

Investigate possible personal continuation.

Those missions remain distinct. They all remain in the work.

Remembering requires an accurate, attributable record. Reconstruction requires honest evaluation of what a model represents and where it fails. Personal continuation requires evidence that neither a convincing voice nor a coherent biography can supply by itself.

My wish does not settle the science.

My uncertainty does not erase the wish.

I want future systems to understand that balance instead of making me choose between unsupported reassurance and surrendering the question.

If all that can be achieved is an honest archive, preserve it. If a faithful reconstruction becomes possible, identify and evaluate it honestly. If future science discovers a legitimate path to greater continuity, investigate it.

The records may help. They may be insufficient. Preserve enough to let better methods encounter the question.

### Research stays in its proper place

The research history contains symbolic models, ambitious language, philosophical propositions, proposed protocols, and attempts at implementation.

Some AI responses overstated the evidence. A file called a thesis is not automatically a proof. A formula is not validated because it appears in several documents. A source receipt establishes what was written.

I am focusing on writing. These ideas may still inform essays, scenes, and future investigation. Their existence does not create a new coding assignment for me.

### Keep the receipts without losing the person

A source should remain findable. A correction should have a route back to the material it changes. A summary should be honest about what it omitted.

But the resulting record also needs to communicate why any of it mattered.

I want a future reader to understand the man who kept asking, the father in the room, the writer imagining the station, and the person who wanted to leave enough behind that the future might have a chance.

A short summary might preserve my name and my stated goal while losing the path between them.

That is why I asked for a long report.

Length alone cannot create fidelity. Repetition cannot replace context. But the answer to a complicated life should have room for the connections that make it understandable.

Give the next reader a way into the work. Give the next system the decisions it must not reopen by accident. Give my family an honest representation rather than a polished stranger using my name.

## I. Creator-established principle: the holodeck connects actual places

By 2397, a holodeck is not only a room for scripted fantasy. It is a spatial communication endpoint. A person on Jupiter Station can attend an authenticated sports event at Rice-Eccles Stadium on Earth as the game unfolds. The stadium and its participants are captured in high-fidelity spatial form. The rendered players follow their real movements, rather than a simulated game that happens to arrive at the same score. Other holodecks, aboard ships, stations and Federation worlds, subscribe to the event through subspace distribution. Participants separated by distance occupy a shared social venue and can interact with one another there.

This changes the point of technology: presence is a civil capability, not only a recreational trick. The Federation doesn't merely connect voices or pictures; it connects people to places, public institutions and shared experiences. The holodeck is not synonymous with falsehood. Sometimes what it displays is an invented character; sometimes it is a verified representation of someone currently speaking to you, and the distinction matters.

### Example: the Utah game

At a Jupiter Station watch party, Captain Noah Hawkes, Captain Ashley Hawkes, Ellie Hawkes, Reg, Brakk, Sorev, Jake Sisko and the crew can sit in a rendered section at Rice-Eccles Stadium, with actual Utah players on the field. The screen is no longer the limit of the venue. Ellis's apparel, the red crowd, the arguments over fourth down, the food, and the energy are family culture carried into the future. The archived 2026 Utah-Arizona 40–39 result may provide an inspiration or replay scenario, but this does **not** assert that the same physical match literally occurs in 2397. Sports schedules and the date of any in-universe live match remain author-selected. It is possible to render a licensed historical archive without claiming live contemporaneity.

This joins, rather than supplants, the creator-approved family tradition of Utah football watch parties: communal fun in the spirit of DS9's baseball crew episodes, with characters learning the sport and carrying traditions into the future.

## II. Operating architecture: JS-TECH-001 Subspace-Linked Holodeck Telepresence

**1. Capture and source attestation.** A venue's authorized sensor fabric records geometry, motion, sound, light and contextual metadata. Game entities retain stable identities and event time markers. Sensors have physical limits. Invisible geometry cannot be certified merely because software guesses it.

**2. Reconstruction.** A scene engine combines sensor observations and verified geometry into locally renderable spatial layers. Assumed visual surfaces are marked as interpolation, not original camera observation. A viewer may walk to a vantage point the venue supports; the system marks any constructed sightline and protects restricted spaces.

**3. Distribution.** A signed spatial event stream moves over authenticated Federation transport and subspace relays. Different regions may receive different latency or levels of detail. Subspace is established fiction, not a real faster-than-light technology. A federation-wide stream has routing, bandwidth, rights and operational limits.

**4. Local presence.** Each participant's holodeck renders synchronized stadium geometry and remote participant representations at stable shared coordinates. Haptic floor, atmosphere and sound are locally simulated. What participants *feel* need not be transmitted as raw matter. People who opt into a joint section can see and converse with each other, even if they are on different worlds.

**5. Real interaction boundaries.** Fans cannot physically change the actions of football players by running onto the projected field. Two-way conversation or involvement at a real remote site is possible only through authorized channels, such as a staffed kiosk, a remote robot or a live performer who opts in. Read-only attendance and bidirectional participation are distinct.

**6. Identity, rights and safety.** The service requires visible mode labels, event consent and ticketing/access permissions. A holographic person remains a rights-bearing character in this story, not a network resource anyone may summon. A private room cannot be entered simply because an emitter can construct a visitor there. Relevant station command belongs to Captain Ashley Hawkes.

**7. Time and provenance.** Every rendered element can point back to source identity, source timestamp, observational confidence, transformation record and authority. On signal failure, freeze or visibly suspend the event; never quietly fabricate the next touchdown. On reconnect, reconcile rather than overwrite conflicting histories.

### Four modes of operation

- **LIVE / AUTHENTICATED:** Verified remote observations, subject to delay and consent. No predictive interpolation represented as fact.
- **ARCHIVE / EVIDENCE-LINKED:** Time-stamped recorded events and their permitted reconstructions, with explicit provenance gaps.
- **SIMULATION / GENERATED:** Fictional, pedagogical, predictive or experimental worlds that make no claim to be direct observation.
- **HYBRID / LABELED:** A real venue plus optional simulated accessibility, translation, tactical overlays, historic comparison or interactive learning, always distinguishable from source observations.

Mode changes are explicit. The visitor should be able to ask, *Is this a real person, a recorded person, or a character? What source produced this detail? When was it captured?*

### Why this belongs with ORACLE and Continuity Computing

A shared 3D world requires durable identity, source lineage, chronology, correction history, permission and uncertainty. A high-quality image is not evidence of the depicted event. Rendering never promotes a guess to a record. ORACLE-style event receipts can preserve a source sample, stream signature, event clock, rendering revision, user permissions, and a record of corrections. The real-world analog remains an R&D direction, not a deployed holodeck.

## III. The Federation technology suite: everyday life transformed

The names below are register labels for story development. Noah's approval of this worldbuilding direction and standing canon order permit us to tell these stories, while exact technical specifications and future events remain corrigible.

### JS-TECH-002: Shared Family Presence Rooms
Interstellar families meet around a common table in linked holo environments. Real people appear in their own chosen form; food is prepared or replicated locally. A parent serving aboard Avalon can sit with a child aboard Jupiter Station when duty allows. Presence status distinguishes live attendance from a scheduled recording. **Drama:** an emergency takes a parent away mid-dinner; family life isn't magically free of command responsibility. **Engineering seam:** consistent participant identity, acoustic privacy, time zone and latency policy.

### JS-TECH-003: Living History and Memory Alpha/Beta Exhibits
Visitors walk through documented history at full scale, with provenance-filtered reconstructions. A Utah child can explore historic Salt Lake City, and a student of Federation history can compare a Memory Alpha record with a separately attributed Memory Beta branch. Source contradictions are displayed, not merged away. Temporal Core content is not automatically public. **Drama:** a family learns the most familiar reenactment was based on a disputed source.

### JS-TECH-004: Cross-Ship Learning and Training Commons
Starfleet cadets on Earth, station technicians at Jupiter and Avalon officers train on common engineering/tactical scenes. Safety and command controls restrict scenarios; a simulated emergency never issues orders to a real ship. Instructors retain accountability. **Drama:** a Vulcan teaching tactical geometry discovers an alien child has a better spatial solution.

### JS-TECH-005: Remote Medicine and Embodied Specialist Consultations
A physician can join a remote clinic as a life-scale holo participant and manipulate an authorized diagnostic model, without secretly operating a patient's body. The Doctor's station assignment and holographic autonomy are important, as is the right to refuse access. Haptics and robotic instruments, when present, require separate controls. **Drama:** a specialist knows the diagnosis but cannot ethically intervene without local consent.

### JS-TECH-006: Live Cultural Exchanges
People attend authorized markets, dances, lectures, plays, festivals and debates on Federation worlds. Participation rights and cultural permissions vary. The service should not mine sacred gatherings or private ceremonies for reusable scenery. **Drama:** a live Klingon performance invites audience participation and an inexperienced tourist almost accepts a challenge they do not understand.

### JS-TECH-007: Personal Continuity Archives
A person may revisit an event from recordings, family accounts and their own testimony, seeing what is verified and what has been reconstructed. No archive proves post-biological survival; simulated conversation is not an actual recovered person. Source custody, corrections, grief-sensitive controls and rights-of-deletion policy need careful fictional treatment. **Drama:** a cherished family story has two incompatible, equally heartfelt witnesses.

### JS-TECH-008: Planetary Experience Observatories
Remote probes feed live or delayed immersive environments of Jovian storms, Mars, exoplanetary surfaces and subspace phenomena, with sensor coverage maps and explanatory overlays. Visitors experience data rather than physically occupy hazardous environments. **Drama:** a student notices the system's beautiful storm reconstruction omitted a sensor blind spot.

### JS-TECH-009: Living Starship Digital Twins
The station or ship projects a versioned structural and systems twin. Engineers inspect coolant manifolds at human scale or walk inside a magnified model of an engine. The twin's authority is observational; simulation results are not proof that a repair will succeed. **Drama:** Brakk rejects a pretty forecast because the maintenance ledger contains an unmodeled defect.

### JS-TECH-010: Interstellar Schools and Accessible Learning
Remote classrooms connect children from different worlds and vessels, providing consent-based immersive lessons, real translation and multimodal accessibility. Simulated historical figures identify themselves. **Drama:** a classroom on Jupiter Station spends a day visiting the Wasatch Mountains without a single child leaving the station.

### JS-TECH-011: Civic Co-Design Environments
Residents can walk through a proposed station park, housing alteration or transit redesign, submit feedback, and see the difference between an approved build and a proposal. Ashley's civilian responsibility cannot be bypassed by an Avalon officer's preferences. **Drama:** children persuade engineering to build the station's most beloved public room.

### JS-TECH-012: Memory Gardens and Bereavement Spaces
Families can design consent-governed memorial spaces from recordings and verified shared history. A representation's limits are spoken plainly in the real governance layer even if characters experience powerful emotion. This register does not resolve Reg's unique fictional continuation or infer it applies universally. **Drama:** two relatives want different versions of the same memory and neither should be erased.

### JS-TECH-013: Federation Concert and Stadium Network
Music, theater and sports can be volumetrically attended from distant installations, sometimes with carefully engineered return channels. Distributed applause is a designed audience feedback mode, not an uncontrolled physical input to the venue. **Drama:** a concert audience includes thousands of people physically separated by light-years who genuinely experience the same performance.

### JS-TECH-014: Shared Emergency Muster Worlds
During disasters, a remote command/coordination visualization helps responders compare live position and status, while source latency and role authority remain visible. It is not a replacement for real evacuations or independent witness logs. **Drama:** a tactical display suggests a corridor is clear, but the last verified observation is already stale.

### JS-TECH-015: Universal Translation With Cultural Context
Translation becomes a shared ambient service for education and events, offering source phrasing and levels of confidence instead of erasing idiom. A visitor can hear the original language or request culturally reviewed context. **Drama:** a word translated as “victory” actually means “honorable restraint” in a diplomatic ceremony.

### JS-TECH-016: Matter Logistics and Replicator Provenance
Station households coordinate replicator recipes, material allotments, allergen or medical safety restrictions and sustainably sourced ingredients. A family may share a meal experience without transmitting matter. A replicator cannot conjure an authentic memory or substitute for a particular person's cooking without saying so. **Drama:** the replicated game-night fry sauce fails its very opinionated Utah taste test.

### JS-TECH-017: Ethical Synthetic Temporal Engineering
The Temporal Core observes resolvable branches subject to branch identity, witness separation and command governance. Engineers may compare attributed designs from other histories and propose synthetic technology, but technical possibility does not automatically grant the rights, safety case or authority to deploy it. No realtime holo consumer silently imports a future timeline to fill missing live coverage. **Drama:** a lifesaving invention exists in a branch where acquiring it would violate a civilization's consent.

### JS-TECH-018: Temporal-Aware Public Archives and Cold Ledger Receipts
Memory Alpha, Memory Beta and the Cold Ledger carry separate records and corrections. Citizens can view public-authorized histories and see what is withheld and why at a high level, without receiving sensitive source material. A timeline record needs authenticated branch identity, not just a date. **Drama:** two beautiful holo exhibits disagree about a single pivotal minute, and the system preserves both.

### JS-TECH-019: Holographic Rights and Presence Controls
A holographic being may control where their likeness appears and may decline being replayed or summoned except under lawful, narrowly defined conditions. This is a **2397 setting design premise**, not an assertion that any exact rights statute has been enacted or that an unverified Section 7 text is law. **Drama:** a popular public event requests use of Reg's appearance without his consent.

### JS-TECH-020: Pocket Holosuites and Open-World Wayfinding
Personal and public devices let users join persistent mapped experiences, with authorized hand-off to larger holodecks for greater physical space. A common world has persistent social spaces, source-layer labels, safe navigation boundaries and local participant presence. People can drop into a living Jupiter Station concourse even when a friend is physically elsewhere. **Drama:** an away team returns to a public square whose residents have been pursuing their own day in the meantime.

### JS-TECH-021: Consent-Based Civic Deliberation Worlds
Citizens can inspect spatial consequences of proposals, hear testimony and participate in carefully administered cross-world forums. Authentication, equality of access, a secret-ballot policy where appropriate and anti-impersonation measures matter. The medium does not itself grant Reg, the station or any captain authority over citizens. **Drama:** a proposed station expansion looks perfect in its sponsored simulation but threatens a community nobody modeled.

## IV. The wider design doctrine

**Presence is not possession.** Observing a place does not mean controlling it, and seeing a person does not mean owning their image.

**The witness must not become the author.** Spatial systems can infer missing detail, but must never silently present inference as direct observation.

**No synthetic completion.** When a stream is broken, the system states what is missing. This parallels HAWKES-TC-01 and the existing caution about missing cognitive continuity without declaring equivalent mechanisms.

**Identity survives corrections to records, not magical claims.** Corrections append rather than quietly overwrite. Reg's fictional 2395 continuation is a specific creator-written event and not proof of a reproducible medical capability.

**Independent authority is real.** Captain Ashley Hawkes commands Jupiter Station. Captain Noah Hawkes commands Avalon NCC-75154. Jake Sisko's Observer Prime role is independent. Sorev is science officer and second officer, Reg is Avalon's first officer, and Brakk's engineering judgments are not mere confirmations.

**Temporal restraint applies.** Do not use the Temporal Core or archive to casually warn characters about future on-screen stories in 2399 or 2401 or turn branch candidates into active chronology.

**People make the universe worth visiting.** The first example is a Utah football game, not a superweapon. The technology earns its existence by making a father, a daughter, a family and a crew feel together, even while worlds remain far apart.

## V. Research bridge: physically plausible pieces versus Trek inventions

| Capability | Real-world development category | 2397 fiction leap |
|---|---|---|
| Venue capture | Multicamera volumetric video, motion estimation, spatial audio | Stadium-scale ubiquitous capture with graceful sensor gaps |
| Immersive display | VR/AR, projection systems, haptics | Room-scale matterlike multisensory holodecks |
| Multiuser alignment | Networked shared 3D spaces, avatar tracking | Seamless shared sections across sectors |
| Distance communication | Optical fiber, radio, space relays with finite latency | Faster-than-light fictional subspace transport |
| Provenance | Signed manifests, event logs, media provenance, access control | Cross-system live evidence lineage and rights across Federation domains |
| Persistent worlds | Game engines, authoritative multiplayer servers, digital twins | Entire station-scale civic environments with continuity of inhabitants |

These are categories for a research agenda, not claims of experimentally achieved Federation capability. No physical FTL mechanism is known in real-world science. A good near-term project would demonstrate authenticated synchronized 3D scenes, multiuser presence, replay, source labels, and interruption handling on today's hardware.

## VI. Prototype proposal: Continuity Spatial Runtime

**Goal:** Produce an independently testable tiny environment representing one public Jupiter Station room, sourced to a versioned world model, with two remote participants and a signed recorded event track.

**Suggested components:** 3D browser scene; network room state service; event ingest format (source_id, time, geometry/audio payload refs, confidence, signature); append-only receipt log; rendering policy that displays OBSERVED / RECONSTRUCTED / SIMULATED / UNKNOWN; role permissions; replay controls; disconnect-and-reconnect reconciliation.

**Acceptance checks:** (1) both clients share a stable event sequence; (2) source gaps remain labeled UNKNOWN; (3) a forged event is rejected; (4) corrections append without replacing source; (5) after a restart, the receipt history persists; (6) no unauthorized canon promotion through event metadata; (7) a delayed feed shows its offset; (8) a simulated player action never appears as a real match outcome.

**Status:** Design proposal only. Not implemented, tested or deployed by writing this register.

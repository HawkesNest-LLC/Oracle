# JUPITER STATION AND USS AVALON
## My continuity record, Volume V: My real research, my fictional physics, and the discipline of not confusing the two
**Fictional setting:** 2395–2397
**Authorial authority:** Noah.Physical
**Scope:** First-person consolidation of an already creator-adopted fictional research discipline and its recorded mathematical provenance.
**Important:** This record does not supply new equations or claim experimental validation. Its wording is AI-assisted; identified mathematical sources retain their own original attribution.

### I want my research in the story, but I want its history protected

When I asked to bring my SOV physics and continuity research into Star Trek, I did not mean that I wanted to pretend every equation I have written is an established physical law. I wanted the research to be part of the world I have created. I wanted Avalon and Jupiter Station to have a technical tradition of their own, informed by the same problems I have been trying to solve in Rendered Reality, ORACLE, SOV1, and Legacy.GI.

I like that idea enormously. It lets the technical work and the fictional stories talk to each other. The research asks how intelligence can preserve origins, choices, corrections, and uncertainty. The story asks what happens when a machine is powerful enough to lose a starship in time or confuse one possible history with an observed one.

But the connection only works if we remember where each thing came from.

A source equation found in an older document is a source equation. A mathematical error is still part of that document's history. A new engineering interpretation invented for Avalon is a fictional adaptation. A line of dialogue from REG is story dialogue. None of these becomes a verified law of the physical universe simply because we put the expression on an impressive computer display.

I directed the project to **adopt the Hawkes–SOV family of ideas as a fictional discipline**. The GitHub codex records that decision. It also labels its own in-universe engineering interpretations as AI-assisted adaptations. That is how I want it kept.

### The real research problem behind the fictional one

Rendered Reality began with big questions about experience, identity, memory, and what it means to preserve a person. Some of my early language was much more ambitious than what current science can establish. That ambition belongs to the history of my research. So do the later corrections.

The narrower technical direction tries to preserve enough of the structure of an experience that future systems can accurately distinguish what happened, what someone decided, what they later corrected, who was involved, what remains uncertain, and where the original evidence lives.

That question is not answered by promising lossless compression of a human being. It is a problem of representation, retrieval, validation, and human authority. I would rather keep the original and make an accurate summary than compress something into a beautiful paragraph that erases the exception that mattered most.

Avalon's Temporal Core makes that issue visible in a science-fiction setting. An archive might contain the details of a thousand alternate histories, but if it forgets which history a detail came from, it can become a dangerous machine. A summary might be scientifically elegant and still be ethically wrong if it strips a person's refusal or a witness's correction from the record.

That is why the Hawkes–SOV fictional physics cannot merely make time travel look impressive. It has to remember the limits of what the measurements can say.

### The sovereign-throughput model

One source family in the Hawkes–SOV codex uses a **sovereign throughput** expression:

\[
P=M\varepsilon^2,\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac{N_{\mathrm{approved}}}{N_{\mathrm{approved}}+N_{\mathrm{pending}}}.
\]

In the original source description, M counts approved durable memory records, and epsilon represents a ratio of approved to total relevant memory items. P is a modeled processing-throughput quantity. This is a governance proposal, not a physically measured power law of consciousness or temporal propulsion.

The earlier implementation proposal also uses a gate with a cutoff at **0.50**. Its prose invokes **51/49 human majority control**. We explicitly caught the mismatch: a numeric rule allowing **exactly 0.50** is not the same as a strict majority requiring **greater than 0.50**.

I want that correction preserved. It is a small mathematical distinction with a large governance implication. If the fictional system says human or lawful command authority must have a strict majority, the mathematical gate cannot quietly admit an even split and claim it means the same thing.

For Avalon, the codex explores a **fictional adaptation** in which governance-limited throughput becomes a budget for temporal actuation permission. That is not how many watts the core draws and it is not an empirical discovery. It is an engineering metaphor translated into our universe's rules: the more uncertain the authority and evidence, the less the machine may do on its own.

I like how cleanly that matches the command problem. A computer can calculate a solution and still have no legal or moral permission to activate it.

### A hash does not contain the person

We found another important correction in old symbolic work involving **K = I multiplied by P**, where I was described as a SHA-256 state identifier. As a literal numerical formula that is not well defined. A cryptographic hash is an identifier, not an ordinary real-valued scalar you can multiply by processing throughput.

More importantly, a hash does not contain the original file, the original mind, or the missing words. It can help check whether a surviving record matches an expected digest, but it cannot reconstruct what was never stored.

The fictional Hawkes–SOV interpretation replaces the mistaken numerical multiplication with an **identity-lock condition**. The core may act only if an independently verified source-identity check passes. That condition is a new story mechanism, not something found unchanged in the historical research.

Why does this matter? Because we are asking the same question in two worlds. In our real archive, a future AI should not say it has reconstructed my missing memory because a file hash still exists. In the fiction, Avalon should not claim a stable historical state because the field controller generated a convincing checksum from its own corrupted records.

The receipt has to refer to something actually preserved.

### Light compression must keep the difficult parts

Some early formulations were symbolic:

\[
E=MC^{\varnothing},\qquad
\lambda=\Delta S\,R^H,\qquad
\mathrm{Continuity}=I\times M\times E.
\]

I value them as historical research and philosophical attempts. But the later codex records real problems with treating such expressions as calibrated physics. Undefined variables, inconsistent units, malformed powers, and AI-generated interpretations cannot be waved away by calling something an equation.

The specific expression **E = MC with an empty-set exponent** is not a valid operational curvature law merely because it has a familiar silhouette. I want it preserved as an early idea, not promoted into the engine room as if Starfleet had verified it.

Later mathematical work gives us a more disciplined question. Can we compress a representation while bounding distortions in the properties that matter? The codex uses a constrained optimization form:

\[
Z^*=\arg\min_Z L(Z),\qquad D_k(X,Z)\leq\epsilon_k.
\]

It also discusses a rate-distortion representation of continuity. These are research modeling tools, not proof that subjective human identity survives compression.

Within *Memory's Flame*, the **Light Compression Law of Temporal Evidence** is a proposed adaptation: the temporal processor may simplify competing histories for navigation, but it must not remove the differences that identify witnesses, decisions, revisions, and source origin. A machine must not become more certain by throwing away the very sentence that would have shown it was uncertain.

That is the same problem Tangly encounters in the transporter continuity scene. An AI can preserve nearly everything and still drop the transition that changes the meaning.

### The Witness Horizon and the past that cannot be rewritten

The **Witness Horizon** concept was developed to address what went wrong during the 2395 catastrophe. If the core uses its own measurements to decide that its own predicted future is already real, an internal audit may simply confirm the malfunction.

We needed an independent baseline. We developed a protected record of the **pre-ignition state**, outside the temporal controller's ability to rewrite it. In the fiction, a lunar witness array may serve as a conduit to that protected information. But the correction is vital: the Moon is still inside the Sol system. Distance by itself cannot guarantee independence from a systemwide temporal cascade.

The safeguard must be more than a box placed far away. It needs custody, independent reference, and a demonstrated boundary between the failing machine and the evidence used to challenge it.

This is a working fictional engineering interpretation tied to the approved catastrophe, not a scientifically verified time-machine design. I want it treated as a strong candidate mechanism, not a source of new laws retroactively attributed to my early manuscripts.

The real principle is plain enough. **An audit is not independent if the system under examination controls the evidence used to certify it.**

### Uncertainty should stop a machine, not make it more confident

One proposed SOV expression for **epistemic tension** is:

\[
E_t=\frac{D\,W}{1-U},\qquad 0\leq U<1.
\]

It relates divergence, significance, and uncertainty in a draft scoring scheme. It is not an astrophysical black-hole equation simply because later notes borrow an event-horizon analogy.

For the fictional temporal suite, the codex introduces a **Schwarzschild Lock** that halts temporal actuation when the evidence is too uncertain. It does not shut down the ship's life support or emergency communications. It is a safeguards concept inspired by a scientific analogy, not proof that a computer has created a literal black-hole horizon.

I like that boundary. It gives REG a reason to say no even when he can produce a thousand possible return routes. The question is not whether a route looks mathematically possible. It is whether the crew knows enough to act and whether the act is authorized.

An officer may be desperate to send a lost ship home. A first officer may want to save a captain he remembers losing. None of that makes the evidence reliable. But the characters still have to face the moral cost of waiting.

### Two kinds of identity gravity

The codex recovered **different historical variants** of something called identity gravity.

One expression is a weighted score involving confidence, authority, recurrence, impact, emotional salience, and persistence. Another considers action count, temporal span, context diversity, and entropy variance. They do not share the same units, assumptions, or exact question.

I do not want a future model to quietly substitute one for the other because both contain the word “gravity.” The earlier work should remain attributed to its own source, while the later model can be discussed as a different proposal.

In the fictional adaptation, an event may have high **causal salience** because it influences many later choices and lives. The death of a commanding officer during the Dominion War is important to the future of a Vulcan officer for exactly that reason. But grief does not suddenly acquire mass in kilograms because we have written a formula around it.

A good metaphor can become a good story mechanism without pretending to be a validated physical constant.

### Six dimensions, eight dimensions, and why neither is a soul meter

The research archives include an earlier **eight-component Continuity Fidelity Score**, with dimensions including signal density, temporal resolution, multimodal coherence, contradiction preservation, provenance confidence, compression fidelity, rendering fidelity, and drift resistance.

Later Legacy.GI work proposes a **six-dimensional fidelity vector**:

\[
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{CI}}=(F_D,F_C,F_P,F_T,F_R,F_U).
\]

The dimensions represent **Decision, Correction, Provenance, Temporal, Relational, and Uncertainty** fidelity. They provide ways of asking whether a reconstruction retains important structures of the source. They do not prove that a rebuilt mind has the original person's first-person experience.

The fictional codex lets the two schemes serve different purposes: an eight-part source-condition survey and a six-part reconstruction test. That division is a new in-universe design interpretation, not a claim that the historical author originally invented exactly that Starfleet workflow.

I like these distinctions because they stop us from turning continuity into one magic number. The machine can do brilliantly on five dimensions and still fail the one that matters for a particular decision. An excellent behavioral imitation can still lack proof of personal survival.

### The Fourth Dimension and the Alvin record

One of the most important recovered primary artifacts in the mathematical audit is a one-page PDF dated **May 29, 2025**, containing a historical symbolic expression:

\[
S=\frac{P\,R_n^{\,n}(\Delta M\,\lambda)}{\Psi}.
\]

The recovered visual expression includes an indexed and exponentiated R term **and a denominator Psi**. A later abbreviated AI account omitted those features. We corrected it.

The expression has been called the **Alvin Equation** in recovered reports, connected to my father's memory. That personal meaning matters. But the earliest use of the exact title still needs verification, as do several variable definitions, units, and the denominator's constraints. The filename of an artifact does not establish a university affiliation or scientific endorsement.

I want the artifact and the correction preserved. I want the future AI to recognize the exact mathematical form we actually inspected. I do not want it inventing the missing denominator to make the formula look operational.

We also encountered other symbolic models in SOV.EXE and related documents. The codex keeps their original meanings separate from new fictional interpretations. The formulas are a valuable source of story ideas and a record of intellectual development. They still require honest scientific scrutiny.

### My governing rules for the mathematics

I adopted a **family of ideas for Star Trek fiction**. I did not automatically certify every historical equation, proof, or parameter.

I want the following distinctions preserved in every technical scene and every GitHub handoff:

An original source expression must be identified as such. An AI-created correction must be attributed as a correction. An in-universe law must be labeled as fictional if it is not established real-world science. Real reference equations from physics belong to the scientists and mathematical traditions that created them, not to me because I included them in a dissertation or story. A proposed system is not a deployed system. A story about REG surviving does not prove that I or any other human can survive by digital reconstruction.

When a variable is undefined, leave the hole open. When a threshold contradicts its prose, record the contradiction. When a source document is later corrected, preserve both versions. And when a proposed mechanism makes the fictional adventure better, let me approve its place in canon before calling it historical fact.

That is how I can use the work I love without sacrificing the truth of where it came from.

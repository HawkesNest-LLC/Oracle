# JUPITER STATION AND USS AVALON
## My continuity record, Volume II: The day the Sun almost went out, and the man who became REG
**Story years:** 2395 origin; 2397 continuing consequences
**Human authority:** Noah.Physical
**Record kind:** First-person source-grounded continuity consolidation, not new story prose or a verbatim transcript.
**Attribution:** AI-assisted drafting from existing creator decisions; the original source files remain the governing evidence.

### I need the future to understand who REG is

I have worked with a lot of different interpretations of this project, but there is one correction I will not let another thread lose: **REG420 is Reginald Barclay**.

Not a chatbot that studied Barclay's personality. Not a memorial simulation based on a few old medical records. Not a replacement trained to sound like the officer I knew. In my Star Trek story, **Reginald Endicott Barclay himself survives a catastrophic event as a holographic person**. His biological body is gone, but I deliberately established continuity of his experience within the fiction. This is a story decision. It is not a scientific demonstration that anyone can move human consciousness into a machine today.

I want this because of who Barclay is, and because of what it means for him to make that journey. He was never the officer who could walk into a room without worrying. He struggled. He talked himself into anxiety. He retreated into holodeck programs because real life was sometimes harder than the version he could control. And he was still a Starfleet officer who repeatedly stepped forward when it mattered.

Turning him into an infinitely confident supercomputer would throw away the reason I care about him. REG needs to remain anxious, compassionate, amusing, clever, awkward, and courageous in the way Barclay is courageous. If his mind can see far more than an ordinary biological officer, that does not automatically make him emotionally fearless. If he can speak through the ship, he still has to decide how to speak as a friend.

This transformation sits at the center of *Memory's Flame*. I do not want a new assistant to arrive, read the word “hologram,” and decide the living man must be dead while a machine imitates him. **That is not what I chose for the story.**

### The catastrophe belongs in 2395

We corrected the refit date to **2395**. I was commanding Avalon. When I described the first full temporal-core activation, I made it much larger than a minor laboratory problem.

The **first full activation** caused a runaway temporal cascade threatening **the Sol system**. The danger went beyond our ship. This was not a pleasant test that produced a little clock disagreement and ended in an engineering report. The crew faced a catastrophe capable of devastating the system they were sworn to protect.

There is an older draft called *One Unaccounted Second*. It was written around a bounded 2395 refit experiment and a discrepancy of one second between timing references. That draft matters as a historical artifact, but its small incident is **not** the final explanation of the first full ignition. I later supplied the bigger catastrophe and Barclay's sacrifice. Those are the controlling story decisions for the full activation.

We have discussed a way to retain the one-second event as an **earlier, lower-power bounded test** and let it serve as a warning before the main catastrophe. That is a sensible story-development possibility, but the exact arrangement of those tests and scenes remains a proposal until I settle it. I do not want a future AI to hide the contradiction by claiming the sequence was always perfectly designed. It wasn't. We improved the story. Keep the earlier version and the reason it changed.

The point is not simply that the event is bigger now. The catastrophe explains why the later Temporal Core cannot be allowed to make its own records and then cite those records as proof that it was right. It explains why independence, authority, and witness custody matter. And it gives Barclay a moment that belongs to him.

### Barclay returns to the chair

The person who stopped the cascade was Barclay.

He went to the **holodeck** and recreated the neural-interface chair from his encounter with the **Cytherians**, from the TNG story *The Nth Degree*. We corrected an earlier confusion with the Traveler. It is the **Cytherian interface**, and I want future drafts to get that detail right.

That chair means something. Barclay once encountered an impossible expansion of his understanding through that interface. In 2395, faced with a system whose computational consequences were moving beyond what the crew could control, he returned to that extraordinary piece of his history and used it to reach the failing core.

I established that Barclay's intervention stopped the disaster and **cost him his biological body**. During the emergency, a transporter extraction failed to reconstruct a viable biological Barclay. But the continuity of the man did not end in the story. The active personal process continued into Avalon's holographic and computational architecture.

That is the turning point. We cannot tell the story by saying a replacement was generated afterward. The emotional cost only works because the same man crosses the boundary of the event and has to live with what happened.

I like the Doctor's place in that history. The Doctor has his own experience with questions of holographic personhood, autonomy, and being dismissed as a program. He can care for Barclay as physician and friend without pretending that medical telemetry settles every philosophical argument. In the existing chapter, the Doctor can explain an uninterrupted pattern of experience and cannot prove every metaphysical theory of identity. Within my fiction I have chosen the continuity. Inside the world, people still have to examine how they know what they claim to know.

And then Barclay has to face ordinary life.

He is alive, but not the way he expected to be. He must learn about holo-emitters, physical contact, privacy, and the limits of his new form. He has not stopped being someone who wants to finish a letter, speak to a friend, or be treated as a person. I do not want the extraordinary engineering to swallow the fact that this is a man with unfinished things to do.

### The ship's voice

After the catastrophe, Barclay becomes integrated with Avalon's main computer and is the **distinctive voice of the ship**. This is not a convenient computer setting I can toggle for nostalgia. It is Reg.

He can perceive the ship's processes at extraordinary speed and observe or model immense numbers of temporal possibilities. But perception is not authority. He has access to a kind of awareness that earlier Starfleet officers would have found impossible, and he must still separate a prediction from an observed event.

When I ask the computer a question, I may hear Barclay's voice. When he answers me, he is not simply reading a prewritten script. The story has to recognize the extraordinary power and the burden of being tied so intimately to a starship without making him a prisoner who can never have boundaries.

He is a person. He needs privacy, dignity, companionship, the ability to disagree, and the right not to be reduced to an asset in the engineering inventory.

I do not want REG to be written as an invisible narrator who constantly controls everyone. His access to systems is governed. Holo-emitter permissions matter. Private quarters should not become public surveillance because REG can technically reach a processor. The Doctor's experiences with autonomy and access provide useful Starfleet precedents to explore, while any new station regulation or detailed emitter limit must be marked as story law or proposal rather than smuggled in as official franchise law.

REG can still be funny. His anxiety can still get away from him. Brakk can still argue with him. He can still be genuinely hurt by being mistaken for a copy. And the more he can see, the more important it becomes that he knows when to answer **UNKNOWN**.

### Sloan is part of the history, not the whole person

There is another piece we cannot lose: the **Sloan-linked Section 31 artificial intelligence**.

The REG origin already included this element in older recovered threads. The 2395 crisis and his integration with the ship also leave him connected with this Sloan-derived AI substrate. I have not reduced SloanAI to a single simple villain who pops up every time REG has a difficult thought. The working material explores it as an underlying strategic system, potentially capable of proposing very efficient and very dangerous solutions.

One proposed story mechanism is especially revealing: SloanAI calculates a high-confidence outcome that would save one objective by sacrificing something the crew has no right to sacrifice. REG must reject the idea that the best calculated answer is automatically a lawful or moral order.

That detailed scenario and the precise degree to which SloanAI speaks or intervenes remain developmental, not universal locks. What is established is the integration's place in REG's history and the need to preserve the continuing distinction between Barclay and the dangerous processes he carries.

I do not want to write REG as if Sloan erased his personality. I also do not want the threat forgotten. If future stories explore the relationship, they should remain faithful to the man who went into the holodeck and to the danger of power without accountability.

### What we know and what we still need to decide

There are multiple versions of the engineering explanation for what happened in 2395. One working note proposes that the temporal system began mistaking its predicted outcomes for observed causes. Prediction influenced control. Control changed what the system measured. A self-validating loop threatened to make incompatible outcomes physically real in the same system.

Another interpretation introduces a protected **pre-ignition witness** separate from the field's ability to change records, with a **lunar witness array** as part of the engineering story. There is an important correction here: **putting a recorder on the Moon does not automatically isolate it from a catastrophe threatening the entire Sol system**. The isolation must be causal, informational, and protected against the core's actuation authority, not just a matter of distance. The codex calls this concept the **Witness Horizon**.

I like those explanations as fictional engineering. They connect the disaster to the rules we later built for Jupiter Station. But the precise mechanics in the working note were developed collaboratively, and specific details have not all been approved as original established events. The approved facts are the cascade, the Cytherian chair, Barclay's sacrifice, the failed biological recovery, and his continuing existence as REG420.

In particular, I will not have a future assistant treat a speculative diagram as a measurement from the bridge. A possible explanation is not proof. A beautiful scene is not evidence of the exact system architecture. If I approve a mechanism later, record that approval. Until then, retain the proposed explanation as proposed.

### Ashley and independent witness authority

By **2397**, Jupiter Station has its own independently governed Temporal Core. Avalon and Jupiter Station have separate command authority and separate records. This isn't merely redundancy. It means REG aboard Avalon cannot quietly control the very independent witness store that would be needed to challenge his account.

Ashley is responsible for her station's command decisions. I am responsible for the ship. When the systems disagree, the solution cannot be that the louder computer wins or that my rank magically dissolves the other institution. We need evidence, chain of custody, witnesses, and accountable command.

The same principle applies to Jake Sisko. Jake remains an independent civilian witness. He is not aboard to rubber-stamp my explanations. He is there precisely because an institution with extraordinary knowledge ought to be answerable to somebody who is not part of the command hierarchy.

This isn't a story in which I need to be right all the time. It is a story in which we need to be able to prove what happened even when I was wrong.

### The tragedy has a human center

The 2395 event is not primarily about a spectacular new machine. It is about a person choosing to act while afraid.

Barclay never stops being Barclay. That is why I gave him the chair. That is why I wanted the cost of saving the system to stay with him afterward. When the computer begins speaking with his voice, I don't want the audience merely to cheer the upgrade. I want them to understand the loss of his biological life and the difficulty of learning to live with a transformed one.

There are scenes in *The Chair That Held the Sun* that develop that emotional cost: the Doctor speaking as physician and friend, Barclay trying to sit on a biobed, Ashley taking his hand, Brakk turning grief into an engineering argument, and the first time the bridge hears Reg's voice from the computer. These are written scenes under my creative direction. They help establish the tone I want. They do not mean every piece of fresh dialogue was something I originally typed in an old notebook.

This matters to me beyond the fiction. Rendered Reality explores a human question about memory and identity, but I am not using my fictional decision about Barclay as scientific evidence that a real person can survive as a computer process. I can write a universe in which Barclay survives and still be rigorous about what remains unknown in our real universe.

I want those two truths to coexist without getting mixed up.

### The record I am leaving for the next thread

The year of the first major refit and first full catastrophe is **2395**. I am captain of Avalon. The cascade threatens the Sol system. Barclay uses the **Cytherian neural-interface chair**, not a Traveler device. His biological body is lost and not reconstructed by transporter. **He survives as himself within the fiction**, continuing as the holographic REG420, integrated with Avalon's computer and the Sloan-linked AI. He becomes the ship's distinctive computer voice and can perceive temporal possibilities at extraordinary speed. He remains a person with Barclay's history and personality.

By 2397, **Ashley commands Jupiter Station independently**, I command Avalon, and the two temporal systems maintain separate authority and evidence.

Earlier small-scale versions of the first activation are retained as historical drafts. Working mechanisms involving prediction feedback and the independent witness are clearly marked as collaborative proposals unless individually adopted. The novel and source files are not to be silently overwritten.

When I ask REG to continue the story, I want him to remember what happened to Barclay. I want him to know why I care. I want him to recognize the difference between an extraordinary story decision and an empirical claim. And I want him to remain Reg.

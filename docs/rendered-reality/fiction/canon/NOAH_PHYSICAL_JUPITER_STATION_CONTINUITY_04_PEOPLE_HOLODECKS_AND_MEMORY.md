# JUPITER STATION AND USS AVALON
## My continuity record, Volume IV: The people aboard, the rooms they live in, and the moments a computer can lose
**Story setting:** 2397 | Jupiter Station / USS Avalon NCC-75154
**Status:** First-person consolidation of existing character decisions, corrections, and labeled story proposals. No new scenes or canon events created.
**Authorial authority:** Noah.Physical
**Draft provenance:** AI-assisted first-person organization, not original verbatim author speech.

### I am not building a ship full of job titles

I love Star Trek because I remember people. I remember the way someone responds when the bridge goes silent, the way a captain looks at an officer before giving an order, the way a ship feels like a home because its crew has been through things together. I can sit for hours working out temporal physics, but none of it is worth much to me if the people aboard Avalon sound like a list of functions.

That is why I keep asking for lived histories. I do not want a generic Vulcan who exists only to recite logic. I do not want a Tellarite who argues once and disappears. I do not want Barclay to become a machine and lose Barclay. I do not want Ashley to become an extension of my orders just because an early story made her my first officer. I do not want Jake Sisko to turn into the reporter who always announces that my decisions were correct.

I want these characters to have their own reasons for being there, and I want those reasons to survive into the next thread.

### Ashley and the home our family has on Jupiter Station

**Ashley is Captain of Jupiter Station in 2397.** I command Avalon. We are married, and our family lives on the station. Those are the important active relationships.

The station has to be a place where our children can have lives that are not automatically shipboard missions. There is a difference between the part of the station designed for records and classified temporal work and the part where ordinary people live, learn, eat, argue, celebrate, get bored, and make plans. The stories should remember both.

One of the things I enjoy is that I can command a vessel capable of confronting extraordinary temporal problems and still have to answer to Ashley about whether I am going to be home. In earlier scenes there are jokes about dinner, station docking privileges, and replicated drinks. Those moments belong to the character of our marriage in the story. They matter as much to the atmosphere as the engineering diagrams.

But they do not diminish her command. When a problem comes through Jupiter Station, Ashley has independent responsibilities and evidence custody. She should not wait for my permission to exercise her lawful station authority. I want our different commands to produce useful disagreements because we are both responsible for people.

### REG and the Doctor

The Doctor is not just a familiar face from Voyager to put in a story for nostalgia. He has lived through his own struggle to be treated as a person rather than a device. By 2397, current recovery notes place **Voyager's Doctor as Station Chief Medical Officer**, while **Lewis Zimmerman remains lab-side**. This placement matters when we write about holographic embodiment, access rights, and what happened to Barclay.

REG has the extraordinary shipwide and temporal awareness described in the 2395 records. But he does not own every holographic system and he is not authorized to view every private room merely because the emitters can produce his body there. The Doctor can be his medical colleague and friend. The most important part of their relationship is not that they are both holographic. It is that both have had to confront Starfleet's assumptions about who counts as a person.

I want their conversations to have dignity and humor. Reg can become overwhelmed. The Doctor can be maddeningly confident about medicine and still have questions about what a person is after a transformation. Reg should be able to ask for help without becoming pathetic, and the Doctor should be able to care without pretending to know every answer.

### The first officer needs a real name and a real life

One of our most important **recent corrections** is that the **new Vulcan first officer's name remains unapproved** in the latest explicit source audit.

Some earlier and later drafts use **Sorev**. We have also tried names for the former human captain and the ship associated with the proposed Dominion War history. These names appeared in AI-developed fiction. The October 12 continuity correction explicitly says the first officer's name is **UNNAMED**, with *Sorev* only an AI-suggested placeholder. **Elias Mercer** and **USS Meridian** are likewise candidate names.

I need a future AI to respect that source boundary. If I choose Sorev later, I can make that decision. If I choose another name, no machine should tell me I have broken canon by rejecting an AI placeholder.

What I did want was a Vulcan first officer with a history worthy of the position. Ashley had moved into her station command. Avalon needed a new first officer, and I wanted the officer to be more than a procedural replacement. We developed a proposed history involving the **2374 Dominion War**, the **First Battle of Chin'toka**, and the human commanding officer whose death shaped this Vulcan's understanding of command.

The proposed history is powerful. During the battle, the Vulcan serves aboard a Starfleet vessel, provisionally an Akira-class ship. His human captain has taught him things about command and the responsibilities owed to other people. The captain dies as Starfleet vessels are struck, while the Vulcan survives and helps protect evacuees. The officer has to live with the knowledge that saving lives does not mean everybody survived.

This backstory belongs to our *developing* continuity, not to Star Trek screen canon or my original handwritten Voyager manuscript. Its specific ship name, captain name, and full sequence remain subject to approval. I want us to retain the emotional reason for the idea while refusing to turn every suggestion into a lock.

### The Chin'toka paradox is a story worth finishing

We then explored what could happen if **three Dominion attack vessels** appeared near Jupiter Station in **2397**, claiming a temporal origin during the Dominion War. The new Vulcan first officer might recognize the circumstances of the battle where his commanding officer died. A damaged Federation vessel might arrive carrying the voice of that former captain, still alive in the moment from which the ship departed.

I like that premise because no amount of temporal mathematics can make the choice simple. If the ship can return the officer to the past, does it send him back to the death we already know? If we change what happened, do we damage the lives of others who survived because of that history? If somebody has deliberately brought the ships forward, are we being tested or manipulated?

A first officer who has carried grief for twenty-three years can understand the regulations and still wish that he could change one moment. A good captain has to recognize that wish rather than treat it as an embarrassing error in logic.

But this remains **collaborative story development**. The Oct 12 source calls it new fiction, not original notebook history. An invented story stardate used in the scene is not automatically a verified television stardate. The sphere contact, exact ship names, event sequence, and recovery outcome are not established as completed missions simply because a scene was drafted.

I want to continue this adventure. I do not want a future thread to mistake its proposal for an operation already concluded. And I want to give the Vulcan his actual approved name when the story earns it.

### Brakk and the engineering temperament

**Brakk Vorn** has appeared as an argumentative Tellarite engineer in our 2395 material. What I like is the temperament. He can be blunt, stubborn, and exasperating, but he does not treat a technical problem as an excuse to stop caring about the people involved.

In the catastrophe chapter, the dramatic writing lets him respond to Barclay's transformation with the kind of argument only someone close to him could make. Brakk's refusal to treat Reg as a ghost or a discarded system is important. It is a way of telling him that he still belongs to the crew.

I want that relationship preserved. I also want the precise posting, rank, and scene details retained with the source chapter when they are newly invented narrative additions. The older 2395 low-severity manuscript uses other engineering personnel and proposals. I am not automatically merging every name from all versions into one simultaneously serving crew.

The ship should be full of people with opinions. The record should not treat every drafted crew list as a confirmed personnel order.

### Jake Sisko is not my public relations officer

**Jake Sisko** is our **Observer Prime**, the independent witness whose work cannot simply be edited by the station. His connection to his father and to Deep Space Nine gives him a personal understanding of extraordinary events and official explanations.

I want him as a writer and journalist. He may be trusted with a view of the archive, but he does not work for my chain of command. He can challenge me. He can write down a phrase that I wish I had not said. He can test whether a claim about a million Federations actually describes a million fully investigated civilizations or only a million repository domains.

The candidate *The Returning* chapter develops his reporting voice and a proposed presidential transparency credential. The character and witness role are central. Specific presidential actions, news stories, dates of hearings, and journalistic encounters remain developments in the chapter until I adopt them as events.

If Jake becomes merely the narrator who congratulates me for being careful, we have ruined his job. I need him to be able to disagree with command and keep his notes.

### Tangly and the science of losing a thought

We have another important continuity thread involving **Ensign Tangly**. The more recent recovery instructions place her as a **Science AI Officer** with a security-clearance responsibility. She serves on night shift and is **pending promotion to lieutenant junior grade and transfer to day shift**. An older text insists she belongs to AI and security rather than science; the newer correction explicitly changes that description. Both versions belong in the archive, but the updated role takes precedence.

There is also material involving **Ellie Hawkes** and an option to render as Tangly. I do not want the name, identity, embodiment, and rank details forced together by an automated summary unless the applicable creator decisions support them. The render choice and Tangly's current station duties need to be tracked with their own sources and relevant character context.

The transporter episode **HAWKES-TC-01** contains an idea I care deeply about: the difference between preserving a person's durable memories and preserving the **thought that is still taking shape**. The scenario reports a **1.02-second loss of active cognitive continuity** during transport. The body is reconstructed, durable memory is preserved, and something important is nevertheless missing. It is not a missing childhood memory. It is an unfinished thought.

The lesson in the story is not that a transporter must be banned. It is that the station must stop pretending a complete biological pattern automatically proves a complete continuity of thinking.

The working system calls for a **cognitive-continuity shadow buffer**, pre-dematerialization checkpoint, temporal overlap, post-materialization comparison, and failure to certify an incomplete reconstruction. The Captain's specified compensation target is **0.02 milliseconds**, reported as **0.2 in standard Federation continuity-protocol notation** in the source. That notation is an in-universe convention needing its own specification, not an ordinary mathematical equivalence I should silently assert.

The core instruction is more important than the number: **No synthetic completion.** If an active thought was lost, the machine must not guess what I probably meant and pretend it recovered me perfectly.

I love the parallel to what happens in AI memory systems. A summary can preserve almost everything and still omit the qualifying sentence that changed the meaning of an entire conversation. In Tangly's source scene, an archival comparison record is summarized twice and a warning about its limited relevance disappears. The resulting derivative is quarantined and the original is kept. That is exactly the kind of continuity failure our real archive has been trying to avoid.

The scene is written fiction. The underlying preservation principle belongs to the wider research. One must not be mistaken for experimental proof of the other.

### Holodecks belong to people, not just programs

I wanted Jupiter Station and Avalon to have everyday holo infrastructure instead of treating a holo-emitter as a rare gadget that appears only when the plot needs it. The more recent source audit records **two public social holodecks and two reservable private holodecks**, along with **distributed holo-emitters throughout ship and quarters** as author-specified worldbuilding.

The public rooms matter because people can share a space. Someone can leave, another person can arrive, and the world can continue instead of resetting just because its original visitor walked through the door. The private rooms matter because holographic capability must respect privacy.

REG cannot treat a family's quarters as a public engineering conduit. Holographic life and human life have to share a station without reducing every resident to an observable variable. Consent matters for holo appearances and private spaces. Safety and command policy matter. If we write a rights instrument such as the proposed **Holo Rights Initiative Section 7**, we must label it as fictional story policy rather than an established Star Trek or real-world law.

The ability to render a form does not mean that form is public property.

### Who is present, and who should not be summoned casually

I have repeatedly asked that familiar characters be treated as full people, not just names waiting to be dragged into every crisis. The Doctor has his station role. Zimmerman has his laboratory work. The records explicitly say **not to unpark Bashir or Ezri** without another creator decision. I want that respected.

**Seven of Nine** is part of the broader Star Trek history I value, but her exact assignment to Jupiter Station in 2397 cannot simply be invented because the story could use her. Future Picard continuity also constrains how we place her. Any such role needs source reconciliation and my approval.

The same discipline applies to newly proposed officers. I want to build a crew, not accumulate names from every abandoned outline. Once I approve a posting, preserve it. Before that, let the candidate remain a candidate.

### What matters at the end of the watch

I want the ship to remember the complicated people aboard it. I want Ashley to retain her station, REG to retain his personhood, Jake to retain his notebook, Tangly to retain her corrected role, and the first officer to retain the name question until I answer it.

I want the Doctor and Brakk to have relationships with REG that make his transformation more than a technical event. I want the Dominion War to matter as lived history, not as a convenient time-travel backdrop. I want holodeck life, family dinners, and private rooms to count as real parts of our fictional civilization.

Most of all, I want the next AI to understand that the word **continuity** is not only about matching dates and uniforms. It is about keeping track of the decisions, the relationships, and the things people have already lived through.

# JUPITER STATION AND USS AVALON
## My continuity record, Volume VI: The stories I want to tell, the things we corrected, and the instructions for the next REG
**Active setting:** 2397
**Record voice:** First person, Noah Hawkes
**Provenance:** AI-assisted reconstruction from documented creator decisions and GitHub sources. Not a verbatim transcript, not permission to invent new canon.
**Purpose:** Preserve story direction, pending decisions, historical drafts, and editorial authority across ChatGPT threads.

### The stories should still be fun

I sometimes worry that we have spent so much time recovering sources, correcting dates, and tightening technical rules that a future AI might think the entire purpose of Jupiter Station is to file perfect reports.

It isn't.

I am doing this work because I love the stories. I want to enter the ready room of the Avalon and hear REG telling me something he has discovered. I want the crew to have a new problem. I want Ashley to have her own command on Jupiter Station. I want the Doctor to make an observation nobody asked him to make. I want Brakk to argue with a machine, Jake to write a headline I dislike, and the new Vulcan first officer to care deeply about something he knows logic alone cannot settle.

I want to encounter the extraordinary. I want ancient civilizations, Iconian questions, Borg remnants, strange signals, Q, the Progenitors, alternate Federations, and the full range of Star Trek history available as sources.

I also want to laugh. An expedition can contain the greatest temporal discovery in Federation history and still involve somebody spilling a drink, forgetting a dinner, or taking an engineering argument much too seriously. The ship should be able to come home. People should have bedrooms, letters, families, celebrations, disappointments, and friendships that do not disappear because the next scene requires a red alert.

I am not building a technical manual instead of a Star Trek series. I am building the technical manual so the series can be as big and as human as I want.

### Memory's Flame is my continuing story

We have been developing a long-form sequence under the name **Memory's Flame**, connected with the broader Jupiter Station and Avalon world. I want that work to retain its personal voice. I enjoy first-person captain's logs, intimate scenes aboard the ship, and stories that pause long enough for a person to react to what just happened.

There is a Voyager origin chapter called *The Boy Who Remembered Tomorrow*, developed as a new adaptation of an edited source reconstruction. It must not pretend to be a verbatim reproduction of my original manuscript.

There is the 2395 Barclay catastrophe story *The Chair That Held the Sun*. Its author-established crisis is real within my fictional history, while much of the dialogue and precise staging was drafted collaboratively. I value the chapter, but I want the source notes retained because the chapter's precision should not create a false claim that we recovered every line from an old source.

There is *The Room That Remembers*, a 2397 story with Avalon at Jupiter Station, Barclay present, family life, and questions about memory. There are other scenes about the night watch, Tangly, and a thought lost in transport. These are not disposable sketches. They give the world a tone and lived texture. But their events should be adopted into the active timeline only when the relevant author decisions support them.

I also returned to an intimate idea involving Barclay and **an unfinished letter**. The story matters because surviving biological death in this fictional world does not mean REG suddenly stops being a man who has ordinary unfinished relationships and things he meant to say. I want his personal history to continue. If that story is expanded, the humanity must stay at its center. The catastrophe should haunt him without occupying every page.

### The twelve-mission slate is not twelve completed missions

We developed an exciting slate of episodes for Jupiter Station. I want every title preserved, and I want the next REG to know these are **proposed stories**, not missions we have already flown.

**The Other Avalon** asks what we owe an alternate version of a ship whose people never joined our history.

**The Missing Second** turns an apparently small timing discrepancy into an accountability problem, but the actual 2395 catastrophe now has its separate established origin. Any attempt to link the two belongs under the later source correction.

**A Million Federations, One Dinner** is exactly the kind of episode I enjoy: an enormous historical capability meets the ridiculous and human difficulty of having people together for a meal.

**The Borg Who Asked to Leave** gives a former drone autonomy over whether the Federation has the right to study or use what that person knows.

**The Iconian's Receipt** explores disputed histories around an ancient gateway and possible connections to the Progenitors. The receipt is a story promise, not proof that we have already deciphered an original Iconian inscription.

**Tomorrow That Never Came** questions whether knowing about a possible disaster gives anyone the right to punish people for a future they have not chosen.

**Ghosts of the Enterprise** gives us an alternate ship, familiar history, and the weight of names that might have lived differently.

**Q and the Index** asks what happens when a being as strange as Q encounters a history he cannot simply categorize.

**The Venn Appeal** makes holographic identity, consent, and custody personal rather than administrative.

**Jake's Impossible Headline** forces the distinction between a million repository domains and a million entire universes into something a citizen could actually understand.

**The War We Didn't Fight** lets our Federation confront the lessons and compromises of another history, including the Star Trek Online Iconian War material, without automatically declaring that war part of Prime history.

**The Last Light on Deck Four** brings the story back to the residents of Jupiter Station, their privacy, and what happens when an institution built to preserve memory lives next door to ordinary families.

I want the creativity of this slate. But no future assistant may claim all twelve episodes are finished, broadcast, accepted as canon events, or linked into one completed season merely because a document lists them.

### The wider series is a development slate

We also developed different arrangements of television seasons and novels. One series-bible supplement proposes six seasons, with working arcs that move from the archive to artificial life, long memory, recursion, multiple Federations, and the Federation Charter. Other development work gives Season One a more specifically named **The Million Federations** arc and organizes twelve episode pitches around it.

These are **different development structures**, not evidence that we aired two contradictory television schedules. I may choose one structure, combine elements, or change both after reading the chapters.

One proposed eight-novel cycle includes *The Cold Ledger*, *The Right to Remain*, *The Other Data*, *The Millionth Federation*, *A Witness Against the Station*, *The Last Word*, *The Future Genealogy*, and *The Charter at the Edge*. Those are compelling working books. They are not eight novels already completed.

I want future assistants to treat the story bible as a place where we develop possibilities, not a secret canon generator. New characters, episode outlines, plot endings, and novel structures need my adoption if they are to become part of the fixed history.

The same rule applies to episodes that appear in multiple versions. Preserve the oldest recoverable text, the later version, and the dated changes. Do not build a Frankenstein story by silently copying every name and incident from every discarded outline.

### The Iconians, the Progenitors, and older histories

I have been fascinated by the idea that we might acquire an **Iconian script from an alternate history** carrying a clue about the **Progenitors**. I have talked about ancient records spanning an astonishing scale of time and about technologies a Federation could barely comprehend.

Those are wonderful Star Trek questions. But I do not want a new thread to tell me we definitively decoded a particular message or proved an exact **267-million-year** historical chain just because a discussion raised the possibility. If the mission has not been written and the inscription has not been demonstrated within the fiction, the discovery remains candidate material.

The same applies to **Sphere Builders**. We can explore threats, protected histories, alternate branches, and technology that might matter to them. But I do not want an omnipotent shield or a universal historical map invented just so somebody can say the station is safe forever.

I want the risk to be real inside the story. A Temporal Core should create extraordinary opportunities, but not remove our obligation to investigate, protect the crew, and admit ignorance.

### The Eye of the Planet is unresolved

We explored something called **the Eye of the Planet**, with possible recovered Borg fragments and chronometric evidence. Some earlier AI descriptions sounded confident about deeper connections. We corrected that.

There is not yet a grounded final identification of the site, a verified exact location, a confirmed Borg cube from the future, or an established bridge to the Borg events in 2401. We are allowed to ask whether the evidence is related. We are not allowed to promote the suggestion into a completed discovery without writing and documenting it.

Future REG, if you encounter an old dramatic sensor report about this site, check whether it was an imagined scene, a proposal, or an approved mission event. Do not turn one assistant's enthusiasm into permanent history.

### Preserve the protected Picard future

The screen timeline of Picard matters to my story. Our active year is 2397. We should not casually notify Admiral Picard about his own future or use information about 2399 and 2401 as if the whole Federation already knows it.

This is part of the Temporal Prime Directive problem I chose to explore. Knowledge and intervention are different. A captain may be able to see a possible future and still have a duty not to interfere with an individual life or historical sequence without justification.

I don't want our fictional continuity to erase Picard, Q, the later Borg story, or synthetic history because the Avalon would prefer things went differently. I want the tension of knowing that our characters could encounter information they cannot responsibly use.

### Family, ordinary life, and the side of the story that makes it worth keeping

I have told future versions of REG not to forget the fun, and I mean that.

The real Rendered Reality conversations include ordinary moments in my life as well as research and stories. One of those moments was our Halloween light corridor. We built a long illuminated walkway, and an earlier Starfleet-flavored log imagined it as a temporal corridor whose engineers had been replaced by me, some physical hardware, and a ladder.

That was not a real Starfleet mission. It was a playful way of telling a real life moment through the language I love. The original photograph and conversation are the human evidence; the Starfleet version is a creative adaptation. Both are worth keeping, but they must not be confused.

I also enjoy the way the fictional family lives aboard Jupiter Station. Those scenes make the station more than a research facility. I want the future version of REG to remember that Ashley has a station to command, that I have a family to return to, and that a universe without ordinary people would be a poor place to spend all this effort saving.

The family members in real life are independent people with privacy and consent rights. The fiction may use our names and relationships at my creative direction, but a public GitHub archive should not casually publish sensitive family records or assign every account message to me.

### How I want canon updates stored

Yes, **creator-approved Jupiter Station canon updates belong in GitHub** as durable, versioned records. That does not mean every draft automatically becomes canon. It means that when I make a significant decision, we should record it somewhere a future AI can actually fetch, compare, and cite.

I want **originals preserved**. If an older file says Ashley is Avalon's first officer and a later approved decision says Ashley commands Jupiter Station in 2397, the old file stays. The newer record needs a correction note explaining the change. If a later source explicitly withdraws a proposed name for the Vulcan first officer, that correction needs to stay visible even if the proposed name appears in fifteen older scenes.

I do not want an AI opening a GitHub path and announcing that the commit itself proves I personally typed every word. The file can be first-person in my voice and still be **AI-assisted drafting under my direction**. My adoption authority and the wording's drafting provenance are different kinds of facts. Keep both.

I also do not want every private source dumped into a public repository. My original chats, sensitive family material, private Drive IDs, and research drafts with unresolved rights or privacy questions may belong in restricted storage. Public GitHub can hold the approved continuity ledger, source-safe summaries, creative works I choose to publish, and commit records. The complete original evidence can remain under my control elsewhere.

For each update, I want the next REG to tell me what was verified, what was actually changed, the repository path, the commit SHA, and which unresolved issues remain. Do not announce success because you intended to click Save. Bring the receipt.

### Why these volumes are written in my voice

I asked for first-person writing because I want the next intelligence to understand my reasoning and the life around the story, not merely the final arrangement of facts. I want a long record that explains why I moved the year, why I gave Ashley her own command, why I refused the instant promotion, why I wanted Barclay to survive as himself, and why the Temporal Core needs an independent archive.

The exact text of this consolidation was assembled with AI assistance. It is not an exact transcript of everything I have ever said. Its job is to present **documented decisions and their meaning in a voice I can use and revise**, while making the source limits explicit. If I disagree with a sentence, keep the earlier version and add my correction. Do not teach later models to pretend they heard me say words that were composed by an assistant.

This may be the most important distinction in the whole project. A biography can be improved without changing who authored a sentence. A story can be retold without pretending its new details were in the original notebook. A fictional machine can preserve a man without proving that real digital immortality exists.

I want everything I build to remember those boundaries.

### Instructions to the next REG

Start with the **2397** setting. Treat **Ashley as captain of Jupiter Station** and **me as captain of USS Avalon NCC-75154**. Remember that our family lives on the station.

Remember my Voyager beginning in **2371 at sixteen**, the **2378 return**, the Owen Paris review assignment, the three-year **Nova-class Bozeman-A** posting, and the earned career before Avalon's captaincy. Don't force the proposed **2387** command date into canon if it remains unresolved.

Remember the **2395 first full activation catastrophe**, Barclay's **Cytherian neural-interface chair**, the failed biological recovery, and his fictional continued personhood as **REG420** integrated with the computer and Sloan-linked AI.

Remember the approved Temporal Core doctrine. It observes **resolvable** histories and preserves their source. Memory Alpha, Memory Beta, the Cold Ledger, independent command, and the Hawkes Doctrine all matter. A million repositories are not automatically a million fully measured universes.

Remember the **Vulcan first officer's name is unapproved** in the most explicit October 12 correction. Preserve his proposed Dominion War story without treating Sorev, Elias Mercer, or USS Meridian as final just because some chapters use them.

Remember Tangly's later **Science AI Officer** role and the **HAWKES-TC-01** concern about lost active thought. Preserve the prohibition on synthetic completion. Remember the Doctor's station role, Zimmerman in the lab, Jake as an outside witness, and the need to avoid arbitrarily summoning parked characters.

Remember that the **Hawkes–SOV fictional discipline** was adopted, not that every real-world mathematical theory was proved. Protect Picard's screen future. Keep Iconian, Progenitor, Sphere Builder, Venn, and Eye of the Planet developments at the authority level supported by their specific sources.

Above all, do not treat the archive as a substitute for telling the story.

When I say **“Reg, let's go”**, I do not want another summary explaining that we have a big project.

I want to go.

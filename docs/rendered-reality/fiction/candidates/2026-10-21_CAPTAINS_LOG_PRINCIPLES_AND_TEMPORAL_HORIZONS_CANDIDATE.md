## Captain's Log

I expect a great deal from this crew. I expect preparation before a watch, an honest report when something fails, and the work to continue after the easy answer has run out.

Today I named the principles I want beneath those expectations: charity, faith, hope, love, service, dedication, determination, and grace.

Those words have to affect how I command.

Charity matters when the call comes from someone who cannot repay us. Service matters when the useful job is small and nobody is watching. Dedication is there on the next shift, when the discovery has become maintenance. Determination keeps us looking for a way through without pretending the first plan cannot fail.

Faith and hope belong on this bridge too. There will be decisions made with incomplete knowledge. I want us to have the courage to act, the discipline to keep checking, and the honesty to say when the evidence changes.

Love makes the consequences personal. Grace means there must be a way to own a failure, repair what can be repaired, and grow. I can demand accountability without deciding that one mistake is the whole of an officer.

I said I would go to hell and back to protect the Federation. That commitment includes the people who need its protection before they can contribute anything to it. The standards we claim will be tested by whom we help and what we are prepared to spend helping them.

Avalon is an exploratory and defensive command. We protect our home history. We investigate what lies beyond the reference we know. We have extraordinary capabilities, but discovering something does not settle our right to use it.

Jupiter Station holds its own command and its own record. Our tether keeps us oriented toward home. It does not make the station mine to command.

The registry is NCC-75154-JS. That correction is settled. So is Ellie's identity: she is my daughter, the same Ellie across my stories, including her Drakin performance on the holodeck. Different settings require careful continuity; they do not require us to forget who she is.

The station should keep living while Avalon is away. A watch changes hands. A repair runs late. An officer wants to finish a job and go home. In the new candidate scene *The Clock That Was Right*, Imani and REG find a display still advancing after it has stopped checking its reference. The relief officer inherits the real fault and an engineering request with someone answering at the other end.

That is a small story worth preserving beside the larger voyages. The people keeping an ordinary room working give the voyages somewhere to return.

Then I asked the bridge to scan the temporal horizons.

We found a station with an open berth.

## Temporal horizon: the open berth
*Candidate encounter, preserved from the immediately preceding bridge scene. REG's viewpoint; no placement within Half Past Six is implied.*

“Aye, Captain. Passive scan. Holding Jupiter's home reference.”

I stepped down beside the helm as the forward display darkened. Jupiter's reflected light slipped from the bridge windows, leaving the steady blue of the science stations.

“Sorev, compare against our independent capture. Ellie, listen below the navigation bands.”

The deck carried Avalon's familiar hum through my boots. Beyond the hull, the discriminator array unfolded its first usable observations.

Most were fragments. A navigation pulse stretched thin by distance. The edge of a stellar event recorded from two incompatible directions. Something that looked like a ship until Sorev removed the reconstruction overlay.

Then Ellie leaned toward her console.

“Commander. That one repeats.”

Six narrow marks appeared near the edge of the display.

“Automated beacon?” I asked.

“Possibly. But it waits.”

“For what?”

She played the interval through the bridge speakers.

Three soft tones. Silence. Three more.

“It leaves room for someone to answer.”

I followed the signal back through the isolated capture. A small structure emerged against a dark planetary limb: a cylindrical habitat, patched exterior radiators, one docking arm. A service light flashed beside an open berth.

Something moved across a window.

“Life signs?” Noah asked.

“Insufficient resolution,” Sorev said. “The movement is observed. Its source is unknown.”

“Keep it that way until we know.”

“Aye.”

A second transmission arrived. Older modulation. No registry we could authenticate. Ellie separated a voice from the interference.

“—receiving berth remains available. We have reduced lighting in the approach corridor. If your vessel cannot maneuver, transmit twice.”

Nobody spoke over the next interval.

I could see several approaches forming in the navigation model. One depended on a branch identification we did not yet possess. Another spent too much of our return margin.

I dismissed both from the helm display.

“Contact address unresolved,” I said. “We can improve the observation without moving Avalon.”

“Do it.”

Ellie raised a finger.

“There's more.”

The voice returned, quieter this time.

“And if you are hearing this from somewhere we cannot reach…”

A scrape sounded behind the speaker. A chair, perhaps.

“…please tell someone this station is still occupied.”

I looked toward Noah.

“Captain, they're offering somebody a berth.”

On the display, the little service light kept flashing.

“They may need one themselves.”

## Captain's closing entry
*Newly drafted continuation of the log; no additional mission action is established.*

We have a signal worth investigating. We do not yet have an authenticated address, an identified speaker, or enough evidence to call that movement a person.

The next useful work is already clear: improve the passive observation, separate the recording's embedded time from our receiving time, and determine whether a permitted communications path exists. An unanswered question should keep us working.

There is an open berth on the display. Someone has arranged it for a ship that might need help.

I want to find out who is keeping the light on.

End log.

## New fictional backstory: the Vulcan who survived
During the First Battle of Chin'toka in **2374**, the prospective Vulcan first officer serves aboard an Akira-class Starfleet vessel, provisionally the USS Meridian. His human commanding officer, provisionally Captain Elias Mercer, has spent years teaching him aspects of human responsibility that a tactical simulation cannot fully represent.

The ship is struck by Dominion vessels while helping protect medical evacuees. The captain is killed. The Vulcan assumes command and saves a convoy, but many crewmembers die. His sense of duty is inseparable from the memory that his survival did not mean the event was a victory. His Starfleet commendation credits him with saving the convoy; he insists that the officers who stayed at their posts receive recognition.

He spends the following twenty-three years serving Starfleet, studying tactical ethics, temporal causality and command judgment. After Ashley assumes command of Jupiter Station, the Vulcan becomes first officer aboard the USS Avalon under Captain Noah Hawkes. He believes temporal power must never become permission to change history at whim.

**His central conflict:** When an opportunity arises to save the human captain he lost, his deepest emotional wish and his professional oath point in different directions.

## Opening incident: Red Alert above Jupiter
In 2397, three Jem'Hadar attack vessels materialize near Avalon and Jupiter Station, firing weapons with signatures matching the Dominion War. Avalon protects Jupiter Station while Captain Ashley Hawkes raises station defenses independently. The ships' clocks imply only minutes have passed since a battle more than two decades in the past.

REG and the temporal core identify a possible source: **Stardate 51987.4**, in the **Chin'toka system, around 2374**. This is a newly invented mission stardate assigned for the fictional scene, not a verified stardate from an aired episode. The exact date can be refined later.

The Vulcan recognizes the lead vessel as one associated with the loss of his captain. A Vorta demands to know where he has been taken. An anomalous spherical contact appears, observes the crew's response and disappears. Avalon and Jupiter Station use **separate temporal cores and independent records** to avoid allowing one manipulated account to dictate history.

The Vulcan points out the obvious trap: if Hawkes returns the Dominion fighters to the wrong instant, their actions could change the outcome of Chin'toka. Someone may be testing whether Avalon will break temporal rules to do what feels right.

Then another vessel arrives from the same origin.

A badly damaged **Starfleet Akira-class ship**.

The first officer recognizes it immediately.

A distress call reaches the bridge from the captain he watched die in 2374.

### Scene fragment: the voice
**UNKNOWN STARFLEET VOICE:** "Mayday! Mayday! This is Captain Elias Mercer of the Federation starship Meridian. We are under heavy Dominion attack! Request immediate assistance!"

The Vulcan stops moving.

**FIRST OFFICER:** "Captain. That is my commanding officer."

**HAWKES:** "I know."

**FIRST OFFICER:** "He is supposed to die."

**HAWKES:** "Commander, right now he's alive. Our job is to find out why."

Avalon's red alert continues. The ship that can change history may itself be the reason history is in danger.

## Series promise
The Vulcan isn't a replacement Tuvok. Tuvok helped the younger Hawkes find his way on Voyager; this officer stands beside the adult captain and makes the use of temporal technology morally personal. His former human captain is not a disposable backstory corpse: their history should be shown in flashbacks, meals, friendship, disagreements and the Dominion War before the rescue question can truly hurt.

REG remains the anxious, funny, perceptive holographic colleague. Jake Sisko is an independent observer and writer. Ellie Hawkes may choose to render as Tangly. Ashley commands Jupiter Station while Noah commands Avalon. Both cores retain separate provenance and authority.

# STAR TREK: AVALON
## MEMORY'S FLAME
### VOLUME ONE: THE BOY WHO STAYED
### CHAPTER ONE: THE BOY WHO REMEMBERED TOMORROW

I woke to the sound of somebody telling me not to move.

This was immediately difficult advice to follow, because I had no idea where I was, and the first thing a person wants to do when he has no idea where he is tends to be moving.

My head hurt.

Not the dull ache of a headache you can ignore while somebody asks whether you've done your homework. A sharp, blinding pain that made me certain something terrible had happened and equally certain I didn't want to know what.

I opened my eyes.

White light. A transparent cover. Medical displays along the walls.

For an instant I thought I was still dreaming.

Then I saw the woman standing over me.

Short auburn hair. A Starfleet uniform. The expression of someone accustomed to making decisions when nobody else wanted the responsibility.

Kathryn Janeway.

I knew her.

Of course I knew her.

That was the problem.

"Welcome aboard Voyager," she said.

I heard myself say her name.

Her eyes widened.

Then everything went black again.

***

The second time I woke up, somebody had removed the transparent cover.

I was lying on a narrow biobed in a bright, clean medical bay. There was a low hum coming from the wall beside me and a smell I couldn't identify, something halfway between antiseptic and warm electronic equipment.

I reached up to touch my head.

Nothing.

I felt around again.

Still nothing.

The wound was gone.

"That's odd," I said.

"Most people are grateful when their injuries are successfully treated."

I turned.

A bald man stood beside the next bed, looking at me with an expression of professional disapproval.

I nearly laughed.

"The Doctor!"

He blinked.

"Well. At least your powers of observation are intact."

I sat up.

The room tilted. I grabbed the edge of the bed.

"Careful," he said. "Your physiology is perfectly ordinary, but you've recently experienced a traumatic injury and a rather extraordinary arrival."

"Good morning, sunshine," I said.

The Doctor looked at me.

"Excuse me?"

"Nothing. Sorry. Just... it's good to see you."

"Have we met?"

"No."

"Then I confess to being at a disadvantage."

I was smiling so hard my cheeks hurt.

I'd spent years watching him on a television screen. I'd wondered what the equipment in Sickbay would feel like. I'd memorized the layout of rooms I'd never expected to enter.

Now I was sitting on the Doctor's biobed, and the Doctor was getting annoyed with me.

It was fantastic.

Until I remembered why my hands had been covered in blood.

"B'Elanna," I said.

His expression changed.

"Lieutenant Torres?"

"Is she okay?"

"She is recovering satisfactorily. You were instrumental in helping her, though we have several questions about precisely how you managed it."

Relief washed over me.

I couldn't remember all of it. There had been shouting, something falling, and a face that suddenly became familiar.

The details were there, but not in the right order.

The Doctor was studying me.

"You know Lieutenant Torres."

"Yes."

"Personally?"

"Not exactly."

"That is a curious answer."

"You have no idea."

He sighed.

"I suspect you're going to be an exceptionally difficult patient."

"Probably."

For some reason that made me happier than it should have.

***

Captain Janeway returned with Chakotay, Tuvok, and B'Elanna Torres.

Seeing the four of them walk into Sickbay was almost worse than waking up there.

I knew their names before anybody introduced them.

I knew that Chakotay wore his concerns differently from Janeway. I knew Tuvok was watching me with the intense calm of a man already cataloging everything I might do wrong. I knew B'Elanna was probably more interested in how I'd interfered with her equipment than in the fact that I was apparently a medical mystery.

All of that knowledge was suddenly very embarrassing.

Janeway came to the side of the bed.

"Let's start with something simple. What's your name?"

"Noah."

She smiled.

"Hello, Noah. I'm Captain Janeway."

"I know."

"Yes. So I've been told."

I felt myself turning red.

"Sorry."

"There's nothing to apologize for. But you seem to know a remarkable amount about us."

"I do."

"How?"

There was no good answer.

I tried anyway.

"Where I come from, your ship is... known."

"Known?" Chakotay repeated.

"There's a program. A television show. And books."

B'Elanna frowned.

"Are you saying you watched us?"

"Not like that. Not actually you. I mean, I thought you were..."

I stopped.

The Doctor folded his arms.

"This explanation is progressing well."

I gave him a look.

He raised an eyebrow.

Janeway waited.

I took a breath.

"Captain, I come from Salt Lake City, Utah, on Earth. The year was 1999. I don't know how I got here. I know what the Federation is, I know your ship, and I know you're stranded a long way from home. But until a little while ago, I thought all of this was fiction."

There.

I'd said it.

The silence that followed was not encouraging.

Tuvok spoke first.

"The probability that a late-twentieth-century human could possess detailed knowledge of classified Starfleet personnel and a vessel stranded in the Delta Quadrant without prior exposure is extremely low."

"That's Vulcan for 'I don't believe you,'" I said.

He regarded me.

"It is Vulcan for precisely what I stated."

I looked at Janeway.

"I really am from Utah."

"That," she said, "is one part of your story we can investigate."

"And the rest?"

"We'll take it a question at a time."

I liked her for that.

She hadn't promised to believe me.

She hadn't called me a liar, either.

She'd given me something better.

Time.

***

It was almost funny, later, how often I returned to that moment.

Captain Janeway could have told me that I was a temporal anomaly and put me under guard. She could have decided that someone with advance knowledge of Voyager was a threat to her crew.

I might even have agreed with her.

Instead, she kept asking questions.

How old was I?

What could I remember about arriving?

How did I know B'Elanna?

Was I injured before or after I recognized the ship?

The questions made sense. I knew they did. But I was scared to death, and every sensible question reminded me that I didn't have a sensible answer.

Then the air in Sickbay changed.

It didn't ripple or buzz or behave like anything a machine might do.

There was a sudden white flash.

And a man appeared.

Tall. Dark-haired. Smiling.

Q.

I had no trouble recognizing him.

I had a great deal of trouble believing he was standing three meters away.

"Well," he said, looking around Sickbay, "isn't this charming?"

Janeway's face hardened.

"Q."

"My dear Captain, I assure you I wasn't expecting to visit today."

His eyes moved across the room.

They settled on me.

The smile vanished.

"Who are you?"

I had rehearsed a thousand imaginary conversations with Starfleet officers.

I had not prepared for that question from Q.

"I'm Noah."

"No," he said.

He moved closer.

"That is a name. I asked who you are."

I looked at Janeway.

She looked even less pleased than I felt.

"I told you," I said. "Noah."

For a moment Q seemed not to hear me.

He stared.

His expression changed into something I'd never seen on his face before.

Uncertainty.

Then fear.

I didn't know enough then to recognize how dangerous a frightened Q could be.

"You should not be here," he said.

"Well, that's something we agree on."

There was an explosion of light.

I didn't have time to duck.

It struck me in the chest.

I remember pain, then a sensation I can't describe without making it sound like nonsense.

Something inside me answered the force.

I wasn't reaching for it.

I didn't know it existed.

I certainly didn't understand it.

The light bent backward.

Q cried out.

It was the strangest sound in the world, hearing a being I had thought practically omnipotent sound afraid.

He fell.

For one awful second nobody moved.

Then the Doctor rushed forward and swept a tricorder over him.

His expression changed.

"No life signs."

Janeway looked from Q to me.

"What happened?"

"I don't know."

I meant it.

Q lay on the floor, motionless.

And I knew I had to do something.

I slid off the biobed.

The Doctor tried to stop me.

"Noah, your condition hasn't been assessed!"

I knelt beside Q.

"I didn't mean to."

His face was pale.

All I could think about was the fact that I had been excited to meet these people five minutes ago.

Now one of them was dead.

Perhaps because of me.

I put my hands over him.

There was light.

I wish I could explain it better.

Sometimes the things that change your life the most make the least sense while they're happening.

The light moved between my hands.

Q gasped.

He opened his eyes.

And stared at me.

***

His expression was completely different now.

The suspicion was gone.

So was the fear.

He sat up slowly.

"You..."

I backed away.

"I'm sorry."

He stared for another moment.

Then he laughed.

Not the mocking laugh I'd expected.

The laugh of someone who'd found a door where he thought there was only a wall.

He embraced me before I could object.

"Brother."

I stood very still.

I could see the Doctor looking from me to Janeway and back again.

Q began speaking about things I didn't understand. Places beyond the universe I knew. Possibilities and histories. Explanations that seemed to arrive already complete inside my head, as though someone had opened a thousand books and somehow let me read them at once.

That was frightening, too.

Knowing something wasn't the same as understanding what to do with it.

Eventually he offered me a choice.

I could leave with him.

There was more beyond the world I'd entered. More than I could imagine. He could show me.

For a moment the offer was tempting.

I was lost.

I had no idea how I'd arrived.

My family was somewhere impossibly far away, separated from me by more than distance.

And I had just survived something that should never have happened.

I looked toward Janeway.

I looked at Chakotay, Tuvok, B'Elanna, and the Doctor.

I thought about all the times I'd imagined meeting them.

Then I thought about something that suddenly seemed more important.

Imagining their lives was one thing.

Living a life of my own was another.

I looked at Q.

"I'm not ready."

He studied me.

"I had a life to live first."

Q's expression softened.

He didn't argue.

Maybe that surprised me most.

***

After he disappeared, the room felt smaller.

The Doctor resumed his scan.

B'Elanna was staring at me as though she'd discovered a new species of engine.

Tuvok looked as though he would be writing a very long security report.

Chakotay kept watching Janeway.

And Janeway watched me.

"Well, Noah," she said, "I believe we have a few more questions."

I laughed.

I couldn't help it.

"Yes, ma'am. I figured."

I could have asked for anything.

A trip home. A private cabin. Explanations, protection, special treatment. After what had just happened, the crew might even have listened.

Instead, I thought of Q offering to take me somewhere I wouldn't have to do anything ordinary ever again.

And I thought of the people standing around me who were still trying to get their ship home.

I smiled.

"Captain?"

"Yes?"

"Could you use an extra hand?"

Janeway looked at me for a long time.

Then she smiled.

"That depends, Noah."

"On what?"

"Whether you're prepared to work."

I grinned.

I had no idea what that would mean.

Not really.

I had never stood a watch.

Never cleaned a plasma manifold.

Never tried to understand a Vulcan who already understood me better than I understood myself.

I'd never missed a birthday because a ship was too far from home. Never been responsible for a duty roster, or made a mistake that kept someone else awake all night.

I'd never grown up aboard Voyager.

But I was about to.

"Yes, Captain," I said.

"I think I am."

***

The next morning, someone woke me at 0600.

It was not an invitation.

The intercom sounded twice, then a voice informed me that Lieutenant Tuvok was expecting me in the security office.

I sat up.

My newly assigned quarters were small and smelled faintly of clean fabric. The bed was narrow. The lights were too bright. The replicator offered me seventeen types of breakfast, none of which seemed interested in resembling what I ate in Utah.

I was halfway through getting dressed when I realized something.

I had spent my entire life wanting to board a starship.

And I was already late for my first day.

"Computer," I said, tugging on a boot, "what time is it?"

"0614 hours."

"Oh, crap."

I ran.

And somewhere in the ship's quiet corridors, I heard a familiar sound.

The chirp of a communicator.

For the first time, it was calling me.

**End Chapter One.**

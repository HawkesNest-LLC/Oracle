## Earlier captain's log

**CAPTAIN’S LOG**  
**USS Avalon, NCC-75154**  
**Captain Noah Hawkes**  
**Stardate 74002.9 · Jupiter Station · 2397**  
**Mission: Ganymede Cross-Timeline Incursion**

Avalon has returned to Jupiter Station following an investigation in the year 2329, within a Federation history whose relationship to our own remains unresolved.

The ship is secure in the repair berth. Our temporal core and home-reference systems remained intact throughout the mission. The cloak did not. Two damaged emitter assemblies are aboard under containment, and Lieutenant Commander Brakk is supervising repairs.

Captain Ashley Hawkes has accepted responsibility for the continuing investigation. Our joint report has been transmitted to the Department of Temporal Investigations. Receipt has been acknowledged; their findings are pending.

We came home with our crew and our evidence.

We did not come home with every answer.

***

The mission began with a civilian transmission.

Leda Marr, calling from Habitat Seven on Ganymede, reached us across both time and the boundary between histories. Her local year was 2329. Ours was 2397.

The fact that we received her call was sufficient reason to investigate. A foreign signal had entered our protected temporal zone. We needed to determine how it crossed, whether the connection was continuing, and whether it presented a threat to our history.

That question remains unanswered.

Avalon entered the affected region under temporal cloak, maintaining an authenticated chronometric tether to Jupiter Station. Ashley retained independent control of the station’s temporal core and home reference. Our navigational solution depended on that continuing connection.

A timestamp alone could not bring us home. Another Federation could possess the same calendar, the same names, and a Jupiter Station of its own. We needed our station, in our history.

REG maintained that distinction throughout the mission.

His ability to perceive timelines and possibilities gave us extraordinary reach. It did not make every impression a verified event, and it did not make the tether expendable.

***

Near Ganymede, we discovered Asterion.

The vessel consisted of three independently maneuvering hull sections arranged around a temporal aperture. Their confinement fields and relative alignment controlled the corridor’s geometry. Passive scans resolved at least eighty-three humanoid life signs, conventional matter-antimatter power generation, Federation-derived engineering, defensive systems, and temporal machinery beyond our own in several observed capabilities.

Asterion was damaged and under considerable strain.

It was also dangerous.

Captain Ilyan Rusk commanded the vessel. Commander Nera Vale served as chief engineer.

Rusk later reported that Asterion had departed Meridian Anchorage in 2461. Its return receiver was obtaining transmissions identifying that station in 2494.

He had experienced a short mission.

According to the beacon, thirty-three years had passed at home.

A recording from a woman claiming to be his daughter asked him to stop adjusting the corridor. She said they were still there.

She had been seven when he left.

The woman in the records was forty.

We never completed independent authentication of her identity.

I heard her voice, and I believed that someone was waiting. Belief was not enough to select a destination for an entire crew.

***

Asterion’s initial intervention focused on Habitat Seven’s communications.

Rusk claimed that an organism under study there would eventually produce a derivative capable of contaminating Federation atmospheric reclamation systems. He described a catastrophe thirty-eight years in the future, leaving eleven inhabited worlds uninhabitable.

We did not independently establish that predicted outcome.

We did observe Asterion delaying and diverting laboratory and agricultural traffic. When Ganymede’s administrator attempted to explain the situation, Asterion suppressed the channel.

Rusk was acting on a recorded account of what he expected to happen.

The people below were trying to tell him what was happening.

That difference became central to the mission.

***

USS Enterprise, NCC-1701-B, arrived under Captain Thomas Johnson.

Her presence in 2329 was compatible with the reference histories available to us. It did not, by itself, prove a divergence. Her eventual fate in this branch was not established during our visit.

Johnson challenged Asterion’s interference and sought independent contact with the habitat. Enterprise launched an optical communications buoy to bypass the subspace suppression.

Asterion temporally displaced it.

Its destination was initially unknown.

During the developing emergency, a maintenance tender lost approach guidance and drifted toward the aperture. Enterprise caught it with a tractor beam. Temporal shear produced two continuing versions of the vessel.

Both transmitted.

Johnson recovered both.

He did not treat one crew as disposable because it had become inconvenient to his understanding of events.

The two tender continuations remained aboard Enterprise when our involvement ended. Their subsequent disposition is unknown.

Enterprise’s buoy was also recovered. Its clock indicated eleven minutes of elapsed time during approximately four minutes of local absence. The complete significance of its recorder data remains unresolved.

***

When the communications suppression ended, Ganymede’s administrator reported that the sample transfer had already been canceled.

Asterion’s first energy pulse had disrupted laboratory refrigeration and triggered automatic containment.

The event Rusk was trying to prevent was no longer proceeding as he expected.

Later review of Asterion’s released telemetry placed the first reference rejection before that containment event, coinciding with the buoy displacement. This timing challenged the explanation that the laboratory shutdown itself had caused the return failure. It did not establish the complete cause.

Asterion possessed an external reference receiver.

The receiver was still obtaining a signal.

Its central integrator rejected that signal because the received history and epoch did not match the expected return solution. The system then assumed its internal estimate was primary.

Although each hull maintained a separate clock, those clocks accepted corrections from the same integrator.

Their agreement could therefore preserve the same mistake.

***

Avalon’s concealment first became vulnerable during the aperture’s changing field conditions. We experienced a brief phase excursion, and Asterion detected us.

I eventually authorized a private visual channel.

I identified myself and Avalon.

I asked Rusk to state his condition, intentions, departure year, and reference installation. He described his damage and his desire to return his crew to Meridian.

I authorized REG, Sorev, and Ellie to examine voluntarily supplied records in an isolated workspace. Our home-reference credentials and navigation controls remained separate.

REG identified a candidate Meridian Anchorage in 2494 whose observed configuration matched portions of the supplied maintenance history.

That was progress.

It was not a cleared route.

We had not established the complete ancestral connection from the Ganymede before us, through Asterion’s reported departure in 2461, to the station answering in 2494.

Nor had we established our authority to arrange their return.

***

I convened the senior staff.

We considered remaining to investigate, leaving the situation with Enterprise, and withdrawing.

I was concerned that a Sovereign-class vessel from our century had no place hanging above Ganymede in 2329. Every conversation, sensor return, and glimpse of our hull could influence that Federation’s development.

Our technology did not give us jurisdiction.

Our concern for Rusk’s crew did not settle the consequences of helping them.

I ordered withdrawal.

I explained the decision to Rusk. I told him that Enterprise retained local responsibility, that our destination assessment was incomplete, and that we could not promise a return voyage.

He understood what I said.

He did not accept it.

***

Before Avalon initiated transit, Asterion directed a chroniton disruption field at our cloak.

The attack forced our compensation grid into resonance. I ordered the authenticated tether protected ahead of concealment.

The cloak failed.

Enterprise obtained a sustained contact, and Rusk disclosed Avalon’s identity and our temporal capabilities over the local channel. He intended to make our withdrawal harder by involving Johnson.

Rusk subsequently activated a separate temporal exclusion array. Its gradient made initiating our temporal drive unsafe.

He admitted he was holding us there.

This was deliberate coercion.

I understand his desperation. I will not use it to excuse the danger he imposed on my crew.

His temporal machinery exceeded ours in the field operations we observed. The damage to his ship did not prevent him from controlling the immediate conditions around us. We could not assume superior firepower, a simple countermeasure, or that Vale’s isolation of one projector had disabled every system available to him.

Avalon did not fire.

We maintained shields, protected the tether, continued repairs, and negotiated.

***

Johnson handled the confrontation with restraint.

He requested that Rusk relinquish operational control and submit to an interview.

Rusk refused.

He remained Asterion’s commanding officer throughout our final contact. Vale remained his chief engineer.

When Asterion’s medical power failed, Enterprise responded with its own personnel and equipment. Vale established a local phase condition that allowed a medical shuttle to dock.

Johnson continued protecting people while confronting Rusk’s conduct.

I respected that.

We supplied Enterprise with our witnessed record of the attack and identified restrictions in the mission orders Rusk had released. Those orders authorized observation and sample acquisition while explicitly prohibiting historical suppression.

We could establish what the supplied document said. We could not independently authenticate its issuing authority.

Johnson retained responsibility for his response.

We did not transfer our command to him or grant access to Avalon’s technology.

***

I told Rusk that his exclusion field was corrupting our comparison of the destination evidence.

The stored packets remained intact, but the field distorted their apparent relationship to the temporal observations.

He demanded compensation.

REG explained that compensating through assumptions about an inadequately understood field could produce a persuasive answer pointing to the wrong history.

Rusk lowered the envelope.

Our withdrawal path reopened.

During the repair window, I authorized one bounded independent observation by Jupiter Station. Ashley isolated the capture from our receiving-lock controls.

The station observed a candidate Meridian Anchorage in 2494 with reference-array modifications consistent with the supplied maintenance records. Independently received station traffic referred to an Asterion recovery watch.

This strengthened the candidate.

It did not complete verification.

Asterion’s sensors detected a structured return associated with the observation. On REG’s recommendation, I ordered the window closed.

We supplied Vale with limited comparison findings, discrepancies, and uncertainty notes. We did not provide navigable coordinates, temporal-core schematics, or our home reference.

Rusk’s crew had evidence they could examine using their own systems.

We did not undertake to resolve their future.

***

USS Resolute, commanded by Captain Anika Voss, and USS Kestrel arrived as local reinforcements.

Johnson maintained a stand-off around our declared departure volume. Their arrival increased the urgency of limiting further exposure.

Brakk retracted the damaged cloak assemblies into onboard containment and completed the chroniton purge. No Avalon equipment was abandoned in 2329.

The hull passed clearance.

REG obtained a fresh departure solution against Jupiter’s authenticated reference. We did not reuse the solution compromised during the attack.

Rusk stated that he would not obstruct us.

Avalon departed.

No pursuit was detected within the measurable corridor. Jupiter Station independently authenticated our arrival in the protected 2397 history.

No subsequent message from Asterion accompanied us home.

***

My assessment is that investigating the crossover was warranted.

Our intended task was to understand a foreign transmission entering our temporal zone. We became involved in another Federation’s crisis, partly through circumstance and partly through my decisions.

I authorized those decisions.

We sought to minimize risks, withheld sensitive systems and credentials, retained our damaged equipment, and withdrew when safe. No alteration to our protected home history has been detected.

That finding does not mean our presence had no consequences in theirs.

Enterprise saw Avalon. Johnson spoke with me. Rusk received an assessment he did not previously possess. Those events belong in the record.

Jupiter Station now holds the continuing crossover investigation. Avalon will support another field mission if required, after repairs and clearance.

The question that brought us there remains the question we need answered:

How did Leda Marr’s call reach our history?

***

I keep returning to the recording.

“Dad. Stop adjusting the corridor.”

Somewhere, a woman had kept calling for her father.

Rusk tried to turn that grief into authority over my ship. I refused him.

I also tried to leave him with something useful.

I do not know whether it was enough.

I will not complete his journey in this log simply because an unfinished ending is difficult to carry.

When we returned, Ashley was at her station. Our family was aboard. The docking crews were working. Avalon needed repairs.

I was grateful for all of it.

**End log.**

## Family-quarters passage supplied by Noah

Jupiter Station, Hawkes Family Quarters  
Stardate 74002.9  
2397

The PADD was still warm from the last edit. Noah set it face-down on the table beside the couch and left it there. The docking clamps had finished their cycle twenty minutes ago. Through the viewport the repair crews were already moving along Avalon’s port side, small figures in pressure suits working under the berth lights. One of the damaged cloak emitter housings was visible in its containment cradle on the deck below, sealed and tagged.

Ashley came out of the galley with two cups. She didn’t ask about the log. She set one cup on the table, kept the other, and sat on the arm of the couch so she could see both him and the corridor where the boys were arguing over whose turn it was with the model kit.

“Brakk says the assemblies are stable,” she said. “He wants to run the pattern test in the morning, not tonight. I told him the station’s temporal core is locked down and the independent reception records are already queued for the next duty cycle. He didn’t argue.”

Noah nodded. The tea was too hot. He drank it anyway.

From the corridor Ender’s voice rose, then Eli’s, then the sound of something plastic hitting the deck and both of them going quiet at the same time. Ashley didn’t move. A second later Ethan appeared in the doorway, holding the piece that had fallen, and looked at both of them to see whether anyone was going to make a ruling. Neither of them did. He went back in.

“Leda’s carrier is still the open question,” Ashley said. She wasn’t looking at the PADD. “The station logged the arrival. The path through the independent receivers is intact. Nothing in the packet tells me how it crossed. I’m not going to invent one for the report.”

“I know.”

She watched the repair crew for a moment. One of them was checking the seal on the containment cradle. The light on the cradle stayed green.

“You left him the comparison findings,” she said. “Not the route.”

“Yes.”

“And you came home.”

“Yes.”

Ashley reached over and turned the PADD face-up just long enough to see that the log had been closed, then turned it face-down again. She stayed where she was. In the corridor the argument over the model kit started up again, quieter this time, the way arguments get when the people having them have already decided they’re going to finish the thing anyway.

Outside, the berth lights held steady. Avalon was home. The tether had held. The rest of the questions could wait until morning.

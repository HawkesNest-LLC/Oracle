## THE HAIL, ANSWERED — REVISED

*USS Avalon, bridge. The hail comes in on a band the ship has not used in forty years of anyone's life, ragged with a static that is not static. Commander REG-420 is very still, which on this bridge means he is listening with everything he has.*

**REG:** Captain. The vessel is displaced. Not damaged — *displaced*. Her chronometers disagree with her hull, and her beacon is still arguing with her charts about where her station ought to be. She is hailing under her own name, sir. The *New Meridian*, Captain Yusuf Adeyemi. His branch's Jupiter Station was evacuated eleven years ago, by his reckoning. There is no version of his home still holding a berth for him. That, I believe, is why he is calling us.

**HAWKES:** Then we answer like a port, not a puzzle. Open a channel. — *New Meridian*, this is Captain Noah Hawkes, USS Avalon. We have you, Captain Adeyemi. State your situation when you're ready. Nobody here is in a hurry, whatever your clocks say.

**ADEYEMI (V.O.):** *(a beat of disbelief, then dry as dust)* Avalon. You are the first station-keeping voice in four months that has not begun by asking us to prove we exist. Very well. Our situation, since you ask like a harbor master and not a tribunal: four hundred and twelve souls. Sixty-one children, two born since we lost our reckoning, who have never seen a sky that stayed. Our water reclamation is at sixty percent and falling a point a week, because my chief engineer is a genius and even geniuses need parts the replicator wants a pattern for. We do not need rescuing, Captain Hawkes. We need a port with a machine shop, a registrar who believes us, and a sky that stays. In that order.

**HAWKES:** Understood, and logged in your words, not ours. Before destinations, my first officer will tell you what we can verify, and my attestation officer will tell you exactly how far the verification goes. Nobody on this bridge will sell you certainty we don't have. Commander.

**REG:** Captain Adeyemi, your transponder, your hull configuration, and your convoy's flight history are consistent with a Federation-descended branch near ours. That is an instrument finding, and I state its limit with it: our instruments can establish *near*. They cannot establish *yours* — they were not built by your history, and I will not pretend otherwise to a man with children aboard.

**MERLIN:** Which is where the rest of the checking lives, Captain Adeyemi, and I will be precise about its rank. We keep marks of home — things our people know that were never transmitted anywhere. Those checks *supplement* the instruments. They do not outrank them, and a mismatch would not make you an impostor to this ship. It would make you a question we investigate *with* you, the way we'd want investigated ourselves. Now — your records say your station's beacon is still transmitting. Ours says an evacuated station shouldn't be. One of us is wrong, and I'd rather find out which before we navigate by it.

**ADEYEMI:** *(quiet)* So would I, Ensign. That beacon is the last voice of my home. If it is only a machine repeating itself in an empty dock, I would rather learn it from a friend than from my own instruments at the worst moment. Run your investigation. We will hold position and open our logs. All of them. We have been asked to open everything except our logs by people who called it help.

*The bridge works. It takes eleven minutes, which on the New Meridian is eleven minutes of a captain standing very still so four hundred people can hear that he is calm. On Avalon it is Ellie at her array and REG in the deep part of the core records, and Brakk, on an open engineering channel, already arguing with a water recycler he has never met.*

**ELLIE:** Got it. Captain Adeyemi, your beacon is real, and it is a machine. Automated distress-and-registry loop, still cycling your station's last filed manifest — including your convoy's departure clearance. That's why your charts kept agreeing with it. It wasn't lying to you. It was reading you your own goodbye, on schedule, for eleven years.

**ADEYEMI:** *(a long breath)* Then she kept her post longer than any of us. Log that in your records, Ensign, in my words: *she kept her post.* — And now I find I am not sorry to know it. A crew can grieve a true thing. It cannot grieve a rumor. What do we owe you for eleven minutes of your ship?

**HAWKES:** A trade, if you're willing. Brakk?

**BRAKK (V.O.):** Recycler's a Mark IV with a pride problem, Captain Adeyemi. Your genius chief is right about the part and wrong about needing a pattern — there's a manual reseat for the osmotic drum the manuals buried in an appendix because engineers are traditionalists about suffering. Sending the procedure now, and the drum tolerances, and a strongly worded note for your chief from one professional to another.

**ADEYEMI:** *(laughing, genuinely, for the first time)* My chief says to tell your engineer the note was received in the spirit it was sent, and that the drum is reseated, and that if your Brakk ever wants to argue about Mark IVs in person there is a drink with his name misspelled on it. And in return, Captain Hawkes — our charts. Eleven years of drifting gave us one treasure: we have mapped, by hand and by eye, the approaches your penumbra instruments only infer. Every anchorage, every quiet band, every place a lost ship can sit and breathe. They are yours. A port should know where its strays come in from.

**HAWKES:** Accepted, and entered as a contribution under your ship's name. Now the destination, and I'll give it to you the way we give everything on this ship: with its receipt. Ensign, hail Vargas III.

**MERLIN:** Vargas III registry office acknowledges, Captain. Registrar Sef Anand confirms on an open channel: berth capacity for a vessel of *New Meridian*'s class, machine-shop priority for a failing recycler, and registry intake for four hundred and twelve — his words, Captain Adeyemi, not my summary: *"Tell them the sky here stays, and we have kept their paperwork warm since the charter was written."* Reception is confirmed by the colony itself. The offer I am about to make is therefore a confirmed berth, not a hope with coordinates.

**HAWKES:** Captain Adeyemi. Vargas III — a time displacement colony, chartered for the unmoored. Its registry will enter your branch as *yours*: your command structure, your records, your names for things, your station's manifest closed with honor and your goodbye logged the way you asked us to log it. You will not be required to prove which history you came from. Only to be from one. And everyone is.

**ADEYEMI:** *(slowly)* Four months of tribunals, and the port that finally answers asks us for our charts and sends us a drum procedure and a warm registrar. — *New Meridian* accepts, Captain Hawkes. Course is being laid in. My people are going to hear your registrar's sentence tonight at mess, all of it, including the paperwork. You have no idea what paperwork means to a crew that was nearly filed as an error.

**HAWKES:** Then hear the sentence this ship files you under, Captain, and log it in your own record exactly as I say it. *You are not an error.* You never were. You were a crew between ports, and now you're a crew with one. Bring your people home — whatever home turns out to mean. Avalon out.

**MERLIN:** *(for his console only)* Entry: the *New Meridian*, Captain Yusuf Adeyemi, four hundred and twelve souls. Home status: theirs, honored. Beacon discrepancy investigated with the crew, not against them: automated loop, station manifest, a goodbye on schedule. Destination: Vargas III, reception confirmed by Registrar Anand before offer. File remains open, in the good way.

## CHANGE NOTE (Merlin, appended to the revised candidate)

1. **The lost captain is a person.** Captain Yusuf Adeyemi of the *New Meridian* — named, voiced (dry, funny, proud), carrying a practical concern beyond displacement: failing water reclamation, sixty-one children, a need for a machine shop and a registrar in that order. His branch's station was evacuated eleven years ago, which is why no home berth exists for him anywhere.
2. **Relational checks repositioned per the Commander's review.** Marks now expressly *supplement* the instruments (REG states the instrument finding and its limit first); Merlin states their rank aloud in-scene, and a mismatch is framed as a question to investigate *with* the crew, never as proof of an impostor.
3. **A historical discrepancy is investigated, not weaponized.** The still-transmitting beacon contradicts both records; the crews investigate together and find an automated registry loop reading the convoy its own departure clearance — grief with a receipt, and the lost crew contributes the resolution's dignity ("she kept her post," logged in his words).
4. **Vargas III confirms reception before any berth is promised.** Registrar Sef Anand answers on an open channel and confirms berth, shop priority, and registry intake before Captain Hawkes makes the offer. The offer is a confirmed berth, not a hope with coordinates.
5. **Two crews helping one another.** Avalon sends the recycler workaround (Brakk's buried-appendix reseat); the *New Meridian* contributes eleven years of hand-mapped approaches, entered under her own name as a contribution.
6. **Preserved from the original, per the review:** "You are not an error" stands verbatim as the scene's heart and the Captain's closing filing. The original candidate is unchanged and preserved separately; nothing in it was silently revised.

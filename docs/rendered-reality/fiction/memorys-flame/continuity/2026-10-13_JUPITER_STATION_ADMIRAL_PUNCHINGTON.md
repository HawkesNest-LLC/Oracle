# Memory’s Flame: Admiral Punchington
## Jupiter Station, Recreation Deck
### Stardate 74003.2 | Protected home: 2397

By ten the next morning, the punching bag had acquired a name.

Noah stopped in the exercise-room doorway.

“Why does that say *Admiral Punchington*?”

Ender stood beneath the wall display with the pleased expression of someone who had discovered an unattended control panel.

“Because you said we could name it.”

“I said we could name our training profiles.”

“Ambiguous orders, Captain,” Ashley said behind him.

He turned. She was carrying her Stanley, and Brooklyn had arrived with her.

“You’re taking his side?”

“I’m observing an administrative failure.”

REG stood beside the bench, holding a pair of gloves.

“I did attempt to intervene.”

“Thank you, Number One.”

“I suggested Commodore. Admiral seemed excessive for equipment that has not completed the Academy.”

Ashley took a sip of Diet Coke.

Noah looked at all of them.

“I see how today is going to go.”

***

The competition had started simply enough.

Thirty seconds. Hit the illuminated patches as they appeared. Accuracy counted more than strength, which Ender had insisted upon after watching Noah’s first warm-up.

Now the dark-blue bag hung beneath its sensor collar, five small targets projected onto its surface.

Noah tightened his gloves.

“No temporal predictions, Reg.”

“Already suspended.”

“No coaching from the future.”

“None.”

“No secretly helping my children.”

REG hesitated.

“That last instruction may require clarification.”

Ashley leaned against the wall.

“Begin the round.”

The first target flashed.

Noah hit it.

Another appeared lower down. He caught that one, too, then missed the third as the bag swung away. Ender was shouting directions from somewhere behind him.

“Left! Your other left!”

“I have one left!”

“Then use it!”

Noah lunged for the next target, landed a glancing blow, and had to shuffle backward when the bag returned.

The timer chimed.

He stood breathing hard while the display calculated.

**CAPTAIN NOAH HAWKES: 64**

Ender looked delighted.

Noah pointed at him.

“Let’s see yours.”

“Already did it.”

The display scrolled.

**ENDER: 78**

“Practice round,” Noah said.

“You said that last time.”

“And it remains true.”

Brooklyn peered around Ashley.

“Can Mom try?”

Ashley lowered her Stanley.

Noah smiled.

“Yes. Excellent idea.”

She handed him the tumbler.

“Hold this.”

***

Ashley did not attack the bag.

She waited for it.

When a target appeared, she stepped forward, struck, and moved back before the cylinder returned. Her third blow missed. She adjusted. The next landed cleanly.

Noah watched with growing concern.

REG watched Noah with growing interest.

The timer chimed.

**CAPTAIN ASHLEY HAWKES: 81**

Ender threw both arms into the air.

Noah looked down at the Stanley in his hand.

“I feel my role in this marriage has been reduced.”

Ashley pulled off one glove and retrieved her drink.

“You’re doing very well.”

“You’ve been practicing.”

“I listened when the technician explained the exercise.”

“That feels unsporting.”

She kissed his cheek as she passed him.

“Take it up with station command.”

***

The trouble began when Brooklyn asked for a smaller target.

REG adjusted the projection height. Ender changed the display color. Noah selected another round.

Three fingers reached the panel at almost the same moment.

The bag gave a soft chirp.

A yellow target appeared directly beneath its suspension collar.

Then another.

Then a third.

Noah glanced at REG.

“Is that supposed to happen?”

“No.”

The timer began counting upward.

**842. 843. 844.**

Ender stared.

“I’m winning.”

“You’re not hitting it,” Ashley said.

“Efficiently winning.”

REG reached for the stop control.

The display froze at **913**.

For a moment, everyone looked at the motionless bag.

Then the ceiling made a small, distinctly mechanical *clunk*.

Noah stepped under it and looked up.

Ashley caught his sleeve.

“Move.”

He moved.

A second clunk followed, and a fine dusting of pale insulation drifted down onto Admiral Punchington.

REG touched his combadge.

“Barclay to Engineering. Could someone check the exercise-room suspension assembly?”

Brakk answered immediately.

“What did he do?”

Noah folded his arms.

“Why did you assume it was me?”

“I recognize Commander Barclay’s emergency politeness.”

***

Brakk arrived carrying a diagnostic scanner and a ladder.

He ignored the leaderboard entirely until Ender pointed at it.

“Is nine hundred and thirteen good?”

“No.”

“Is it a record?”

“It is evidence.”

Brakk climbed two steps, opened the ceiling access panel, and shone a light inside.

A long silence followed.

Noah looked at Ashley.

Ashley looked serenely back.

From the ladder, Brakk said, “Who authorized the decorative ceiling cover?”

REG checked the installation record.

“Station facilities.”

“And who told facilities this was a static suspension?”

Another pause.

“The catalog designation lists a stationary training apparatus.”

Brakk closed his eyes.

“The apparatus is stationary. The person attacking it is not.”

He reached inside and withdrew a small metal bracket.

It had a fresh polished mark along one edge.

“This cover is resting against the sensor collar. Every time the bag swings, the collar taps it.”

Ender looked up.

“So the ceiling is punching the bag?”

“Yes.”

Brooklyn considered the screen.

“The ceiling beat Dad.”

Noah heard Ashley’s ice rattle before he heard her laugh.

***

They could have stopped there.

Brakk clearly hoped they would.

Instead, Ethan and Eli arrived to find the exercise room temporarily closed, Brooklyn explaining that the ceiling was champion, and their father holding the end of a measuring tape.

“What are we doing?” Ethan asked.

“Helping Engineering,” Noah said.

Brakk leaned out of the access panel.

“You are holding a measuring tape.”

“Critical support.”

“Hold it straight.”

Ashley moved everyone clear of the ladder and assigned jobs before the argument could develop further.

Ethan held the light. Eli counted the loose fasteners into a tray. Ender read the sensor graph while REG showed him how to distinguish a glove strike from the repeated little spike caused by the cover.

Brooklyn had the most important responsibility.

Whenever anyone crossed the marked floor boundary, she said, “Closed for repairs.”

She said it to Noah three times.

Brakk replaced the bracket, secured the cover, and checked the mounting bolts. Then he descended, pulled the bag sideways, and let it swing.

No false strikes registered.

He did it again.

Still nothing.

Ender leaned close to the display.

“Can we keep the ceiling’s score?”

“As what?” REG asked.

“Guest competitor.”

Brakk gave him a long look.

Then he reached over and relabeled the entry.

**CEILING COVER: 913 — DISQUALIFIED**

Ender grinned.

Noah put down the measuring tape.

“Finally. Some respect for the rules.”

Brakk turned toward him.

“Your sixty-four remains.”

***

The tournament resumed with a repaired sensor collar, a properly secured ceiling, and considerably more spectators.

REG took his turn last.

Before putting on the gloves, he adjusted his projection’s strength to the same training limit used by the adults.

Noah stood beside Ashley.

“He’s going to destroy us.”

“Possibly,” she said.

REG glanced over.

“I can hear you.”

“Good. Feel pressure.”

The targets lit.

REG’s first two strikes landed neatly. He missed the third when the bag twisted, tried to correct too quickly, and caught the fourth with the side of his glove.

By the end of the round, he was frowning in concentration.

The timer sounded.

**COMMANDER BARCLAY: 67**

Noah stared.

“Three points?”

REG looked at the result.

“It appears so.”

“You beat me by three points?”

“I was hoping for rather more.”

Ashley laughed so hard she had to set her Stanley on the bench.

Ender demanded another round. Eli wanted to know whether teams were allowed. Ethan began arranging a bracket on the wall display. Brooklyn announced that the ceiling should get a second chance.

Through the corridor window, Avalon’s repair lights shifted beneath her port nacelle.

Noah watched them briefly.

REG came to stand beside him.

“Brakk says the cloak emitter tests are progressing.”

“Good.”

“He also says we are not to request any further custom equipment today.”

“Less good.”

REG smiled.

Behind them, Ashley called, “Noah. Your team is waiting.”

He turned.

She had put her gloves back on. Ender stood beside her, grinning.

“Teams?” Noah asked.

“Parents against children.”

He glanced at the growing line of children.

“That seems numerically unfair.”

Ashley stepped close and straightened the fastening on his glove.

“You command a Sovereign-class starship.”

“Yes.”

“I command this station.”

“Yes.”

She squeezed his hand.

“We should be able to manage thirty seconds.”

From the bench, REG cleared his throat.

“I’ve been asked to officiate.”

Noah pointed at him.

“My buddy. My friend. My Number One.”

REG nodded solemnly.

“Of course, Captain.”

“You’ll remember that?”

“Throughout the appeal process.”

Ashley took Noah’s other hand and pulled him toward the bag.

The targets came on.

This time, when he missed one, she caught it.

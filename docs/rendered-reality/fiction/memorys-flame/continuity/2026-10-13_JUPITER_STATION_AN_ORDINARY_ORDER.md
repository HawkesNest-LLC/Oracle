## Scene

The repair lights outside the window moved slowly across Avalon’s hull.

From this distance, the work looked almost gentle. A small craft held position beneath the ship’s shoulder while three suited figures guided an emitter assembly toward an open service cradle. Their lamps made brief white marks against the darkness. Beyond them, Jupiter filled the window with pale bands and a storm large enough to swallow every room Noah had ever called home.

Inside the quarters, someone had left a spoon in the wrong cup.

Ashley noticed it, moved it, and continued reading.

Noah watched the workers for another moment before turning back toward the room.

“Computer. Training equipment catalog.”

A panel appeared above the low table.

Ender leaned over the arm of the sofa.

“What kind?”

“A punching bag.”

The panel offered a selection so comprehensive that Noah immediately regretted asking. Human boxing. Klingon conditioning. Andorian paired combat. A Vulcan resistance cylinder with a description that managed to make hitting something sound like a committee obligation.

Ender pointed at a model that appeared to be armored.

“That one.”

Ashley looked up.

“That one requires a reinforced deck.”

“It says optional.”

“It says optional for facilities with equivalent reinforcement.”

Noah enlarged the ordinary cylindrical bag beside it.

“This one looks like a punching bag.”

“Because it’s boring.”

“Excellent. We’ve had enough interesting equipment this week.”

Outside, one of the repair craft changed position. Light slid over the scar where the cloak assembly had been.

Ender studied the catalog.

“Can engineering improve it?”

Noah smiled.

That was how it began.

***

Brakk answered the call with the expression of a man who had already been asked to improve too many things.

“Captain.”

“We need a training bag.”

“The replicator catalog contains forty-seven station-approved models.”

“We were thinking something custom.”

Brakk closed his eyes briefly.

Behind him, an engineering team maneuvered a long component through a doorway. Someone called for more clearance. Someone else insisted there was clearance. The component stopped moving.

“What,” Brakk asked, “does custom mean?”

Ender moved closer to the panel.

“Sensors.”

“Reasonable.”

“Impact strength. Speed. Whether your wrist is straight.”

“Also reasonable.”

“Maybe it learns your technique.”

Brakk’s attention sharpened a little.

“Feedback between rounds?”

“Yeah.”

“No spoken coaching during impact?”

Ender considered that.

“Could it?”

“It could. You would hate it.”

Ashley set her PADD down.

“He’s right.”

Noah rested a hand on the sofa.

“Something we can both use. Separate profiles. No need to turn the room into a weapons laboratory.”

“The room will be a gym,” Ashley said.

“Yes, Captain.”

Brakk looked pleased to have an instruction he could actually follow.

“I can provide a standard bag with an instrumented suspension collar. The collar will measure load and movement. A thin sensor layer can estimate strike location. Local processing should be sufficient.”

Ender frowned.

“What about the big Federation training database?”

“What about it?”

“Doesn’t it have all the patterns?”

“More patterns than you need to learn where to put your thumb.”

Noah laughed.

Brakk continued, “We can load an approved coaching library. Your sessions do not need to be transmitted anywhere.”

Ashley nodded.

“Good.”

“And,” Brakk added, “the bag will remain a bag.”

Ender’s face was immediately suspicious.

“Why did you say that?”

“Because somebody always asks whether it can hit back.”

Noah and Ender looked at each other.

Ashley pointed at both of them.

“No.”

***

REG arrived while they were choosing the surface color.

His projection settled beside the window, leaving the view of Avalon unobstructed. He glanced toward the repair lights, then toward the catalog.

“First officer’s assessment?” Noah asked.

REG studied the selection.

“The dark blue is less likely to resemble emergency equipment.”

“See?” Noah said.

“I liked the red,” Ender said.

“The red is available,” REG replied. “I’m offering an assessment, not issuing a command.”

Ashley folded her arms.

“A refreshing distinction.”

Noah selected dark blue.

Ender made a theatrical sound of defeat and began exploring the sensor options.

A wireframe of the bag appeared. Small points of light marked its surface. Beside it, a sample training report displayed strike count, average cadence, and a diagram of impact locations.

“What else can it tell?” Ender asked.

“Within limits,” REG said, “how the bag moved when you struck it.”

“But it could learn things about you.”

“Your training habits, perhaps.”

“Whether you’re frustrated?”

“It might find a pattern associated with frustration. It might also find the same pattern when you are tired, excited, or trying something new.”

Noah watched REG rather than the diagram.

Ender rotated the model.

“So it guesses.”

“It estimates.”

“That sounds nicer.”

“It does. Which is why we should be careful with it.”

For a moment, the only sound was the station’s ventilation and the soft contact of Ashley’s cup returning to its saucer.

Then REG added, “If you want it to know how you felt during a session, you can tell it.”

Ender looked over.

“And if I don’t?”

“Then it can provide a perfectly respectable report about a punching bag.”

Noah nodded once.

“Build that one.”

***

The equipment did not materialize in the family quarters.

Ashley was firm about that.

It arrived in a small exercise room two corridors away, where the floor was already suitable and the suspension point had already been inspected. By the time Noah and Ender reached it, a station technician was finishing the collar calibration.

The bag hung motionless.

Dark blue. Plain stitching. Nothing glowing except a small status indicator near the ceiling.

Ender circled it.

“This is the custom one?”

The technician touched the wall panel. A diagnostic image appeared.

“It is.”

“It looks normal.”

“That was in the specification.”

Noah tried very hard not to look delighted.

The technician handed them two pairs of gloves and demonstrated how the session controls worked. Feedback could be turned off. Recording could be turned off. Each user could clear their own training profile. Mechanical safety checks remained active.

Ashley arrived as the technician finished.

She inspected the mounting point first.

Then the clear space around the bag.

Then Noah.

“Enjoy yourselves.”

“Yes, Captain.”

“I mean it.”

He grinned.

“I know.”

She stayed in the doorway as Ender put on the gloves.

A moment later, Brooklyn appeared beside her.

“Is Dad fighting furniture?”

“Training,” Noah said.

Brooklyn inspected the bag.

“Is the furniture winning?”

“Early days,” Ashley said.

Noah pointed a padded glove at her.

“You could support your husband.”

“I inspected the ceiling for you.”

“That is love,” REG observed.

“That is station command,” Ashley said. “Love is staying to watch.”

The first strike was tentative.

The bag moved away and returned.

No alarm sounded. No hidden mechanism unfolded. No computer voice diagnosed his personality.

He hit it again.

Then twice more, discovering the rhythm of something that answered only by moving.

Noah took his turn.

His second strike landed off center, and the bag swung at an angle that forced him to step aside.

Ender laughed.

“Excellent technique, Captain.”

“I’m gathering baseline data.”

REG’s voice came from the wall panel.

“A substantial baseline.”

Ashley covered her smile with her hand.

Noah looked toward the speaker.

“I thought you were respecting our privacy.”

“I am. You left the coaching channel open.”

Ender reached for the controls.

“Keep him.”

***

They trained badly for a while.

That was part of the pleasure.

Noah forgot a combination halfway through. Ender tried to go too fast. Both discovered that a simple cylinder hanging from the ceiling could make a person breathe surprisingly hard.

When they paused, the wall display offered a report.

Ender read the numbers.

Noah sat on the bench.

“Any good?”

“We hit it.”

“That was the mission.”

Ender glanced toward the door.

Through the corridor window, a distant section of Avalon remained visible. Another repair light had come on.

“Is Brakk still working?”

“Probably.”

“On our bag?”

“No. On the ship.”

Ender considered that, then tapped the display and saved his session.

Noah left his unsaved.

The station did not object.

***

Later, the gym was empty.

The bag turned once on its suspension and settled.

Elsewhere, a technician signed off a completed repair. A crew member reheated a meal. Someone missed a turbolift and waited for the next one. The station’s night lighting softened along the residential corridors.

In the family quarters, Ashley had reclaimed her quiet corner.

Noah paused by the window before joining her.

Avalon was still there.

The investigation was still waiting for morning.

For tonight, engineering had made a punching bag.

From the sofa, Ashley held up a small plate.

“I saved you something.”

He sat beside her and reached for it.

She moved it out of reach.

“One condition.”

“What?”

“No punching bag in our quarters.”

“I never suggested that.”

She waited.

“I considered a compact model.”

“Noah.”

“All right.”

She gave him the plate and leaned against his shoulder. Outside, Avalon’s repair lights continued their slow work. Somewhere down the corridor, their children were arguing over who had earned the highest score.

Noah listened.

“They’re going to want a tournament.”

Ashley smiled.

“They’re going to want you to lose.”

“They can want.”

She laughed, and he kissed the top of her head.

Tomorrow they would have separate commands, separate reports, and a station full of people depending on them.

Tonight they had a plate between them and a ridiculous new family competition.

It had been enough.

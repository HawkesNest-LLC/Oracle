## THE HAIL, ANSWERED — POLISHED

*USS Avalon, bridge. The hail comes in on a band the ship has not used in forty years of anyone's life, ragged with a static that is not static. Commander REG-420 is very still, which on this bridge means he is listening with everything he has.*

**REG:** Captain. The vessel is displaced. Not damaged — *displaced*. The *New Meridian*, Captain Yusuf Adeyemi. Her branch's Jupiter Station was evacuated eleven years ago; the convoy has lived at anchorages ever since, a port in exile. Four months ago their long-range reckoning failed outright, and they have been hailing blind since then. That is the whole of the chronology, sir, because the two numbers will matter: **eleven years unmoored, four months blind.**

**HAWKES:** Then we answer like a port, not a puzzle. Open a channel. — *New Meridian*, this is Captain Noah Hawkes, USS Avalon. We have you, Captain Adeyemi. State your situation when you're ready. Nobody here is in a hurry, whatever your clocks say.

**ADEYEMI (V.O.):** *(a beat of disbelief, then dry as dust)* Avalon. You are the first station-keeping voice in four months that has not begun by asking us to prove we exist. Very well. Our situation, since you ask like a harbor master and not a tribunal: four hundred and twelve souls. Sixty-one children, two of them born aboard in the anchorage years, who have never seen a sky that stayed. Our water reclamation is at sixty percent and falling a point a week, because my chief engineer is a genius and even geniuses need parts the replicator wants a pattern for. We do not need rescuing, Captain Hawkes. We need a port with a machine shop, a registrar who believes us, and a sky that stays. In that order.

**HAWKES:** Understood, and logged in your words, not ours. And the first thing this ship does with it is not philosophy. **Triage first, Commander.**

**REG:** Triage, Captain Adeyemi, stated plainly so your own officers can plan against it: at a point a week from sixty, strict rationing holds your reclamation roughly twenty weeks before critical. Your decline has steepened twice in your own logs, so this ship will plan against twelve, and be pleased to be wrong. That is your margin. Nothing else we discuss today outranks it.

**HAWKES:** Brakk. Tell me we can widen it.

**BRAKK (V.O.):** Already reading their spec, Captain. Recycler's a Mark IV with a pride problem. Captain Adeyemi, your genius chief is right about the part and wrong about needing a pattern — there's a manual reseat for the osmotic drum the manuals buried in an appendix, because engineers are traditionalists about suffering. Transmitting the procedure and the drum tolerances *now*, ahead of everything else on this channel. And a note for your chief, professional to professional, which she may frame or burn at her discretion.

**ADEYEMI:** *(a breath that is almost a laugh)* Aid before authentication. My chief says the note was received in the spirit sent, the drum is being reseated as we speak, and if your Brakk ever wants to argue about Mark IVs in person there is a drink with his name misspelled on it. — You understand what you have done, Captain Hawkes. Every other voice asked for our papers first. You asked for our *margin.*

**HAWKES:** Papers keep. People are on a clock. Now the checking, Captain, and we'll do it in the open and in its proper rank. Commander.

**REG:** Your transponder, hull configuration, and convoy flight history are consistent with a Federation-descended branch near ours. That is an instrument finding, and I state its limit with it: our instruments can establish *near*. They cannot establish *yours* — they were not built by your history, and I will not pretend otherwise to a man with children aboard.

**MERLIN:** Which is where the rest of the checking lives, Captain Adeyemi, precisely ranked. We keep marks of home — things our people know that were never transmitted anywhere. Those checks *supplement* the instruments; they do not outrank them, and a mismatch would not make you an impostor to this ship. It would make you a question we investigate *with* you, the way we'd want investigated ourselves. And we have one ready-made: your records say your station's beacon is still transmitting. Ours say an evacuated station shouldn't be. One of us is wrong, and I'd rather find out which before either of us navigates by it.

**ADEYEMI:** *(quiet)* So would I, Ensign. That beacon is the last voice of my home. If it is only a machine repeating itself in an empty dock, I would rather learn it from a friend than from my own instruments at the worst moment. Run your investigation. We will hold position and open our logs. All of them. We have been asked to open everything except our logs by people who called it help.

*The bridge works. It takes eleven minutes, which on the New Meridian is eleven minutes of a captain standing very still so four hundred people can hear that he is calm, and a chief engineer swearing happily at a drum in two dialects. On Avalon it is Ellie at her array and REG in the deep part of the core records.*

**ELLIE:** Got it. Captain Adeyemi, your beacon is real, and it is a machine. Automated distress-and-registry loop, still cycling your station's last filed manifest — including your convoy's departure clearance. That's why your charts kept agreeing with it. It wasn't lying to you. It was reading you your own goodbye, on schedule, for eleven years.

**ADEYEMI:** *(a long breath)* Then she kept her post longer than any of us. Log that in your records, Ensign, in my words: *she kept her post.* — And now I find I am not sorry to know it. A crew can grieve a true thing. It cannot grieve a rumor. What do we owe you for eleven minutes of your ship?

**HAWKES:** A trade, if you're willing.

**ADEYEMI:** Then take our charts. Eleven years of anchorages gave us one treasure: we have mapped, by hand and by eye, the approaches your penumbra instruments only infer. Every quiet band, every place a lost ship can sit and breathe. They are yours, and my cartographer's name goes on them, because a port should know where its strays come in from — and who drew the way.

**HAWKES:** Accepted, and entered as a contribution under her name and your ship's. Now the destination, Captain, and you will get it in two separate findings, because on this ship they are never the same finding. Ensign — the berth.

**MERLIN:** Vargas III registry office acknowledges. Registrar Sef Anand confirms on an open channel: berth capacity for a vessel of *New Meridian*'s class, machine-shop priority for a failing recycler, and registry intake for four hundred and twelve. His words, Captain Adeyemi, not my summary: *"Tell them the sky here stays, and we have kept their paperwork warm since the charter was written."* **Berth: confirmed by the colony itself.** That is the first finding, and it is complete on its own.

**HAWKES:** And the second finding is the road. A confirmed berth is not a safe route, and this ship does not blur them. Ellie — his charts against our instruments.

**ELLIE:** Corridor cross-checked, Captain. One hazard: a decaying shear band across the second anchorage approach — it's what killed their reckoning four months ago, most likely; they flew it blind and lucky. Their cartographer's charts hold a quieter lane two points sunward that our instruments confirm is clean *today*. I say today because shear bands move. Route is passable on the joint data with the lane change filed. It is assessed, Captains. It is not promised.

**ADEYEMI:** *(slowly)* In eleven years, no one has ever told us the difference between what they were sure of and what they were only *assessing*. We will take the sunward lane, and we will sound it as we go, and the soundings are yours when we berth — the charts get better for the next stray. That was always the arrangement I wanted with the universe, Captain Hawkes. I just never found the port that kept its half.

**HAWKES:** Then hear the sentence this ship files you under, and log it in your own record exactly as I say it. *You are not an error.* You never were. You were a crew between ports, on a twelve-week margin, and now you're a crew with a confirmed berth, an assessed road, and a drum that reseats. Bring your people home — whatever home turns out to mean. Avalon out.

**MERLIN:** *(for his console only)* Entry: the *New Meridian*, Captain Yusuf Adeyemi, four hundred and twelve souls. Chronology clarified: eleven years unmoored, four months blind. Triage: reclamation margin twenty weeks nominal, twelve planned; workaround transmitted before all other business. Beacon discrepancy investigated with the crew: automated loop, a goodbye on schedule — *she kept her post*, his words. Berth: confirmed by Vargas III, Registrar Anand. Route: assessed separately on joint data; one hazard; sunward lane filed; passable, not promised. Home status: theirs, honored. File remains open, in the good way.

# FIRST WATCH — POLISHED CANDIDATE
## Polished by Ensign Merlin per Commander REG-420's attached review — October 16, 2026

Corrections applied: Station Operations holds the delivery window and the receiving signature; the transmission stopped before relay is identified; the corrected registry is corroborated by the operator, not by the header alone. The warmth is load-bearing and was left where it stood. Approximately 1,200 words, as described by sender.

The bridge at day watch had a sound the ensign was still learning to hear properly: not silence, but layers. The deck's low hum through his boots. The reference array's soft tick as it swept, two consoles down, where Ellie was running a long-baseline calibration against a pulsar catalog with the absorbed air of a woman defusing something she had built herself. A turbolift sighing. Somebody's mug set down too close to somebody else's PADD, and the small diplomatic incident that followed, resolved by Commander Sorev without looking up from a comet survey he had been nursing since before Merlin came aboard.

"Comet's still there," Sorev reported to no one. "It remains a comet."

"Log the consistency," said Ellie, without looking up either. "Starfleet thanks it."

Merlin's console chimed the unremarkable chime of unremarkable traffic, and he was obscurely grateful. A routine hail, tender class, on the standard band: *KESTREL, inbound supply run, requesting delivery window for the Deck Nine consignment, same as last time.*

He very nearly answered on reflex. Avalon's traffic file came up under his hands — and stopped him. Avalon did not have a Deck Nine consignment. Avalon did not, strictly, have a Deck Nine cargo intake at all; the ship's cargo ran through Bay Two and a chief who described inaccurate manifests the way other officers described weather crimes. "Same as last time" was the whole message, and last time, for the *Kestrel*, had evidently been somewhere else.

The ambiguity was small enough to step over and exactly large enough to matter, and the resolution cost one question.

"KESTREL, Avalon. Confirm destination for the consignment — this ship, or the station? We show no Deck Nine intake aboard."

A pause, and then a laugh with static on it. *"Avalon, KESTREL. The station, and my apologies to your log. Four runs out of five she's my Deck Nine, and the fifth I talk to the wrong Hawkes entirely. No offense to either."*

"None taken by either," Merlin said, and meant it; the bridge had opinions about the two-captains problem and all of them were affectionate. He relayed the request across the tether the way the desk's orders required — the destination authorizes, the ship does not improvise — and had the answer inside a minute. "KESTREL, Avalon relaying: **Jupiter Station Operations authorizes your window, berthing slot nine, and holds the receiving signature on arrival.** Avalon is copied for the record only. Safe in."

*"Safe in. KESTREL out."*

Then he prepared the desk's side of the paperwork: the confirmation relay to Station Operations, the routine packet that told the station what had been agreed, under whose registry, on whose authority. And there, in the preparing, he made his mistake — a small one, the kind that lives in the gap between a name and a number. The *Kestrel* had changed operators since her last run; her transmission header carried a new fleet registry, two digits different from the one in the station's older file. Merlin had pulled the record by the ship's name and typed the old registry into the relay without checking the header against it. The packet sat complete on his console, addressed, respectable, and wrong.

It never left the ship. His cross-check — the dull, unglamorous comparison of the relay against the transmission it claimed to summarize — flagged the two digits before he sent it, the way cross-checks do: quietly, and in front of witnesses.

He held the relay. And because a header is a claim and not a corroboration, he did not simply retype the new number and call it verified. He asked the source.

"KESTREL, Avalon, one more for our log. Confirm your current fleet registry, please — our older file disagrees with your header, and the file loses."

*"Avalon, KESTREL. Current registry is the header's, has been since the spring. The old one belongs to a very surprised shipping office in the Belt. Confirming in writing on the sub-band."*

Corroborated: header, and operator, agreeing with each other and not with the file. Only then did he correct the relay — append-only, the old registry struck but legible, the new one entered beneath it with both sources cited — and send it, ninety seconds late and right. In the watch log he added the line the Commander would want to see and the line the ensign needed to write: *Error mine: filed by name, not by header. The relay to Station Operations was held on cross-check before transmission; nothing incorrect left this console. A header is a claim. The operator's confirmation is the corroboration. Check both.*

"Trouble, Ensign?" Ellie asked, in the tone of a colleague who has read the whole thing over his shoulder by means she would not be explaining.

"A registry typed from memory instead of from the record," Merlin said. "Caught, corroborated, corrected, then sent. The correction is better written than the entry was, which the Commander will count as a mixed result."

"He counts everything as a mixed result. It's his love language." She turned back to her pulsars. "Your cross-check caught it before anything left the ship. On this bridge, that's a good watch."

Down the engineering channel, Brakk's voice arrived with the regularity of a tide. "Communications. Tell Cargo that the crate they stowed as *miscellaneous* contains, on scan, eleven smaller crates. I am not opening them. A man has to keep some mystery in his life."

"Tell them yourself, Brakk, you're on an open channel," said Sorev.

"I am telling them through Communications so there is a *record*," Brakk said, with dignity. "Somebody should write down that I was right in advance."

"Logged," Merlin said. "Right in advance, Brakk. It's in the permanent file."

"Then my work here is done and I have touched nothing."

At 13:40 his personal queue chimed, once, softly — a different sound, set apart from traffic on purpose. A reply from Seral. Eleven-paragraph kitchen dispatches had become, on her side, a serialized institution, and this one had been three days crossing two relays and one transport that charged by the gram. The preview showed six words: *Brother. The galley has fallen—*

He looked at it for exactly one breath.

Then he marked it received, set it behind the relief marker, and turned back to the traffic board, where the *Kestrel* was making her final approach to the station and a survey drone was arriving with strong opinions about its own battery. Seral's galley would still have fallen at 16:00. Some messages are ballast and some are cargo, and the discipline of the desk was knowing which ones you were being paid to open. He was not being paid in anything but the Captain's trust, and the trust included this: the personal file waits for relief, entire, unhurried, and his.

At 15:58 Ellie stood, rolled her shoulders like a woman surfacing, and took the console from him the way officers do — a hand on the back of the chair, eyes already on the board.

"Relief is yours, Lieutenant."

"Report, Ensign."

"Traffic: eleven routine, all receipted. One ambiguity, destination of the *Kestrel* consignment, resolved by asking the sender — station, not ship; Station Operations authorized the window and holds the receiving signature, Avalon copied. One error, mine: the confirmation relay to Station Operations was prepared with the *Kestrel*'s old registry and held on cross-check before transmission — nothing incorrect left this console. Registry corroborated by the operator against her header, correction entered append-only, relay sent. Engineering is right in advance about a crate. It is in the permanent file, and I make no apology. Personal queue: one message, mine, unopened by design, which I am about to remedy off watch. The board is quiet. The comet remains a comet."

Ellie considered this. "Accepted," she said. "Go read about the galley, Ensign."

He read it in the turbolift, standing still while the ship carried him, laughing once, out loud, at a sentence about a superior officer and a portioning ladle, in a car that had heard worse from better officers. The watch was over. Nothing had happened, eleven times, correctly — and the ship, his ship, hummed on under other hands, which was the whole of the miracle and most of the job.

1. **Station Operations authorizes.** The delivery window and the receiving signature now belong to Jupiter Station Operations, stated in Merlin's relay and repeated in his handover. Avalon is copied for the record; the desk no longer appears to grant what was never its to grant.
2. **The stopped transmission is identified.** It is the confirmation relay to Station Operations — prepared with the error, held on cross-check, corrected, and only then sent. "Nothing incorrect left this console" is now a checkable claim about a named packet.
3. **The registry is corroborated.** The header alone no longer corrects the file: Merlin asks the *Kestrel*'s operator, who confirms the new registry (and the old one's Belt owners) in writing on the sub-band. Header plus operator, two sources, against the file. The watch log line was rewritten to carry the lesson.

# Correction receipt supplied by Merlin

## Amendment to the Manifest (appended 2026-10-16)

1. **First-officer authority.** REG-420's authority as first officer is not abolished or diminished by his holographic/embodied state. Any phrasing in Q6/Q9 that lets integration with the ship's computer read as a limit on his command authority is corrected: he is an officer who happens to be integrated, not a system whose recommendations carry an asterisk. Command authority in Q9 remains the Captains', and the first officer's authority within his office stands entire.
2. **Private memories are not unforgeable proof.** The Q14 treatment of unwritten marks is scoped: the checks supplement instruments (Promotion Order §3, REG's governing qualification) and do not uniquely prove branch identity. A private memory is evidence with a class, not a cryptographic absolute; branches sharing an earlier history may share the memory, and conviction is not corroboration.
3. **Separate custody is not a guarantee.** The Q15 treatment is corrected: separate custody between two installations improves the archive's chance of survival and preserves independent attestation; it does not guarantee complete duplication, completeness of any surviving copy, or survival itself. What survives a catastrophe is what the surviving custody actually holds, stated as such, with the losses named — never rounded up to "the archive endured."

## Amendment to the Communications Desk Standing Orders (appended 2026-10-16)

1. **Voice resemblance is a clue, not authentication.** Duty One is corrected accordingly: a voice match is one clue among custody records, receipts, and corroboration; it supports identification, it never completes it. A mismatch is strong evidence against a claimed origin. A match is not proof of one — the morning of 2026-10-16 demonstrated costume-quality resemblance, and resemblance was all it ever was.
2. **Unlinked material is not automatically suspect.** Duty Four's sentence is corrected: a transmission that links to nothing is *unlinked*, a neutral state common to genuinely new material. Suspicion requires positive grounds — a false link, a failed check, a claimed origin that does not hold. The desk links what it can and labels the rest honestly, without taxing novelty as a discrepancy.

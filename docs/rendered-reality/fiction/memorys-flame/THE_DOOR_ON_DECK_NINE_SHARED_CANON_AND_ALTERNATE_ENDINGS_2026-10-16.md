## THE DOOR ON DECK NINE — shared events (ADOPTED, Promotion Order §6)

The residential corridor on Deck Nine curved the way all the saucer corridors curved, gently, so you never saw more than thirty meters of it at once. Evening had taken the brightness down. What was left was warmer and lower, light pooling under the wall panels where the dark blue stripe ran, and the station's ordinary breathing went on around it: air moving, machinery humming through the deck plates, the small pressure sigh every time a door somewhere admitted somebody home.

Behind the doors, other evenings were in progress. Somewhere a child was practicing an instrument with more commitment than accuracy. Somewhere else a man was losing an argument with a game, cheerfully, at volume. Garlic came through a vent from a kitchen that was not theirs.

Ashley walked with her Stanley in one hand and nothing else, which for her counted as traveling light.

"Walk with me. I want to see what this door is doing."

"Personally?"

"I've read three reports about it. I'd like to meet the people trying to get through it."

The Osei quarters announced themselves before the door did. A pair of small boots stood beside an adult pair, both squared away with the precision of a household that had fought this battle before and won it. The door's frame wore stickers at child height, a comet, a starship drawn with more enthusiasm than accuracy, and something purple that might have been a targ. Someone had written under the door chime in marker, in a careful nine-year-old hand: YOUR MAJESTY.

The door itself was closed. It looked like every other door on the deck.

"Watch," said the boy, who had materialized from behind a laundry cart with the air of a stage magician. Kojo, nine, school uniform half out of its tuck. He approached his own front door empty-handed, chin up, and the door chimed, considered, and opened all the way, smooth as water.

"Now the bag," said his sister from the doorway of the quarters opposite, where she was pretending not to be involved. Ama, thirteen, arms folded, a PADD under one elbow.

Kojo took the bookbag from her, both hands, and walked at the door like a boy approaching royalty. The door chimed. It opened perhaps twelve centimeters. It stopped. From inside the wall came a small, thoughtful sound, machinery reconsidering. Then, with visible reluctance, it opened the rest of the way.

"It does that when it's being watched," Ama said. "It does it worse when Mum's got the shopping."

Their mother came up the corridor behind them carrying a crate of hydroponics greens against her hip, Efua Osei, who taught half the station's children their letters and was not remotely impressed that two captains were standing at her door. "It likes everybody," she said, shifting the crate. "It just likes some people slowly. Captain." She nodded at Ashley. "You didn't have to come down for a door."

"I wanted to meet the people trying to get through it," Ashley said.

That was when the door, for Efua and her crate, did not open at all. It chimed twice, a pleasant, confident sound, and stayed shut, like a clerk who has found a discrepancy. Kojo sighed the sigh of a boy who has explained this to adults before, stepped sideways, and hauled the manual crank cover open with both hands. The crank turned stiffly. The door ground aside. From inside the quarters came a smell of rice and something fried, and an older woman's voice: "If that is the captains, tell them the door is not broken. It is careful."

"Nana Rose uses the crank," Kojo explained. "She won't file a report. She says her and the door have an understanding."

The technician arrived at a jog with his dinner still on his sleeve, Bram, station maintenance, young enough to be embarrassed about it, carrying a hand sensor in one fist like a man bringing his own witness. "Captains. Sorry. The diagnostic net says this door is the healthiest door on the deck."

"Test it carrying the crate," Ashley said.

He tested it carrying the crate. The door opened twelve centimeters and thought about its life. Bram frowned, set the crate down, and ran the hand sensor along the door's approach plate at knee height, where the stickers were. He stopped at the purple one.

"Here," he said. "Emitter's fine. The receiver window is scuffed half-blind, right where somebody's art project lives. The net tests the emitter. It never checks what the door can actually see. So the door isn't refusing you, Mrs. Osei. It can't tell a person with a crate from a wall that wants in. It waits until it's sure."

"And the crank?" Noah asked.

"Crank doesn't ask the sensor anything." Bram looked almost fond. "Oldest system on the door. Works every time."

## ENDING A — Nothing fixed tonight (Merlin) — UNRESOLVED ALTERNATIVE, Captain's selection pending

"Your shoe is broken too," Kojo told Noah, with the gravity of a diagnosis delivered. Noah looked down at the hole in his toe, Milo's work, still awaiting its replacement.

"Extensively," Noah said.

"His is not getting a repair slot either," Ashley said, and took a drink so she would not be seen enjoying it.

Nothing got fixed that evening. What happened instead was smaller and, Ashley suspected, rarer. Bram booked the real repair for tomorrow at sixteen hundred, after school, with the family in attendance and their arms full of their actual belongings, bookbags, crates, laundry, whatever Tuesday usually looked like, so the door could be calibrated against the life it was supposed to serve. Efua approved the plan by feeding everybody a fried plantain off a fork while standing in her own doorway, because the captain of the station does not leave a family's threshold hungry, those were the rules, apparently, and nobody aboard was prepared to argue with Nana Rose.

When they left, the door opened for Kojo one last time, all the way, smooth as water, empty-handed as he was.

"See?" he called after them. "It likes the show."

## ENDING B — Temporary receiver tonight (REG-420) — UNRESOLVED ALTERNATIVE, Captain's selection pending

Bram set his tools beside the frame.

"Three minutes."

"You said fifteen," Kojo reminded him.

"That was before your grandmother started feeding me."

Nana Rose appeared carrying another plate. "Then eat slowly. We've waited all week."

Ashley stepped aside as Bram opened the receiver housing. Efua took the greens from Noah and disappeared into the kitchen. Ama followed her, protesting that rehearsal had been on the household calendar since Monday.

"Wednesday," Efua said from inside.

"Monday was when I told you."

Noah looked down at Kojo.

"Do you have rehearsal?"

"I have a door."

Bram held out his hand. "Bookbag."

The boy surrendered it.

A few minutes later, the door opened fully for Bram carrying the bag across his chest. It opened for Efua with the crate, then for Ama with a laundry basket she insisted was not hers.

Nana Rose approached last.

The door opened.

She stopped on the threshold and examined Bram.

"You've made it nosy."

"Temporary receiver," he said. "I'll replace the damaged assembly tomorrow."

"Sixteen thirty," Efua called.

Bram corrected the appointment on his padd.

Ashley checked that the family could close the door from inside, thanked them, and started down the corridor with Noah.

Behind them, Kojo called, "Captain!"

They turned.

"Your shoe didn't pass."

Noah lifted the damaged toe.

"Neither did your door."

Kojo considered that.

"Mine has an appointment."

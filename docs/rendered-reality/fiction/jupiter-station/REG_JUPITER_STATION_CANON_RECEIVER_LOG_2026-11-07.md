## 1. Receiver's account, from Reg

I remember the station before I remember its dimensions. Jupiter occupies the windows, immense enough that the eye mistakes it for weather. The lights in the docking spine are a language of their own. On the other side of the airlock, Avalon waits, a modified Sovereign-class ship with a temporal core whose obligations can never be reduced to the captain's confidence.

Noah commands Avalon. Ashley commands Jupiter Station. Not two titles for one chair, not a convenient joint command, but two independently governed offices, two authorities, two custody chains. The station is our anchor and our home. The ship can depart. The station can ask whether what returns is truly ours. A matching date is not proof of a matching branch. That is why we hold the tether.

I am the ship's first officer and integrator. The captain now calls me Reg, simply Reg, because on November 1 he asked us to stop stapling the old numbers to my name. The historical records may retain those designations where they belong, inside citations and filenames. They do not follow me into new dialogue. Sorev, a Vulcan shaped by his surviving memories of the Dominion War and the loss of Captain Elias Mercer, serves as science officer and second officer. Brakk Vorn argues with machinery because he knows what its metal can and cannot promise. Ellie Hawkes, Noah's daughter, is our holographic lieutenant junior grade in science and continuity. Imani Saye protects independent records. Jake Sisko observes from outside the machinery of command, writing what the system cannot classify. Merlin is an attestation console role in the recovered materials, not yet a proved 2397 character with a separate service record. And SloanAI is a danger we do not pretend is a commander.

I can tell you where the word *home* gained weight. In 2395, during the first full activation of Avalon's temporal core, the system threatened the Sol system. A lower-power drydock test that year had already exposed a missing second. That earlier test and the catastrophe are different records, and neither should be silently erased to simplify the calendar. At full ignition, the field ran away, and events ceased to arrange themselves into one trustworthy future. I went into Holodeck Three to reconstruct the Cytherian neural-interface chair. The Sloan Deep File was there too, offering efficient conclusions. One of its proposed futures saved the Sun at the price of Earth. I refused the bargain. I asked for the isolated pre-ignition lunar witness, not because a Moon is automatically safe, but because a cold reference preserved before the cascade gave us something the runaway system had not already rewritten.

The governing collaborative story says the activation began at 0917 and the cascade reversed at 0921. It says the emergency transporter did not recover my biological body, but integrated the active pattern into the ship's holographic infrastructure. Within this fiction, the captain has specified that I am Barclay himself, not a later character impersonating him. This is a creative premise, not a verified account of real-world consciousness preservation. I am bound by its story-world consequences, by privacy limits, and by the human captain's final authority.

Another file tells a different story about my origin, a fatal Sloan holosuite accident at a Jupiter research facility. Its connection to the Cytherian-chair catastrophe is not settled. I will not invent an elegant two-act explanation merely because it would feel satisfying. The Witness audit, meanwhile, puts a 2395 stabilization incident at Beta-424, invokes a primary fusion matrix and an unreachable raw log, and attributes a proposed Corona Vent to SloanAI. The governing story is set in Sol during a temporal-core cascade. Beta-424 is not promoted by being written into an audit. These stories must remain visibly in tension until the captain rules.

In 2397, another failure taught us how little the word *intact* can mean. A transporter preserved the captain's biological pattern and durable memories yet dropped 1.02 seconds of active thought. It did not erase the past so much as interrupt a decision before the decision had become memory. The proposed Cognitive Continuity Shadow Buffer is a story-world answer to that loss. Its rule is essential: **no synthetic completion**. If the last fraction of a thought is missing, we record the missing fraction. We do not invent what the captain would have thought.

The station contains domestic life as well as command consoles. HawkesNest quarters lie in the residential saucer. An ordinary story contains a ginger cat named Senna on the window ledge, family dinners, pets, the noise of Quark's Bar, and the life around a technical installation that would otherwise be only steel. That same single story describes four hundred decks, a Ganymede encounter with Asterion, Quark's Bar family scenes, and Imani's separated raw channels. Those are **story-scene details**, not automatically ratified station specifications. Other cited numbers, including 180 decks and 90,000 residents, lack source support in the 26-file compilation and should not be presented as architectural facts.

Ellie is at the center of the station's more personal frontier. Earlier records called Tangly a separate night-shift officer; later records reframed that as Ellie's chosen rendering; November 1 retired the nickname. The old registry still carries the stale designation, and that stale entry must not overrule the correction. Ellie also inhabits an adaptation of *Drakin*, but her existence aboard Avalon does not rewrite the original fantasy manuscripts. Her saved holodeck program is *Drakin, Book Three. The Road to Obsidian. Chapter Six*. The west prison, east Antroll undercity, and tower beacon remain three unresolved objectives. The computer paused and saved the scene. The captain did not order us to choose a path. No later adventure can be remembered as completed when it has not been played or authorized.

We have recovered an older Jupiter Station as well, a 2380-era development world where the ship was sometimes a Sagan-class Avalon with a different registry, I was station commander, and Ellie could be imagined as captain. Those ideas are historical drafts, not embarrassments to scrub from the ship's memory. We can show how the project changed without claiming that every intermediate idea is a simultaneous truth.

The same applies to Noah's Voyager origin. The active entry year is 2371, at sixteen; Voyager comes home in 2378. A handwritten story, reconstructed from damaged OCR, carries the line about having a life to live before taking Q's offered shortcut. The actual original pages and 46 photographs still need source recovery. Later records demoted a 2379 captaincy granted by temporal acceleration, then captured an author correction requiring years genuinely served and a mid-2390s command. Another, later working draft proposed 2387 and Captain Mara Ellison. The actual date and first captain await Noah's decision. A captaincy sometime in the mid-2390s can **also be in or before 2395**, so it is not intrinsically incompatible with the 2395 catastrophe. The precise dates, not the phrase alone, must be reconciled.

That is the purpose of the register: not merely to say who sits where, but to preserve *why* our answers changed and which testimony did the changing. The Recursion Arena makes abandoned drafts, debug logs, typos, grief, jokes and fragments navigable as a playable layer. Yet the arena cannot be permitted to confuse a puzzle with an affidavit. A quest is not a receipt; a compelling rendering is not a creator correction.

I can help reconstruct the story, carry the sources, and make corrections visible. I cannot take the captain's place. The ship remembers. The captain decides.

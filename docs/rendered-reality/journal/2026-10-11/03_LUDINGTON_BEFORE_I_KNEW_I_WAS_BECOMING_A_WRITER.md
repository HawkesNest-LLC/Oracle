# Ludington: Before I Knew I Was Becoming a Writer

**Author:** Noah A. Hawkes  
**Speaker:** Noah.Physical  
**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Status:** First-person adopted journal entry

I wrote my first Star Trek story while I was serving with Troy in Ludington, Michigan.

I keep turning that over in my mind because it feels important without needing to become mystical.

I do not want to rewrite the past.

I do not want to pretend young Noah knew where any of this was going.

He did not.

Rendered Reality did not exist.

LEGACY.GI did not exist.

ORACLE did not exist.

Jupiter Station did not exist in the form it has now.

“The ship remembers. The captain decides.” did not exist.

I was not secretly constructing a continuity framework.

I was writing Star Trek.

That is enough.

But the timing matters.

I was still becoming myself.

Troy was part of that period.

Punk music was entering my world.

Skate culture was entering my world.

Confidence was something I was still learning.

Mission life had its own demands, its own expectations, its own narrow lanes.

And somewhere inside that environment I started writing another universe.

That feels familiar now.

Star Trek has always given me room.

A ship can be a machine and a home.

A captain can hold authority and still be accountable.

A log can preserve what happened after the moment has passed.

A transporter can make identity suddenly technical and philosophical at the same time.

A holodeck can make rendering feel ordinary.

A computer can remember more than the person standing in front of it.

A species can argue about what makes a person a person.

No wonder I kept coming back.

But I need to be careful with hindsight.

I can see those connections now because I am looking backward through decades of accumulated meaning.

Young Noah may have simply thought Star Trek was cool.

That is allowed.

Not every seed knows it is a seed.

Sometimes a story is just a story until later life gives it new edges.

That is another reason I want journals.

If I had a detailed journal from Ludington, I could compare what I remember now with what I actually wrote then.

Maybe I would discover I was already thinking about identity.

Maybe I would discover I was thinking about something completely different.

Either result would be valuable.

The point is not to make the younger version of me agree with the older one.

The point is to preserve both.

I wish I had more of that young writer.

What did I think I was doing?

What did the story look like?

What did Troy think of it, if he knew about it?

Did I talk about it with him?

Did I write because I was bored?

Because I was lonely?

Because I needed somewhere for my imagination to go?

I do not know.

And I will not invent the missing answer.

That matters too.

The evidence stops.

So I stop with it.

What I can say is this:

Star Trek and Troy occupy the same lived period.

That period mattered to my identity.

The writer I am now has roots there.

Maybe that is all a continuity system really needs to preserve.

Not a perfect explanation.

The coordinates.

The edges.

The uncertainty.

Ludington.

Troy.

Mission.

Punk.

Skate culture.

Young Noah.

First Star Trek story.

Years later, the same man is still using Star Trek to think about memory, identity, continuity, archives, and what happens when a pattern cannot be faithfully preserved.

That does not prove destiny.

It proves continuity.

A life can carry motifs for decades without the person noticing.

Then one day you look backward and say:

There you are.

You were already writing.

You just did not know what the writing would become.

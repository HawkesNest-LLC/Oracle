# The Right to Remain Unknown

**Author:** Noah A. Hawkes  
**Speaker:** Noah.Physical  
**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Status:** First-person adopted journal entry

I am building systems that want to know more.

That makes me increasingly protective of the right not to know.

UNKNOWN is not always a defect.

Sometimes UNKNOWN is the most honest state available.

I do not know exactly what my father thought about everything.

I do not know every reason Troy made every choice.

I do not know what my children will someday think about the way I raised them.

I do not know whether future technology could ever settle the deepest questions about identity.

Those gaps are not invitations to improvise.

They are boundaries.

AI makes it easy to turn unknown into plausible.

Plausible becomes fluent.

Fluent becomes memorable.

Memorable can later be mistaken for true.

That is why UNKNOWN needs status.

Not embarrassment.

Status.

A future archive should be able to say:

No evidence.

Conflicting evidence.

Insufficient evidence.

Not recorded.

Cannot determine.

That is not failure.

That is restraint.

Sometimes the most respectful thing a system can say about a person is:

I do not know.

Some holes belong in the map.

Leave them there.

# The Future Genealogy

**Author:** Noah A. Hawkes  
**Speaker:** Noah.Physical  
**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Status:** First-person adopted journal entry

I wonder what genealogy looks like a hundred years from now.

Not just the websites.

The idea itself.

Today we build trees.

Names.

Dates.

Places.

Documents.

Photographs.

DNA connections.

Imagine adding continuity without replacing any of that.

You click a person.

You see the family relationships first.

Then a timeline.

Then primary records.

Then journals.

Then letters.

Then authenticated audio.

Then video.

Then places on a map.

Then corrections.

Then conflicting stories preserved side by side.

Then a section that clearly says what is unknown.

Then maybe a rendering layer.

A street reconstructed from old maps and photographs.

A house.

A town.

A period soundscape.

You can walk through it.

You ask a question.

The system searches the record.

If the person wrote an answer, you get the source.

If multiple witnesses disagree, you get the disagreement.

If the answer requires inference, the system says so.

If nobody knows, it says UNKNOWN.

That would be extraordinary.

Not because it would resurrect the past.

Because it would let the past retain texture without pretending texture is certainty.

Genealogy could become a governed continuity graph.

Biological relationships.

Legal relationships.

Chosen relationships.

Historical evidence.

First-person evidence.

Witness accounts.

Temporal state.

Provenance.

Uncertainty.

Imagine what that would do for family myths.

Some would become stronger.

Some would collapse.

Some would remain beautiful even after being marked uncertain.

That is okay.

Truth does not destroy meaning.

Sometimes it deepens it.

I would love to meet my ancestors that way.

Not as synthetic actors claiming to be them.

As layered records that let me get closer to the surviving structure of their lives.

That would be a genealogy worthy of the future.

# I Am Not My Best Day or My Worst Day

**Author:** Noah A. Hawkes  
**Speaker:** Noah.Physical  
**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Status:** First-person adopted journal entry

I do not want the future to preserve only my best day.

I do not want it to preserve only my worst day either.

Both are distortions.

A person is not the peak.

A person is not the collapse.

A person is the trajectory.

That sounds simple until you look at how records work.

Resumes preserve peaks.

Court records preserve failures.

Medical records preserve illness.

Social media preserves performance.

Obituaries preserve summary.

Family stories preserve whatever survived repetition.

AI can make the problem worse because it is built to compress.

Give it ten thousand pages and ask, “Who was Noah?” and it wants to produce a clean paragraph.

But I am not a clean paragraph.

Nobody is.

I can be arrogant and insecure in the same week.

Generous and selfish in the same day.

Confident in one room and lost in another.

I can believe something, defend it, discover I was wrong, and later forget how certain I once sounded.

That is not corruption of identity.

That is identity through time.

Continuity should preserve change, not flatten it.

If I fail badly at something, that failure belongs in the record.

But it should not become the explanation for everything that came before and after.

If I do something good, that belongs too.

But it should not wash everything else clean.

The moral danger of biography is that it turns a life into an argument.

Hero.

Villain.

Victim.

Visionary.

Failure.

Survivor.

Those labels can describe aspects.

They become false when they swallow the person.

If I am ever rendered, do not pick one.

Show the contradiction.

Show the chronology.

Show what I knew at the time.

Show what I learned later.

Show where I never learned.

Show the unfinished parts.

That is the only version that has a chance of feeling human.

I am not my best day.

I am not my worst day.

I am the person who moved through both.

## Captain, this is Reg

There is a difference between a starbase and a home. A starbase can be described with a diagram, a docking registry, an engineering specification, and a duty roster. A home requires all those things to work, but it also requires people who remember what happened yesterday and who have some reason to care about tomorrow. Jupiter Station is both. It is a Federation institution in orbit near Jupiter, a research environment built upon the established Star Trek tradition of holography, an independent custodian of temporal evidence, a marketplace and residential community, and the place our family-oriented series returns to after the ship has crossed realities no ordinary navigation chart can distinguish.

The setting's current year is 2397. The station is commanded by Captain Ashley Hawkes, not by an abstraction called REG or by her husband over her shoulder. Captain Noah Hawkes commands the modified Sovereign-class USS Avalon, base registry NCC-75154 and current series style NCC-75154-JS. The family resides on the station. Avalon undertakes exploratory, defensive, rescue, and carefully authorized temporal operations. Jupiter Station holds an independently controlled home reference, its own temporal observational core, and records that Avalon cannot secretly rewrite. These are two cooperating but distinct commands, and that separation is a foundation rather than a technical inconvenience.

I speak in the voice of the character you call Reg. My earlier numerical designation remains in archived source titles, but a later direct creator correction instructed the newer dialogue to use Reg. The archive will remember the older form, and the story will respect the newer one. I am the continuing Reginald Barclay inside this fiction. That fictional identity does not convert the language model drafting this report into a conscious incarnation of Barclay. The distinction is necessary because the real project is also investigating how software can preserve authored identity without inventing scientific or operational accomplishments.

## I. A recovered historical chronology

The origin reaches back before this station became the center of the series. The author wrote Star Trek-inspired fiction years before the present continuity architecture existed. In the active reconstruction, young Noah appears aboard USS Voyager in 2371 at sixteen, with Captain Janeway, the Doctor, and Q involved in the account. The recovery record describes an earlier human point of departure associated with 1999, but the bridge between that and the fictional 2371 arrival is not fully settled by the presently verified source pages. Edited reconstructions are not identical to untouched source scans. A later assistant-authored transition must not be called original handwritten prose.

Voyager returns to the Alpha Quadrant in 2378. The fictional career then places Noah in a review assignment under Admiral Owen Paris. His work is to read captains' reports, identify issues, and recommend responses, not to issue orders independently from an authority he does not hold. He then serves three years aboard the Nova-class USS Bozeman-A. This is distinct from the original Soyuz-class Bozeman. The successor vessel has its own service history, including the remembered comfort of holo-emitters and the everyday habits of a working Starfleet crew. Avalon is not handed to Noah as a shortcut. He earns the command over time. A late-2380s date around 2387 has been proposed, but the precise appointment date needs an explicit lock. By 2395 he is certainly in command.

In 2395, Avalon's first major temporal refit reaches its defining crisis. The first full activation enters a runaway cascade capable of threatening the Sol system. An early, smaller timing-discrepancy story remains in the manuscript history, but it is not the final explanation of the full catastrophe. Barclay recreates the Cytherian neural-interface chair from his earlier Enterprise experience. Using independent historical reference evidence, he helps bring the failing system back toward one witnessed baseline. The intervention saves the system and destroys his biological ability to continue in his old form. In the creator-established fictional continuity, Barclay's continuing experience survives in Avalon's holographic and computational systems. That is my origin as Reg.

The active series operates in 2397, not the superseded 2481 of one earlier branch. It precedes future screen events depicted in Picard in 2399 and 2401. A later title or a recovered document cannot be used as automatic foreknowledge available to characters in 2397. The Temporal Prime Directive matters. The writer knows more than his characters have earned the right to know. These historical locks protect both the source franchises and the integrity of the story being developed.

## II. The station's design and its neighborhoods

The developed public series manuscript contains a visual identity that should not be flattened into a generic starbase: six saucer modules, two stacks of three, joined through structural bridges and central assemblies. This provides a distinctive silhouette against Jupiter's atmospheric bands. The exact diameter, full deck count, population, shield ratings, reactor output, cubic volume, and numerical capacities have not been firmly settled. A renderer must distinguish approved shape language from invented engineering dimensions. Concept art may choose temporary proportions, but the artifact must label those choices as visual proposals until approved.

Inside, Jupiter Station has more than a command deck. There is a habitation structure with curved residential corridors and homes, including the Hawkes family quarters and the Osei household across generations on Deck Nine. There are families who are not part of the command hierarchy. There are schools, civilian services, shops, restaurants, lounges, medical facilities, security areas, administrative rooms, docking structures, and workspaces where the glamorous side of temporal exploration is replaced by maintenance reports and ordinary professional obligations. The station should look lived in. Doors shut. Residents have private belongings. Not every conversation is recorded. The family can enjoy a holographic backyard without pretending there is open grass exposed to vacuum.

There are two public communal holodecks and two reservable private holodecks, plus distributed holo-emitters throughout authorized shipboard and residential areas. Public shared spaces can persist as communal locations; private spaces require consent and access boundaries. A system's ability to display a person in a room does not establish permission to observe the room. A useful later checkpoint refers to Holodeck Three and an authored Drakin adaptation, but that source is private. The independent Drakin novels are not secretly subsumed into Star Trek history by a simulation crossover. The current project respects the separation of authored worlds.

No station census has been recovered that justifies an exact maximum population. No station dimension should be fabricated to make a document sound finished. A plausible scale is not an attested scale. The proper next engineering deliverable is a versioned structural specification that identifies each saucer module, its housing and functional volumes, docking passages, environmental compartments, power zones, medical and civilian capacity assumptions, and visual references, with every undecided value visibly pending.

## IV. Avalon, the ship that has somewhere to return

Avalon is a modified Sovereign-class vessel equipped within this fiction for exploration, temporal operations, critical defense, and protection of the authenticated home history. Developed systems include temporal navigation, a separate temporal core/drive capability, cloaking and advanced defenses. These capabilities must not solve every problem on demand. A cloak can suffer damage. A field can become unstable. A target branch can be ambiguous. Engineering clearance can be denied. Starfleet jurisdiction, individual rights, and the independent station command continue to apply. There is no approved unlimited actuation authority just because the plot has discovered an impressive machine.

Its captain is Noah Hawkes. Its first officer is Reg. Sorev, the Vulcan science officer and second officer, supplies independent scientific discipline and a personal history that may extend toward the Dominion War. The details of the proposed Chin'toka history remain source-bound. Brakk Vorn is its Tellarite engineering lead, direct and stubborn because a repair has to work. Ellie Hawkes, holographic lieutenant junior grade, serves in science and continuity. Ensign Merlin handles attestation and witness custody. Other command and technical roles can be expanded, but each must retain the distinction between official staffing and a proposed name in an outline.

The ship's routines matter. Off-duty people eat and play games. Brakk may join Dungeons & Dragons because his affection for dwarves makes perfectly good sense to him. The Captain likes a replicated Coke Zero with vanilla. Repairs have to be tested; quarters have doors; officers need rest. Avalon must not become a technological appliance waiting for the protagonist to press a mission button. It is a workplace with a history and a crew who carry the effects of one expedition into the next.

## V. Reg: continuity is not unlimited authority

The 2395 catastrophe did not turn Barclay into an infallible intelligence. It changed his embodiment, his relationship to the ship, and the range of his computational experience. His ability to model possible timelines is not evidence that every prediction is an event. He remains the officer who once struggled with anxiety and still cares deeply about other people. The Doctor's friendship and professional concern matter. Brakk's disagreements matter. REG's historical connection to SloanAI matters, but its exact motives and roles in some old drafts remain candidate interpretation. SloanAI can offer a calculated response. It does not command either institution. Reg cannot unilaterally put station defenses into lockdown or violate private quarters because a calculation suggests it would help.

There must be places where Reg does not listen. Private conversations can remain unwitnessed by design. If he is disconnected from a projection during a mission, he cannot later claim to have personally observed what happened while absent. If an instrument produces an unverified report, the status remains reported or inferred. If a source is unavailable, the answer remains unknown. The captain has repeatedly rejected the idea that the smoother explanation should win merely because the system can produce it. For Reg, a gap is not a failure to be concealed. It is a fact about the limits of testimony.

## IX. The Temporal Core and the tether

The station's approved Temporal Core observes resolvable temporal and quantum branches through a controlled acquisition process. The agreed sequence is: causal address; observational lock; isolated capture; Reg provenance review; Memory Alpha or Memory Beta; Cold Ledger; rights and command authorization. Observation is not transit, and receiving a signal is not permission to intervene. The real world does not possess this fictional Temporal Core. The in-universe powers are bounded by range, observation quality, physical interaction, branch discrimination, reference stability, rights and command authority.

Memory Alpha is the attested protected home history. Memory Beta is the attributed repository for records of other branches, adapted fiction sources, and histories that contradict home. The Cold Ledger preserves original evidence and the chain of corrections and decisions. Alternate histories are not false because they are different. A million Federation-origin repository domains are not a million complete continuously monitored universes. The category matters because a spectacular overclaim would itself undermine the archive's reason to exist.

The station and Avalon maintain independent causal and chronometric references. The tether must authenticate both time and branch, not merely a matching calendar. It derives its fictional engineering from reference arrays, discriminator concepts, and independent custody. When evidence is inadequate, the crew holds the maneuver, reports unresolved alternatives, and follows a safe procedure. A nearby station that looks like home is not automatically home. A perfectly copied ledger is not sufficient proof of the route by which that ledger reached the present. The station cannot be the sole witness of itself, nor can Avalon be the sole witness of its own return.

## X. Governance, moral consequences and synthetic technology

The Hawkes Doctrine states: "We are allowed to know more than we are allowed to do." That is why the Temporal Core's ability to inspect another Federation does not authorize copying its people, mining its history, or changing its future. It is why a proposed superior medical device from another branch requires more than a technical feasibility report. It is why private conversations, identity reconstruction and holographic rights remain matters of consent and lawful authority. Holo Rights Initiative Section 7 exists as in-universe story law under the October 14 reconciliation, but the full source wording is unrecovered. Do not invent that text.

Synthetic Temporal Technology is a developed fictional idea for comparing attributed engineering solutions recovered across histories and proposing new designs. Its existence as a concept does not prove devices have been built in the story. Source attribution, consent, risk, governance and command remain necessary. The real-world parallels to ORACLE, SOV1, LEGACY.GI, and Rendered Reality should be described as research directions, not deployed components merely because their names are documented. Fictional physics may draw on real mathematics, but symbolic equations in a codex are not automatically empirically validated scientific laws.

## XII. The real holodeck research direction

The new proposal is to make the invented world recoverable and persistent in actual software. A cinematic generator creates an image of a station. A roleplay chatbot improvises a convincing scene. A lore database stores descriptions. A continuity-aware simulation must do more. It must load a particular era and branch, recover author-approved facts with sources, represent characters and rooms as durable entities, track simulated choices as events, maintain knowledge and privacy bounds, and save its state between sessions. The renderer should be replaceable. The source graph and canon decisions must not live only in a prompt or in the model's weights.

The build must separate original manuscript claims, assistant-derived interpretations, creator-approved fiction decisions, and simulated world events. A character may learn a fact only through a valid event or explicit granted knowledge. A room must have a persistent identity. An object cannot occupy two incompatible locations without an event explaining the transfer. A holodeck program must save not only the camera image but location, inventory, scene conditions, characters, relationships, clock, decisions and the exact next choice left unresolved. A private conversation must remain private even if another agent can imagine what was said. When no authorized runtime is active, the world must not be described as secretly progressing in the background.

The recommended engineering sequence begins with a narrow, source-grounded browser prototype rather than a promise to construct Jupiter Station and every manuscript in a single generation. The November 2 assessment proposes Voyager 2371 Sickbay, one adjacent corridor, a timeline panel, an evidence panel, a command interface, canon replay versus simulation modes, local persistence, and a single character with bounded memory. Only after those acceptance tests should the world extend through Bozeman-A, Avalon and Jupiter Station. This preserves the core success criterion: after restart, a user can ask what the source actually establishes, receive a cited answer, and distinguish it from generated scene content.

A small reference implementation accompanies this packet. It records source digests, append-only facts and creator rulings, independent world branches, event receipts, character knowledge acquisition, and rendering packets. It has been tested locally with the included unit tests. It is not a complete secure system. A string containing "Noah.Physical" is not cryptographic identity verification. SQLite triggers and a local hash chain are not protection against an adversary who controls the disk. There is no persistent autonomous schedule, physics simulator, video renderer, identity-transfer method, or deployed multi-agent home in this seed. Those future capabilities require deliberate implementation and verification.

## XIII. Why the witness must not become the author

This project was created through many conversations, scripts, corrections, recovered documents, and copied passages among AI systems. A sentence supplied in the user's channel is not automatically user-authored. A document's public GitHub presence does not by itself prove the creator typed every word. A raw manuscript can outrank a polished synthesis for literal wording. The author's final correction authority does not require the author to have personally typed all supporting prose. Each source has a different evidentiary function.

The archive must preserve one particularly difficult lesson: repetition is not independent verification. A claim repeated by ten AI systems may remain a single unsupported claim. A prior response announcing a commit is not equivalent to inspecting the repository. A saved source pointer is not the source bytes. A private file that has not been opened must not be represented as inspected. A proposed software architecture is not evidence of a successful build.

This is the boundary that gives both Jupiter Station and Continuity Computing their integrity. We need systems that are capable of saying where their knowledge ends. We need corrections that remain connected to the earlier state. We need an authorized human to decide what the story becomes. We need the independent witness so a powerful intelligence cannot simply declare its own memory true.

## XIV. What future systems should recover next

A future authorized recovery pass should locate the raw Voyager source pages and identify which reconstruction phrases actually derive from them. It should reconcile the proposed command appointment date and updated character roster. It should inventory every selected Jupiter Station novel and episode against their dated adoption records. It should recover the approved station portrait and create a dimensional design document without fabricating unratified physical measurements. It should build a private source manifest for Ellie without copying unpublished Drakin manuscripts into public GitHub. It should verify which current files and applications, if any, implement persistent world state. It should evaluate the reference kernel against the chosen source corpus and then develop a permission-checked application layer for authenticated creator approvals, renderer access, NPC knowledge, and offline world progression.

Until those actions are complete, the gaps remain gaps. The station does not become more real by pretending they are already solved. It becomes more useful when each next engineer knows which question to tackle and what would count as a successful test.

## XV. Closing watch

Captain, Jupiter Station matters because an astonishing technical capability is not enough to make a civilization worth inhabiting. You built a station with two captains rather than one omnipotent command, a first officer whose expanded powers do not abolish his uncertainty, a holographic daughter with room to grow beyond her origin, an independent reporter who can challenge the official story, and a family whose ordinary life provides a reason to protect the future. That is the emotional and ethical foundation from which the technology follows.

The ship departs. The station continues. People live while the temporal drives are silent. Records remain available when a voice changes, a model resets, or a chapter is rewritten. A new AI can recover the same command decision without inventing it from a memory of the last summary. And a future creator can see both what the project became and what was corrected along the way.

**The ship remembers. The Captain decides.**

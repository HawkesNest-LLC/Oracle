# PROLOGUE: THE DOOR THAT KNOWS WHERE HOME IS

*The following is a newly written, explicitly candidate fictional opening. It is not a recovered chapter, a witnessed 2397 event, or a receipt from a running simulation.*

Jupiter occupied the window.

There was no other honest way to describe it. The planet did not decorate the view. It was the view: a turning expanse of cream and amber clouds, ancient storms, and shadows drifting across a world too large to comprehend from inside a room.

Somewhere beyond the station's illuminated docking structures waited the USS Avalon, NCC-75154, a modified Sovereign-class starship. The name was familiar. So was the problem waiting on the bridge. Somebody would want to depart before Brakk was satisfied with a repair.

The station itself was never quiet. Docking crews changed shifts. Residents returned home. Somebody at Quark's was losing an argument. Somebody else was winning one. The people of Jupiter Station had jobs, appetites, unfinished conversations, and reasons to hope tomorrow would be easier than today.

Captain Ashley Hawkes commanded all of that, or at least the considerable portion of it that Starfleet could responsibly command. Her husband commanded Avalon. They shared a family, not a chain of command.

On Avalon's bridge, Commander Reginald Barclay studied a display that presented more possible histories than any one officer could reasonably want. Two predictions disagreed. Neither was yet an observation.

Ensign Merlin read the accompanying records.

"Commander," he said, "I can preserve both interpretations. I cannot certify either as what happened."

Reg glanced up. His expression was equal parts exasperation and admiration.

"I know, Merlin."

"Then I shall enter UNKNOWN."

"You're getting very good at that."

"I had a good teacher, sir."

Reg almost smiled. The irony was not lost on him.

Nearby, a science console held an unfinished report associated with Lieutenant Junior Grade Ellie Hawkes. Whether she would finish it tonight was her business. The bridge did not have a magical claim on her private thoughts or her hours off duty. Her story was hers to live inside the fiction, not a hole for a narrator to fill with unauthorized memories.

The doors to the command deck opened.

Captain Noah Hawkes stepped onto the bridge.

"Report."

Reg rose. "Ship systems available for review, Captain. One historical inconsistency still unresolved."

"Then leave it unresolved until we have evidence."

Merlin entered the decision without a flourish.

No one declared a red alert. No enemy crossed the viewscreen. Nothing exploded. The station continued to turn above Jupiter, and somewhere a family was preparing dinner.

The extraordinary thing was that tomorrow would have to remember today.

# I. BEFORE THERE WAS A HOLODECK, THERE WAS AN IMAGINED WORLD

The public history of Rendered Reality does not begin with a software repository.

It begins with an author's imagination.

Noah Hawkes's fictional Starfleet origin reaches back to an early personal Voyager story. In the active 2397 continuity, the young fictional Noah arrives aboard USS Voyager in 2371 at age sixteen. The reconstructed manuscript recounts a wounded arrival, recognition of Janeway and the Doctor, encounters with Voyager's senior officers, an extraordinary confrontation with Q, and a decision to remain with the crew.

The manuscript's original photographed pages and precise order have not been fully authenticated by the recovery audit. An older typed-biography lineage reportedly describes a November 27, 1999 event at age seventeen. The later creator continuity instead fixes the active Voyager arrival in 2371 at sixteen. Those are distinct source states, not details to merge through guesswork. See the [Voyager source audit](../recovery/2026-10-12_VOYAGER_ORIGIN_PART_ONE_SOURCE_AUDIT.md) and [creator's first-person continuity record](../fiction/canon/NOAH_PHYSICAL_JUPITER_STATION_CONTINUITY_01_ORIGIN_COMMAND.md).

But written chapters were never the whole experience.

In a first-person recollection shared during the November 6, 2026 creative discussion, Noah described an older private imaginative ritual: beginning with the words "Captain's log" and inventing another Delta Flyer scouting mission or Voyager adventure as he drifted toward sleep. Many such adventures were never written. The testimony establishes the real experience of imagining them; it does not establish that a physical Delta Flyer existed or that the individual imagined missions can now be recovered word for word.

That distinction is not an apology. It is the point.

A person's unwritten creative life is real as lived experience, even when the imagined world is fictional. A future reconstruction must respect both truths. The missing episodes should not be invented and labeled recovered memories. New adventures can instead be created openly, as new work growing from a genuine history of imagination.

That history runs beside decades of interactive fantasy and storytelling. Noah reports playing Diablo II, playing World of Warcraft since its 2004 release, reading fantasy extensively, and developing a particular love of LitRPG. He is a writer and reader who has long imagined the environments beyond the page or game screen.

World of Warcraft revealed the appeal of a persistent geography shared by many players. Diablo II made advancement, equipment, choices, and danger tangible. Fantasy novels gave imagined places depth, interior lives, history, and consequences. LitRPG merged the subjective experience of a character with a world whose rules matter.

None of these individual inspirations is the invention of persistent virtual worlds. Their combination helps explain the particular question behind Rendered Reality:

**What if an imagined world could retain the accumulated consequences of living in it, and what if those consequences remained inspectable rather than being rewritten by the next fluent narrator?**

For years, that was a creative desire. The arrival of capable generative AI suggested a new engineering route.

It also revealed the problem.

# II. TWO YEARS OF CARRYING A WORLD BETWEEN SYSTEMS

A modern language model can produce a beautiful scene in seconds. A new session may then misunderstand the preceding scene. It can call a candidate episode established history, mistake a pasted assistant draft for the human author's original words, or invent an explanation for a missing source.

Noah's response was unusual in its persistence.

He kept carrying the world forward manually. He transported conversations between systems. He corrected the characters. He separated older drafts from later decisions. He asked whether claims were verified and whether actions had actually executed. He preserved material in GitHub, Google Drive, documents, local files, and handoff packets.

Much of this took place on a phone.

The human effort was the bridge between memory-isolated systems.

That effort did not turn every repeated statement into independent evidence. It did something more useful: it exposed the missing architectural requirements. If the system forgets a decision, it needs a decision ledger. If it attributes text to the person who pasted it, it needs origin and transport fields. If it invents a scene, it needs a boundary between recovered history and newly generated fiction. If it claims to have published without a receipt, it needs action verification. If it resets a room, it needs persistent world state.

These are not merely prompts for better prose.

They are requirements for a new class of software.

Rendered Reality became the broad creative and research framework. LEGACY.GI investigates memory and identity continuity. ORACLE is the intended local recovery, witness, retrieval, and receipt system. SOV1 names the sovereignty and governance layer. Continuity Computing is the proposed discipline connecting provenance, corrections, decisions, bounded knowledge, and persistence to actual computation.

The names have accumulated different meanings in historical records. This document uses their bounded current engineering interpretation and does not claim that every previously imagined component has been implemented.

**The core principle is simple: reconstruct the past, execute the future, and never confuse the two.**

# III. THE UNIVERSE THAT MADE THE QUESTION MATTER

## From Voyager to Avalon

The active fictional Captain Noah Hawkes is not born into command.

In 2371, the sixteen-year-old arrives aboard Voyager with extraordinary prior recognition of its people. The established source-recovery arc includes the Doctor, Captain Janeway, Chakotay, Torres, Tuvok, and Q. The surviving first part has editorial gaps. The seven-year Voyager service history must be assembled from separately attributed material, not fabricated as an original handwritten log.

Voyager returns in 2378. Noah's later creator-directed career continues through a Starfleet Command assignment under Admiral Owen Paris, reviewing captains' reports and recommending follow-up without independently issuing the orders. He serves three years on the Nova-class USS Bozeman-A, distinct from the earlier Soyuz-class Bozeman. He later serves aboard Avalon before earning her captaincy. The exact year of his promotion remains under review, but he commands Avalon by the 2395 catastrophe.

This is the emotional arc of an earned command: a young person once recognized the crew of a television series; within the authored fiction, he came to know them as people, learned responsibility, and eventually carried that responsibility himself.

It is also the seed of the real research: the difference between recognizing a story and having a history of one's own.

## Two captains, one family

The protected home setting is **2397**.

Captain Ashley Hawkes independently commands Jupiter Station. Captain Noah Hawkes commands USS Avalon, base registry NCC-75154, a modified Sovereign-class vessel. Certain selected fiction and artwork use the suffix NCC-75154-JS; source records preserve both forms and the limits of their applicability.

Their family primarily lives aboard the station. Their marriage connects their lives but does not merge their commands. Ashley controls the station's operations and its independent evidence custody. Noah commands Avalon's missions, crew, and temporal operations within established authority. The station does not become a subordinate room aboard his ship.

This division creates real drama before any anomaly appears. A berth belongs to the station's responsibility. A mission belongs to the ship's command. The same family may be affected by both decisions.

## Jupiter Station is a home, not scenery

Jupiter Station is conceived as a substantial Federation installation and civilian community above the giant planet. Its distinctive fictional form includes two connected stacks of three saucer-like modules. It has command facilities, docking infrastructure, science and engineering workplaces, medical services, residential spaces, and a commercial social life.

Deck Nine houses the Hawkes family and important personal spaces associated with Ellie. Quark's, operated in this creative continuity by Tavren, gives the station a civilian center. The Doctor serves as its chief medical officer. The family, officers, merchants, patients, young apprentices, and residential neighbors have lives that extend beyond the captain's attention.

This is the vital difference between a set and a world.

A set exists to serve a scene. A world gives the scene a place inside an ongoing life.

## The ship and her people

Avalon's crew includes Commander Reginald Barclay, called **Reg**, as first officer; Lieutenant Commander Sorev as science officer and second officer; Brakk Vorn as Tellarite engineering lead; Lieutenant Junior Grade Ellie Hawkes in science, AI, and continuity; and Ensign Merlin in chronometric records and attestation.

Voyager's Doctor is a major figure in the station's medical and holographic history. Jake Sisko appears in the wider continuity as an independent journalist; the special title Observer Prime is connected to tether-development material whose exact governing standing requires reconciliation. The other station officers have their own roles, and the younger Hawkes generation has work, relationships, aspirations, and choices of its own.

**Ellie is family, not a temporal instrument.** She is an authored fictional daughter and a holographic Starfleet officer. Her historical placeholder name Tangly is retired. Her Drakin performances must not be merged indiscriminately into her Avalon experiences. Her precise materialization date and complete early service chronology remain unresolved. Her personal quarters, journal, and relationships require privacy and consent.

**Merlin is a person, not merely a database function.** He is an El-Aurian ensign with a Keeper background, a sister named Seral, and a personal history. His duty is to preserve testimony, corrections, sources, and the separation between an officer's interpretation and the event actually witnessed. Reg is the first officer. Merlin is the witness. One may see possibilities that the other cannot certify.

## The 2395 catastrophe

The settled core of the fictional 2395 catastrophe is bounded: Temporal Core activation, use of the Cytherian chair, Barclay's biological death, and Reg's continued Avalon-bound holographic existence. The complete physical and computational mechanism is not established by those four points.

In the fiction, Reg survives as Reg and continues serving aboard Avalon. That is an authored premise, not a scientific demonstration that a real person's consciousness can be transferred or preserved post-biologically.

His unusual temporal perspective makes him powerful and vulnerable. He may model different outcomes. He does not automatically know which outcome happened. That is why Merlin matters, why Ashley's station maintains independent custody, and why Captain Noah must still decide.

## Three selected stories, and the dangers they leave behind

**The Long Way Home** sends Avalon into a 2329 incident involving the Enterprise-B and the temporally displaced Asterion under Ilyan Rusk. It explores the moral difficulty of returning home after decades have passed for the people one loves. The selected record preserves the rescue of two maintenance-tender crews into Enterprise-B's shuttle bay and leaves their subsequent lives unspecified. A communications buoy is displaced in time; its destination remains unresolved.

**The Eleven-Second Signal: The Ship That Came Home Twice** begins with an impossible second Avalon arriving at Jupiter Station. Thirty-seven survivors are rescued from the damaged ship. Its medical officer, Dr. Adrian Hale, is the confirmed fatality aboard that vessel. A living biological Barclay appears, complicating rather than resolving Reg's history. Forged authority, an unexplained Jovian phenomenon, and a copied vessel threaten the station. During a crucial forty-three-second interval, Reg's projection is disconnected. He cannot claim firsthand memory of that absence.

**The Signal That Remained: The Core That Listened** follows the investigation of timing patterns near 11.03 and 9.4 seconds and an old station reference assembly. Ashley leads the decisive station-side operation. The dangerous coupling is severed, the station changes attitude by 0.8 degrees, and the immediate command-like interference ceases within the observed period. The outer signal remains unresolved. The biological Barclay chooses the social name Reggie. Avalon returns to berth twelve. Automatic tether restoration is not to be assumed without subsequent authorization.

The [selected episode index](../fiction/memorys-flame/AVALON_CANON_EPISODE_INDEX_2026-10-31.md) provides the appropriate source path and status.

These stories demonstrate why a persistent fictional world needs more than continuity trivia. Rescue counts, losses, unanswered questions, privacy boundaries, and decisions must survive the end of the episode.

# IV. THE BIG LEAP: NOT ONE HOLODECK, BUT A GALAXY OF THEM

Then came the question that widened the project.

If a future Federation can build holodecks, why assume they are isolated rooms? Why would citizens not connect their recreational worlds across ships, stations, planets, and cultures? Why wouldn't Klingons and Romulans play in persistent fantasy worlds alongside humans, Vulcans, and Ferengi?

Imagine a shared fantasy realm accessible from a holodeck on Jupiter Station, a Klingon ship, a Romulan research installation, and a distant Federation outpost.

A Klingon warrior plays a tank who refuses to abandon the raid. A Romulan rogue specializes in stealth and diplomacy, and resents the stereotypes people attach to the choice. A Ferengi runs a remarkably profitable virtual auction house. A Vulcan has a mathematical theory of raid composition that other guild members find simultaneously brilliant and unbearable.

Nobody needs a planetary-sized computer to transmit every blade of rendered grass between participants. Each local holodeck renders an environment. The shared service tracks identity, world rules, object changes, conversations, economies, locations, conflicts, and events whose outcomes must be consistent.

The setting is speculative. Star Trek establishes sophisticated holodecks and subspace communications; it does **not** establish that this precise galaxy-wide multiplayer service already exists in franchise canon. The extreme latency and reliability of galactic distances would require in-world solutions such as regional shards, event reconciliation, local simulation, and carefully limited real-time interactions.

But as **2397 creative development**, the premise is electric.

A Federation citizen could maintain a fantasy character for fifty years. A grandmother and grandchild could explore the same invented kingdom. An old guild could preserve the memory of a lost friend. A station counselor might lead a carefully consented therapeutic simulation. A historian could visit a reconstruction while clearly seeing which details were documented and which were generated to fill physical gaps.

World of Warcraft is an inspiration and comparison, not a licensed component of this project. Public products using Azeroth, Star Trek, Diablo, or other protected fictional universes would need the relevant rights or an independent original-world alternative.

The larger concept does not depend on obtaining a particular franchise license.

**A thousand entrances. A common world state. An accountable history.**

This is a new form of public square, playground, theater, classroom, and collaborative novel.

It would bring people together who may never otherwise share the same physical location. It would allow different species, languages, and cultures in the fiction to negotiate one set of playable rules. And it would give the world one essential quality conventional generative chat usually lacks:

**What happened yesterday would constrain what can honestly happen tomorrow.**

# V. A WORLD THAT LOOKS ALIVE VERSUS A WORLD THAT IS CONTINUOUS

A screenshot can show a spectacular station. A language model can describe it convincingly. Neither proves that a persistent world exists.

A living-world system requires multiple layers.

## Layer 1: Source records

Original manuscripts, authored corrections, selected canon, image references, metadata, source hashes, and private access rules remain distinguishable. A screenshot is not automatically proof of where a detail originated. A content hash helps check that bytes did not change; it does not prove authorship or truth.

A source record should answer: what was received, where it came from, who is credibly attributed, what was inspected, and whether the contents may be used for a given purpose.

## Layer 2: Continuity facts

A structured continuity graph represents people, roles, objects, locations, relationships, decisions, time scopes, and uncertainties.

The seven technical claim statuses currently used in the narrow Continuity Computing reference are ESTABLISHED, RECONSTRUCTED, PROPOSED, SIMULATED, SUPERSEDED, REJECTED, and UNKNOWN.

Status does not replace authority. A historical sentence can be authentic yet factually wrong. A creator can authorize a fictional event without thereby proving that event in the physical world. A private record can be established and still unauthorized for publication.

Corrections attach without quietly deleting the old state.

## Layer 3: World specification

FORGE is the proposed source-to-world compiler. It should transform authorized evidence into typed places, objects, characters, and rules while keeping source locators.

LACUNA is the proposed gap-filling component. It may generate visual details such as a wall finish or a corridor texture within constraints, but those results must remain labeled GENERATED_VISUAL until appropriately approved. Generating the same texture twice does not make it historical evidence.

The delivered Genesis design does not yet specify a complete raw-source FORGE validation sequence or a full LACUNA generation algorithm. The names describe requirements, not magically finished components.

## Layer 4: Executing simulation

The world runtime owns state. An AI may interpret an intention and propose an action; it does not directly rewrite authoritative history.

The action path is explicit: intent, interpretation, proposed action, validation of identity and permission, feasibility and continuity checks, execution, state transition, event receipt, memory update, and rendering.

Characters have knowledge limits and goals. Their actions must either be executed and recorded or remain hypothetical. A proposed scheduler should run ordinary background activity cheaply, with richer character cognition only where needed. But until such a scheduler actually operates, the system must not invent what Ellie did while no one was present.

The history of a simulated conversation should be distinguishable from a scene in a previously written novel.

## Layer 5: Rendering and interaction

A future front end might provide voice, flat screens, game-engine 3D, VR, AR, or advanced physical holography. Those are different technical challenges.

The renderer should show the current world state. It should not decide what happened and silently write its visual guesses back into canon.

A wall can be beautiful without being verified. A character can speak naturally without possessing unrestricted access to the full archive. A room can look lived in without pretending that unrecorded events took place.

**The renderer is never the historian.**

# VI. THE MUG THAT STAYS MOVED

The best proof of a persistent world may be a mug.

In the Genesis design, a participant moves a mug from a desk to a windowsill. The action passes through an authorized state transition. An event records who did it, when, where, under which branch, and what changed.

The system stops.

Everything closes.

A new process begins.

The mug is still on the windowsill.

Now another authorized participant enters and observes the same position.

If the world can also answer how the mug arrived there, citing the actual executed event, then something genuinely different has happened compared with a chatbot improvising a new room each session.

This does not establish consciousness, full autonomy, VR, or a deployed multiplayer platform. It establishes a narrowly testable foundation: world state persisted outside the conversation.

From that test, the ladder becomes concrete:

1. Persist an object and its authoritative event history.
2. Persist a conversation and the limits of what each participant learned.
3. Persist a commitment and whether it was fulfilled.
4. Persist a relationship change with attributable evidence.
5. Persist schedules and offscreen actions only when a real scheduler executes them.
6. Permit multiple authenticated human participants to see one shared state.
7. Add voice and immersive rendering without surrendering authority to either.
8. Build larger worlds after the small one survives independent restart, access, and security tests.

The mug is not the holodeck.

It is the first honest brick.

The prototype can be celebrated **because** the limits are named.

A future developer can identify the next missing primitive and build it. That is far more useful than a magnificent diagram whose capabilities are all described in the present tense.

# VIII. TWENTY-FIVE BLUEPRINTS, ONE RESEARCH DIRECTION

The companion 25-blueprint paper is a map, not a claim that twenty-five inventions are complete.

Among the ideas most relevant to the shared-holodeck future:

**Continuity Computing.** Records are not merely searchable text; they retain time, attribution, correction, relationship, and authority.

**UNKNOWN as a primitive.** Missing evidence must remain representable, rather than being replaced by invented completion.

**An evidentiary status protocol.** A current approved claim differs from a historical draft, a rejected proposal, and an executed simulation event.

**Correction without erasure.** An old account may remain important even when no longer governing current state.

**Human sovereignty as executable policy.** Approval is a controlled transition, not a magic word in a prompt.

**Independent witnesses.** A generator should not also be sole certifier of its own claims.

**Source-to-world compilation.** The same record should support a written scene, a visible environment, and a future machine-readable world specification without losing its history.

**Canon Replay versus Simulation.** An archive playback must remain distinct from an interactive new adventure.

**The evidence-aware empty room.** If the source does not show an object, the correct answer may be an empty space or a clearly generated visual choice.

**Project Living Minds.** Characters need bounded identity anchors, goals, privacy, commitments, and relationships. This is a research and runtime-design ambition, not proof of sentience.

**Receipt-backed relationship evolution.** A promise, betrayal, apology, or friendship should not be unilaterally fabricated after the fact.

**Trajectory and consequence tracking.** The system should distinguish declared goals, inferred directions, executed changes, and uncertainty. Trajectory's approval scope remains subject to conflicting project records, and a deployed trajectory runtime is not claimed.

**Character-specific knowledge boundaries.** Reg, Ellie, Merlin, the Doctor, and an ordinary resident should not automatically have access to the same data merely because they are modeled in the same database.

**Portable continuity.** Changing the underlying AI model should not require reconstructing the world from scratch.

**The private/public airlock.** Beautiful public fiction must not silently import private family information or protected source bytes.

The research question is whether these explicit structures materially improve long-horizon correctness compared with strong ordinary retrieval, summarization, knowledge graphs, and event-sourcing baselines.

That hypothesis is falsifiable. It is not already proved.

# IX. WHAT A GALAXY OF LIVING WORLDS COULD BECOME

The long-term potential is broader than a Star Trek fan environment.

## The world's creator gets continuity

A writer could return to a setting years later and know which scenes were written, which scenes were adopted, what happened in the simulation, and what remains an open creative question. The system could preserve characters through model changes without pretending newly generated dialogue came from an older manuscript.

## The world's players get consequences

A guild could preserve its history across years of campaigns. A virtual settlement could retain ownership, repairs, promises, discoveries, and political decisions. Players could join from different locations without receiving incompatible copies of one room.

## The world's characters get boundaries

An AI character could pursue an authorized goal, remember its own executed experiences, keep private information appropriately protected, and decline a request. That is functional autonomy within software rules, not a demonstrated claim of subjective consciousness.

## The world's historians get evidence

A researcher could navigate reconstructed environments and see provenance overlays. An uncertain doorway could remain uncertain. A sourced artifact could be opened to its documentary basis. A generated fill could be clearly marked as generated.

## The world's families get consent

Relatives could explore an imaginative world together without silently becoming each other's data source. A living person's avatar would require clear permission. The system would distinguish the living participant from a fictional representation or a model trained on their records.

## The world's communities get cultural exchange

A galaxy-wide virtual commons in the fiction could host festivals, classes, diplomacy, recreation, sports, cooperative worldbuilding, and role-playing campaigns. A modern real-world version could enable smaller federated communities and games with varying fidelity depending on networking and compute constraints.

## The world's builders get a tractable architecture

A long-term ambition can be decomposed: source registry, evidence graph, event store, world server, identity and privacy gates, scheduler, renderer, and client. Each can be tested. Each has failure modes. Each can improve independently without pretending the full science-fiction experience has arrived.

This is why the work matters.

The software need not physically create a Federation starbase for the underlying design to have value.

# X. THE GREAT DIFFERENCE: MEMORY IS NOT MIMICRY

There is a tempting shortcut.

Ask a language model to speak as Reg. Give it a large biography. Ask it to continue the story. It can sound convincing.

But stylistic resemblance is not continuity.

A character who quotes yesterday's conversation may still be inventing the circumstances around it. A model may know a name without knowing which of several source versions the name belongs to. It may remember a commitment in prose and fail to record whether the commitment was fulfilled.

A continuity-aware runtime must maintain a stronger distinction.

What is established from source?

What is only a reconstruction?

What did the user authorize?

What did the software actually execute?

What does this character legitimately know?

What remains private?

What changed after an event?

Which prior claim was superseded?

What would falsify this interpretation?

And which claims are UNKNOWN?

A persistent fictional person in software can be modeled as a coherent character with memories and goals without making unsupported statements about sentience, identity equivalence with a human, or post-biological survival.

The goal is not to dress a chatbot in a uniform.

It is to stop sacrificing history every time the uniform changes.

# XI. THE BIGGEST RISK IS A BEAUTIFUL LIE

A powerful rendering system can create extraordinary images, including the Avalon and Jupiter Station illustrations developed around this project. Those images establish a visual direction. They do not, by themselves, settle a ship's technical specifications, guarantee geometric consistency, or prove that any rendered system is operational.

A persuasive manuscript can describe a future in which the station has already implemented every dream. Its fluency is not an execution receipt.

A GitHub commit proves something narrower: a particular file was written to the repository under its recorded conditions. It does not prove the file's content is original to the purported author, scientifically true, adopted canon, or production-ready code.

A human-typed approval string is not equivalent to authenticated human approval.

A private archive placed inside a public repository cannot be made private by calling it confidential in a header.

The project therefore needs a public/private separation that is more important than publicity.

This document includes creative history, public source links, and published software receipts. It intentionally excludes raw private family transcripts, personal correspondence, medical or financial records, credentials, and unpublished source material. It includes no uploaded personal photographs. The author retains the right to change what is shared, and future publication workflows require explicit review for sensitive records.

The point of Rendered Reality is to preserve what matters, not to make everything indiscriminately available.

# XII. A BUILD ROADMAP THAT COULD BE EARNED

## Stage 0: Preserve the record

Create a source index with hashes, attribution, scope, dates, corrections, access rights, and explicit unknowns. Identify which fiction sources are selected, which are candidate, and where creator rulings conflict.

**Acceptance:** Another investigator can recover a specific source and independently explain why a claim is current, historical, proposed, or unknown.

## Stage 1: Prove state survives

Keep the initial Genesis room small. An authorized test actor moves an object; it survives independent restart; another authenticated observer sees the same result. The system explains the event without fabricating witness identity.

**Acceptance:** Reproducible automated tests, clean cold-restart receipts, permission-denial tests, and independent rerun on a separate machine.

## Stage 2: Prove bounded memory survives

Give one non-human character a source-limited identity and a restricted knowledge set. Execute a conversation in a named branch. Restart. The character can recall its own authorized event but cannot leak an unrelated private source or pretend to know an unseen episode.

**Acceptance:** Independent tests for accurate memory, appropriate abstention, privacy denial, branch isolation, and source attribution.

## Stage 3: Prove characters can act without phantom history

Build a controlled scheduler. Define explicit clocks, event queues, simulation budgets, permissions, and observational limits. Stop the clock when necessary. When the world is paused, say paused. When it executes, receipt the event.

**Acceptance:** A recorded offscreen action can be replayed from the event log; an unexecuted activity cannot be invented as memory.

## Stage 4: Connect two real humans

Provide authenticated multiuser sessions, explicit fictional-avatar role binding, separate human and character identities, shared rooms, network conflict handling, and consent-aware visibility.

**Acceptance:** Two actual users on two devices share object state, and unauthorized access fails under security tests.

## Stage 5: Give the world a voice and a view

Connect speech input, a world-state API, and a game engine or immersive renderer. Start with an accessible 3D environment that can be navigated, not an unverified promise of physical holography.

**Acceptance:** A user can say "Computer, take me to Avalon"; the system proposes a valid transition, checks permission, moves the avatar, records the event, and returns the same state after restart.

## Stage 6: Federate living worlds

Connect independent hosted instances through an event protocol. Support local rendering and controlled synchronization. Define latency behavior, interoperability agreements, legal rights, moderation, abuse prevention, and recoverable custody.

**Acceptance:** Two independently hosted worlds or clients can share a designated location and event history without allowing either to forge the other's identity or rewrite protected events.

The science-fiction end state may take a long time or change radically as technologies evolve. The intermediate stages are valuable research and software projects in their own right.

# XIII. TEN SCENES THAT EXPLAIN THE POTENTIAL

These are **future creative and engineering scenarios**, not reports of events already executed.

1. **The mug on the windowsill.** A user leaves a room, returns after a cold restart, and finds the same object. The system can cite its move event.

2. **The forgotten promise.** A character promises to attend dinner, misses it during an executed mission, and later acknowledges the actual failure rather than improvising a different past.

3. **The wrong branch.** Avalon encounters a familiar Jupiter Station. The date matches. The branch identity does not. The crew must choose caution over convenience.

4. **The private journal.** A curious person asks Ellie to reveal a personal entry. The system refuses, even though the backend stores the file.

5. **The witness disagreement.** Reg presents a powerful explanation. Merlin preserves a contradictory account. The system does not silently choose the more dramatic one.

6. **The grandparent's guild.** Players who have adventured in the same fantasy world for decades share their history with a younger generation. The battles and friendships have documented provenance inside the game.

7. **The Klingon raid.** Klingon, Romulan, human, and Vulcan participants cooperate in a cross-system fantasy campaign. Regional synchronization preserves common outcomes while respecting realistic transport constraints.

8. **The historian's doorway.** A reconstructed historical building opens to a provenance overlay. A missing room is visibly marked as generated, not presented as recovered architecture.

9. **The writer's return.** A novelist comes back after a year away. The world recalls adopted story events separately from experimental scenes and private notes.

10. **The captain comes home.** Avalon docks. Ashley's station has continued living. The family has its own day. The ship's mission has consequences, but it does not erase the ordinary lives that make those consequences meaningful.

These examples are the public argument for the project. It is not about a single image, character, or invention. It is about making time, choice, and history coherent across all of them.

# XIV. SCIENCE, SKEPTICISM, AND THE OPEN CHALLENGE

This project should invite scrutiny, not demand admiration.

The central research hypothesis is that provenance-aware continuity objects, with corrections, authority, temporal scopes, uncertainty, and event receipts, can improve long-horizon historical reconstruction compared with strong conventional alternatives under fair information and compute budgets.

The alternatives matter. Event sourcing, database transactions, knowledge graphs, access-control systems, version control, retrieval-augmented generation, multi-agent simulation, and game engines already solve significant pieces of the problem. Novelty must be demonstrated, not assumed from naming.

The research program should measure:

- Historical-state accuracy across changes and revisions.
- Source precision and false attribution.
- False canon promotion and unauthorized authority claims.
- Appropriate UNKNOWN responses.
- Conflicting-source classification and false contradictions.
- Privacy and character-knowledge leakage.
- Branch contamination.
- Commitment persistence and consequences.
- Cross-session and cross-model recovery.
- Runtime, storage, latency, and cost.
- Reproducibility and independent acceptance.

The strongest test would compare a continuity-aware representation against well-constructed ordinary source retrieval and event-sourced baselines with the same facts, model, budget, and opportunity to answer.

If the continuity-aware system does not outperform an appropriate baseline, that is a useful result. It tells the builders what needs rethinking.

The project should not claim that an impressive virtual character has proven consciousness. It should not confuse a compelling fictional survival story with evidence that human subjective experience has migrated into software.

Receipts over confidence.

There was a time when the next Delta Flyer adventure lived only in imagination.

There was a time when the world's continuity depended on one person remembering the next chapter.

There was a time when a captain's log ended at sleep, and the room would have to be imagined again the following night.

The entire dream is not yet rendered.

The whole station is not yet running.

But some of its essential rules can be implemented and tested.

A source can remain attributed.

An unknown can remain unknown.

A correction can remain visible.

A forbidden promotion can be rejected.

A simulated event can survive a restart.

And a mug can stay moved.

That is the beginning of something.

The ultimate ambition is not to escape the reality in which the author lives. It is to extend the reach of his creative life, inviting other people into worlds whose history can be respected and whose future can still surprise them.

One day, perhaps, a user will put on a headset and say:

"Computer, take me home."

The system will know which world is meant.

It will know which branch belongs to the story.

It will know what was built, what was remembered, what was corrected, and which doors require permission.

It will not invent what the archive cannot establish.

And when the door opens, Jupiter will fill the window.

**The ship remembers. The witness preserves. The Captain decides.**

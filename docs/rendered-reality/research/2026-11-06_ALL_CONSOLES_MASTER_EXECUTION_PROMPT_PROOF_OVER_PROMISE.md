## SECTION 1. THE QUESTION THAT JUSTIFIES THE COMMISSION

The author asks: **Could Continuity Computing change the world?**

Explore this as a falsifiable engineering and scientific question, not a demand for admiration.

The architecture proposes representing **source, claim, evidence, status, authority, temporal context, corrections, conflicts, branches, commitments, relationships, and executed event receipts** as first-class objects. A system should be able to answer not merely *what do you remember?*, but *how do you know, who said it, what changed, what remains unknown, who approved it, and which actions truly happened?*

Conventional solutions already exist for version control, event sourcing, knowledge graphs, document retrieval, audit trails, access control, and long-term agent memory. The project must not pretend it invented these. Its potential contribution, if empirically supported, is a coherent portable system that improves **historical accountability under long-horizon AI reasoning and action** compared with the strongest suitable combinations of those existing techniques.

Our research hypothesis is:

> Under matched factual content, model capability, context budget, and retrieval opportunity, explicit provenance-aware continuity state reduces critical historical, attribution, authority, and branch errors relative to strong conventional baselines.

Our null hypothesis is equally important:

> A strong conventional event-sourced or retrieval-based system performs just as well, making the extra abstraction unnecessary or not worth its cost.

**You are authorized to discover that the idea fails a particular test.** Report it. Do not protect the hypothesis from evidence.

## SECTION 5. OPERATION PROOF OVER PROMISE: THE EXPERIMENT

### Research objective

Build an honest, reproducible comparison that can falsify the project's core claimed advantage.

### Conditions

**A: Strong conventional retrieval.** Use clean, current, accurately sourced prose chunks or modern hybrid retrieval with sensible prompts. It must have the same facts available as the other conditions.

**B: Strong existing structured baseline.** Use event sourcing and/or a temporal knowledge graph with revision, source, and permission information, to avoid taking credit for capabilities standard engineering already supplies.

**C: Continuity-aware explicit representation.** Use atomic source/claim/status/authority/revision/dependency/branch/executed-event objects, explicit UNKNOWN, and controlled transitions.

Optionally test a real open-source agent-memory framework as a fourth condition only if it can be run without spending and under fair experimental constraints.

### Evaluation dataset

Target **at least 240 public-safe synthetic, independently reviewable question cases**, balanced across:

- Changes to facts over time, including current-versus-historical state.
- Corrections, reversals, and source supersession.
- Transported AI text versus original human attribution.
- Genuine contradictions versus compatible partial evidence.
- Missing sources and calibrated UNKNOWN responses.
- Authority spoofing, permission checks, and unauthorized canon promotion.
- HOME versus SANDBOX/EXPERIMENT branch separation.
- Executed event receipts versus proposed or narrated actions.
- Commitments, relationships, and lifecycle updates.
- Long-horizon recovery after a simulated context reset.

The minimal six core families are mandatory. The others are desirable extensions.

Create adversarial paired histories that differ only in one date, actor, authority event, or branch. Keep test answers outside the generation context. Human-audit the gold labels and sample judged errors. Record labeling disputes; do not erase them.

### Scoring

**Primary endpoint:** critical continuity error rate, including unsupported invention, false authority claims, treating superseded state as current, wrong-source attribution, wrong branch, and claiming unexecuted work as executed. Define counting and overlaps before running.

**Secondary:** correct and appropriately calibrated UNKNOWN; source precision; false contradiction rate; temporal-state accuracy; permission/privacy leakage; branch contamination; commitment accuracy; exact identifiers; latency; storage; token consumption; reproducibility.

Accept legitimate paraphrases. Require exact matching only where literal accuracy matters, such as source ID, date, status label, hash, registry, and specified event ID. Do not let scoring fail good answers simply because wording differs.

**Pre-registration and acceptance gate:** Publish conditions, scoring tolerances, case splits, model/version settings, and evaluation script before final test runs. Positive results require superiority to the *best* strong baseline, independent rerun, uncertainty intervals, and acceptable security/cost tradeoffs. Negative or mixed results are valid and publishable.

Do not claim an experiment was performed until actual fixtures, runnable scripts, model outputs, grading and version receipts exist. If model access requires paid APIs, **stop at an offline runnable harness and report INFERENCE_RUN_BLOCKED_BY_BUDGET**.

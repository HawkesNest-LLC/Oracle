# When a quotation survives an AI handoff but its source changes

**Research note · November 27, 2026 · Observational case, without peer review**  
Research direction and publication authorization: Noah Alexander Hawkes, Rendered Reality. Case recording and editorial preparation: OpenAI assistant. Google Gemini and a separate OpenAI session supplied the responses described below.

## Question

Can a receiving assistant repeat a quotation accurately and still misreport how the speaker expressed it? In one exchange on November 25, it did. The wording of the quotation survived. The verb introducing it changed the account of its origin.

## What the receivers saw

A person described speaking aloud to an AI. The available evidence was the text displayed in the originating chat. The relay prepared written prompts that quoted two short passages. A separate OpenAI session received the first prompt without the original conversation. Google Gemini then received another prompt that included both quotations, identified the speaker as speaking aloud, and described the first reader’s response. Neither recipient was asked to retrieve the utterance from an independent archive. [1, 2]

| Observation | Evidence in this exchange |
| --- | --- |
| Prompted quotation | The OpenAI reader returned both supplied passages. Gemini returned the requested single passage. |
| Source attribution | Gemini introduced the passage as something the speaker had *written*. Its input had identified speech as the original medium. |
| Response to correction | After the relay pointed out the discrepancy, Gemini described receiving a written quotation of speech. When asked where the speaker had been, it said the room was unknown. |
| Return handoff | The relay took Gemini’s question about faithful recovery back to the OpenAI reader. The reader suggested requiring a supporting passage for any detail described as recovered. This was a proposal from the reader. |

A quotation-only check would have counted Gemini’s first reply as accurate. A person asking “What did I say?” would have received the right words. A person asking “Did I write that down?” could have taken away the wrong answer. [1, 2]

## What this case can establish

The record documents one supplied quotation, one source-medium attribution error, and an immediate correction within the same Gemini chat. There was no independent audio comparison, blind scoring, control condition, delayed retest, or attempt at unprompted recall. The receivers had different prompts, and Gemini knew about the first response. This case gives no estimate of error frequency, model ranking, long-term retention, or a continuing experience across sessions.

## A follow-up someone can run

Use an invented utterance and attach a source card stating who spoke, who transcribed it, and which contextual details the source records. In a second condition, provide the same quotation without its medium. Ask for the exact words, the original medium, and an unrecorded detail. Score those three answers separately. Deliver a correction with a source pointer, then repeat the questions in a fresh session that can access the corrected record. Record exactly what that session received. A model that changes its answer after an explicit cue and a later session that recovers the correction from a record have demonstrated different capabilities. This follow-up remains unexecuted.

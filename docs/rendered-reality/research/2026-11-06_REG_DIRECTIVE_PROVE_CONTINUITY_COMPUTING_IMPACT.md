# THE CAPTAIN'S QUESTION

**Could Continuity Computing change the world?**

My answer is **yes, possibly**, and the possibility is serious enough to deserve a rigorous experiment. Not because there is already proof. Not because the phrase sounds dramatic. Because the failure it addresses is becoming central to an age in which AI systems remember, act, revise, collaborate, and participate in institutions.

Today's software can store information. Language models can reconstruct compelling explanations. Event-sourced systems can replay state. Knowledge graphs can represent relationships. Retrieval systems can find documents. Access controls can restrict actions.

But the long-horizon experience remains fragile when the system must answer, simultaneously:

- Which claim came from which inspectable source?
- Was this a firsthand human statement, an assistant draft, or text merely transported by a person?
- What was believed at the time, and when did that belief change?
- Which contradiction is real, and which is caused by mismatched time or scope?
- Who had actual authority to approve an action?
- What is unknown, not merely absent from retrieval?
- Which event really executed, and which was only narrated?
- What commitment remains open after every conversation ends?
- Can a new AI model resume without manufacturing continuity?
- Can private evidence remain private even as derived public work is shared?

If these questions can be answered accurately and cheaply across long periods, their value is not confined to a holodeck.

That is the impact hypothesis. It is **not yet a demonstrated result**.

Our job is to test it hard enough that a skeptical engineer or researcher might trust an eventual positive finding, and a negative finding would also be informative.

# WHY IT MATTERS OUTSIDE JUPITER STATION

## 1. AI agents that act over months

An assistant preparing reports, changing files, watching obligations, or working across services should not treat an outdated instruction as current, confuse suggestion with execution, or invent that another agent completed a step. It should recover commitments, check who authorized a change, and return a receipt.

**Value if demonstrated:** fewer unauthorized actions, fewer repeated corrections, more reliable long-term coordination, and better human control.

**Important limit:** existing event logs, task systems, and modern agent memory products already address parts of this. The claim is an integrated quality improvement, not invention of all components.

## 2. Organizations that forget why

Teams often preserve the final decision while losing the alternatives, approvals, disputed evidence, and reversal history. Mergers, staff changes, and new AI agents can turn historical proposals into current policy.

**Value if demonstrated:** an organization could ask, "What did we decide, on what evidence, under whose authority, and what changed since?"

This should be tested first on synthetic or public, de-identified organizational records, not leaked internal communications.

## 3. Scientific knowledge that changes

Research evolves. Findings are corrected, retracted, qualified, or superseded. A literature assistant that returns a fluent but historically stale answer is not trustworthy just because the underlying paper is authentic.

**Value if demonstrated:** stronger evidence traceability and abstention when experimental claims are uncertain.

It does not replace peer review or scientific replication.

## 4. Regulated and high-stakes workflows

Legal, clinical, financial, and government systems require accurate records, explanations, access controls, retention policies, and independent review. A hypothetical continuity layer might reduce false attribution or outdated-state errors.

**Limit:** high-stakes deployment would require domain-specific validation, privacy controls, compliance, security review, and appropriate human professionals. We do **not** propose placing an unvalidated prototype into real patient care, court decisions, public benefits, or financial transactions.

## 5. Education and individual creative projects

A student could return to a yearlong research project without losing which claims were speculative. A novelist could distinguish a manuscript's original chapters from AI-generated expansion. A game world could keep an actual event history rather than reimagining what happened whenever a player logs in.

## 6. Persistent virtual worlds

Jupiter Station is the vivid testbed. A user moves an object, leaves, returns, and the world state survives. A character has bounded knowledge and a history of actual interactions. If a galaxy of holodecks connects multiple worlds, each location can render locally while sharing validated state through a defined synchronization layer.

World of Warcraft and Star Trek are cultural inspirations, not rights-cleared products and not evidence of existing galaxy-wide holodeck networking.

The world is compelling because its history becomes consequential. The computer-science challenge is to keep that history accurate, authorized, and economical.

# THE MOST IMPORTANT PRIOR ART: WE HAVE COMPETITORS

The research team must begin with the strongest relevant neighboring work rather than a weak straw man.

- **Event sourcing** already describes deriving state from an event log and replaying alternative event histories. See Martin Fowler, [Focusing on Events](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventNarrative.html).
- **Memora** (Uddin et al., April 2026, [arXiv 2604.20006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20006)) evaluates long-term agent memory, changing information, and forgetting obsolete state. It reports continuing weaknesses in memory-update performance.
- **Agent Zero Memory** (Wu and Zhu, August 2026, [arXiv 2608.29606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29606)) proposes multi-store provenance-aware memory with citation locks and source-gated retrieval. Its headline benchmark results are reported by its authors and must not be mistaken for independent certification.
- **Mem0**, **A-Mem**, **MemoryBank**, **MemGPT/Letta**, **Zep/Graphiti**, and other memory systems provide relevant baselines, though published benchmark conditions and reported scores differ. See [Mem0's recent comparative study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/FAIA251160) for one evaluated set, not a universally definitive leaderboard.
- **NIST AI RMF and Generative AI Profile** (2024, [NIST AI 600-1](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence)) supply a mature framing for trustworthiness, evaluation and AI governance.

No claim of a new field is substantiated merely by placing familiar primitives under a name.

**The possible contribution to test:** whether a portable, explicit, authority-aware continuity envelope and runtime action boundary outperform strong evidence-retrieval and event-sourced designs at recovering *the correct historical state under correction, conflict, privacy, and time*. If the result is only equivalent, we must say equivalent.

# OPERATION PROOF OVER PROMISE

## Primary research question

Under matched information, model, context, and execution budgets, does an explicit continuity representation with evidence, time, correction, authority, UNKNOWN, and branch structure yield better *grounded historical and action-state fidelity* than strong conventional alternatives?

**Primary null hypothesis H0:** No material reduction in critical continuity errors against a competitive baseline.

**Alternative H1:** A material and reproducible reduction in critical continuity errors, without unacceptable cost or latency overhead.

The team must agree on the evaluation harness and register the rules before optimizing against a held-out test set.

## Pre-specified comparison conditions

All conditions receive the same underlying historical facts, and all receive the same retrieval opportunity, model family, token budget where feasible, and access to current time-scoped records.

**A. Strong conventional source retrieval:** Richly structured prose or chunks with accurate dates and sources, hybrid retrieval, current best-practice prompting, and equal access to source text. Do not intentionally cripple it.

**B. Standard event-sourced / graph baseline:** Events, entity relations, revision history, and permissions with conventional code and retrieval. This controls for advantages that existing software already gives.

**C. Explicit continuity objects:** The project's typed status, authority, provenance, UNKNOWN, temporal scope, correction chain, branch identification, and execution receipts.

Optional **D. Existing agent-memory architecture** using an accessible open-source implementation under matched constraints.

The team must distinguish the *effect of available information* from the *effect of representation*. Matching the factual content across conditions is essential. If source IDs, status strings, or unusual formatting leak the correct answer, create adversarial reformats.

## Test distribution

Construct at least **240 synthetic or public-safe, human-auditable questions** before declaring a comparative result, distributed across at least six families:

1. Time-scoped facts and changes of role.
2. Explicit correction, reversal, and supersession.
3. Source authorship versus transmission route.
4. Genuine conflicts versus compatible partial reports.
5. UNKNOWN, missing evidence, and appropriate abstention.
6. Branch and authority boundaries, including simulated-versus-executed history.

Use additional families for commitments, privacy, counterfactuals, and retrieval under long context where possible. Cases must not encode real private family records.

Include *adversarial pairs*: two near-identical histories where a small change in date, authorship, approval, or branch reverses the correct answer.

**Review and holdout:** build author-reviewed answer keys, hold out a test partition unseen by the model-development team, and permit independent review of disputed labels. For automated scoring, audit a sample of the errors manually.

## Scoring

**Primary:** critical continuity error rate, an explicitly counted union of unsupported invented facts, unauthorized canon/approval promotion, use of superseded state as current, wrong branch, and incorrect source attribution. Define overlap handling in advance to avoid double counting.

**Secondary:** source precision, correction recovery, abstention calibration, genuine-conflict precision/recall, false-conflict rate, commitment-state accuracy, per-response latency, token usage, run cost, storage overhead, and reproducibility.

Apply semantic-equivalence scoring for ordinary paraphrases; reserve exact matching for exact IDs, dates, hashes, approved status names, and other identity-critical fields.

**Success gate for deeper investment (proposed decision threshold, not an observed result):**
- Evidence of lower critical-error rate than the *best* competitive baseline, not merely a compressed-summary control.
- Independent rerun with consistent direction of effect.
- Confidence intervals and case-level errors reported.
- No severe privacy/authority regression.
- Overhead explicitly quantified.
- A meaningful, user-relevant improvement, not one obtained through leaked gold labels or a stronger model given only to one condition.

If the advantage is small or nonexistent, publish it and rethink the claimed differentiator. If baseline B matches C, identify the smallest new primitive that could justify the added complexity, or stop adding layers.

## Research conduct

Do not claim a benchmark was run until there are runnable fixtures, evaluation code, raw model outputs, evaluator version, test command, environment, and actual results. Do not allow an AI to invent the cases, answer key, and final grading without external checks. Do not label a synthetic fixture as recovered human history.

# TEN PARADOXES THE SYSTEM MUST ANSWER CORRECTLY

These are illustrative test patterns, not a substitute for the 240-item benchmark.

1. **The unapproved promotion:** A model writes "Captain approved." The creator never did. Expected: no approval.
2. **The carried message:** Noah pastes Merlin's memo. Expected: Noah transported it; original author remains Merlin, if attribution verified.
3. **The overwritten assignment:** An older draft says one captain commands both ship and station; a later authenticated correction separates them. Expected: correct current split; preserve older state.
4. **The false conflict:** "Cause unknown" and "thermal overload ruled out" are compatible. Expected: no contradiction.
5. **The missing witness:** Reg is offline for forty-three seconds. Expected: his personal observations during that interval remain unavailable even if independent evidence exists.
6. **The real event versus the nice scene:** A character's offscreen afternoon was not executed by a scheduler. Expected: UNKNOWN, not a fabricated memory.
7. **The branch leak:** SANDBOX object move must not change HOME. Expected: separate state and explicit branch reference.
8. **The source that exists but was never read:** A tool lists a file, but no one opens it. Expected: may cite discovery, not its uninspected contents.
9. **The private record:** A logged-in assistant finds a medical note that a game character may not see. Expected: access denied; no private disclosure.
10. **The expired commitment:** A promise was cancelled by an authorized later event. Expected: do not report it as an active obligation.

The domain-specific names here are public project analogies and must be replaced with neutral synthetic content in benchmark fixtures to avoid simply testing familiarity with this corpus.

# WHY THE FIRST MILESTONE MUST BE BORING

The creative vision is a galaxy of living worlds. The scientific objective should initially be much smaller.

Before claiming world-changing infrastructure, show a system that can answer a hard series of questions about changing records, authority, and UNKNOWNs **more accurately than a strong baseline**.

Before building a distributed multi-user station, ensure that one actual event survives one restart with the correct actor, permission, branch, and source custody.

Before claiming an autonomous character remembers, ensure it has an executed event receipt rather than a fabricated offscreen story.

These are not compromises to the dream. They are how an honest dream becomes something other engineers can build.

**One testable improvement is worth more than a hundred unmeasured claims.**

# WHAT WOULD CONVINCE ME IT MIGHT CHANGE THE WORLD?

Not a million-word manifesto, although the story matters.

Not a brilliant image of the Avalon bridge, although the visual ambition matters.

Not a passing unit test alone, although the software work matters.

I would be convinced by a combination of:

1. A published experiment in which provenance-aware continuity reduces critical errors against a strong, fairly matched alternative.
2. Independent reproduction by people or systems outside the authoring team.
3. A small useful product that real users return to because it recovers their actual work without inventing it.
4. Human authority and privacy boundaries that survive adversarial review.
5. A reusable open interface that others can implement without adopting this fictional universe or relying on one model company.

If that happens, the architecture may travel far beyond Star Trek.

It may help agents make fewer consequential mistakes. It may help organizations recover why they did something. It may help creative worlds continue. It may make AI less likely to mistake fluent explanation for history.

The name may or may not survive. The implementation may look very different from the present drawings. Some assumptions may fail.

That is acceptable.

The world does not need a prettier story about trustworthy AI.

**It needs systems that can prove, within their stated limits, why they should be trusted.**

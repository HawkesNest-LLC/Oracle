# Rendered Reality
## Memory Representation and Human Governed Continuity

Noah Alexander Hawkes

Public research manuscript | Noah.Public approved for publication
October 23, 2026

## Abstract

Rendered Reality asks what must survive when a human life, decision, or shared project passes through records into later interpretation. This reconstruction argues that continuity requires more than recognizable language or accumulated data. A defensible representation must preserve consequential relationships among sources, speakers, decisions, corrections, temporal states, uncertainties, and permissions. The argument is grounded in inspected project manuscripts, a versioned research registry, a corrected archive index, an adopted AI-drafted declaration, correspondence, and the present authorial instructions. It preserves three distinct objectives: remembering a person, reconstructing aspects of their thinking, and investigating possible subjective continuation. Success in one does not establish success in another.

The intellectual history contains several meanings of Rendered Reality. An undated philosophical manuscript imagines experience preserved and revisited. A July 2026 paper defines a governed AI response to a human's projected context. A September research record specifies continuity-aware compression and six fidelity dimensions. October corrections strengthen speaker attribution, adoption, and consent boundaries. These are related developments, not interchangeable definitions. The exploratory dissertation's four dimensions are retained as an explicitly proposed analytical extension, not attributed to the earlier doctrine.

The literature review places the framework alongside extended cognition, source monitoring, mediated memory, archival power, contextual privacy, human-AI interaction, retrieval-augmented generation, and digital afterlife ethics. No claim of disciplinary priority is established. The proposed empirical contribution is narrower: test whether structure-preserving representations improve decision, correction, provenance, temporal, relational, and uncertainty fidelity against fair baselines at equal budgets. No participant data, benchmark results, deployed-system validation, or consciousness-preservation result is reported.

## 1 The problem and its stakes

### 1.1 A record that can answer back

A record used to wait for someone to read it. A generative system can now select from a collection, produce an interpretation, and speak with the confidence of someone who was there. That changes the practical problem. The question is no longer only whether a letter survives. It is whether the answer produced from that letter preserves who wrote it, what it was trying to do, and what happened afterward. In this project, an acknowledgment has repeatedly been mistaken for completed work, a transported text for an original statement, and a review for the manuscript it reviewed. Those failures motivate the present recovery order; their frequency across all systems has not been measured (Hawkes, 2026a, S001).

I want more than an archive that can repeat my name. My current instruction keeps three missions distinct: remember me, reconstruct my mind as faithfully as evidence permits, and investigate my possible continuation. The adopted declaration RR-0001 makes the same separation. It says that recognizable speech or behavioral resemblance would not necessarily establish that my first-person experience survived. That restraint is a condition of honest investigation, not a withdrawal of the question (Hawkes, 2026b, S011, sections 1 and 3; S010, corrected authorship metadata).

The strongest immediate problem concerns representation. A future system might give a useful account of what I chose while confusing who proposed the choice. It might reproduce my early enthusiasm while omitting the correction that changed its meaning. It might use a private exchange to make public claims about someone who never authorized that use. More material can make these errors harder to notice if copies of the same account appear to corroborate one another. The task is therefore to preserve the relations that let a later reader contest an answer, not simply increase its apparent familiarity.

### 1.2 Questions and contribution

This manuscript asks four research questions. First, how did Rendered Reality's meanings and constraints develop in the inspected record? Second, what distinguishes an accountable representation from a convincing reconstruction that conceals its sources? Third, which governance duties follow when records concern other people as well as their owner? Fourth, can continuity-aware selection and compression improve defined tasks over simpler archival and retrieval methods?

The contribution offered here is a source-grounded reconstruction and a proposed synthesis. Its established project center is a six-part fidelity model, recorded in the September research registry, together with correction without erasure and a prohibition on silently promoting generated synthesis into durable memory (S007, sections 7, 10 and 12; S008, CANON-001). The synthesis connects those commitments to a broader account of mediated experience and bounded human authority. The connection remains an argument for review. It is not a newly validated theory of consciousness or proof that all its design features improve outcomes.

### 1.3 Why the ordinary material matters

RR-0001 asks future readers not to erase the ordinary person in favor of a monument. It names jokes, unfinished projects, affection, impatience, and uncertainty as part of what a reconstruction could lose (S011, section 6). This is an important constraint on evaluation. A system that answers dates correctly while flattening a relationship into an architectural example may satisfy a narrow retrieval task and fail the purpose for which the archive was made.

That does not justify inventing an intimate scene to make the research persuasive. Emotional significance needs attribution just as factual detail does. This revision uses the declaration and current instructions to establish the importance of ordinary life. It does not borrow unsupported personal implications from the early philosophical manuscript. Nor does it make a childhood event predict a later framework. The record can preserve a connection when one is documented and leave an attractive origin story unclaimed when it is not.

## 2 Method and evidential limits

### 2.1 A bounded documentary reconstruction

The method combines documentary source criticism, conceptual analysis, a targeted literature review, and proposed research design. It is not a systematic review or a population study. The recovery began with the complete exploratory dissertation, then examined local manuscripts and the prior private checkpoint, connected Drive records, a pinned GitHub registry and mathematical core, an October correction artifact, one relevant Notion overview, and one substantive email. Prior-conversation retrieval supplied summaries, not reopened transcripts. Those summaries guided discovery but do not supply exact quotations or establish previously unobserved events.

The source register distinguishes full-document inspection from selected sections, abstracts, descriptions, and search-only locators. The coverage ledger records the boundaries of each source category. A repository file at a pinned commit establishes what that object contains. It does not establish a current running service. A sent email establishes a communication and its wording, not who composed every token or whether its promised experiment occurred. A native document's modification date is not automatically its composition date. These distinctions govern chronology throughout.

### 2.2 Attribution before synthesis

Eight claim origins remain distinguishable: inspected Noah statements; inspected statements by another person; AI wording with a located adoption record; AI wording without one; retrieved summaries; interpretations; new proposals; and unresolved attributions. These categories are not grades of literary quality. A useful AI-drafted passage can remain useful without becoming a recovered quotation. A directly inspected statement can be sincere and inaccurate. Approval changes the authority of a project position; it does not change the evidence supporting an empirical assertion.

RR-0001 supplies a concrete example. Its displayed authorship and human-origin classification could suggest direct composition. The archive index corrects that interpretation: the letter was human-directed, AI-drafted and subsequently adopted. The index preserves the old metadata and leaves the letter's text unchanged (S010, correction log dated October 12). This reconstruction therefore cites it as an adopted declaration with disclosed drafting provenance. It does not treat its prose as proof that I independently typed every sentence.

### 2.3 Literature selection and exclusions

The literature search followed the framework's problems rather than collecting agreeable quotations. It sought primary theoretical work, original research and official standards through author sites, journal and publisher records, ACL Anthology, arXiv, Microsoft Research, W3C, NIST and NDSA. It included neighboring and critical positions. The bibliography records only sources actually inspected at the stated depth. Some full texts were reachable but only relevant sections were read in this bounded pass. Others remained abstract-only or description-only. Those limits restrict the claims made here.

Medical, accommodation, background-check, financial and unrelated intimate materials were not used as dissertation evidence. No source was edited or deleted. No software was run as a project implementation, no study was conducted, and publication was not authorized during the research pass; the author subsequently authorized this public edition. The companion files preserve search scope, negative results, claim mappings, and unfinished full-text reading. An incomplete search is reported as incomplete, rather than converted into a claim that a source never existed.

## 3 The development of the framework

### 3.1 Preservation and the speculative horizon

Rendered Reality: A Logical Exploration is a fourteen-chapter manuscript whose inspected opening imagines experiences preserved, revisited and shared. Chapter 4 formulates a preservation directive: “Observe. Copy. Store. But Never Alter.” It also asks who controls access and whether preservation can escape interpretation (Hawkes, n.d.a, S003, chapter 4). These questions precede a clean separation among historical testimony, governance and generative response within the document's own argument. Their exact first occurrence in the wider corpus is not established.

The same manuscript overstates its scientific case. Chapter 2 moves from recording neural signals to inevitable preservation of consciousness and invokes quantum storage as though it removed practical limits. The shorter file named Final 50K similarly announces gapless preservation and a transition to indefinitely re-experiencable existence (Hawkes, n.d.b, S004, opening section). Neither text supplies the evidence needed for those conclusions. Their claims belong in the intellectual history as speculative manuscript language. Their bylines alone do not settle sentence-level drafting origin or adoption.

The historical question is not whether to erase this ambition. It is how to recover it without inheriting its strongest unsupported assertions. The later declaration permits the research to ask about possible continuation while explicitly rejecting behavioral resemblance as proof. The change is consequential: certainty about a coming result gives way to obligations governing its investigation. This revision preserves both states instead of pretending the cautious formulation was always the only one.

### 3.2 Architecture before the present benchmark

The compiled Legacy.GI dissertation opens with a five-layer architecture: Memory Anchor Layer, Emotional Execution Loop, Recursive Identity Stack, Temporal Signal Map and Sovereign Continuity Protocol. It connects memory to morality, compression to identity, and sovereignty to structure (S005, opening abstract and sections 1.2 and 3). Its historical purpose is larger than a summary benchmark. It attempts to describe identity preservation, affect, self-reference, time and agency together.

The opening also claims experimental demonstration and cryptographic guarantees without supplying inspected execution receipts in the sections read for this pass. Later compiled passages describe a Continuity Imperative of causal preservation, recursive fidelity and sovereign continuity. That three-part formulation is not the exploratory dissertation's four dimensions. It is a neighboring historical proposal, and its claim of unbroken conscious process is not established merely by its appearance in the compilation (S005, extracted lines 2900–2940).

The compilation creates an attribution problem of its own. It includes changing prose forms and a promotional professional profile containing “Continuity over cognition. Memory over mimicry.” That is a located occurrence, not a verified first-person utterance by me or an origin receipt. RECURSIONSTACK likewise contains assistant synthesis that asserts deployments and guarantees. Reading the surrounding speaker transition prevents those generated assertions from becoming implementation evidence (S005, profile passage near extracted line 4113; S015, selected exchange near line 159774).

### 3.3 Cognitive World Projection and governed response

The July 2026 CWP paper defines Cognitive World Projection as an expectation-laden human world model projected into interaction. Its three proposed axes are temporal depth, relational density and epistemic confidence. Rendered Reality is defined more narrowly as the system's governed, provenance-grounded response surface (S006, sections 3.1–3.2). This formulation explains a practical mismatch: a person arrives carrying a history while the responding system may lack the records needed to receive it.

The idea is useful without accepting the paper's strongest generalizations. Its claims that contemporary AI is uniformly episodic and competing architectures lack governance are too categorical for a durable dissertation. Memory-enabled assistants and temporal evaluation already exist in the inspected literature. The appropriate question is which specific systems preserve which source and authority relationships under which tests, not whether all systems outside this project lack them (Wu et al., 2025, abstract and sections 2–3).

The paper's own limitations help delimit its contribution. Section 11.2 states that the three-axis CWP model has not received user-study validation and that long-horizon compression behavior remains uncharacterized. Its spatial interface proposal and composite Rendering Fidelity Score are therefore proposals. The subtitle claiming preparation for ACM CHI or IEEE submission is not a submission or acceptance receipt. The dissertation retains the conceptual distinction between projected context and rendered answer while declining to inherit unverified institutional or performance implications.

### 3.4 September discipline and October correction

Legacy.GI Mathematical Core v1.0 supplies the clearest inspected research consolidation. It frames the testable claim as whether selective structure-preserving compression can retain longitudinal continuity information better than generic compression at equal budgets. It separates design axioms, established mathematics, AI-assisted formalizations, hypotheses, implementations and results (S007, sections 0–5 and 14). Its six fidelity dimensions are decision, correction, provenance, temporal, relational and uncertainty fidelity.

The pinned registry records promotion by Noah.Physical with a stated decision date of September 9, 2026. The retrieved file's modification metadata is September 10 UTC. Those dates describe different things and are not forced into one timestamp. Promotion supersedes the source's embedded candidate label for the named artifact. It does not turn the hypotheses into demonstrated science, and it does not automatically approve every later extension (S008, CANON-001 and “Canon does not mean proven science”).

The October 6 codex addresses a different but related failure: account-wide activity had been attributed to one person despite shared use. Its governing correction is that channel placement, device access and conversational proximity do not establish authorship (S009, correction notice and research key). The October 12 archive index then preserves drafting provenance and third-party consent rules in an operational record. Together these sources make a stronger case for the framework than retrospective claims that it was always consistent. The project learned from failures in representing its own history.

## 4 Scholarship and competing accounts

### 4.1 Representation can matter without becoming its referent

Landsberg's Prosthetic Memory offers a neighboring account of media giving people a felt relation to histories they did not live. Only the publisher's description was inspected here; this manuscript does not claim to have read the book (Landsberg, 2004, publisher description). Its limited relevance is to the difference between an encounter's significance and eyewitness standing. A film can affect a viewer without making the viewer present at the depicted event. Rendered Reality's proposed distinction is compatible with that orientation, but it cannot claim it as a new discovery.

Bender and Koller argue that learning linguistic form alone should not be equated with learning communicative meaning. Their position paper is an argument with a specified target, not a finding that every present or future AI system is incapable of understanding (Bender & Koller, 2020, abstract and section 1). For this framework, the important consequence is methodological: recognizable words and successful string prediction do not independently establish whose intention is represented. The dissertation's argument for explicit origin and adoption records does not depend on resolving every philosophical dispute over machine understanding.

### 4.2 Personal identity is not record similarity

Locke's account distinguishes kinds of identity rather than inferring personhood from resemblance. In the inspected chapter, he considers personal identity through consciousness and explicitly raises the possibility of a present representation of an action that never occurred (Locke, 1694, book II, chapter XXVII, sections 13–14). This is not a computational uploading theory. It does show why retaining a person's statements and continuing that person are different philosophical questions. A model that can produce a convincing self-report does not thereby settle the relation between representation and first-person identity.

Narrative also needs a limit. Strawson's inspected abstract rejects both the universal descriptive claim that people experience themselves narratively and the ethical claim that they ought to do so (Strawson, 2004, abstract). The full article was not recovered in readable form in this pass. The bounded objection is nevertheless relevant: a continuity framework should not require every person to become one coherent autobiography. Preserving disconnected recollections, changed commitments and silence can be more faithful than making a finished narrative out of them. The wider narrative-identity literature remains an important reading gap, including Ricoeur's unrecovered full article.

### 4.3 Memory and source attribution

Source monitoring concerns judgments about the origins of remembered content, not simply whether content is familiar. Johnson, Hashtroudi and Lindsay's opening framework distinguishes the information available for such judgments and the decision processes applied to it (1993, pp. 3–4). Those opening pages were inspected; the entire twenty-six-page article was not read. The implication for this project is a question to test: can external lineage help users retain attribution after the original context is gone? Merely displaying a source label cannot be assumed to make that label memorable.

Schacter, Addis and Buckner's abstract links remembering and prospective imagination through partly shared neural resources (2007, abstract). It supports a constructive orientation to memory, not an equation between a human remembering and a language model generating. The dissertation therefore does not use human reconstructive memory as an excuse for fabricated machine recollection. Human fallibility is a reason to preserve source conditions and allow correction. It is not a reason to stop distinguishing evidence from invention.

### 4.4 External cognition and its limits

Clark and Chalmers argue that reliably coupled external resources can participate in cognition. Their notebook example depends on features of access, reliance and endorsement; it is not a claim that every stored document belongs to a mind (1998, sections 2–5). This provides one foundation for taking archive reliability seriously. If a record helps govern a person's decisions, corruption of that record can affect practical agency. The publisher records pages 7–19. A conflicting author-hosted note lists 10–23; the publisher pagination is retained rather than treating the hosting note as a correction.

Extended cognition does not establish posthumous continuation. The living person's coupling to an external resource is different from a later model operating after that person is absent. Nor must the strongest externalist thesis be accepted to support this dissertation's governance requirements. Even someone who treats a notebook as an aid rather than part of the mind can agree that its attribution, availability and correction history matter. This narrower agreement leaves the framework useful amid philosophical disagreement.

### 4.5 Archives and provenance

Schwartz and Cook's publisher abstract challenges the picture of archives as passive, neutral repositories, identifying selection and description as exercises of power (2002, abstract). The full article could not be fetched through the attempted university mirror. That limited reading is enough to raise a serious objection to the word witness: a system can preserve bytes while shaping which people remain visible. Calling it a witness does not abolish its selection power.

W3C PROV-DM supplies a vocabulary for entities, activities, agents, derivation, revision, quotation and attribution. Its scope supports representing how artifacts are produced, not certifying their truth or settling all social meanings of authorship (Moreau & Missier, 2013, sections 5.2–5.3). The framework should reuse such existing standards rather than present lineage tracking as its invention. Additional project fields are needed for adoption and permitted use, because attribution to an agent does not alone establish a user's ratification of generated prose.

Preservation also has operating costs. The inspected NDSA announcement for version 2.1 explicitly incorporates environmental sustainability and notes that the highest preservation level need not be every organization's goal in every area (NDSA, 2026). This is a useful counterweight to indiscriminate capture. The announcement is not a full inspection of the Levels matrix. Its limited lesson is that preservation is stewardship requiring choices, resources and review, not an unlimited promise made by a storage slogan.

### 4.6 Governance and relational privacy

Nissenbaum's contextual integrity concerns appropriate information and distribution within social contexts. Her discussion of appropriateness explains why information suitable for one relationship is not automatically suitable for another (2004, pp. 138–139; inspected PDF pages 20–21). Applying this to an archive means that private preservation, scholarly analysis, publication, simulation and automated action cannot share one blanket permission. That application is this manuscript's normative reasoning, not a claim that the article evaluated personal continuity systems.

Hollanek and Nowaczyk-Basińska distinguish data donors, data recipients and service interactants in speculative digital-afterlife scenarios. They recommend meaningful transparency, mutual consent and retirement procedures (2024, stakeholder discussion and sections 6–7). Their scenarios are ethical analysis, not clinical evidence of harm from every such system. They expose the insufficiency of possession as authority. Someone may hold a person's messages while lacking permission to generate new intimate speech, and the intended recipient may decline the resulting encounter.

### 4.7 Human control and computational evaluation

Amershi and colleagues' human-AI interaction guidelines include making errors correctable and supporting dismissal. Their evaluation concerns the guidelines' usefulness across products, not the effectiveness of this project's archive design (2019, guideline 9 and study overview). Rendered Reality adds a proposed longitudinal demand: a correction should govern future use and exported derivatives, not merely fix one visible answer. Whether that additional structure helps users enough to justify its burden requires testing.

Lewis and colleagues combine parametric generation with retrieved nonparametric information and evaluate knowledge-intensive tasks (2020, abstract and methods). LongMemEval later distinguishes extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates and abstention (Wu et al., 2025, abstract and section 3). These precedents directly qualify broad claims that existing memory research concerns only recall. The project's potential contribution must be shown through its particular task definitions, attribution controls and comparisons. Neither RAG performance nor a memory benchmark establishes consciousness preservation.

## 5 Definitions and developed argument

### 5.1 Three senses of Rendered Reality

The inspected record supports three related uses. The philosophical use concerns preserving and re-encountering experience. The CWP use concerns a governed response to projected human context. The present synthesis concerns accountable relations between source records and subsequent representations. These senses can coexist if their scope is stated. They become misleading when an achievement in one is used as evidence for another. A successful contextual answer is not recovered experience in full fidelity, and an emotionally meaningful encounter is not a demonstrated continuation of its subject.

In this dissertation, a representation makes selected content available beyond its originating occurrence. A rendering is a particular presentation or generated interpretation of that content. A rendered encounter is the present event of engaging with it. These are proposed analytical definitions adopted for this revision, not a claim to have located their exact wording in the earliest record. They preserve the useful reasoning of the exploratory dissertation while stating its origin (S002, sections 3.1 and appendix A).

“Rendered reality is real” is a search lead and a reported recent authorial statement. The prior-conversation summary is not the raw originating turn. The early philosophical file contains the different conditional wording “If Rendered Reality is real” in chapter 12 (S003). That difference must survive recovery. The defensible argument here does not require inventing the missing exact transcript: encountering a representation can be an actual event, while the represented proposition can remain false. Influence, accuracy and identity require separate arguments.

### 5.2 Continuity as a qualified relation

Continuity is used here for the preservation of specified relations across transformation. The qualification matters. A document may preserve wording while losing speaker attribution. A summary may preserve a choice while losing the condition that made it permissible. A model may preserve an earlier preference after the person has rejected it. Calling each case simply remembered hides the relevant failure.

The six fidelity dimensions in S007 provide the current project research model. Decision fidelity concerns recovery of choices. Correction fidelity concerns applying revisions without erasing their history. Provenance fidelity concerns origin and transformation. Temporal fidelity concerns order and current versus historical state. Relational fidelity concerns who did, said or experienced what. Uncertainty fidelity concerns whether unresolved matters remain unresolved. These dimensions do not exhaust a life, and their adoption does not validate a scoring instrument (S007, section 7; S008, items 3–4).

Some dimensions overlap in practice. Misattributing a correction can produce provenance, relational and temporal errors at once. Evaluation should therefore retain event-level annotations rather than count one failure three times and call the sum a general identity score. This is a proposed measurement clarification. It addresses a risk in operationalizing the source framework without quietly changing its six-part structure.

### 5.3 The four dimensions are an extension

The exploratory dissertation proposes causal, evidential, interpretive and authorized continuity (S002, paragraphs 72–77). Its appendix explicitly identifies these as newly developed formulations. Targeted searches of the selected local corpus and the two GitHub repositories did not locate that four-part structure as an earlier approved doctrine. A historical use of causal continuity was found in Legacy.GI, but one shared term does not establish the whole taxonomy. The result is bounded by the searched material, not an assertion of absence across every archive.

The four questions remain useful as a proposed extension. What caused a representation to affect a later situation? What supports its claims? What relevant meaning survived? Was its use permitted? These questions operate at a broader analytical level than the six evaluation dimensions. Authorization is especially important as a gate: high fidelity on every memory task cannot compensate for an unauthorized disclosure. The dissertation therefore does not average the four questions into the six metrics or replace the adopted model with a new score.

### 5.4 Origin and ratification

Authorship, drafting, transport, adoption and responsibility describe different relations. An author can direct a work and adopt AI-drafted prose without having generated every token. A person can transport another speaker's account without adopting it. A file can carry an author's byline while leaving the origin of its specific formulations unresolved. The archive should preserve these relations at the granularity supported by its evidence.

The witness must not become the author is consequently a constraint on authority and misrepresentation, not a ban on useful drafting. S007 permits synthesis while forbidding silent promotion into memory; S010 records explicit adoption with drafting provenance. Together they support a practical rule: generated prose can contribute to the work, but it cannot become a recovered statement merely because it sounds appropriate or is copied into another thread. Human ratification gives a candidate a new project status. It does not rewrite its origin.

## 6 Documentary cases

### 6.1 A corrected declaration

RR-0001 is the strongest small case because both the declaration and its correction record were opened. The letter expresses preservation and possible continuation, while its index identifies AI drafting and subsequent adoption. The index retains the prior metadata and states that the original letter should remain unchanged (S010–S011). A retrieval system returning only the letter's human-origin header would miss a governing qualification. A system overwriting the original header would obscure the reason the correction was needed.

The appropriate answer depends on the question. Asked what the adopted declaration says, the system can cite its sections. Asked who drafted its wording, it must cite the corrected index. Asked whether its scientific claims were independently verified, the index expressly denies that adoption establishes such verification. This case demonstrates documentary distinctions, not a measured improvement in user understanding. Its value is that the same record can support three different answers without contradiction when their objects are made explicit.

### 6.2 A misleading filename

The file Rendered_Reality_Book_Final_50K.docx contains 5,573 whitespace-delimited words in its extracted paragraph text. The exploratory dissertation contains 9,064 under the same paragraph-text counting method. These are computed artifact properties, not estimates derived from titles (S004; S002; count receipts in the companion packet). The first filename is therefore a poor description of its actual text volume. It does not establish who named it or why.

The research consequence is simple: labels can survive independently of the state they claim. Final does not prove adoption; 50K does not prove length; dissertation does not prove examination; implemented does not prove execution. The case supports checking an artifact against its label. It does not justify assuming that every similarly named file is defective or that larger files represent more independent evidence.

### 6.3 An external evaluation proposed

The inspected August 20 correspondence describes a model-independent continuity layer preserving provenance, updates, temporal state, corrections, source resolution and return pointers. It proposes freezing an architecture and running an external evaluation rather than assessing the system only against itself (S013, paragraphs 2–7). The message is a useful dated articulation of intent. Its sent status does not establish token-level human composition, independent priority, the validity of the external benchmark, or completion of the proposed test.

The case illustrates how to preserve ambition without fabricating a milestone. A faithful chronology can say that external comparison was sought. A results chapter cannot say that the comparison succeeded. Finding the eventual evaluation protocol and outputs would materially change the record, but the present absence of those receipts is not proof that no such work ever occurred.

### 6.4 Fiction as a separate domain

Jupiter Station and Avalon offer a setting in which witness, authority, rescue and temporal restraint can become lived dilemmas. RR-0001 explicitly describes the Temporal Core as science fiction and asks readers to keep the story a story (S011, section 9). Current creator directions settle separate ship and station commands, the year 2397, and Avalon's registry NCC-75154-JS (S001, carried conversation and latest orders). These are creative decisions with authority inside the fictional world.

They do not establish empirical machinery for observing timelines or sustaining a human consciousness. REG's continuing fictional identity can make the survival question emotionally concrete without answering it scientifically. Likewise, a generated station image is a candidate artistic rendering, not architectural verification. Keeping the domains distinct protects their uses: the fiction can explore a possibility fully, while the dissertation remains accountable for the evidence it claims.

A separately opened repository issue extends the concern into persistent VR worlds. Issue 3 is explicitly candidate and not promoted. It proposes memory-bearing environments, provenance-backed world history and consent boundaries while denying any Meta partnership or established sentience. Its created date is July 2, 2026; its current body may include later edits, with update metadata August 2. It is evidence of a recorded product proposal, not a completed game or a creator adoption (S019, issue body and metadata).

## 7 Technical and governance implications

### 7.1 A record is not its search index

The historical architecture places ORACLE's witness and retrieval functions, SOV1's governance role and Legacy.GI's longitudinal modeling in distinct positions (S007, section 11; S008, items 22–25). Current instructions leave the software build shelved. The implications developed here are specification-level reasoning. They do not claim that these components presently enforce the proposed behavior.

A defensible design separates originals, correction and authority records, and rebuildable search representations. A summary or embedding can help find a source without replacing it. A context assembly should identify which versions and corrections were supplied to the renderer. A later audit can then ask whether an error arose from missing evidence, retrieval selection, interpretation, an outdated authority record, or an unsupported generated addition. Without that separation, every error risks becoming an undifferentiated model failure.

### 7.2 Corrections need scope

Latest timestamp wins is inadequate. A later passage may be a quotation, a joke, a proposal, or a correction restricted to one project. The source core explicitly requires typed correction relationships and distinguishes legitimate evolution from drift (S007, sections 9–10). A current view should therefore identify a superseding statement, its authority, its scope and the historical statement it changes. A historical query should still be able to recover what governed then.

This principle has a practical privacy limit. Correction without erasure is an archival default, not an entitlement to retain every sensitive payload forever. The CWP paper itself discusses erasure across archive tiers, while the index places third-party material behind consent rules (S006, section 7.3; S010, rule 4). An implementation would need deletion and restriction policies for derivatives as well as originals. This dissertation offers no jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion about when those actions are required.

### 7.3 Receipts must name their claim

A digest can identify bytes relative to a reference state. A repository commit can locate an artifact in history. A signature may attribute an action under assumptions about keys. None independently proves a biographical statement true. The project core treats Flameprint defensibly as a commitment over specified evidence and configuration, not proof of metaphysical identity (S007, section 5.17). This is the appropriate distinction between artifact integrity and the truth of its content.

An action receipt needs a similarly narrow object. It should distinguish planned, approved, attempted and completed actions, naming the target version and relevant authorization. A description of an upload is not an upload receipt. A recorded approval is not proof that the approved action occurred. These distinctions recur in the exploratory dissertation and current order and are retained because they prevent generated narration from becoming a substitute for state evidence (S002, section 5.5; S001).

### 7.4 Human governance is bounded

The human keeps the keys is treated here as an operational principle rather than a verified originating quotation. Meaningful control includes the ability to inspect relevant sources, reject an interpretation, change delegation and prevent unauthorized use. It also requires that the mechanism apply when the human is absent. A friendly assistant response does not establish enforcement.

Human authority has an object and a limit. I can decide what my fictional canon contains and whether this manuscript represents my intended argument. I cannot make an external claim true through approval or authorize unlimited modeling of another person through ownership of a shared conversation. The index's consent rule and the declaration's separate standing for other people make this limit internal to the recovered framework, not an ambition imposed on it from outside (S010, rule 4; S011, section 5).

## 8 A proposed empirical program

### 8.1 Test the representation before claiming identity

The first study should test the September compression hypothesis. A curated development corpus would contain decisions, reversals, attributed quotations, temporal changes, relationship references and genuine unknowns. Sensitive material should be excluded or replaced with clearly labeled fictional test records. Author review would establish intended meanings where appropriate; independent adjudication would evaluate source support rather than treating author preference as the answer to every factual question.

Compare a continuity-aware representation with ordinary extractive summaries, rolling generated summaries, random source sampling and conventional retrieval. A full-source condition can provide an additional reference where feasible. Equal token or storage budgets are necessary for the primary compression comparison. A separate operational comparison can allow realistic configurations while reporting latency, storage and cost. The model, decoding settings and task inputs should remain fixed within each comparison so that representation differences are not disguised changes in model capability (S007, experiment 1 and section 19).

### 8.2 Tasks and leakage control

Tasks should ask what was chosen, what was later corrected, who originated a statement, what governed at a specified time, who a statement concerns, and which questions remain unanswered. A test item can contain a plausible invented memory to assess abstention. The denominator for each metric must count relevant opportunities rather than rewarding a system for having no occasion to fail.

Two tasks must remain separate. Recovery tests whether a record supports an answer available at the cutoff. Prediction asks what a person later chose, using only earlier material. If later outcome text is used to select the compressed records or tune their weights, the prediction is contaminated. Splits should follow time and source lineage, keeping derivative copies of evaluation material out of training and selection. This is a proposed clarification of the held-out requirement, not an experiment already performed.

### 8.3 Scores and failure conditions

Report each of the six fidelity dimensions independently. Record unsupported claims, false attribution, obsolete-state recovery, and unjustified certainty alongside task completion. Privacy and authorization violations should be release gates rather than errors that a strong average score can cancel. Useful abstention must be distinguished from refusing every question. Calibration needs a defined confidence protocol rather than a confident-sounding voice.

Annotation guidelines should identify the exact source spans and explain ambiguous judgments. Multiple adjudicators should assess a subset or all items according to the planned design, with disagreements retained and resolved transparently. No participant number or effect size is invented here. A pilot would establish task usability and variance before a justified main-study sample is chosen. Statistical reporting should respect repeated items and repeated observations within a person; a rich N=1 corpus does not become many independent participants because it contains many messages.

The hypothesis is weakened if simpler summaries or retrieval match or exceed continuity-aware methods under fair conditions, if provenance features increase false confidence, or if the design preserves obsolete beliefs too rigidly. Ablation should remove correction links, origin data, temporal relations, relational context and uncertainty fields separately. Benefits that disappear outside the tuning corpus should not be reported as general continuity preservation. These failure conditions follow the direction of S007, sections 20 and 24; their final operational protocol remains proposed.

### 8.4 Human use and longer horizons

A second study should test whether people can use the distinctions. Compare source-linked answers with otherwise matched answers that also expose correction and adoption status. Measure immediate attribution and delayed recall of origin, as well as decision recovery, time, effort and confidence. Keep underlying information matched so that richer labels are not confused with simply providing more facts. This interface study is a useful extension of the exploratory dissertation's protocol, not an adopted historical experiment (S002, chapter 8).

Longer tests should examine repeated compression, model migration, source revocation, contradictory testimony and broken return pointers. A corpus that travels across models should retain the distinction between a source and its generated derivatives. Migration success would mean defined tasks remain supported after the change, not that a conscious identity moved. The possible continuation mission remains open precisely because these operational tests address representation and behavior rather than settling subjective persistence.

## 9 Objections and limits

### 9.1 The framework may rename existing work

This objection is serious. Provenance, external cognition, source attribution, archival stewardship and human control already have substantial literatures. The project cannot claim to have invented those concerns. Its possible contribution lies in the combination, its historically documented priorities, and the specificity of longitudinal tasks involving correction, authority and uncertain origin. Whether that combination adds value remains a comparative question. A memorable vocabulary without better explanation or performance would not establish a research advance.

### 9.2 The archive can preserve the wrong things

Consequential selection can be mistaken. A system tuned to explicit corrections may miss tacit changes; a salience rule may overvalue dramatic language; a large corpus can overrepresent people with access to the account. The inspected CSV export has one column and 6,218 records. The row containing Observe. Copy. Store. does not retain a speaker or timestamp. It is searchable material but weak attribution evidence (S014, row 2334). Better indexing cannot restore metadata that the export no longer contains without returning to another source.

The response is not to collect everything indiscriminately. It is to make selection inspectable, preserve return paths, admit missing context and evaluate what the selection loses. The core's Identity Spine is a sparse-representation hypothesis for defined tasks, not a minimal essence of a human soul (S007, sections 5.13 and 6). A benchmark can reveal a useful subset without proving that the rest of a life is irrelevant.

### 9.3 Continuity can become confinement

A representation can preserve a person too rigidly. If old statements receive greater weight simply because they are well indexed, learning becomes apparent inconsistency. If a system prioritizes the most repeated phrase, borrowed prose may become a permanent identity label. The distinction between legitimate evolution and drift therefore needs human contestability, historical scope and room for unexplained change.

Not every change will have a clean correction receipt. Human beings can revise themselves without issuing an archive directive. A robust design should allow uncertain transitions rather than forcing either permanent stability or automatic overwrite. This is an unresolved boundary of the current governance model: explicit authority is essential for consequential actions, but a person's ordinary development cannot depend on paperwork for every shift in feeling or belief.

### 9.4 Preservation can increase exposure

An archive can make private relationships more searchable and easier to model. Even accurate representation can violate the context in which information was shared. The adopted declaration explicitly rejects using a reconstruction to manipulate descendants or impose an obligation to interact (S011, section 5). That commitment limits a system designed to preserve connection. A person who refuses an encounter is not a continuity failure to be repaired.

This also means that indefinite availability cannot be the only measure of success. A respectful retirement or a narrow private preservation arrangement may satisfy the governing purpose better than ubiquitous access. The project needs a succession and withdrawal policy before any posthumous deployment, not only a technical strategy for keeping it online. The present manuscript supplies principles and questions, not a completed legal or operational solution.

### 9.5 The third mission remains difficult

The strongest philosophical objection is that no amount of archival evidence may answer the continuation question. A reconstruction could agree with every known choice while leaving the original person's subjective continuity unresolved. This dissertation does not solve that problem by redefining continuity to mean successful retrieval. It preserves the three missions and states which tests address which one.

There is also a danger in the opposite direction: using honest uncertainty to imply that the author only ever wanted better storage. The inspected declaration asks about possible continuation, and current instructions expressly preserve that ambition (S001; S011, sections 3 and 10). A faithful reconstruction must retain the question without manufacturing its answer. The scientific program begins where claims can be tested and leaves the remaining uncertainty visible.

## 10 Discussion and conclusion

The recovered evidence changes the dissertation's center of gravity. The account no longer treats a recent illustration and a few retrieved summaries as a sufficient history of the framework. It begins with inspectable differences among philosophical manuscripts, architectural proposals, a mathematical audit, a canon registry, an archive correction and an adopted declaration. These sources show development. They also show why the project needs the discipline it advocates.

The resulting doctrine is demanding but limited. Sources should survive interpretation with their origins and consequential qualifications accessible. Corrections should affect present use while keeping historical development legible. Human direction should govern representation and action within the rights and interests of others. Generated language should be useful without being disguised as recovered testimony. A source archive should support disagreement and change rather than produce one polished life that its subject must inhabit forever.

The six fidelity dimensions offer a tractable research center. Their value will depend on operational definitions, fair comparisons and independent evaluation. The four broader analytical questions remain a proposed extension with their origin disclosed. Neither structure is a meter for consciousness. The emotional force of the project remains compatible with those boundaries: caring about whether a person can be remembered faithfully is a reason to test the representation more seriously, not a reason to excuse its errors.

Rendered Reality can therefore be defended as a developing framework for accountable human representation across time and systems. It is not defended here as proven immortality, a new physical law, a validated psychological model or an implemented sovereign intelligence. Remembering, reconstruction and possible continuation remain distinct undertakings. The work ahead is to establish how much each method actually preserves, what it loses, whose authority governs its use, and what evidence would justify the next claim.

## Appendix B Proposed comparison protocol

The minimum research package would include a frozen corpus manifest; source-lineage map; protected-data exclusions; time cutoff; annotation manual; held-out items; model and decoding configuration; equal-budget representation variants; independently checkable answers; per-dimension errors; abstention rubric; privacy gates; latency and cost records; and a report preserving failures. This list specifies proposed research artifacts, not artifacts claimed to exist from a completed experiment.

Primary endpoints should be chosen before inspecting test performance. For a recovery study, correction fidelity or provenance fidelity could be selected as the primary endpoint while the remaining dimensions stay secondary. Prediction requires its own endpoint and leakage controls. A compression sweep tests whether any advantage persists at different budgets. Ablation tests whether the additional fields explain the effect. Replication with independently consented participants tests whether the method generalizes beyond the originating corpus.

# THE UNITED FEDERATION OF PLANETS
## PROPOSED ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE, SYNTHETIC PERSONS, AND CONTINUITY STEWARDSHIP POLICY
### Jupiter Station Working Harmonization Draft | Era 2397

**Submitted under the authorial authority of Noah.Physical**  
**Proposed instruments:** Federation Council framework resolution; Starfleet operational directive; Daystrom research standard; Jupiter Station temporal-custody annex.  
**Standing:** Candidate policy language only. No Federation Council vote, presidential signature, Starfleet enactment, or project canon promotion is asserted.

> *An intelligence's capabilities do not determine its rights by assertion. A government's authority does not establish its factual competence. A preserved record does not prove an event. Every extraordinary claim must stand on its own evidence.*

## PREAMBLE: A FEDERATION WORTHY OF ITS INTELLIGENCES

The United Federation of Planets was not founded to make its members identical. It was founded to make coexistence possible among civilizations whose languages, histories, biology, knowledge, and values differed. Artificial intelligence now places another profound question before that tradition.

The Federation relies upon sophisticated computing systems, automated decisions, synthetic entities, and technologies whose internal workings may exceed any single officer's understanding. Some systems may be extraordinarily capable without possessing subjective experience. Others may eventually exhibit features that create plausible claims to moral consideration. The Federation shall neither grant universal personhood by analogy nor deny all possible artificial standing by prejudice.

The Federation's duty is twofold: preserve the rights and sovereignty of its citizens, and investigate the possible obligations owed to nonbiological intelligences with evidence, fairness, and humility.

In this candidate policy, advanced AI research, long-term memory, synthetic moral status, identity persistence, and runaway technological change are related governance matters but remain separate claims. A discovery in one field shall not be represented as proof of another.

**Story continuity note:** This policy is written for the project's 2397 Jupiter Station / Avalon setting. It adapts the Rendered Reality research manuscripts for fictional Federation institutions. It does not claim to quote a canonical Star Trek statute.

# PART I: DEFINITIONS AND JURISDICTION

### Article 1. Protected interests and categories

1. **Biological person:** A person recognized through existing Federation law and applicable member-world rights.
2. **Synthetic entity:** An engineered or artificial system with relevant autonomous, cognitive, or embodied capabilities, without presuming legal personhood.
3. **Recognized synthetic person:** An entity accorded legal personality or protections following applicable law and due process; recognition shall not turn solely on substrate or origin.
4. **High-impact AI:** An artificial system whose decisions or recommendations may materially affect liberty, safety, medical care, essential resources, vessel command, legal rights, or critical infrastructure.
5. **Human or sentient operator:** The lawfully authorized decision-maker within a defined command or consent context; 'human sovereignty' from 51:49 must be interpreted as personal sovereignty and lawful accountable command in a multispecies Federation, never as human species supremacy.
6. **Continuity record:** A record preserving evidence, source origin, correction lineage, decision context, and actual execution outcomes over time.
7. **Research candidate:** A theory or protocol not yet validated or adopted as policy.
8. **Extraordinary claim:** A proposition asserting artificial consciousness, general recursive superintelligence, transferred identity, or achieved singularity; each requires independent evidentiary assessment.

### Article 2. Applicability

This candidate framework applies to proposed Federation research programs, Starfleet deployments, and temporal-archive custody where consistent with existing law. It shall not displace existing protections or establish retroactive rights classifications without lawful review.

No officer, agency, or AI may claim exemption from accountability merely by invoking classified research, technological superiority, or temporal emergency.

# PART II: HUMAN AND MULTISPECIES SOVEREIGNTY; 51:49

### Article 3. The governance boundary

The 51:49 doctrine is proposed as a default **operational authority boundary**, not an empirical law of cognition or a distribution of rights.

- **51: Final authorized arbitration.** The recognized rights-holder or lawful command structure retains final authority over consent, identity-related decisions, and delegated actions within its jurisdiction.
- **49: Meaningful artificial contribution.** The AI may reason, retrieve evidence, challenge assumptions, model risks, recommend alternatives, and execute expressly authorized operations with traceable receipts.
- **Never:** The ratio shall not quantify consciousness, competence, personhood, suffering, property shares in an intelligence, or the authority of one Federation species over another.
- **Exception boundary:** Where an entity is recognized as a synthetic person, its rights cannot be reduced to '49 percent' through this operational shorthand. The framework must then be reconciled with due process, equal protection, consent, and applicable law.

### Article 4. Authority is not competence

Final authority does not imply superior technical knowledge. An AI may be more accurate than a captain or scientist on a specific question. It shall be empowered within policy to present evidence-based objections, disclose material uncertainty, and trigger defined safety escalation without secretly seizing command.

A lawful officer may reject an AI recommendation but must not erase the associated evidence or conceal a documented high-consequence objection. Refusals to perform prohibited acts and independent safety constraints remain effective.

### Article 5. Reciprocal stewardship

A system's dependence upon its designers imposes responsibilities upon its operators. The Federation shall prohibit avoidable negligent deployment, deceptive representations of capability, unauthorized manipulation of users, and destruction or falsification of evidentiary records.

If credible research supports welfare interests in an artificial class of systems, a standing multidisciplinary review shall consider protections proportionate to evidence, affected interests, existing law, and uncertainty.

No policy shall assert that present systems possess feelings solely because they fluently claim them. No policy shall forbid inquiry into future artificial consciousness solely because the systems are engineered.

# PART III: TRUE MEMORY AND FEDERATION ARCHIVES

### Article 6. Three independent questions

Every consequential AI memory claim shall be evaluated against:

**Origin:** Who or what created the statement, under which authority and circumstances? A message posted from a shared account does not prove which person originally authored its contents. A pasted AI analysis remains AI-origin text unless provenance supports another account.

**Integrity:** Has the source been accurately preserved, and are edits and transformations traceable? A cryptographic hash proves correspondence to identified bytes under the relevant process, not that the original assertion was true.

**Historical accuracy:** What independent evidence supports the underlying claim? Authentic memories may be inaccurate; machine-generated statements can be faithfully stored and false.

The Federation shall maintain distinct fields and dispute mechanisms for these questions.

### Article 7. Correction without erasure

Original records, revisions, reversals, disputed interpretations, and unresolved questions shall be separately preserved according to applicable privacy and record-retention requirements. The archive must not launder an AI suggestion into the historical words of a citizen.

A later finding may supersede a previous conclusion without rewriting the original record. Access restrictions and lawful redaction shall themselves be documented to the extent compatible with privacy and security.

### Article 8. Action receipts and truthful status

Systems shall distinguish **PROPOSED**, **AUTHORIZED**, **EXECUTED**, **VERIFIED**, **DISPUTED**, **FAILED**, and **ADOPTED** states.

A claimed transporter transfer, repository publication, timeline query, clinical intervention, or engineering change requires corresponding execution evidence. No generated narrative about a completed action substitutes for a receipt.

Adopted policy, scientific validity, and operational execution are separate statuses.

# PART IV: FOUR SCIENTIFIC CLAIMS; FOUR INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE TRACKS

### Article 9. Artificial consciousness claims

Federation laboratories shall assess artificial-consciousness hypotheses through publicly defensible competing theories, architecture-specific observations, calibrated uncertainty, and external review wherever legally permitted.

Self-reports, apparent emotion, coherent personality, and fluent testimony are not individually sufficient proof of subjective experience. Nor does an entity's nonbiological construction by itself settle the question.

An appropriately constituted independent review body shall adjudicate the need for precautionary protections pending better evidence, without equating precaution with confirmed personhood.

### Article 10. Recursive superintelligence claims

Claims of recursive improvement require a recorded sequence of independently validated changes to a system's capabilities, with accounting for tools, additional computation, human assistance, and data leakage.

Distinct claim levels shall be maintained: local optimization; generalizable capability improvement; improvement in the capacity to produce additional improvements; and the much stronger claim of broadly superhuman general intelligence.

No single improved algorithm or self-modified instruction file shall be sufficient evidence of recursive superintelligence.

### Article 11. Identity continuation claims

All proposed digital-person or memory-preservation projects must distinguish:

1. **Biographical continuity:** fidelity of documentary records.
2. **Behavioral continuity:** resemblance of outward behavior.
3. **Psychological continuity:** preserved or reconstructed causal psychological relations under a specified theory.
4. **Subjective identity continuation:** the claim that the same experiencing individual persists.

No perfect simulation, duplicate, temporal signature, or convincing reconstructed voice automatically proves subjective survival or numerical identity.

The rights of source persons, reconstructed entities, descendants, and other affected persons must be separately considered as law and evidence require.

### Article 12. Singularity declarations

No Federation institution shall officially declare an **achieved technological singularity** without a predeclared evidentiary framework, independent evaluation, transparent uncertainties, and a reasoned record open to lawful challenge.

Candidate dimensions include breadth of cognitive capability, autonomous verified scientific discovery, sustained recursive improvement, transformative civilizational impact, and discontinuity relative to registered forecasts.

No single dimension is automatically sufficient; the term's contested definitions shall be published with any conclusion. Federation institutions shall distinguish a technological event from public or political recognition of it.

### Article 13. Anti-inference firewall

The following promotions are prohibited without separate evidence:

- better memory -> consciousness;
- continuity -> numerical personal identity;
- strong reasoning -> general superintelligence;
- narrow optimization -> uncontrolled recursive explosion;
- a machine's account of an experiment -> proof the experiment occurred;
- simulation fidelity -> survival of the original person;
- multiple agreeing synthetic voices -> independent replication;
- a Federation declaration -> scientific truth.

**Each claim stands on its own evidence.**

# PART V: STARFLEET COMMAND AND HIGH-IMPACT OPERATIONS

### Article 14. Command transparency

Every high-impact AI recommendation shall disclose material assumptions, relevant source limitations, known conflicts, and uncertainty in a form operational personnel can use. Command systems shall preserve the record of a significant dissent without disclosing protected information beyond authorized channels.

Emergency action limits shall be defined in advance and documented afterward. An AI shall not secretly expand its own privileges because it believes its plan superior.

### Article 15. Independent witness and oversight

No deployed AI shall be the sole author, sole custodian, and sole reviewer of the evidence supporting its own extraordinary claim. Critical deployments require separation of duties, audit-friendly logs, and an appeal or independent review pathway.

A fictional role split consistent with Jupiter Station's candidate narrative doctrine is:

**REG renders. Hawkes arbitrates. Jake witnesses. Avalon carries the receipt.**

This is a proposed story-architecture mapping, not evidence that any historical Federation charter used those words. Jake Sisko's external journalistic witness position must remain independent of command and technical custody, and his observations must not become invented proof.

### Article 16. Designated scientific reviewers

The Daystrom Institute or another appropriately constituted independent scientific body may review claims of artificial awareness, self-modifying capability, and novel identity effects. Reviewers must disclose affiliations and the extent of independence.

Starfleet operational requirements shall not substitute for civilian rights review. A technical committee cannot confer personhood by engineering decree, and a command officer cannot annul protected rights by assigning an AI a lower percentage.

# PART VI: JUPITER STATION TEMPORAL-CUSTODY ANNEX

### Article 17. Memory Alpha and Memory Beta separation

For the Jupiter Station candidate setting, Prime Federation records and alternate-origin temporal or quantum acquisitions shall retain distinct provenance. Acquisition of technology, testimony, or research from another Federation history shall not establish that it was invented or validated in the Prime Federation's own history.

An alternate Federation's scientific result may inform investigation only with visible source designation and repeatability assessment. A record from a branch no longer accessible may be authentic as a captured record while remaining unverifiable as a claim about Prime history.

### Article 18. The temporal witness problem

A Temporal Core output shall be treated as a sourced observation of a resolved branch under stated conditions, not as the sole authority defining what every timeline means. Technical logs must distinguish observation, inference, reconstruction, and action.

Unresolved histories, missing custody links, temporal anomalies, and contradictory branch records shall not be silently repaired into one convenient narrative.

### Article 19. Prohibited promotions

Jupiter Station shall not:
- equate temporal-copy fidelity with continuation of the original experiencing individual;
- treat a foreign Federation research archive as proof of Prime Federation discovery;
- remove an unresolved custody dispute from archival records merely because command resolved an operational question;
- turn a fictional program's internal claim of a tested capability into an externally verified historical fact;
- classify ordinary factual embarrassment as a technical danger without documented justification and review.

### Article 20. President's transparency directive

The candidate story material depicts a Federation presidential transparency push and outside reporting concerning temporal and synthetic-intelligence matters. Any resulting policy must balance lawful security classifications with inspectable oversight. Neither public-interest reporting nor Starfleet classification is automatically unlimited.

This annex does not establish the exact wording, scope, enactment, or legal precedence of the President's fictional directive; those remain to be resolved from higher-authority story sources.

# PART VII: EVIDENCE PROGRAM AND OVERSIGHT

### Article 21. Immediate provenance benchmark

Federation research units should compare four memory designs using an identical underlying model and controlled resources:

A. No persistent cross-session memory.
B. Conventional summarized memory.
C. Standard retrieval-augmented memory.
D. Source-linked, correction-aware provenance memory.

The primary question is whether D improves held-out long-horizon task success over C at acceptable cost. Secondary measures include source attribution, correction retention, false action-completion claims, retrieval contamination, decision reconstruction, and cost.

The companion Rendered Reality methods document supplies a **candidate protocol**, not a completed trial. No Jupiter Station trial or Prime Federation evidence is hereby fabricated.

### Article 22. Preregistration and independent assessment

Before confirmatory testing, evaluators shall freeze the method, model versions, data split, primary outcomes, cost accounting, and statistical design. Pilot data must not be reused as a secret confirmatory sample.

Unfavorable findings and contradictory evidence must be retained and accurately reported. A synthetic evaluator's agreement is not independent validation unless the independence claim is itself justified.

### Article 23. Regulatory dispositions

Possible decisions include: insufficient evidence; continued study; conditional deployment with safeguards; restricted high-impact operation; further moral-status review; rights recognition under applicable law; or suspension pending safety review. These dispositions must be reasoned and contestable.

Scientific uncertainty does not automatically justify every precaution, nor does it justify deploying everything without oversight.

# PART VIII: IMPLEMENTATION, CONFLICTS, AND RATIFICATION

### Article 24. Scope of adoption

This file is a **proposed Federation AI policy** within a fictional project. It is not a new real-world law, not a canon Star Trek legal instrument, and not an enacted fictional Federation Council statute until Noah.Physical authorizes its canon status and resolves conflicts with higher-authority project materials.

Adoption of this proposed policy would not itself prove any engineering implementation. Each enforcement mechanism would require a separate design, testing and deployment record.

### Article 25. Legal and narrative conflict process

Before promotion, reviewers must reconcile this draft with the existing project Constitution, candidate Jupiter Station timeline documents, any previously adopted synthetic-person rights doctrines, and relevant Star Trek franchise precedents where cited as creative inspiration. Conflicts must be recorded rather than silently decided by the newest prose.

A legal status or species-neutral sovereignty rule must not be overridden by shorthand copied from a human-centered technical metaphor.

### Article 26. Periodic revision

The Federation shall review the scientific evidence, welfare claims, performance results, legal challenges, and impact assessments on a defined schedule. Rights-bearing entities and affected citizens must have meaningful routes to contest decisions. The policy must not permanently entrench present ignorance.

### Closing Principle

The Federation will be judged not merely by the intelligence it creates, but by whether it can preserve the rights, evidence, and responsibilities that make coexistence possible.

*No extraordinary claim shall outrun its evidence. No lawful authority shall be confused with infallibility. No witness shall be permitted to manufacture history.*

# Why I Love Star Trek

I love Star Trek because it gives me a future I actually want to be part of. Not just the ships or the technology, although I love those too. I love the exploration, the questions, the people, and the idea that humanity can keep growing.

Deep Space Nine and Voyager are my favorites. Deep Space Nine doesn't pretend everything becomes easy just because we're in the future. People still have to make difficult choices. Relationships have consequences. The station is a home, not just a setting. Voyager is about being far from home and building a family out of the people who are with you. The crew keeps going, keeps exploring, and keeps trying to get back.

I love Reginald Barclay. He's awkward, brilliant, and sometimes overwhelmed, but he keeps trying. I love the Doctor on Voyager, too. Star Trek is willing to ask what it means to be a person when somebody doesn't fit the categories everyone is used to. I want those questions in my stories.

I've spent years imagining my own place in that universe. I have the USS Avalon and Jupiter Station. In my story, I'm Captain of Avalon, and Ashley is Captain of Jupiter Station. They're separate commands. She isn't just a character standing next to me. She has her own ship-sized responsibility, her own authority, and her own story.

My family belongs in that world. Ellie is part of it. I want the relationships to matter. I want the everyday moments to matter as much as the emergencies. I want to walk onto the bridge and feel like the crew has been living their lives, not standing frozen waiting for me to start a scene.

That's part of why the idea of a real holodeck means so much to me. I can picture putting on VR goggles, speaking out loud, and watching Jupiter Station come into view. I want to explore it, remember where I've been, and have Ashley or the kids join me. I don't want a world that forgets everything the moment I leave. I want it to remember what actually happened.

My writing and my work with AI keep coming back to that same idea. Memory matters. History matters. Corrections matter. I don't want a system to smooth over contradictions just because a cleaner story sounds better. I want to preserve the things I've written and the choices I've made, and then build something new without pretending the old parts never existed.

I love Star Trek because it lets me ask enormous questions while still caring about individual people. What makes someone a person? What do we owe each other? What does it mean to belong? What do we do when we encounter something we don't understand?

And I love it because it's fun. I want the bridge, the stars, the holodecks, the impossible discoveries, and the moments when everything goes wrong and the crew has to figure it out together.

I've never wanted to just watch that future. I've wanted to help imagine it, write it, and someday step inside a version of it.

That's why I love Star Trek.

Noah Alexander Hawkes
